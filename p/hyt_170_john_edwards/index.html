Life story

Negative search in Stepney, the parish that contained Limehouse until 1730 (FreeREG St Dunstan detail pages read). No Elizabeth Edwards baptised in Middlesex 1660-1680 is the daughter of a John Edwards, chirurgeon or surgeon. The Stepney Elizabeths are daughters of John Edwards, ginman, of Meeting House Alley, and Hester (17 Feb 1669/70); Robert, labourer, and Elizabeth (25 Sep 1670); Robert, cord maker, of Spitalfields (27 Oct 1674); Edward, mariner, of Limehouse, and Mary (1 Dec 1675); Thomas, carpenter (6 Apr 1678); and Edward, mariner, of Limehouse, and Alice (born 5 Apr, baptised 8 Aug 1679). Elsewhere there is Elizabeth daughter of John and Elizabeth Edwards, Clerkenwell, 18 Jun 1661. The only John Edwards baptised in Middlesex 1666-1674 in FreeREG is the son of John, waterman, and Anne, of Meeting House Alley (24 Mar 1667/8), not the goldsmith (born 1670). The silver historians' 'chirurgeon of Limehouse' (Antiques, July 1922, p. 1) is still not tied to any English record. Grade unchanged (Probable). ¶3a

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade · 1 search with no result · 1 open question

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Graded on his link to Elizabeth Edwards. John Edwards, chirurgeon (surgeon), of Limehouse, London, came to Boston shortly before 1688 with his son John Edwards (b. England 1670, d. Boston 1746), the goldsmith, partner of John Allen about 1695-1700. Antiques and K. C. Buhler (1951) state that Elizabeth, wife of John Allen, was the goldsmith's sister, so a daughter of the chirurgeon. Probable on a published secondary statement only; no will, deed or baptism naming Elizabeth as his daughter has been read. ¶1

Searches that found nothing 1

Negative searches: K. C. Buhler's article (Early American Silver and its Makers, 1979) hedges the chirurgeon himself: 'His father, a chirurgeon, is thought to have come to Boston in about the year 1685... the question remains thus far unanswered, was it in Old or New England?' (archive.org snippet chain). The Boston register 1630-1699 (Rec. Comm. 9) has several contemporary John Edwardses (John and Martha, 1689; John and Hannah, 1691; John Edwards and Amey Warren m. 1693; John Edwards and Sibella Newman m. 1694, the goldsmith), and no entry that names the chirurgeon or his wife. So his link to Elizabeth rests on the silver historians alone and stays Probable; his wife (Elizabeth's mother) is not found. ¶2

Open questions and the next record to read 1

Test record: the Stepney baptism of John Edwards, goldsmith, c.1670-71, or a Stepney or PCC record of John Edwards, chirurgeon. ¶3b
