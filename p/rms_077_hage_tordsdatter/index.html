Life story

The same passage cites a document 'sub dato 27de Junij 1712' concerning Heimdal ('ved da passerede Skifte'). read the Øvre Telemark protocol no. 3 (DA 25692) headings for 1712: 15-16 Apr 1712 at fol. 116b-117a (image 121) is followed directly by 9 Jul 1712 at fol. 117b (image 122). No 27 Jun 1712 Heimdal entry was recorded in the protocol, so the 1712 skifte (possibly after Hage's husband Knud) was probably a private or lensmann skifte not copied into the court book. ¶3

Named at the end of the 1733 probate of Anders Knudsen, søndre Heimdal, Treungen (Øvre Telemark skifteprotokoll no. 4, fol. 401b): Ole Salvesen, married to the deceased's sister Torbiør Knudsdatter, was to keep 'S[ver?]moderen Hage Tordsdaatter' for life with clothing, food and care. The word before her name is hard to read; 'Svermoderen' (mother-in-law) would make her the mother of Torbiør Knudsdatter and so, probably, of Anders Knudsen. Speculative until the 1712 skifte at Heimdal (cited in the same passage as 27 Jun 1712, protocol 1703-23, DA 25692) is read. ¶1

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Upgrade (Speculative to Probable, 1 Nov 2026): the hard word at the top of DA 25668 image 406 (fol. 401b), line 2, was re-read on a full-resolution crop. The passage runs: 'at Ole Salvesøn, der er gift med afdøde Anders Knudsøns Søster Torbior Knudsdaatter, skal forsørge Swermoderen Hage Tordsdaatter til hendes [dødsdag] forsvarligen med Klæder og Føde samt fornøden opvartning'. The word reads 'Swermoderen' (svermoderen, the mother-in-law); the S, w, -er- and -moderen are all clear, and no other reading fits the letters. So Hage Tordsdatter was the mother of Ole Salvesen's wife Torbiør Knudsdatter, whom the same sentence calls the sister of the deceased Anders Knudsen. Raised from Speculative to Probable as Anders's mother: the record names her as mother of his sister, and her lifelong care is settled inside Anders's own estate, which points to her living on his farm. Not Confirmed, because the entry does not say Anders and Torbiør were full siblings (both are Knud's children, but a half-sibling through another wife of Knud is not excluded) and does not call Hage Anders's mother in so many words. ¶2
