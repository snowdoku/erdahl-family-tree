About the family

Wife of Thomas Hayter of White Sands, Lot 64, PEI, and mother of Isabella (b. 1840), James (b. 1842), Jane Eliza (b. 1845) and Sarah Catharin White Hayter (b. 1849), according to the PEI baptism indexes. Probably also the mother of Thomas Hayter (b. England 1831) - see her husband's record for why that is Probable rather than Confirmed. ¶1

Family history (secondary): Ron Hayter's PARO genealogy file (1994, 1996) also gives her as Martha Elizabeth Gibbs and says she died before the 1881 census. In 1996 he noted, as an untested candidate only, a Martha Elizabeth Gibbs, daughter of Thomas and Mary Gibbs, christened at St James, Paddington, London, on 6 September 1812 (brother William christened 2 April 1815). Speculative lead; no marriage of a Thomas Hayter to a Gibbs was found in the FamilySearch England marriage indexes 1820-35. ¶10

Life story

Origins (FamilySearch signed in; no grade change). No Gibbs family in the Nova Scotia church-record index of 1815-1840 fits as her parents: the indexed Gibbs entries are the Cape Breton (Sydney) family of James and Ann Gibbs, Patrick Gibbs x Joanna Cox (Halifax RC, 1838), John Gibbs x Ruth Parks (1833), Thomas Gibbs x Ann McNab (1808; she d. 1832), and the Halifax brides Sarah (x John H. Brock, 1825) and Charlotte (x William Robins, 1833). None is tied to her. Leads only: the GRO regimental birth index has Ellen Gibbs (1833) and William Gibbs (1834) born at Halifax to a 9th Foot family (FamilySearch 1:1:68KX-8Q6B); Ron Hayter's family history notes 'Mr. and Mrs. K. C. Gibbs' arriving on PEI on 11 Sep 1832, five weeks after the Halifax baptism (test record: the Charlottetown Royal Gazette shipping news, Sep 1832, for the vessel and her port of departure). ¶16

Maiden name - candidates tested in wave 6, none proved. (a) WHITE: the 1829 Thomas Hayter x Elizabeth White marriage at St Martin-in-the-Fields is ruled out, because that couple stayed in England (Kensington, 1851). Murray River had several White households in 1864, so 'Sarah Catharin White Hayter' (b. 1849) may honour a neighbour. (b) WAKE: 'Wake' is a middle name in the next generation: Joseph Wake Hayter (b. 1883), son of Thomas Hayter and Grace Amelia Moore, and Humphrey Wake Moore (b. 1874), son of Humphrey Moore and Jane Eliza. In 1881 that Jane Eliza (Janett) Moore was of Scottish origin and Anglican, so she was not Jane Eliza Hayter. The Wake name therefore most likely came from the Moore side, not from Martha Elizabeth. (c) TOLMAN: Charles Tolman Hayter (b. 1884). A Charles Tolman married a Hannah Wake at Bridgwater, Somerset, on 2 April 1832, so the Tolman and Wake names travel together. Again this is probably Moore-side kin, and it is a lead only. ¶3

Maiden name confirmed again: 'Martha Elizabeth Gibbs' in the Halifax baptism of 1832, as in the PEI baptism card of 1840. The Paddington christening of a Martha Elizabeth Gibbs (6 Sep 1812, daughter of Thomas and Mary) remains a candidate only; not checked on the image this session. ¶15

Maiden name GIBBS: the PEI baptism card for Isabella Hayter, born 5 June 1840 at White Sands and baptised 21 February 1842 by Rev. John McLennan, St John's Presbyterian Church, Belfast (record book 2, page 133), names the father as Thomas Hayter and the mother as 'Martha Elizabeth Gibbs' (Canada, PEI Baptism Card Index 1721-1885, FamilySearch; card image read). The card is the PEI Division of Vital Statistics abstract of the church register, so a faithful index. Name changed to Martha Elizabeth /Gibbs/. ¶8

Death: before 1881. Thomas Hayter, 73, was a widower in the 1881 census of Lot 64 (page 42, family 166). The 1863 relief to 'Mrs Thomas Hayter' shows her alive then. So she died 1863-1881. ¶11

1841 census: she is very probably the woman aged 16-45 in Thomas Hayter's household of six at Lot 64 (1841 census of PEI, Kings County District 15, image 524), which puts her birth between about 1795 and 1825. The census names only heads of family. The household was Church of England (image 525), which fits the infant baptisms of her children in 1842 and 1851. The birthplace page (image 526) counts 3 or 4 English-born and 3 Island-born members without saying which. It therefore cannot show whether she was an English immigrant or an Island-born second wife, and the second-wife possibility stays open. ¶7

Her maiden name is unknown. The middle name 'White' given to her daughter Sarah Catharin White Hayter in 1849 is a possible pointer to a family surname; that is a guess to test, not a finding. ¶2

Gibbs on PEI (leads only; no person added). (1) NATHANIEL GIBBS, shipwright, died Charlottetown 12 Dec 1849 'in the 79th year of his age', having 'resided on this Island for nearly forty years' (The Islander, 21 Dec 1849, R. J. Reid transcription, Island Register rjreiddeaths.html); he was on the Assembly's pauper relief lists of 1842-1847 (Journals of the House of Assembly of PEI, Canadiana oocihm.9_00960_40-45). Born about 1771 and on PEI from about 1810, he could be of Martha Elizabeth's family only if she was born or left in England before he came; nothing ties him to her, and he is not the 'K. C. Gibbs' who arrived in 1832. (2) Later PEI Gibbses are a William Gibbs (beacon work, 1852), Edward Gibbs (1856) and James Gibbs (1858) in the Assembly journals, and Mattins Gibbs of Cascumpec, Lot 6, d. 1 Aug aged 30, leaving a wife and three children (Islander, 1861-79 series); the Island Register surname list puts Gibbs only in Lots 2, 5, 6 and 35 (western and central PEI, far from Lot 64). None is a 'K. C. Gibbs'. (3) The Halifax baptism was Catholic, and the Lot 2-6 Gibbses were in a largely Catholic district, but that is no evidence of kinship. ¶19a

Bridge search at the PEI end (no grade change, stays Probable on the link to Thomas Hayter per audit 11). No PEI record found on FamilySearch names Thomas junior with her: his 1862 marriage has no parents column, the 1856 lease (recited in the c.1895 Land Purchase deed) names no parents, and no Hayter estate file or will survives for the family before 1921. See the note on Thomas Hayter senior for the records still to read: Thomas junior's death registration of 1906-1911 if it exists (PARO), and her own death, 1863-1881, not yet found. ¶21

Poor relief, 1863: The PEI House of Assembly's supply resolutions of 1863 granted £1 for the relief of "Mrs Thomas Hayter", paid through Mr. James Bell of White Sands, Lot 64. She was listed as a wife, not a widow, and the family lived at White Sands. She is very probably Martha Elizabeth, wife of Thomas Hayter senior, who had himself been on relief as "Thomas Hayter, in destitute circumstances" in 1854–56. ¶5

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Grade (unchanged, Probable) on her link to Thomas Hayter junior (see his page): the card names her as Isabella's mother in 1840, but no record names her as the mother of Thomas junior (b. England 1831), and a second marriage after 1831 is not excluded. ¶12

Upgrade: Probable to Confirmed, graded on her link to Thomas Hayter junior (see his page). The St Mary's (Roman Catholic) baptism register of Halifax, Nova Scotia, entry no. 264 of 4 August 1832, names Thomas George, aged nine months, as the 'lawful son of Thomas Hayter and Martha Elizabeth Gibbs' (FamilySearch DGS 005190390, image 124; image read). The continuity to Thomas junior of Lot 64 (birth about October 1831, immigration 1832, the same couple named as Isabella's parents on PEI in 1840, no rival Hayter family) is set out on her husband's record (Thomas Hayter). This also answers the earlier doubt that a second marriage after 1831 was not excluded: she is named as the mother of a child born about October 1831. Supersedes the 'Grade (unchanged, Probable)' note and the 'Probably also the mother of Thomas Hayter' wording on this record. Coordinator: veto to Probable if the Halifax-to-PEI identification is read as Probable under audit 4. ¶14

Corrections 2

Correction: the earlier note that Isabella's reported birth might come from a Hooper baptism is superseded: the card plainly reads Hayter, Thomas and Martha Elizabeth Gibbs, St John's Presbyterian, Belfast. The 'White' and 'Wake' maiden-name leads are superseded by Gibbs. ¶9

Correction: Audit 11 (2 Nov 2026): lowered from Confirmed to Probable, graded on the link to Thomas Hayter junior (see his page). The St Mary's (RC) baptism at Halifax, 4 Aug 1832 (entry 264, DGS 005190390 image 124; the auditor checked the saved crop: 'Thomas George aged nine Months lawful son of Thomas Haytar and Martha Elizabeth Gibbs'), names the parents of an infant Thomas George. The question is the step from that infant to Thomas Hayter of High Bank, Lot 64. That is an identification across places (Halifax, Nova Scotia, to Prince Edward Island), and audit 4 assigns it to the parents' grade. No record between 1832 and the son's 1862 marriage names him with these parents: the 1841 census lists only the head and an unnamed boy under 16, the 1862 marriage register has no parents' columns, and the 1856 lease to 'Thomas Hayter, the Younger' has been seen only in a family history. So the identity rests on (1) the birth month (about Oct-Nov 1831 against the 1901 census's October 1831), (2) the 1832 immigration year, (3) the parents found on PEI by 1840, and (4) the absence of another Hayter family on PEI. Points 1-2 are a name-and-age match, and point 4 is elimination. Both stay Probable (BRIEF grading rule; audit 3: 20 years or more with no record naming the child, and the far-end records name no parent). The pair match of audit 8 rule 3 needs the two people to be found TOGETHER at both ends; here the parents are paired at both ends, but the child is not named with them at the PEI end. The match is very strong, so this is the top of Probable. ¶20a

Alternatives considered and set aside 2

Maiden name (lead only): Her daughter born in 1849 was baptised Sarah Catharin White Hayter. An extra name like "White" was often the mother's maiden name or the surname of a godparent. The White family were prominent landholders at Murray River and White Sands. It is worth testing whether Martha Elizabeth was a White. This is speculative and needs the 1851 baptism register entry or a marriage record. ¶6

Paddington candidate: the 1812 St James Paddington christening of a Martha Elizabeth Gibbs (daughter of Thomas and Mary) is not in the FamilySearch indexes. A Martha Elizabeth Gibbs born about 1815 died at Shoreditch in 1874 still named Gibbs (GRO death index), and another died at Chelsea in 1856; either may be the Paddington child, so that candidate is no stronger. ¶17a

Searches that found nothing 3

No marriage of any Hayter-variant groom to a Martha in 1820-1832 was found in FreeREG, the England-wide marriage index or the county marriage collections of Somerset, Bristol, Dorset, Wiltshire, Hampshire, Devon or Westminster. Either the marriage is in a register not yet indexed, or Martha Elizabeth was a second wife married in PEI after 1832, in which case she may not be the mother of Thomas Jr (b. 1831). ¶4

English marriage (negative): FamilySearch historical-record searches (signed in) for a marriage of Thomas Hayter with Martha (Elizabeth) Gibbs in England, 1822-1840, and for any Martha Gibbs with a Hayter spouse, return only the PEI baptism card for Isabella. The Thomas and Martha Hayter couples in the Wiltshire church records index are the later Wiltshire family already ruled out. The Paddington candidate (bapt. 6 Sep 1812) is still untested; the marriage may be in a London register that is not indexed. ¶13

Baptism search (negative): FreeREG, all counties, has no 'Martha Elizabeth' Gibbs baptism 1795-1820. The 25 plain 'Martha Gibbs' baptisms of 1804-1815 are in Warwickshire, Norfolk/Suffolk, Kent, Herts, Somerset, Lincs and Bermondsey (16 Jun 1811); none is a Martha Elizabeth, and FreeREG holds no Paddington baptisms, so the St James Paddington candidate (6 Sep 1812, daughter of Thomas and Mary) is untouched. London Gibbs baptisms 1810-16 in FreeREG (Bethnal Green, Covent Garden, Holborn, Stepney, Clerkenwell) show no Martha. ¶18a

Test records: the 1856 Cunard lease of 100 acres at High Bank to 'Thomas Hayter, the Younger' (PARO, Lot 64 leases); the 1861 census return for Lot 64, if the household schedule survives with names; the elder Thomas's Charlottetown Poor House admission or death entry (about 1885); or Thomas junior's death or burial record (1901-1911), if it names his parents. The upgrade note is superseded on the grade only; the baptism reading stands, and the earlier 'Probable' notes are restored, with the Halifax baptism as their strongest support. The baptism does settle her as the mother of the Halifax infant, so a second marriage after 1831 is no longer an objection. ¶20b
