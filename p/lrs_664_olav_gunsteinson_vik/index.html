Life story

Bygdebok (Nissedal sokn, Vik gnr. 7, canvases 143-144, and Søftestad, canvas 451): 'Gunstein Olavson, son [av] Olav Gunsteinson Vik' took over Søftestad by marrying the widow Sigrid Gjermundsdtr.; under Vik the book lists Olav Gunsteinson's children as [..] (took over half of Vik), Eivind (m. Ragnhild; took over half of Vik), Gunstein (m. the widow Sigrid Gjermundsdtr.), Astri (m. Aslak), [..] (m. Tore), Torbjørg (m. Torjus) and Ingebjørg (m. Pål). Matr. 1685: Olav Gunsteinson held all of Vik, 4 tønner. 'Olav Gunsteinson d truleg [i] 1690-åra'. The book adds that Gunstein sold 5 [merker] of his father's land in Vik in 1692. Dates ABT (word-box rebuild). ¶1

1664 vicar's census, Nissedal annex (35508 view 77 left, p. 126): farm Vik, 4 t, two farmers aged 40, the first name read '[Oluf?]' (abbreviated, uncertain) and 'Torbiørn'. The book likewise says the 1660s lists name Olav at Vik and that 'Dei er båe 40 år', so Olav was born about 1624. ¶2

Corroboration of the book's Vik family: skiftekort 6856 view 1464, Vik i Nissedal, 7 Mar 1719: Even Olssen and Ragnild Wraalsdatter, both dead; children Sigri and Hagel; land in Vik 2 tønner (= the book's Eivind Olavson m. Ragnhild, who took over half of Vik). It does not name Gunstein. No card for Olav Gunsteinson himself was found among the Vik i Nissedal cards (views 1464-1467 carry only 1719, 1722, 1729 and 1730s entries). ¶3

Jurisdiction correction for the test records: Kviteseid prestegjeld with its Nissedal annex belonged to Øvre Telemark ØSTFJELSKE sorenskriveri around 1689-1703 (the Digitalarkivet municipality list for østfjelske skifteprotokoll 25699 includes Kviteseid; the vestfjelske protocols 25690/25691 and tingbok A 17, 29625, list only Fyresdal, Lårdal, Mo, Rauland and Vinje). So a 1690s probate after Olav Gunsteinson is to be sought in the østfjelske skifteprotokoller (no. 2 1694-97 = 25697; no. 1 1689-94 not yet identified), and Gunstein's 1692 sale of land in Vik in the østfjelske tingbok A I 18 1691-94 (29643) or pantebok 1692-1701 (14504), not in the vestfjelske books. read vestfjelske 25691 views 70-119 (Oct 1700-Nov 1701): no Nissedal entries, as expected. ¶6

The bygdebok's Vik text (canvas 144, re-) adds: 'Lensmann Jon [..] vitna at [..] Olav Gunsteinson Vik [hadde] 4 søner' and that Olav let them divide the farm before he died; 'Det vart [drøfta] på dette tinget om Vik var stor nok gard til 2 brukarar'. So a court session (probably about 1701-02, the dates on the same lines) recorded sworn testimony naming Olav's four sons. ¶7a

Not raised. Searched østfjelske skifteprotokoll no. 2, 1694-97 (25697, 145 views, image n = sk2009012062 + 4-digit n, no register at either end; the hand is faint): view map v12 ~No 10, v22 No 17 (Dec), v72 No 40 (6 Sept 1695), v73 No 41, v77 No 48-49 (Sept), v78 No 50 (16 Sept) and No 51 (Oct 1695, Brunkeberg?), v79-81 a long estate, v82 'Anno 1696' (March), v92 No 66 (April 1696, Seljord). Headings of the autumn-1695 circuit (v72-81) were scanned without finding a Vik/Nissedal probate, but the farm names in most headings were not legible at the scale read, so this is not a negative. The bygdebok (canvas 144) says only that Olav 'd truleg [i] 1690-åra' and let his sons divide Vik before he died, so there may be no probate. Also: the 8 Oct 1701 skifte after Sigri Giermundsdaatter at Søftestad (25699 No 39, view 64) lists among the Fastegods land that came to 'Enchemanden Gunsteen Olsen' by inheritance after a father, plus shares after a childless brother; whose father is not legible enough to use. A careful transcription of that passage (view 64 left foot, right head) is the cheapest test of the Vik descent. ¶8a

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Graded on his link to his son Gunstein Olsen (see his page): Probable. The relationship comes from a compiled bygdebok only; it is supported by the patronymic, the farm-to-farm move the book documents, and the 1692 land sale in Vik. No primary record read names Gunstein as his son. ¶4

Grade unchanged: Probable. ¶7c

Open questions and the next record to read 3

Test record: the 1692 deed by which Gunstein Olsen sold land in Vik (Øvre Telemark tingbok/pantebok for 1692; the book cites it as land of his father), or a skifte after Olav Gunsteinson in the 1690s (not on the 6856 Vik i Nissedal cards; look in the Øvre Telemark skifteprotokoll 1689-97 and in the tingbok for the odel division between his four sons that the book mentions). ¶5

Test record: that tingbok entry (Øvre Telemark østfjelske tingbok A I 20b 1699-1700 = 30508, or the 1700-04 volume) would name Gunstein as Olav's son and settle this link. ¶7b

Next: østfjelske tingbok A I 20b (30508) for lensmann Jon's testimony (the bygdebok's surrounding lines mention 1701 and a 1728 dispute involving Hans Gunnulvson of Vik bnr. 6, so the vestfjelske tingbok about 1728 is a second place to look). ¶8b
