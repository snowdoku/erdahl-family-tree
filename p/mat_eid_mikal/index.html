About the family

Elder brother of John P. Eidem. His is the one gravestone in the family that spells the patronymic out - Mikal PAULSEN Eidem - which is what fixes the father's name as Paul and so corrects every American 'Eidem, P.' in this line from Pedersen to Paulsen. ¶7

Wife and children, from the Find a Grave family panel: wife Beret Eidem 1872-1922, and four daughters — Minnie Eidem Lund 1895-1987, Emma Clara Eidem Harstad 1899-1946, Mable Berntine Eidem Clemensen 1904-1975 and Christine Pauline Eidem Clemensen 1912-2002. Wave 2's extract of the Minnesota birth index gives the parents of Christine Pauline (b. 23 December 1912, Antelope Valley, Deuel County) as 'EIDEM MIKE' and 'BETSY SLINDE' — so Beret's maiden name was SLIND, and Mikal's marriage is a second Eidem-Slind match in this colony. ¶17

His children as the 1921 book lists them, from the family's own answers: 1. Paul (22 Apr 1893), 2. Minnie (10 Jul 1895), 3. Clara Alma (14 Jan 1897), 4. Emma (10 Jun 1899), 5. Edwin (18 Feb 1902), 6. Mabel Berntine (22 Apr 1904), 7. LUDVIG (30 Oct 1907), 8. Joseph (7 Jun 1910), 9. Christine Pauline (23 Dec 1912), 10. INGVALD (26 Jan 1916). ¶25

Life story

His gravestone is the only one in the family that spells the patronymic out — 'Mikal PAULSEN Eidem' — and it is what fixes the father's given name as Paul and ties the Zoar Lutheran burials to the 1904 printed biography of his brother Peder. ¶1

His gravestone is the only one in his family that spells the patronymic out, 'Mikal PAULSEN Eidem', which is what fixed his father's given name as Paul. (An earlier reading of this project had him married to Brynhild Kulsetmoen, with Anton Bernhard, Nickolai and Benda Elvina as his children; see the Correction notes below — that family belongs to Anders Andersson Eidem.) ¶2

Gravestone, photographed and read: a flat rose-granite marker with ruled bars down each side, inscribed 'FATHER / MIKAL EIDEM' with '1869' down the left margin and '1923' down the right. Beside it lies the matching marker of his wife: 'MOTHER / BERET EIDEM / 1872 - 1922'. A matched pair, cut together. ¶14

Baptism: born 29 JANUARY 1869 and baptised 14 March 1869 at Selbu church. Selbu parish register 1869-1888, page 1 serial no. 14. Father Paul Ingebrigtsen, mother Karen Pedersdatter, residence entered 'Eiden' for Eidem. The parish clerk's copy (Selbu 1860-1877, p. 109 no. 13) dates the baptism 29 March 1869 instead. ¶21

Printed source: his given name, spelled Mikal, and his birth year 1869 occur on page 206 of Selbu i fortid og naatid, bind 4 (1992), chapter BRENNHAUGEN. ¶26

Emigration, Confirmed: Trondheim police office, emigrant protocol no. 7 (1885-1888), page 22, group 00148, sequence 247, line 39 - 'Mikkel P. Eidem', male, year of birth 1869 (calculated), age 17, unmarried, household position 'Soen' (son), residence Selbo, destination 'Chicago Ill', ticket paid in America. Expedited 8 April 1886, steamer Hero, Allan Line. He travelled with his mother Karin Pedersdatter (line 38) and his nine-year-old brother John (line 40). An age of 17 on 8 April 1886 fits his birth of 28 January 1869 exactly. ¶8

Emigration: left Trondheim 8 April 1886 aged 17 with his widowed mother and his nine-year-old brother John, on a ticket paid for in America. Trondheim police office, Emigrant protocols no. 7, group 00148, page 22, line 39, person 0019, entered as 'Mikkel P. Eidem', ug (unmarried), household position Soen (son), residence Selbo, destination Chicago Ill. ¶23

1895 Minnesota state census: 'Mike Eidem', 26, born Norway, head of a household in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota (sheet mnv290_73 p. 184 line 8), with his wife 'Betsy Eidem', 23, born Norway, their son Paul M. Eidem aged 2 years 1 month born Minnesota, his mother Carrie P. Eidem 61, and his unmarried brother John Eidem 19. ¶9

He is buried at Zoar Lutheran, Deuel County, South Dakota, as are his mother Kari and his brother John P. Eidem. ¶10

1895 state census: Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, page 184, line 8 - 'Eidem Mike, 26, M, W, born Norway', resident of Minnesota 9 years 1 month and of the enumeration district 5 years 2 months. Aged 26 in June 1895 he was born about 1869, matching the 28 January 1869 on the Zoar Lutheran stone. Nine years and one month in the state puts his arrival in May 1886 - the same month his younger brother John P. gives in 1905. The brothers emigrated together. ¶12

He and Berit later crossed the state line into Antelope Valley Township, Deuel County, South Dakota, where their daughter Christina Paulina was born on 23 December 1912. ¶13

1895 Minnesota state census: Mehurin township, Lac qui Parle County, page 0184 line 8 - 'Eidem Mike, 26, M, W, born Norway, 9 years 1 month a resident of this State, 5 years 2 months a resident of this enumeration district, occupation FARMER, employed 12 months in the past year, father of foreign birth YES, mother of foreign birth YES'. The residence figures put his arrival in Minnesota in May 1886 and his move to Mehurin in April 1890 - so the family first landed somewhere else in Minnesota, almost certainly in Augusta township where his brother Peder and Andrew Eidem already were. ¶19

Census: in June 1895 his household was five people - himself, his wife Betsy (23), their son Paul M. (2 1/2), his widowed mother Carrie P. (61) and his younger brother John (19). ¶20

Church office, Mehurin congregation, 1900: at the organisation of the Mehurin Norwegian Lutheran congregation (Mehurin norsk lutherske menighet) in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota, "Mikkal Eidem" was one of three men elected to the board of deacons/trustees. The congregation was organised at a meeting in Lund's school house on 12 November 1899 by the Rev. I. Tharaldson and adopted its constitution and elected these officers on 6 January 1900. Its synod was the Forenede kirke, the United Norwegian Lutheran Church. The other officers were A. A. Henningsgaard (chairman), Roald R. H. Lee (secretary), Jacob Jorgensen (treasurer), Ole Kravik and H. J. Jellum (the other two deacons) and I. A. Gudal as klokker, the parish clerk and precentor. The congregation had 64 souls in 1900 and 80 in 1914 and built its church in 1910-11 at a cost of $2,500-$2,700 in section 4 of Manfred Township. ¶4

Church office, Zoar congregation, 1915: the officer reported for Zoar Evangelical Lutheran congregation (Zoar evangelisk lutherske menighet, also recorded as Soar frimenighet), Antelope Valley Township, Deuel County, South Dakota, in 1915 was "b. M. Eidem" - the secretary. Zoar was a Hauge Synod congregation organised in 1896, with about 40 souls in 1899 and 89 in 1916, a church costing $2,000 and a burial ground of two acres, which is the Zoar Lutheran Cemetery where he is buried. Its only pastors to 1916 were E. M. Petterson (1896-1901) and T. T. Boe (1901 onwards), the latter resident at Marietta, Minnesota. ¶5

America, from Selbygbogen (1921) pp. 149-150: 'Mikal P. Eidem, Marietta, Minn., brother of the foregoing, born 29 Jan. 1869 at Brendhaugen in Selbu. Emigrated 1886 and came to Lac qui Parle Co., Minn. Soon after married Beret Eliasdatter Slind, born 6 May 1872 at Slind (Reiemoen) in Selbu, and sister of Mrs. Peder P. Eidem. She had come from Selbu in 1892.' He and his brother John lived about four miles south of Marietta, on the west side of the state line, in Deuel County, South Dakota - which is why this nominally Minnesota family buried its dead at Zoar Lutheran in Deuel County. ¶24

Burial plot: Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, lot 1, with his wife Beret (d. 1922), Anton B. Eidem (d. 1962), Marlys Ann Eidem (d. 1934), an undated Mable Eidem, and Oline G. Eidem (d. 1955). ¶16

Death, how it happened - from the Norwegian-American press, datelined the day after. Minneapolis Tidende (Minneapolis, Minn.), Thursday 15 March 1923, page 7, under the headline 'Ma[n]d kvalt i Grusgrav' (Man suffocated in a gravel pit): 'Madison, 10de Marts. Mike Eidem, 60 Aar gammel og boende soendenfor Marietta, blev kvalt, da han soegte Tilflugt mod en voldsom Storm i [et] Hul, som Kvaeget havde gravet op paa Leroy Pratts Farm, 3 Mil soendenfor Marietta. To andre, Gunder Harstad og Elias Slind, der var sammen med Eidem, blev fundet bevidstloese, men blev snart bragt tillive igjen. Eidem blev kvalt, da Jorden styrtede sammen over ham. Han havde vaeret paa en Auktion paa Pratts [Farm].' Translation: 'Madison, 10 March. Mike Eidem, 60 years old and living south of Marietta, was suffocated when he took shelter from a violent storm in a hole the cattle had dug out on Leroy Pratt's farm, three miles south of Marietta. Two others, Gunder Harstad and Elias Slind, who were with Eidem, were found unconscious but were soon revived. Eidem was suffocated when the earth collapsed over him. He had been at an auction on Pratt's farm.' ¶27

So he died in an accident, not of illness: caught by a violent storm while walking home from a farm auction, he and two neighbours crawled into a hollow the cattle had dug in a bank on Leroy Pratt's farm three miles south of Marietta, and the earth came down on them. The other two were dug out unconscious and revived; he was not. The dateline of 10 March 1923 is the day after his recorded death date of 9 March 1923. ¶28

Context: a farm auction in early March is a dispersal sale at the end of the crop year, and the whole neighbourhood attended. That is why three men of the colony were on the road together in a blizzard. ¶30

The case for this identification 2

Birthplace Selbu is inferred from his age and from the family's origin, not from a retrieved Norwegian record. ¶3

Identification: the "Mikkal Eidem" of 1900 and the "M. Eidem" of 1915 are rated a Probable rather than a Confirmed identification with this man. Neither entry prints a patronymic or a middle name. The identification rests on the rarity of the Eidem surname in these two adjoining townships, on his age (31 in January 1900, the right age to be elected a trustee), and on his burial in the churchyard of the very congregation whose secretary "M. Eidem" was. He appears to have held office in both of the settlement's congregations - the United Church one in Minnesota and the Hauge Synod one four miles west across the state line in South Dakota - which was ordinary behaviour in a settlement where the same dozen families filled both. ¶6

Records that disagree 1

Contradiction, recorded rather than smoothed: the paper gives his age as 60. Born 28 January 1869, he was 54. No other Eidem brother died on that date - Peder lived to 1935 and John to 1963 - the place is right to the township and the date matches to the day, so this is Mikal and the printed age is wrong. Newspaper ages for immigrants commonly are. ¶29

Corrections 4

Correction: Mikal Paulsen Eidem's wife was not Brynhild Kulsetmoen. The Lac qui Parle County marriage index records two separate Eidem weddings that this tree had conflated. On 22 March 1893, certificate B93398, 'EIDEM, MIKAL' married 'SLIND, BERETH E.' On 6 May 1901, certificate C01496, 'EIDEM, ANDERS A.' married 'KLEGSETH, BRYNHILD' - Klegseth being the clerk's rendering of Kulsetmo. Brynhild was Anders A. Eidem's wife, and the delayed birth registration of their son Anton Bernhard (filed 1935, Lac qui Parle cert. 1901-10783) names the father as Anders A. Eidem and the mother as Brynhild Kulsethmo in so many words. ¶11

Correction: the stone does NOT spell out the patronymic. The project's wave-2 dossier states that this is 'the only stone in the family that spells the patronymic out', and that it is what fixed the father's given name as Paul. The photographed stone reads only 'MIKAL EIDEM'. The form 'Mikal Paulsen Eidem' exists in the Find a Grave index title and nowhere on the granite, so the evidence that Paul was the father does not come from this stone and has to rest on the 1904 printed biography of Peder P. Eidem instead. ¶15

Correction: Anton Bernhard 'Tony' Eidem (1902-1962), Benda Elvina Eidem (1908-1999) and Nickolai 'Nick' Eidem (1904-1981) are NOT Mikal's children. All three are recorded on Find a Grave as children of ANDERS ANDERSSON EIDEM (1852/3-1932) and Brynhild Kulsetmoen, who married on 6 May 1901; and all three memorials carry biographies written by a descendant of Anders's brother Bardo saying so explicitly. Mikal's own four children are the daughters listed above. ¶18

Correction: the register gives his birth as 29 January 1869; the American records say 28 January. A one-day difference, with the register preferred. ¶22
