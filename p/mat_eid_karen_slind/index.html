About the family

Wife of Peder Paulsen Eidem, whom she married in Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota in 1888; buried at Zoar Lutheran as 'Karen P. Eidem, 7 Sep 1863 - 30 Nov 1915', the P. being her husband's patronymic used as a surname. A Slind of Selbu, like Oline: the two families intermarried twice in two generations. ¶4

Parentage: Selbygbogen (1921) p. 149 gives her as 'Karen Eliasd. Slind, born 7 Sept. 1863 in Selbu, daughter of Elias Larsen Slind (b. 1826) and Marit Estensdatter (b. 16 Apr. 1832 at Guldseth)'. Page 150 adds that Beret Eliasdatter Slind, born 6 May 1872 at Slind (Reiemoen), who married Mikal P. Eidem, was her SISTER - so the two Eidem brothers married two Slind sisters. ¶15

Her mother came out too: Selbygbogen p. 174 records 'Marit Estensd. Slind, Marietta, Minn., born 16 Apr. 1832 at Guldseth, was married to Elias Larsen Slind who died in Selbu. She came to America in 1907 and lived here with her daughters and died 7 Dec. 1917.' Kari was one of those daughters. ¶17

Life story

Known in America as Carrie and buried as 'Karen P. Eidem, 7 Sep 1863 – 30 Nov 1915'. Her husband's 1904 biography names her as 'Carrie (Kari?) Slind', born in Norway in 1863, married in 1888. ¶1

Context: Slind is a farm in Selbu, Sør-Trøndelag, two parishes' worth of neighbours from Eidem. ¶2

Gravestone, photographed and read: she shares one monument with her husband, inscribed 'KAREN P. EIDEM / SEPT. 7, 1863 / NOV. 30, 1915' below 'PEDER P. EIDEM / NOV. 4, 1858 / OCT. 23, 1935'. Both dates Confirmed from the stone. ¶8

Burial plot: Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, lot 2, with her husband, her mother-in-law Kari P. Eidem, and Mrs. Marit E. Slind (1832-1917) — a Slind of the previous generation lying in the same lot, which is the strongest hint on the ground that Marit E. Slind is Karen's own mother. Not proved. ¶9

Emigration: she emigrated in April 1885 and came to Lac qui Parle County the same year; married Peder P. Eidem there on 13 October 1888. They had no children. She and Peder travelled back to Selbu in 1899 and returned in July 1900. She is the 'Kari Paulsen Eidem, b. 1864, residence America' of the Trondheim emigrant protocol of 25 July 1900 - entered under her husband's surname, which has previously caused her to be misread as Peder's sister. ¶16

Her own emigration to America, before her 1888 marriage, has NOT been found. The Trondheim emigrant protocols were swept for every Slind, Slinde and Slinden of Selbu and the only Kari of about the right age leaving before 1888 are Kari T. Slind b. 1861 (1 June 1881, for Byron, Minnesota) and Kari E. Slind b. 1860 (22 April 1885, for 'Vilson Dakota'); Kari R. Slind b. 1863 leaves on 12 June 1889 for La Crosse, Wisconsin, which is a year too late. She may have gone out under a patronymic alone, without the farm byname, in which case only a page-by-page reading of the Selbu entries in protocols nos. 5 to 7 will find her. ¶7

THE EXACT NEXT RECORDS, in order of cheapness: (1) her 1888 Lac qui Parle County marriage return - the county register, not the state index, since Minnesota marriage returns of the 1880s often name both fathers; (2) the 1900 federal census for Peder P. Eidem's household in Lac qui Parle County, which gives her year of immigration and the number of children borne and living, and so brackets her emigration; (3) the Trondheim police emigrant protocols nos. 6 (1882-1885) and 7 (1885-1888) for a woman born 1863 leaving Selbu for Minnesota, which would give her patronymic and farm in one line; (4) the Selbu parish baptisms for a Kari born 7 September 1863, which would give her parents outright; (5) her 1915 obituary in the Madison or Dawson, Minnesota papers; (6) the paid Minnesota Department of Health copy of certificate 1915-MN-006336. ¶14

Return crossing, Confirmed: Trondheim police office, emigrant protocol no. 10 (1895-1902), page 191, group 01392, line 5, sequence 2506, expedited 25 July 1900, ship Salmo, line Hvide Stjerne (White Star) - 'Kari Paulsen Eidem', female, year of birth 1864 (calculated), age 36, married, occupation Arb, residence AMERICA, destination 'Marietta, Minn', ticket paid in America. She is travelling with her husband on the line above and, on the line above him, the nineteen-year-old Oline Gundersen Slind of Selbo. She and Peder had gone home to Selbu on a visit and were bringing two young Slinds back with them. ¶5

Congregation and burial: she died 30 November 1915 and is buried at Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, Antelope Valley Township, Deuel County, South Dakota. Her death falls within the pastorate of the Rev. T. T. Boe, who served Zoar from 1901 and lived at Marietta, Minnesota, and it is covered by Zoar's surviving ministerial register of 1895-1957 on Minnesota Historical Society microfilm M678, reel 44 [ALC 129]. Hers is the earliest of the family's recorded deaths at Zoar and so the earliest burial entry (begravelse) to look for. ¶3

HER PARENTAGE IS STILL NOT SETTLED, and I record here what was eliminated so nobody repeats it. The cheapest hoped-for route - her 1915 Minnesota death certificate, which the brief expected would carry a mother's maiden name - does not work on free sources: the record exists (Carrie Eidem, d. 30 November 1915, Lac qui Parle County, certificate 1915-MN-006336, MNHS legacyId 109306) but the Minnesota Historical Society's free index publishes NO images for deaths in any year and populates the mother's-maiden-name field only for 1955-2001. That negative was established by the slind-us agent and I confirm it stands. ¶10

That is a real finding and it cuts two ways. Either Peder and Kari's children are among the applications where the parent fields were never captured, or - and this must be said - HER MAIDEN NAME MAY NOT HAVE BEEN SLIND AT ALL. The only authority for "Slind" is a single secondary source, the 1904 printed biography of Peter P. Eidem in the Compendium of History and Biography of Central and Northern Minnesota, which says he "married Carrie (Kari?) Slind in 1888" - and the question mark is the transcriber's. Her gravestone at Zoar Lutheran reads only "Karen P. Eidem, 7 Sep 1863 - 30 Nov 1915", with the P. for her husband Peder. No primary record read by anyone in this project gives her a maiden surname. ¶12

I therefore leave her at Probable and do NOT attach her to any Slind parent. Attaching her to Gunder Olsen Slind's household, which is the tempting move, would require Beret Gundersdatter (b. abt 1814-15) to have borne a child at forty-nine, and there is no evidence for it. ¶13

Clean negative on her obituary. She died 30 November 1915. Searching the Minnesota Digital Newspaper Hub for 'Eidem' between 25 November 1915 and 31 March 1916 returns 21 issues and for 'Slind' 5 issues; every hit in a Norwegian-language paper (Minneapolis Tidende of 16 December 1915, 20 January 1916 and 3 February 1916; Fergus Falls Ugeblad of 1 December 1915) is a Norwegian shipping or passenger list in which 'Eidem' is a Tromso shipowner's name, not a death notice. Nothing for her in any Minnesota paper digitised to date. ¶20

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Rated Probable rather than Confirmed on the identification of the 1900 returning passenger with the woman buried at Zoar: the protocol gives only her married name, a calculated birth year of 1864 against a gravestone date of 7 September 1863, and no patronymic, so the match rests on the married surname, the age, the stated American residence and the destination rather than on a patronymic. Nothing contradicts it. ¶6

Confidence raised from Probable to Confirmed: a printed source compiled in 1920-21 from the family's own answers gives her birth date, her birth farm, both her parents, her emigration year, her marriage date and her death date, and every one of them fits the records already in the tree. ¶18

Corrections 1

Correction: the printed 1904 biography of her husband gives her birth date as OCTOBER 7, 1863 - 'Miss Carrie Slind, who was born in Norway October 7, 1863' - while her gravestone at Zoar Lutheran reads KAREN P. EIDEM / SEPT. 7, 1863. I read the original print on archive.org, not a transcription, so this is not a transcription slip. The DAY agrees across two wholly independent sources and the MONTH does not. Both are recorded and neither is discarded. I keep the gravestone's 7 September 1863 as the entered date, because a family-set inscription is a better witness to a birth date than a county biography's prose; but the 1904 book was printed in her lifetime from information her husband supplied, so it is not a weak witness either. Note also that her 1900 emigration entry calculates to 1864, so there are now three readings of her birth and the day 7 is the only element all the dated ones share. ¶19

Searches that found nothing 1

A new and unexpected negative: the Social Security NUMIDENT reverse-parent lookup, which names both parents on every application and which I swept to exhaustion for this surname, contains NO child of Peder P. Eidem who recorded a mother surnamed SLIND. Searching "slind" across all nine Application (SS-5) file units returns 102 records, of which the ten in the E-G file unit are every applicant surnamed E-to-G with a Slind parent anywhere in the record: Ingvald Eidem (mother Bertha Slind), John Alvin Eidem (mother Oline G Slind), Barbara Ann Eidem (mother Oline G Slind), David John Eidem (mother Bernice), Edna Berntine Eidem (mother Mary Slind), Christine Slind Emerson, Linda Slind Edwards, Theresa Caroline Glenn and two others. Peder and Kari's children are not among them. ¶11
