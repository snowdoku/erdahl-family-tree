About the family

David Erdahl's maternal great-great-grandfather, the father of Mayme O. Eidem and so the grandfather of Kathryn (Eidem) Hayter. Identified 11 Oct 2026, the first time this ancestral quarter has been reached at all. ¶1

Named as the father on his daughter's South Dakota birth registration: 'MAMIE EIDEM, state file no. 14515, b. 18 Dec 1906, Union County, mother's name ANNA SATTER, father's name SIVERT EIDEM'. Named again, spelled Sivert, in the 2013 obituary of his youngest daughter Myrtle, which says she 'was born January 4, 1911 near Elk Point, SD to Sivert and Anna (Satter) Eidem' and that 'she grew up on the Brule Creek on the farm settled by her Norwegian immigrant father'. His gravestone spells him Severt. ¶2

David Erdahl's maternal great-great-grandfather, the father of Mayme O. (Eidem) Eidem. Named as SIVERT EIDEM on his daughter Mamie's South Dakota birth certificate of 18 December 1906 (State File Number 14515) and on his son Samuel's (771318), and as 'Sivert' in his daughter Myrtle's 2013 obituary. The 1909 certificate of his son Amos (39448) abbreviates him to 'G EIDEM', which is a clerk's slip. His gravestone has Severt S.; the S. is the patronymic, for his father Sivert Johnson Eidem. ¶8

Sibship, confirmed and ordered: 'He was the youngest member of a family of five, two girls and three boys, namely, Mrs. SIGRI THOMPSON, Mrs. MARIT WEN[N]BLOM, JOHN SIVERTSON EIDEM and [O]LE SIVERTSON EIDEM, who have all preceded him in death.' That is exactly the sibship this file carries, stated by the family in 1940. ¶30

Children, named with their 1940 homes: 'To this union six children were born. Mrs. MI[L]TON LEER, Mrs. ERNEST OVERDAHL of Elk Point, Mrs. RAYMOND NELSON of FAITH; MRS. PALMER EIDEM OF MARIETTA, MINN.; SAM EIDEM of MECKLING and AMOS EIDEM also of Elk Point.' 'Mrs. Palmer Eidem of Marietta, Minn.' is Mayme Olene Eidem - so a South Dakota newspaper named her as this couple's daughter in 1940, sixty-two years before she died and seventy-three years before Myrtle Overdahl's obituary said the same. An independent, contemporaneous confirmation of the keystone link of this whole line. ¶33

Life story

Born 5 August 1859 in Selbu kommune, Sor-Trondelag, Norway - the same parish from which the Lac qui Parle County Eidems came. So BOTH of Kathryn (Eidem) Hayter's grandfathers' families, her father's and her mother's, carry the Eidem farm name out of Selbu. That is why her parents could both be Eidems without the marriage being a close-cousin match, and it is strong independent support for the project's Selbu hypothesis. ¶3

He settled his own farm on Brule Creek, near Elk Point in Union County, in the far south-eastern corner of South Dakota, and the family worshipped at St Paul Lutheran Church, rural Elk Point. He married Anna Olene Satter in 1898 and they had at least six children: Bertha Agnes (1900), Samuel Gerhard (1901), Selma, Mamie/Mayme (1906), Amos (1909) and Myrtle (1911); an Alice Mabel Eidem, 1904 - 25 March 1906, buried in the same churchyard, may be a seventh who died in infancy. ¶4

Baptism: BORN 5 AUGUST 1859 and baptised in Selbu church on 4 SEPTEMBER 1859 - Selbu parish register, Selbu local parish 1859-1868, births and baptisms, FOLIO 6, ENTRY 101. Read from the scanned page: 'No. 101, foedt 5/8, doebt 4/9, Barnets fulde Navn SIVERT, aegte, Foraeldrene Gb. SIVERT JOHNS[EN] BRYNNILD JOHNSD[ATTER] EIDEM' - 'Gb.' being gaardbruger, farmer. THE BIRTH DATE ON THE PAGE IS THE DATE ON HIS ELK POINT GRAVESTONE, TO THE DAY. A Norwegian parish clerk in 1859 and a stonecutter in Union County, South Dakota in 1940, with no possible contact between them, both give 5 August 1859. ¶22

Godparents (faddere), six of them, read from the scan and carried by no index: TORSTEN B. EIDEM, JOHN N. LANGLI, JOHN S. EIDEM, KARI S. EIDEM, SIGRI O. [farm abbreviated] and BRYNNILD S. EIDEM. 'John N. Langli' - N for Nielsen - is on every reading his own MATERNAL GRANDFATHER John Nielsen Langli, standing godfather to the grandson; 'Torsten B. Eidem' - B for Bersvendsen - comes from the paternal great-grandmother Sigri Bersvendsdatter's family, and a Torsten Bersvendsen was husmann at Naesodden in the same Eidem cluster in 1865. Selbu godparents were close kin and this set reaches both sides of the family. ¶23

1865 census: person 005 at rural residence 0058 'oestre Eidem', Selbu, loepenr 62 - 'Sivert Sivertsen', aged 7 (the enumerator ran a year high; he was 6), birthplace Selbo Prgj., 'Soen' (son), unmarried. He was living on his father's freehold farm with his parents, two brothers, two sisters, his widowed grandmother Sigri Bersvendsdatter aged 81 and an elderly unmarried great-aunt. ¶24

Emigration: he left Norway as a child. The family's crossing falls between 3 April 1866, when his eldest brother married in Selbu church, and 30 April 1867, when the Trondheim emigrant protocols open - so he was six or seven when he crossed, and his father was dead in Union County before he was thirteen. See the note on his father for the nine negative searches that close the window. ¶25

Context: the farm he was born on was gnr 56 bnr 2, 'Eidem oestre soendre' in Selbu, which his father owned outright - two horses, eight cows, thirteen sheep and four goats, with a cottage holding (Luddumoen) under it. His family were the only freeholders among the four generations of this maternal line so far traced in Norway. ¶26

1870 census: 'Seivert', 11, son, the youngest in the household of 'Seivert Johnson', 55, and 'Emma' [Brynhild], 52, on Brule Creek, Union County, Dakota Territory, 23 August 1870 (page 10, line 25), with his sisters Sarah [Sigri] 21 and Mary [Marit] 19 and his brother Ole 14 - his first record in America, four years after the 1866 sailing and at eleven the right age for his August 1859 baptism. ¶38

Gravestone: he and his wife share one stone in Saint Paul Cemetery, Elk Point, inscribed 'EIDEM / Anna O 1872 MOTHER 1937 / Severt S 1859 FATHER 1940'. ¶9

Born on the farm Eidem in Selbu, Soer-Troendelag, the same parish and the same farm name as his daughter's future husband's family. He emigrated to the Norwegian colony at Elk Point in Union County, South Dakota, where his father Sivert Johnson Eidem, his brothers John and Ole and his sisters Sigri and Marit are all also buried. His daughter Myrtle's obituary describes the churchyard of St. Paul Lutheran Church, rural Elk Point, as 'her family's burial ground for three generations and 150 years'. ¶10

His whole census life, found for the first time. He was in BRULE township, Union County, South Dakota - the Brule Creek Norwegian settlement whose church is St Paul Lutheran in rural Elk Point - in every federal census from 1900 to 1940, never in the town of Elk Point. That is why earlier searches missed him. The surname is also mis-indexed in two of the five: 'Eidesen' in 1900 and 'Eiden' in 1920. ¶11

Confirmation: 'S. S. Eidem was a member of one of the first confirmation classes of the ST. PAUL CONGREGATION, which took place in the OLD LOG CHURCH IN 1875. Surviving members of that same class are ANDREW SOLEM and JOHN KJOSE.' So the first log church of St. Paul's, Brule Creek, was standing by 1875, and the ministerial book holds that class list. ¶29

1880 census: 'Sivert', son, white, male, 20, 'working on railroad', born Norway, in the household of his widowed mother Brynhild Johnson, 62, with his brother Ole 23, in Brule Township, Union County, Dakota Territory, 4 June 1880 (ED 4, sheet 393D, line 32). The family is indexed under the patronymic Johnson. Age 20 fits his birth of 5 August 1859. The railroad work is the first record of his occupation. ¶41

Land patent, 1891: he received a United States land patent on 9 November 1891 for the NORTH-EAST QUARTER OF THE SOUTH-EAST QUARTER OF SECTION 1, TOWNSHIP 92 NORTH, RANGE 50 WEST OF THE 5TH PRINCIPAL MERIDIAN, Union County, South Dakota - forty acres. General Land Office document number 5582, accession STA-SD2020__.298, recorded in the index as "SIVER S EIDEM". He was 32. ¶20

Land, the family pattern: his forty acres lie in the same section 1 of T92N R50W in which his mother Brynhild ("Brynel Johnson Eidem") patented the S1/2 of the south-east quarter and the N1/2 of the north-east quarter of section 12 on 10 June 1873, and in which his elder brother John Sivertson Eidem patented the W1/2 of the north-west quarter on the same day in 1873. Three members of one family held parts of one section over eighteen years. A fourth Eidem, John Erickson Eidem, patented the N1/2 of the south-west quarter, the SW1/4SW1/4 and the NW1/4SE1/4 of the same section 1 on 10 December 1874. The section was effectively an Eidem section. ¶21

Marriage: 'Mr. Eidem was united in marriage to Miss ANNA OLIN[E] [Satter] of the BRULE CREEK CONGREGATION in the FALL OF 1898.' The season and her congregation are both new; the file had only the year. Her own 1937 obituary gives his name in the Norwegian form, SIVERT SIVERTSON EIDEM - so the 'S.' of 'S. S. Eidem' is the patronymic Sivertson, son of Sivert Johnson Eidem, exactly as the project inferred from the birth certificates. ¶32

Death and funeral: 'For the past year Mr. Eidem had been failing gradually. Then on SUNDAY, JULY 21, the intense heat affected him and he was confined to his bed until his DEATH ON MONDAY EVENING AT 8:25 O'CLOCK.' With the sub-head 'Passed Away At Farm Home Mon.' and a funeral held on the afternoon the paper went to press (Thursday 1 August 1940), the Monday is 29 July 1940. The file previously carried only the year. 'Funeral services were held this afternoon at 1:30 at the home and 2 o'clock at the South St. Paul church. Burial was in the church cemetery.' ¶34

Pallbearers, and they are a kinship document: 'REUBEN, SAM and JOEL WENNBLOM, GUST ABRAHAM, OLAF HOFSTAD and GILBERT EIDEM.' The three Wennbloms are the sons of his sister Marit (Eidem) Wennblom; Gust Abraham is the son of his wife's sister Lizzie (Satter) Abraham, and is the 'Gust Abraham' born 1889 whom the wave-3 agent found in the South Dakota birth index under mother's maiden name Satter. A Norwegian funeral's pallbearers are nephews, and this list ties the Wennblom and Abraham households to his own without any other record being needed. ¶35

Church office: a life-long member of the St. Paul congregation, trustee and deacon for a number of years, and one of seven members of the building committee for the present church building, to which he gave freely of his time and money. 'He was a true [p]illar of the St. Paul Congregation.' ¶36

1900 federal census: Brule township, Union County, South Dakota, Ancestry image 4120605_00155 - 'Sever S Eidesen, head, born AUGUST 1859, Norway', with 'Anna Ilene' (wife, b. March 1872, South Dakota) and 'Bertha Agnes' (daughter, b. March 1900, South Dakota). Three people, two years after the marriage. The stated month and year of his birth agree exactly with the 5 August 1859 on his gravestone at Saint Paul Cemetery, Elk Point. ¶12

1910 federal census: Brule township, Ancestry image 4449870_00447 - 'Sivert S Eidem, head, b. 1860, Norway', with Anna O. and five children: Bertha A. (1900), Samuel G. (1902), Selma O. (1904), Mamie O. (1907) and Amos S. (1909). ¶13

Census age drift: every census from 1910 onward is indexed 'b. 1860', but the 1900 census gives 'August 1859' and the gravestone 5 August 1859. The 1860s are census-day arithmetic on a stated age; 1859 is right. ¶18

1915 South Dakota state census: 'S S Eidem, b. 1860, Norway'. 1925 South Dakota state census: 'Severt S Eidem, b. 1860, Norway'. ¶14

1920 federal census: Brule township, Ancestry image 4390883_00432, indexed 'S Seaver Eiden', head, b. abt 1860 Norway. EIGHT people - himself, Annie, and all six children: Bertha, Samuel, Selma, Mamie, Amos and Myrtle. This single sheet confirms the entire sibling list and shows there was no seventh child. ¶15

1930 federal census: Brule township, Ancestry image 4548146_00092 - 'Sivert S Eidem, head, b. abt 1860, Norway', with Anna Olivia and four children still at home: Selma Olivia, 'Minnie Ovedia' (Mayme), Amos Stefan and Myrtle Sadie. ¶16

Death and burial: he died in 1940 at Elk Point and is buried in Saint Paul Cemetery, Elk Point, under a joint stone with his wife reading 'EIDEM, Anna O 1872 MOTHER 1937, Severt S 1859 FATHER 1940'. Only the year of death is cut, so only the year is recorded here. ¶5

His own parents and siblings, below, rest on Find a Grave's family links together with the dates cut on the Saint Paul Cemetery stones, and are rated Probable rather than Confirmed until a Selbu parish register entry is read for them. ¶6

His own six children are entered in this tree as mat_kob_bertha, mat_kob_samuel, mat_kob_selma, Mayme Ovidia "Mamie" Eidem, mat_kob_amos and mat_kob_myrtle. His siblings, from Find a Grave's family links and the Elk Point stones and therefore unproved, were John Sivertson Eidem (30 Jan 1842 - 1881), Sigri Sivertsdatter 'Sarah' Eidem Thomson (1849-1921), Marit Eidem Wennblom (29 Nov 1851 - 22 Feb 1925) and Ole Sivertson Eidem (8 Oct 1857 - 29 Jan 1948), all buried in the same churchyard. ¶7

1940 federal census: Brule township, NARA T627 roll 3869 image 133 - 'Sever Eidem, head, b. abt 1860 Norway', a widower of eighty living with his unmarried daughter Mayme, 33. He died later that year. This is his last census. ¶17

Obituary, Confirmed, and it names the parish AND the farm: Leader-Courier (Elk Point, South Dakota), Thursday 1 August 1940, sixty-ninth year no. 46, page 1, continued page 8. Headline 'S. S. Eide[m]... Was One of C[o]unty's O[ldest] Pioneers'; sub-head 'Passed Away At Farm Home Mon.'; jump head 'RESIDENT OF COUNTY SINCE 1866 EXPIRES'. It opens: 'IN SELBO IN OLD NORWAY BY THE SIDE OF ONE OF ITS NUMEROUS LAKES IS A "GAARD" OR TRACT OF LAND CALLED [E]IDEM. THERE S. S. EIDEM WAS BORN AUGUST 5, 1859. HIS PARENTS WERE SIVERT JOHNSON EIDEM AND BRYN[H]ILD JOHNSDATTER EIDEM.' The lake is Selbusjoen and the farm is ostre Eidem. This is the family's own statement, in print, of the parish and the farm they came from. ¶27

Emigration, from the same obituary, in detail: 'Sivert Johnson Eidem and family... sold his little home and made preparations for this journey, and in due time set sail for this country. This was sometime in APRIL, 1866, and they landed at QUEBEC, CANADA, several weeks later. The party traveled by rail to Chicago and on to [-], Ia., which was the end of the railroad at that time. There some of their friends, who had immigrated sometime before and who had homes of their own here, met them and they travelled by OX TEAM on to Sioux City, which at that time was only a trading post. Mr. Eidem and his party finally arrived at the Sioux river... and in the morning took the ferry... [to] the other bank of the river on South Dakota soil. They traveled up along the BRULE CREEK and decided to locate on the land which had been the home of S. S. Eidem ever since.' He was six years old. The Quebec route is the Trondheim timber-ship route of the 1860s, not the New York route. ¶28

Seven grandchildren living at his death in July 1940 (five at his wife's death in March 1937). ¶37

June 1940: on 20 June 1940 he gave his daughter Mayme in marriage to Palmer G. Eidem at St. Paul Lutheran church (Leader-Courier, Elk Point, 27 June 1940). He died five weeks later, on 29 July 1940. ¶39

Estate: on 5 October 1940 the County Court of Union County ordered notice of Amos Eidem's petition to be appointed administrator of the estate of S. S. Eidem, deceased - so he died without a will, or none was offered. The notice is addressed to his six heirs at law: Bertha Lehr [Leer], Selma Nelson, Sam Eidem, Mamie Eidem, Amos Eidem and Myrtle Overdahl. The hearing was set for 28 October 1940 at 2 p.m. in the County Judge's office, Courthouse, Elk Point; signed 'By the Court: O. G. Beck'. Published in the Leader-Courier, Elk Point, 10 October 1940. As heirs at law these six are his children, which proves the sibship independently of his obituary; Mayme is still 'Mamie Eidem' here, her married name being the same. The outcome of the hearing and the inventory are in the Union County probate file. ¶40

Records that disagree 1

Contradiction, recorded: the 1940 obituary says all four of his siblings had died before him, which would put Ole Sivertson Eidem's death before 29 July 1940. Find a Grave memorial 121775505 dates Ole 1857-1948. Both cannot be right. His sister Marit Wennblom's own 1925 obituary names 'two brothers, Ole and Severt Eidem' as her survivors, so Ole was alive in February 1925. Either the 1948 stone belongs to a younger Ole Eidem (the memorial's only inscription is the single word 'Father'), or the 1940 obituary is loose about a brother who had moved away. Unresolved; the South Dakota death index is the next record. ¶31
