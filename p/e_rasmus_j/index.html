About the family

Eldest son of the late Jørgen Rasmussen Erdal, per the overformynderi entry in skifteprotokoll A 8 (c.1736-40). That entry reads: 'Erdall, after the late Jørgen Rasmussen... his eldest son Rasmus Jørgensen, who has now married, for his patrimony 81 rdl..., of which 1 pund 5¼ merker of land in the farm Erdall'. ¶2

Wife Ragnilde Lassesdatter died in 1783. Children in her 1783 probate: Lasse (dead, leaving son Jørgen), Lars, Brithe (at Nedre Flo) and Malene (married Lars Trulsen Aaning). Jørgen (born about 1741, died 1767) predeceased her without heirs. ¶4

Life story

Freehold farmer (selveier) of 1 pund 5¼ merker at Erdal. ¶1

Guardianship protocols: a ward with his brothers Lars and Ole under their father Jørgen (1723). He inherited after his maternal grandfather (morfar) Lars Rasmussen (1727). His guardian in 1736 was Rasmus Rasmussen Erdal. In 1740 the court noted 'this boy had married two years ago Lasse Flo's daughter', so he married about 1738. From 1734/1741 he was guardian of his half-sister Brite Jørgensdatter. ¶3

Guardianship 1740: guardianship protocol no. 2, fol. 15, margin note: '1740: befandtes denne Dreng... selv at være [be]gift med Amund Lassesen Floes D[a]tter[?] og sine Midler self at have annammet, saa at den anordnede Curators [Ansvar] er fri' (in 1740 this young man was found to be married to Amund Lassesen Flo's daughter[?], and to have received his means himself, so the appointed curator was released). The kinship word reads like 'Datter' (daughter). But Amund Lassesen was only about 23 in 1732, so a daughter could not have been married by 1740. The tree's wife, Ragnilde Lassesdatter, was Amund's sister, so either the clerk erred or the word is to be read otherwise. The note does confirm a marriage to the Nedre Flo family by 1740. ¶12

Burial, read from the scan: Innvik ministerialbok 1750-1816, burials, Anno 1797, p. 321: under '1 Januar' a group of at least five burials, the first of the year, beginning with the lensmann Ole Larsen Faleide (78), includes 'Rasmus Jørgensen Erdal 77'. A group burial on 1 January means the bodies were buried together, often after being held over the winter; he probably died in late 1796, and the index's 1 Jan 1797 is the burial date, not the death date. ¶8

He remarried after 1783. The probate in skifteprotokoll A 19 (1790-95, fol. 344b-345) lists his first-marriage children and grandchildren (Jørgen Lassesen aged 21, so about 1794). It also appears to list a daughter of the later marriage, ?Anne Rasmusdatter, married to Jørgen Andersen Aaning; that reading is uncertain. ¶5

Aaland 1932: Aaland reports a court division in 1786: at the demand of the holder Rasmus Jørgensen, Erdal bruk nr. 3 was split into two equal holdings between 'eldste sons son Jørgen Lasseson' (his eldest son's son) and 'andre sonen Lars Rasmussen' (his second son); the new holding became nr. 4. This shows Rasmus alive in 1786 and independently confirms the descent of Jørgen Lassesen. The court book (åstadssak) of 1786 is the primary record. ¶11

Aaland 1932: Aaland's Erdal history (bruk nr. 3): 'S. Rasmus, † 77 a. g. 1797, br. 1736, sk. 1746. * Ragnh. Lassed. N.-Flo, † 70 a. g. 1783': died 1797 aged 77, tenant from 1736, deed 1746, married Ragnhild Lassesdatter of Nedre Flo (died 1783 aged 70). This agrees with the burial and probate records. Aaland adds that his son Jørgen held the bruk from 1763 (died 1767 aged 26), and that his second son Lars took a deed in 1775. ¶10

Records that disagree 2

CONFLICT: the probate is indexed 1792-95, but the burial index gives 1797 (date shown as 01-01, age 77). Check the church-book page. ¶6

Conflict with the probate in A 19 (protocol labelled 1790-1795, fol. 344b): the opening lines of that probate, with its date and the name of the deceased, are lost on the black-and-white scan, so its date rests on the protocol's range and on Jørgen Lassesen's age of 21. The burial register is a dated, contemporary entry and is trusted for his death (before 1 Jan 1797). If the A 19 probate is really from 1794-95, it is probably not after Rasmus himself; it may be after his second wife, which would explain why a later-marriage daughter appears with the first-marriage heirs. Reading the closing pages of that probate (images 347-349 of source 24239) for a dated session would settle it. ¶9

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Not to be confused with Rasmus Jørgensen of Sætre/Sæten (a soldier, married 1775 and 1793), a different man. ¶7
