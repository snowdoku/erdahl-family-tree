Life story

Rev. Joseph Bennett, missionary of the Society for the Propagation of the Gospel. He was appointed in 1761 'in the thirty-fourth year of his age', so born about 1727-28. He served at Horton, Falmouth, Newport, Cornwallis and Windsor, Nova Scotia, and from about 1775 was an itinerant missionary (Cape Sable). The SPG report of 1780 says he was confined at Windsor, disordered in body and mind (Eaton, 1891, pp. 72-73, 82-83). As 'Joseph Bennett of Windsor, Nova Scotia', he and his wife Elizabeth mortgaged four houses outside the south gate of Cork, the Trap House in Cork, and their share of Ballincalloe (Ballincollow), Co. Limerick, 'then in the possession of William Bennett', to 'William Creed, then late of Limerick city', by a deed of 1 Nov 1765 executed at Halifax (Registry of Deeds memorial 160893). Eaton thought he died soon after 1780. But on 18 May 1787 'Rev. Joseph Bennett of Cork city, clerk, and Elizabeth (als Hodder) his wife' leased a house outside the South Gate of Cork, next to the holdings of Bartholomew Bready (memorial 259686), the Bartholomew Brady who was a south-gate tenant in 1765. So he returned to Cork. ¶1

Memorial 160893 read (FamilySearch signed in, 2 Nov 2026; book 241 p. 551, image 284; opening read on the image, the rest from FamilySearch's machine transcript). 'Bennett & wife to Creed', registered 3 June 1766. Deed of 4 November 1765 by which 'Jos[eph] Bennett of Windsor in the province of Nova Scotia, Cl[er]k, and Eliz[abe]th Bennett his wife', in consideration of £300, granted to 'W[illia]m Creed then late of Lim[eric]k' the following: their houses outside the South Gate of Cork, held by tenants including Bartholomew Brady; a house in Cork called the Trap House; and 'their proport[io]n of the farm of Ballincollow' in Co. Limerick, 'then under tenure to Mr W[illia]m Bennett'. The deed is subject to a proviso of redemption, so it is a mortgage. The witnesses were William Haskill of Halifax, Nova Scotia, and Patrick Dickson, then of Halifax and now of 'Bally bringe' [as transcribed], Co. Limerick, gent.; Dickson swore the memorial at Cork on 20 June 1766. The memorial states NO kinship between Bennett and Creed, so it cannot raise any grade. It does confirm that the grantor was the Nova Scotia missionary and that he held a share of the Bennett farm of Ballincollow. ¶3

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade · 1 open question

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Probable as a son of Samuel Bennett junior and Anne Collis (collateral; brother of Mary Bennett): the Blennerhassett Pedigree (about 1733-36) names Joseph as the third son of Samuel and Anne. The missionary's share of Ballincollow was held by William Bennett, who was Samuel's son William of Ballincollow, gent., 1749-1777, with a son William Collis Bennett. His birth about 1728 falls between Samuel's marriage in 1709 and his death before 1735. The identification rests on the name, the family property and the date; no record found states his parentage. Eaton says he 'came from England', which probably reflects his ordination and appointment in London. ¶2a

Open questions and the next record to read 1

Test record: Alumni Dublinenses (Trinity College Dublin entrance, c.1744-48, giving his father's name and county), or the SPG candidates' papers (USPG archive, Bodleian). Added as the documented link between the Creed and Bennett families. ¶2b
