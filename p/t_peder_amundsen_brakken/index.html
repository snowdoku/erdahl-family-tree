Life story

Of the farm Brækken (also written Sogge-Brækken) in Grytten parish, Romsdal. He is named as 'Peder Amunss.' of Brækken in the baptism of his daughter Anne on 29 Oct 1791 at Grytten church, with his wife Eli Pedersdatter as mother. ¶1

An earlier child: a stillborn twin of a 'Peder' of Brækken was buried on 25 Dec 1787. If that is this Peder, he and Eli had married by 1787 and the couple had already lost twins four years before Anne was born. The register gives no patronymic in that entry, so this is a probable rather than certain identification. ¶3

His occupation is not stated in any entry found. Brækken was a small holding under Sogge on the shore of Romsdalsfjorden, and the neighbours named in these entries - Østigaard, Brevig, Bakke - are all places in the same cluster of farms. ¶4

He and Eli stood together as godparents at Brækken on 3 Jan 1793, at the baptism of Marit, daughter of Anders Anderssen and Marit Børresdatter of the same farm - the register spells him 'Peder Amundsen' there. ¶2

How sure are we?

Speculative. A plausible candidate only. Treat this link as a question, not a fact.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence lowered from Probable to Speculative (audit, 13 Oct 2026). The records about this person stand, but his identification as the father of Anne Pedersdatter (wife of John Johnsen Sætnes) is now Speculative: see the note of the same date on Anne Pedersdatter, which names the records (Grytten confirmation 19 Oct 1834, p. 495 no. 5, 'Anne Pedersd. fra Lesjøe'; Lesja marriage 13 Mar 1816, pp. 834-835). ¶5

Corrections 1

Correction: no longer linked as father of Anne Pedersdatter (13 Oct 2026), because she was a Lesja woman - 'fra Lesjøe' in her daughter's 1834 confirmation, married in Lesja in 1816 as a maiden of Kloppen - and she fits the 1801 Lesja household of Peder Ingebretsen and Anne Pedersdatter at Eeneboe, whose sons Peder and Ingebret match her 1816 co-godparents. The Grytten Anne Pedersdatter born 18 Oct 1791 at Brækken remains his daughter; he stays in the tree. ¶6
