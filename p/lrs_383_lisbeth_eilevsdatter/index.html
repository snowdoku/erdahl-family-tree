About the family

Children: Endre (baptised 7 Oct 1735), Mons, Anna (born about 1728, Anna Iversdatter), Randi and Eli (born about 1736), all named as her and Iver's children in 1739; also an infant son Johannes, buried with her on 13 Mar 1739. Endre, Anna and Eli are again the first-marriage children in Iver's division of 25 Sep 1779. ¶5

Parents linked at Probable: Eilef (Elløf) Olsen of Botten (see his page) and Maritte Endresdatter (see her page). The probate after 'Elløf Olsøn' of the farm Botten in Ladvig skipreide, 20 Jun 1730 (Ytre Sogn skifteprotokoll 3b, fol. 433b-434a), divides the estate between his widow Maritte Endresdatter and their children together ('hendes med den Sal. Mand Sammen auflede børn'): Ole (of age), Giertrud (dead, leaving a daughter Maritte Endresdatter), 'Elizabeth' (2nd daughter, myndig, of age) and Brithe (3rd daughter, a minor). Evidence: (1) the farm: she lived and died at Botten (Botnen), and her husband Iver Monsen of Botten held her probate there in 1739; (2) the name: Elisabeth is the formal form of Lisbeth (Lispet), and her patronymic is written Elevsdatter (1730), Ejlefsdr. (1734), Ellevsd. (1735), Ellev[s] (1739) and Eilefs D[atte]r (1779), the same name as Elløf; (3) the age: she was 42 at burial in Mar 1739, so born about 1697 and of age in 1730; (4) a sister: at the baptism of her daughter Sijnneve (Synnøve) at Eivindvik on 23 Oct 1730, four months after the probate, the last godmother was 'Birthe Elevsdatter Botnen' (Gulen ministerialbok 11440, canvas 24, right page), and 'Berthe Ellevsd.' was again godmother to her son Endre on 7 Oct 1735; this fits Elløf's daughter Brithe, a minor of Botten in 1730. An Ole Eilefsen Botnen, betrothed in 1736, fits the son Ole; (5) her son Endre bears the name of her mother's father (Maritte Endresdatter), though he may equally be named after her brother-in-law Endre Johansen Eiken (inference). Why the grade is not Confirmed: no record states that Iver Monsen's wife was Elløf's daughter. In Jun 1730 Elisabeth is listed with no husband, yet Iver Monsen Botnen and Lisbeth Elevsdatter had a daughter baptised on 23 Oct 1730 (no illegitimacy mark). So either the clerk omitted her husband, or they married between Jun and Oct 1730 after a premarital conception (inference). Their marriage has not been found. Synnøve (Oct 1730) is the earliest child found; her daughter Anna, entered as born about 1728 only from late ages (73 in 1801, 75 at burial in 1803), is probably younger (see Anna's notes). If a child of the couple is found baptised well before Jun 1730, the omission explanation must hold, or the Elisabeth of 1730 is another woman. ¶6a

Life story

Churching: 'Iver Botnens g[ift]e Lispet' (Iver Botnen's wife Lisbeth) was churched at Brekke on 4 Nov 1735 after the birth of Endre. Gulen ministerialbok 1729-1735, canvases 85-86. ¶4

Probate: on 8 Oct 1739 a probate (skifte og deling) was held at Botten after 'Dannequinde Lisbeth Elling[s]datter[?]', between her widower Iver Monsøn Botten and their children Endre, Mons, Anna, Randi and Eli, all minors, with their paternal uncle Peder Monsen of Indre Takle present for them. Ytre Sogn skifteprotokoll 5, Nr. 78, fol. 125a and. ¶1

Name: her burial of 13 Mar 1739 writes 'Lispet Ellev[s]', and the 1739 probate's 'Elling[s]datter' is probably a clerk's slip or a misreading; the baptism of her son Endre on 7 Oct 1735 writes 'Lispet Ellevsd.' (with a godmother 'Berthe Ellevsd.', perhaps a sister), and her husband's 1779 division writes 'Lisbeth [E]ilefs D[atte]r'. Three of four records give Eilev (Ellev, Eilef), so she is entered as Eilevsdatter. ¶2

Burial: 'Item g[ifte] Lispet Ellev[s] Botnen gl: 42 aar' (married woman Lisbeth Eilevsdatter of Botnen, aged 42) was buried at Brekke on 13 [Mar] 1739, together with 'Barn Johannes Ivers[en] Botnen', her infant son; she probably died in or after childbirth (inference). Her birth about 1697 is inferred from the age of 42. Gulen ministerialbok (Digitalarkivet source 11441), canvas 13. (Supersedes the earlier inference that she died before 8 Oct 1739 with birth before about 1710.) ¶3
