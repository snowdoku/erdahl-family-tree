About the family

Oline Slind's paternal grandmother, and an EIDEM by birth. Her marriage entry of 24 May 1841 names her 'Berith Gundersdt Eidem', born 1816 in Selbu, and her father as GUNDER JOHNSEN EIDEM. She married Ole Gundersen Slinden, who was 24; she is given as born 1816 in the marriage entry, 1815 in the 1875 and 1891 censuses and 1814 by her age in the 1865 census. ¶1

HER MOTHER IS NAMED AND HER CHILDHOOD HOUSEHOLD FOUND, which wave 3 could not do. The 1825 census of Selbu, rural residence 0060 Eidem, household 1, enters: 'Ole Olsen, 74, Gaardm.' and 'Marit Johnsd., 76, Hustru' - the farmer and his wife - then 'GUNDER JOHNS., 39, INDST.' (inderst, a lodger holding no land of his own) with his wife 'BRYNHILD ERIKSD., 28, Hustru', and their four daughters: 'BERET GUNDERSD., 10', 'Maglie Gundersd., 9', 'Sigrid Gundersd., 6' and 'Marit Gundersd., 2'. Beret aged 10 at 27 November 1825 is Berith Gundersdatter Eidem, born about 1815. ¶10

Her father's standing is now known and it was low. Wave 3 had only the one line in her marriage entry naming 'Gunder Johnsen Eidem' and could say nothing about him. The 1825 census makes him an INDERST - a lodger or sub-tenant living in another man's house with no land and no lease, the bottom rung of the rural hierarchy below even the husmann (cottager with land). He was thirty-nine, had four small daughters, and was living in the household of an elderly farming couple at Eidem. His daughter married a Slind leaseholder's son in 1841 and so moved up a rung. ¶11

Her sisters, now in the tree, are Maglie born about 1816, Sigrid born about 1819 and Marit born about 1823. Her own baptism, about 1815, is in the Selbu ministerialbok and is not in the Digitalarkivet index under the father's name; it is the record that would give her exact date and her godparents. ¶14

Life story

This is the oldest documented link between the Slind and Eidem farms of Selbu, and it stands two generations before Oline Slind married John P. Eidem in Minnesota. The marriage of a Slind to an Eidem in the 1880s in Lac qui Parle County was not a new acquaintance made in America; it repeated something the two farms had already done in Selbu in 1841. ¶2

Baptism: 'Bereth', baptised on the 20th Sunday after Trinity 1815 (8 October 1815, computed), Selbu baptisms 1815 p. 222 no. 4; father indexed as 'Gunder Jens.' (probably a misreading of Johns.) of Brennan, mother Brynhild Eriksd., Hustrue (wife). Her parents had married on 11 June 1815. Index only; the scan was not read. ¶17

Death and burial: 'Berit Gundersdtr', born 1815 at Eidem, died 18 April and buried 24 April 1891, Selbu burial register 1878-1891, p. 405; the next of kin ('paarorande') is entered as Ole Gundersen, her husband's name. Identification strong: name, birth year 1815 and birthplace Eidem agree with her censuses and marriage entry, and the next of kin is her husband. Index read; scan not read. ¶18

Baptism read by the orchestrator from the scan (index pd00000009559024 reads the father 'Gunder Jens.', a misreading): 'Et hjemmedøbt Pigebarn kaldet Bereth, født d. 15de Septbr. Forældre: Gunder Johns. Brennan og Hustrue Brynhild Eriks D. Faddere: Michel Johns. Moen, Tosten Eriks. Auglan, Marith Johnsd. Brennan, Ragnhild Johnsd. Narnæs, Ingebor Gundersd. Kolset' - Selbu 1815, p. 222. ¶20a

For the selbu-eidem agent: her father Gunder Johnsen Eidem of Selbu, living about 1841, is a point where the Slind and Eidem lines meet and should be reconciled with whatever Eidem genealogy that agent builds, rather than duplicated. ¶5

Printed source: her family is on printed page 203 of Selbu i fortid og naatid, bind 4 (1992), at the end of the chapter EIDEM (YSTER-EIDEM), pp. 184-204. The page carries Berit twice, BOTH of her recorded birth years 1815 and 1816, her marriage year 1841, the farm name Slind, her husband's given name in its 1992 form Ola, her son Gunder Olsen Slind's birth year 1844, and her father's name in its 1992 form Gunnar Jonsen. Nine agreements on one page. ¶6a

THE LOOP THE BRIEF ASKED ABOUT, and it appears to close. Volume 4's Eidem chapters are headed in capitals with the sub-holding in parentheses: EIDEM (YSTER-EIDEM) at p. 184, BRENNHAUGEN at p. 205, EIDEM (USTIGARDEN) at pp. 208 and 211, EIDEMSNESSET at pp. 214, 215, 224, 226, EIDEM (OPPIGARDEN) at p. 227, MYRANESSET at p. 233, EIDEMSMYRA at p. 234, EIDEM (NEGARDEN) at p. 237. BRENNHAUGEN - the husmannsplass where the Selbu church books place Pal Ingebrigtsen and Kari Pedersdatter, the parents of John P. Eidem - falls between the Yster-Eidem chapter and the Ustigarden chapter, and page 206 of the Brennhaugen chapter carries the word Yster-Eidem. So Brennhaugen is a cottage under YSTER-EIDEM, and her own family held Yster-Eidem itself. If page 203 bears this out, then Oline Slind's paternal grandmother was born on the farm whose cottage her husband was born in, and the Slind-Eidem marriage of 1841 in Selbu and the Slind-Eidem marriage of 1901 in Minnesota were the same two households doing the same thing sixty years and four thousand miles apart. NOT PROVED. Ordering volume 4, pp. 184-207 - twenty-four pages, the Yster-Eidem chapter and the Brennhaugen chapter together - settles it either way, and it is the single best twenty-four pages anyone could order for this tree. ¶7

The two findings are in fact compatible, and the honest reading is this: both agents place Berith in the FREEHOLDING family of Yster-Eidem and Paul in a HUSMANNSPLASS - a cottager's holding - under that same farm. They shared a farm, a parish and a surname, and they stood on opposite sides of the sharpest social line in rural Norway. No blood link between them has been proved, and none should be assumed. What is documented is that the Slind and Eidem families of Selbu were intermarrying by 1841, two generations before Oline Slind married John P. Eidem in Minnesota - so that American marriage was the renewal of an old acquaintance, not a new one. The record that would settle the blood question is the Selbu baptism of her father Gunder Johnsen, about 1780-1790, and the pre-1801 Selbu register is scanned but untranscribed. ¶9

Also in that household were 'Svend Eriks., 19, Td.' (tjenestedreng, farmhand) and 'Elen Eriksd., 24, Tp.' (tjenestepike, maidservant) - two young servants with her mother's patronymic, almost certainly Brynhild Eriksdatter's brother and sister working on the same farm. ¶12

Correction to this project's own reconciliation note above, written earlier on 12 Oct 2026 and wrong in one particular. That note said Berith belonged to the FREEHOLDING family of Yster-Eidem. She did not. Her father Gunder Johnsen was a landless INDERST - a lodger with no holding at all - at Eidem in the 1825 census, and her mother was Brynhild Eriksdatter. The freeholders of Eidem oestre soendre, gnr 56 bnr 2, are a different family again: they are Mayme Eidem's line, on the other side of this tree. ¶15

So the structural finding is sharper than a loop closing or failing to close: 'EIDEM' in this tree is THREE UNRELATED FAMILIES living on one cluster of farms in Selbu, at three different levels of a rigid rural hierarchy. Mayme Olene Eidem's people were gaardbrugere og selveiere - owner-occupying freeholders at Eidem oestre soendre, with two horses, eight cows, thirteen sheep and four goats. Paul Ingebrigtsen, David's great-great-great-grandfather on the other side, was a husmann med jord - a cottager holding a patch called Braendhaugen in return for labour days, with one cow, one calf, six sheep and two goats. Berith's father was an inderst, below both. All three took the farm's name as a byname and all three carried it to America as a surname, which is why this project spent two days believing one Eidem family had married another. No blood link between any two of the three has been proved. ¶16

1865 census: 'hans Kone', wife of the tenant farmer Ole Gundersen at Slinden, aged 51, in a household of eight. 1875 and 1891 find her still there. She had at least six children: Gunder born 1844 (Oline's father), Beret born about 1845, Gunder born about 1848, Peder born about 1853, Brynild born about 1856 and Sigri born about 1861. ¶3

Death: she died before 30 November 1897, because her husband's burial entry of 9 December 1897 describes him as 'Enkemand', a widower. She is present in the 1891 census, so she died between 1 January 1891 and 30 November 1897. Her burial is not in the Digitalarkivet index for those years under any Slind residence, which is a gap in the transcription rather than evidence that it is not in the register. ¶4

Notes on method and coordination

Coordinator's reconciliation, 12 Oct 2026, because two agents reached different-sounding conclusions about her on the same day and the tree should not carry only the weaker one. The question asked was whether she connects by blood to Paul Ingebrigtsen of Braendhaugen, David Erdahl's great-great-great-grandfather on the other side of this family, since both carry the Eidem farm name. One agent reported that the loop APPEARS TO CLOSE: her family stands on printed page 203 of Selbu i fortid og naatid volume 4, at the end of the chapter EIDEM (YSTER-EIDEM), with her father given in the book's modernised spelling as Gunnar Jonsen, and Paul's cottage Brennhaugen is a holding under that same Yster-Eidem farm. The other agent reported NO CONNECTION: her 1841 marriage entry names her father as Gunder Johnsen Eidem, and there is no Gunder of any patronymic among the seventy-seven people enumerated at Eidem in the 1801 census, so she descends from the freeholding Yster-Eidem line through her mother Brynhild Eriksdatter, which is also the line of the other Marietta Eidems. ¶8
