About the family

Her husband's will of 9 May 1676 names his wife as Anne, so Alice (d. 1668) was not his wife then and the will does not name Thomas's mother. Savage gives Elder John's (first) wife as Margaret. Her place as Thomas's mother still rests only on the Kingston gravestone record and the 1902 Prince Genealogy, so she stays Speculative. ¶4

Parentage still unknown. The Prince Genealogy (typescript, p. 7) says outright: 'Her pedigree is not known'. It says she was at Watertown with John Prince from 1633, and that 'Mrs.' was a title of respect. A large Honor family lived at Leighton Buzzard, Bedfordshire (Emmison, Bedfordshire Parish Registers vol. 31, 1945; djvu text read). Its baptisms 1595-1620 include Ann daughter of Henry Honor (1607) and a later Henry son of Henry (year unclear in the OCR, between 1607 and 1650), but no Alice (OCR reading, so a missed entry is possible). The FreeREG leads already noted (Kingston upon Thames 1611/12; Puttenham 1619) remain the only Alice Honor baptisms of the right years. No parents entered. ¶10

Life story

Death: 'PRINCE, Alice Honor, w. of John, —, 1668' (Vital Records of Hull, deaths p. 72, g.r.; page image). A gravestone entry, not a contemporary register entry. ¶1

Consistent, not proof: Isaac Prince (Thomas's presumed brother) and Mary had daughters 'Aallse' (17 Dec 1680) and 'Onner' (26 Oct 1701) at Hull (Vital Records of Hull, births pp. 31-32, OCR), and 'Alce, d. Isaac' was baptised at Scituate on 10 Apr 1681 (Vital Records of Scituate, vol. 1, p. 300, page image read). ¶3

Further evidence (compiled): Lincoln's History of Hingham (1893, vol. 3 p. 120) says Elder John Prince married Alice Honor at Watertown in May 1637, and that all his children, Thomas included, were by her and baptised by the Rev. Peter Hobart of Hingham. The Prince Genealogy memoir agrees, citing a family record of 1728 that gives seven sons and two daughters by Alice. The Hull vital records list a gravestone, 'Alice Honor, w. of John', 1668 (day and month not given). She stays Speculative because none of this is a contemporary record of Thomas's baptism. The record that would raise her is the Rev. Peter Hobart's own baptism list for Hingham (the source behind Lincoln), showing Thomas Prince baptised in 1658 as a child of John and Alice. ¶5

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

2 on the grade · 1 on the identification · 2 corrections · 2 alternatives set aside

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Confidence raised from Speculative to Probable, by bracketing her son Thomas's birth with two primary records. Elder John Prince's will of 9 May 1676 calls Thomas 'my youngest Son', 'yet under age', so he was born after 9 May 1655; the Suffolk deed of 1680 (Lib. XII, 58-59) shows Thomas acting and signing as an adult, acknowledged 31 January 1680/1, so he was born by about January 1660. Throughout 1655-1660 Elder John's wife was Alice: the Hull gravestone record calls her 'w. of John' at her death in 1668, and his second wife Anna was then still the wife of William Barstow of Scituate (d. 1668). In the same deed Thomas and his sisters call Anna 'our loveing mother Ann Prince the Relique of Elder John Prince', the usual form for a stepmother. This is inference from records, not a record naming Thomas's mother, so Probable rather than Confirmed. It agrees with the Kingston G.R.1 memorial ('s. Elder John and Alice Honor of Hull') and Lincoln's History of Hingham. ¶7a

Upgrade: Probable to Confirmed, graded on her link to her son Thomas Prince. The record is a contemporary printed obituary: the New England Weekly Journal of 15 July 1728 reported the death at Middleborough of Samuel Prince Esq., 'born at Boston May 1649', 'the 4th son of Mr. John Prince Ruling Elder of the Church of Hull', and said of the Elder: he 'Married Mrs. Alice Honor, by whom he had 7 Sons & 2 Daughters, & died August 16, 1676 in the 66th year of his Age... The sons as they grew up, took to Sea. John the eldest died at Hull, Joseph at Quebeck, Job was lost in the Channel of England, Benjamin died at Jamaica, Isaac at Boston, & Thomas the youngest at Barbadoes.' The notice was written by Rev. Thomas Prince, Samuel's son and so Alice's grandson, and is reprinted from his own annotated copy of the paper in NEHGR 5 (1851): 378-379. Continuity to the man in the tree: 'Thomas the youngest' who died at Barbados is the 'my youngest Son' Thomas, under age, of Elder John's will of 9 May 1676 (Suffolk Probate 6:128), and the Thomas Prince whose widow Ruth's probate account of 1704 names his sons Thomas, Benjamin and Job; the tree already records his death at Barbados about January 1696. No rival mother: the Elder's second wife Anne (widow of William Barstow) married him only after Barstow's death in 1668, ten years after Thomas's birth, and the 1728 notice makes all seven sons Alice's. Caveat for the auditor: this is a near-contemporary published notice by a family member (a grandson whose father was Alice's son), not a parish or town register entry; it names the relationship outright, which is why it is graded Confirmed rather than Probable. The earlier 'Identity (Speculative)' note and the Probable rating are superseded. ¶9

The case for this identification 1

Identity (Speculative): named as mother of Thomas Prince (b. 1658) only in the Kingston gravestone record ('s. Elder John and Alice Honor of Hull', Vital Records of Kingston, p. 371, G.R.1) and the 1902 Prince Genealogy. What would raise it: a contemporary record of Thomas's birth or baptism naming his parents, or Elder John Prince's probate naming Thomas. ¶2

Corrections 2

Correction: the 'mother' with whom her son Thomas was to live under his father's 1676 will was his stepmother Anna (Barstow), not Alice, who had died about 1668. ¶6

Correction: Audit 11 (2 Nov 2026): lowered from Confirmed to Probable, graded on her link to her son Thomas Prince. The raise rests on the obituary of Samuel Prince in the New England Weekly Journal of 15 July 1728, written by his son the Rev. Thomas Prince ('Married Mrs. Alice Honor, by whom he had 7 Sons & 2 Daughters... Thomas the youngest at Barbadoes'). That notice is a contemporary record of Samuel's death, but for Thomas's parentage it is a family statement made by a grandson about 70 years after Thomas's birth (about 1658) and 60 years after Alice's death (1668). It is not a record of the birth, baptism, marriage or probate that ties Thomas to Alice. It was also read only in the OCR text of an 1851 reprint (NEHGR 5:378-379), not in the newspaper. A late family statement counts as a compiled source, which caps the link at Probable (BRIEF: compiled sources; audit 6). The Probable rests on the bracket: Elder John Prince's will (9 May 1676) calls Thomas his youngest son, under age; the 1680/1 Suffolk deed shows Thomas of age; Alice was the Elder's wife until 1668; and the second wife Anne was still married to William Barstow until 1668. The 1728 notice strengthens that bracket but cannot replace a record. Test record (unchanged): the Rev. Peter Hobart's Hingham baptism list (the source behind Lincoln's History of Hingham) for Thomas, son of John and Alice Prince, about 1658; or an original image of the 1728 newspaper. The upgrade note is superseded on the grade only; its readings stand. ¶11

Alternatives considered and set aside 2

Caveat: the 1637 date of her marriage comes only from compiled sources; a record of Elder John's marriage to Alice before 1655 would close the gap. ¶7b

Leads only, not entered: FreeREG nationwide search for Alice Honor (fuzzy) baptised 1600-1622 finds Alice d. of Thomas Honner, Kingston upon Thames, Surrey, 8 Mar 1611/12, and Alice Honer (father's forename not given), Puttenham, Surrey, 12 Jun 1619 (both 'Other Transcript'). Nothing ties either to Mrs Alice Honor who married Elder John Prince at Watertown in 1637. ¶8
