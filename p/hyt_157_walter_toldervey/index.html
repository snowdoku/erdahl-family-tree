About the family

Sister-and-brother-in-law chain (reported, not yet read by this fleet): WikiTree (Tolderbury-2, Toldervey-9, Toldervey-10, Tolderby-3) reports (1) Katherine Toldervey married Edward Garrard of Inkpen at East Shefford, 5 Jul 1626 (FamilySearch England Marriages 1:1:NVML-WQ1); (2) Ursula Toldervey married John Milton at East Shefford 1621/2 (already in the tree's notes, 1:1:6D9R-BRWY); (3) Edward Garrard of Inkpen's will, PCC 1633 (TNA PROB 11/163), names 'John Prince' and 'John Milton' as his brothers-in-law; (4) Elizabeth Toldervey married 'John Prynce' at Great Shefford, 26 Sep 1606 (Findmypast Berkshire Marriages index, from the bishop's transcript); and (5) a Latin record of 8 Dec 1630 (the Inkpen administration) names Walter's widow Ann, his son-in-law Edward Gerrard and John Prince, cleric, of Great Shefford (abstracted in J. B. Prince, The Prince Family of Hull and Boston, 1976, Appendix 2). If these read as reported, Katherine (Walter's daughter by the Visitation), Ursula and Elizabeth were sisters, and Elizabeth Prince is Walter's daughter. ¶13

Parents: John Toldervey, esquire bedell of Arts (d. 1567), is entered as his Probable father (John Toldervey) and Alice as his Speculative mother (see her page); see their notes. WikiTree reports that the 1976 Prince Family appendix abstracts John's 1567 will as naming Walter among the younger children, and that the 1612 PCC will of 'William Toldervey of Oxford' (PROB 11/119) names Walter as uncle. ¶16

Life story

Toldervey wills at Oxford: the index of wills proved in the Oxford University Chancellor's Court lists John Toldervey, 'one of the Bedels' (1567), and 'Alice Toldervey, of St. Peter's-in-the-East, widow (probably of John following)' (1597). These are the John and 'Alyce' whom Noyes-Gilman makes Walter's parents. Alice's will of 1597 is the record most likely to name a son Walter, and perhaps his children, including Elizabeth (Oxford University Archives, Chancellor's Court wills; queued as S0224). Not read. Walter's parents are not entered. ¶5

Record placing him with the Princes: Walter Toldervey was a witness, with Joseph Nixon and John Parsons, to the will of Thomas Harvey of West Shefford alias Great Shefford, Berkshire, dated 1 Sep 1620 and proved in the Prerogative Court of Canterbury 30 Dec 1620 (Register Soame fol. 114; abstract no. 1312 in Lea and Church, Abstracts of Wills in the PCC, read in full text). Great Shefford is the parish where the Rev. John Prince and his wife Elizabeth had daughters baptised in 1614, 1615/16 and 1619/20 (Noyes-Gilman, from the register; Ancestry 9841 index for the first two). So a Walter Toldervey was in the Princes' own parish in the same year as their daughter Ursula's baptism. ¶6

Oxford: the Register of the University of Oxford (vol. II pt 1, pp. 307-308) lists Walter Toldervey among the Oxford citizens who took the oath to the university on 4 Oct 1613, the year Henry Toldervey was mayor. With the Archdeaconry of Berks administration of 'Walter Toldervie, Inkpen, gent.', 1630 (Inkpen is about 10 miles from Great Shefford), this gives one man of the name moving from Oxford (1613) to the Shefford-Inkpen district (1620-1630). No second Walter Toldervey was found in the Oxford registers, the Berkshire probate index or the PCC abstracts searched. ¶7

William Toldervey's will read on the image: TNA PROB 11/119/320, 'I William Tolderbey of the Cittie of Oxford', dated 30 Jan 1611[/12], proved 12 Apr 1612 by the widow Elizabeth. Burial in St Aldates ('St Towles') churchyard 'over against the North dore'. Legacies: 'unto my lovinge mother Joane Burrett five pounds', 'unto my uncle Walter Tolderbey five pounds', 'unto my brother James Tolderbey five pounds', a silver spoon to the Shoemakers' company, the residue and a house lease to 'my deare and lovinge wife Elizabeth Tolderbey'. So in 1612 Walter was the uncle of an Oxford Toldervey, presumably his brother's son (the mother had remarried as Burrett). This does not touch his link to Elizabeth Prince. ¶20

Ursula Toldervey married John Milton at East Shefford (original place given as East Shefford; the index normalises it to Great Shefford) on 17 Feb 1621[/2] (England, Berkshire, Parish Registers index, 1:1:6D9R-BRWY), in the Rev. John Prince's own parish, eight months before Edward son of John and Elizabeth Prince was baptised there. She is the only Toldervey in the Berkshire indexes 1570-1660, and very probably a sister or kinswoman of Elizabeth (Toldervey) Prince. Circumstantial only: the entry names no parent. ¶11

Administration: the printed index of the Archdeaconry of Berks court has '1630 Toldervie, Walter, Inkpen, gent., ad.' (administration, register ii, fo. 197). Inkpen lies in west Berkshire, about 10 miles south of East Shefford, where the Rev. John Prince was rector from 1619. This places a Walter Toldervey, gentleman, near the Prince family before 1630, which fits Noyes-Gilman's claim that Elizabeth Prince was Walter's daughter, but an administration names only the administrator, so it does not prove the link. The original grant and bond (Berkshire Record Office, archdeaconry administrations 1630) would name the administrator, perhaps a child or son-in-law. A Walter Toldervey also witnessed a will proved in the PCC on 30 Dec 1620 (Brown's Somerset abstracts, no. 1312). He stays Speculative. ¶4

Heraldic record naming him as a father: the Garrard pedigree drawn up in Elias Ashmole's hand for the Berkshire Visitation of 1665-6 (MS. Rawl. D. 86b fol. 92; printed in Rylands, Four Visitations of Berkshire II, p. 131, 'Garrard of Newbury') gives 'Edward Garrard of Inkpen' and his third wife 'Katherine da: to Walter Foldervey [sic] of the Citty of Oxford'; the printed index files it as 'Foldervey, Katherine; Walter', an obvious misreading of Toldervey. The informant was Joseph Garrard of Newbury, aged 38 in March 1664/5, very probably Katherine's son. This is the first record found that names Walter Toldervey of Oxford as the father of a daughter, and it puts that daughter at Inkpen, where Walter died by 1630. ¶12

Occupation: Joseph Toldervey, son of Walter Toldervey, apothecary, of Oxford, was bound apprentice to John Williams of the Goldsmiths' Company for seven years on 17 Jan 1617 (londonroll.org, event GSDM2225). This fits the Oxford citizen of 1613 and gives Walter a son Joseph. ¶14

Edward Garrard's will read on the image: TNA PROB 11/163/610 (the reference was found tonight: Register Russell, PROB 11/163/610), 'I Edward Garrard of Inkpen in the County of Berks gent', dated 23 Jan 1630[/1], proved May 1633. Overseers clause: 'And I hartily intreat my Cosen Roger Garrard of [?S]plamborne, Mr John Prince my brother in lawe, and Mr John Squire of Brimpton, and my brother in lawe John Milton to be my Overseers to this my last will and testament'. A memorandum (about the end of Michaelmas term 1630) is witnessed 'before my sonne in lawe John Williams and my Nephew John Prince in his owne howse'. The wife is not named in the will. The 'brother in lawe' wording, which took from WikiTree, is now read on the image. ¶18

Identified as the father of Elizabeth (Toldervey) Prince, wife of the Rev. John Prince of East Shefford, by Noyes-Gilman Ancestry (1907), p. 306 ('daughter of Walter and Ann Toldervey and granddaughter of Rev. John Toldervey, "Bedell of Arts, University of Oxford," and his wife Alyce'). No record is cited there. The book makes him a son of John Toldervey, esquire bedell of Arts at Oxford (elected 1540; dead by 10 May 1567, when Thomas Hutchinson was elected in place of 'Tollerbie' deceased), but that is not entered as a link. ¶1

Existence: a Walter Toldervey appears among the citizens of Oxford in the city lists printed in the Register of the University of Oxford, vol. II part 1 (index: 'Toldervey, Walter (cit. 1613); 308'). ¶2

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

4 on the grade · 1 record that disagrees · 1 search with no result · 4 open questions

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 4

Confidence Speculative: compiled claim only, partly at odds with the 1728 statement that Elizabeth was 'a Daughter of Dr. Tolderbury D.D. of Oxford'. ¶3a

Upgrade: Speculative to Probable, graded on his link to his daughter Elizabeth (see her page), wife of the Rev. John Prince. The father's name comes only from a compiled work (Noyes-Gilman Ancestry, 1907, pp. 306-307, unsourced: 'daughter of Walter and Ann Toldervey'), but it is now corroborated by an independent record that puts a Walter Toldervey at Great Shefford in 1620, among the Princes' neighbours, and by his Oxford citizenship, which fits the 1728 family statement that Elizabeth's father was 'of Oxford'. The surname difference is no obstacle: the Oxford register itself spells the family Toldervey, Tolderbery, Tolderbey and Tollerbie (index to vol. II). The relationship is still inferred, not stated in any record, so it is not Confirmed. ¶8

Grade: stays Probable, graded on his link to his daughter Elizabeth (see her page). The Visitation names Walter as Katherine's father, not Elizabeth's; Elizabeth is joined to the family only through the brother-in-law wording of Edward Garrard's will and the 1630 administration, neither yet read here, and 'brother-in-law' could in principle come from another of Garrard's three marriages. The rival Christopher Toldervey is now effectively excluded: the Visitation puts Walter's daughter at Inkpen and calls him 'of the Citty of Oxford', matching the 1728 'of Oxford'. ¶15a

Grade unchanged (Probable, graded on his link to his daughter Elizabeth Tolderbury),. Edward's third wife was Katherine, daughter of Walter Toldervey of Oxford (1665 Visitation); Ursula Toldervey married John Milton in 1621/2; so 'Mr John Prince my brother in lawe' fits John Prince as husband of another Toldervey sister, Elizabeth. That is still an inference (a brother-in-law can also be a sister's husband or a wife's sister's husband from another family), so the link to Elizabeth is Probable. ¶19a

Records that disagree 1

Contradiction weighed: the 1728 obituary calls her father 'Dr. Tolderbury D.D. of Oxford'. No Toldervey took the B.D. or D.D. at Oxford in 1571-1622 (Clark's Register II pt 3), so the title is wrong whoever her father was; it cannot pick out a rival. Named rival: Christopher Toldervey (student of Christ Church 1569, M.A. 1574, canon of Lincoln 1583), the one university-educated Toldervey of the generation, who is probably the 'Christopher Toldervey late of London, Esq.' whose son and heir Christopher of Chartham died in 1618 aged 31 (monument at Ash-next-Sandwich, Kent). Nothing links Christopher to Berkshire or to the Princes, while Walter is found at Great Shefford, so Walter is preferred; the rival is the reason this stays Probable. ¶9

Searches that found nothing 1

FamilySearch indexes: the only Toldervey (any spelling, Tolderv*) in the Berkshire indexes 1604-1628 is Ursula x John Milton, 17 Feb 1621/2. The 1606 Great Shefford marriage, a Katherine Toldervey x Garrard marriage, and John son of John Prince baptised at Great Shefford in 1612 are NOT in any FamilySearch index; they must come from another index (Ancestry 9841 or the Berkshire FHS transcripts). The images are center-only: West Shefford parish register (FHL film 251842, DGS 8088319) and West Shefford bishop's transcripts 1606-07, 1612-17 etc. (FHL film 1279467 items 18-19, DGS 7566488) are locked from home, and no image-bearing index record exists to open them by 'View Original Document'. No Toldervey is indexed in the England, Oxfordshire, Parish Registers 1538-1904 collection (3293406), whose Oxford city coverage is mostly burials. Unrelated lead noted: 'Eliz. Toldervey' married 'Dan. Caldwall' at Cheshunt, Hertfordshire, 27 May 1616 (England Marriages, 1:1:NLSV-GJ6). She cannot be Elizabeth (Toldervey) Prince, who was having children by John Prince at Great Shefford in 1614-1620. Added (FamilySearch, signed in, 2 Nov 2026). ¶17
