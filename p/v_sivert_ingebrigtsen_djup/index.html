About the family

His own father was named Ingebrigt; no record of him has been found. The 1701 male census for Djup lists only Knud Larsen (born about 1671), a tenant with leased land and a 'legsmand' or militia-district man, and Ole Olsen (about 1665), plus a stepson Peder Pedersen (1699) - no Ingebrigt, so the family probably came to the farm after 1701. ¶4

Parents: his father is entered as Ingebret Siursen of Godøy, whose son Siur was baptised at Borgund on 7 September 1701 (index). This link is Speculative. His own Confirmed label stands for his life at Djup, his children and his 1766 probate, all from records read; it does not extend to this parent link, which the new father carries as Speculative. See Ingebret's notes for the reasoning and the record that would settle it. ¶6

Mother, Speculative: Marrete, wife of Ingebret of Godøen, churched 20 Nov 1701 after the 7 Sep 1701 baptism of Siur son of Ingebret Siurss. (py00000000089013). Like the father link, this depends on the unproved identification of that Siur with Sivert of Djup. ¶8

Life story

Tenant farmer at Djup (Diub, Dyb) on the island of Giske in Borgund parish. The Borgund church books record him as the father of seven children baptized between 1728 and 1749: 14 November 1728, 22 November 1733, 17 April 1735, 27 April 1738 (Marthe), 18 June 1744 (Ingeborg), 3 December 1747 and 21 December 1749. The register spells his name Siur, Siufr, Sifvert and Siur Ingebrightsen indifferently - Sivert and Siur are the same name in Sunnmøre usage. The first baptism in 1728 puts his marriage about 1727 and his own birth about 1700. ¶1

Bygdebok: Borgund og Giske bd. 3 (nb.no ff134a2c9670bf6b5cbe848b7a7f9404, canvas 496, Dyb, rebuilt from word boxes): 'Sjur Ingebrigtsen Godøy (1701-66)', brukar 1727-66; '1728 [g.] m. Ingeborg Pedersdtr.... d. 1742, 45 år'; '1743 [g. m.] Inger Rasmusdtr. Karlsbakk (1707-80)'; successor Jørgen of Store Kalvøy (1735-84), m. 1768 'Ingeborg Sjursdtr.' (f. 1744), who held the bruk 1768-84; Ingeborg remarried 1786 (Ole Olsen Lille-Nørve). This gives his first wife's patronymic (Pedersdatter, b. about 1697), and names his birthplace as Godøy, born 1701. Secondary source. ¶9

Second marriage: married at Borgund on 1 January 1744 as a widower ('Enchemand') of Diub to Inger Rasmusdatter, Pige, of Carlsbachen (Borgund ministerialbok 1739-1761, index pv00000002823273; betrothal indexed 1743). His first wife, the mother of the children baptised 1728-1738, died before late 1743; her name is not yet found (a Diub burial of a wife in 1738-1743 is the next record). ¶7

His estate was registered on 14 August 1766 at the farm 'Dyb' in Giske by the Sunnmøre sorenskriveri (district recorder), protocol 3A 22, folio 736b. The probate index names no heirs; the protocol page itself would list them and is the record that would confirm his children one by one. ¶2

Probate: the protocol page itself has now been read (Sunnmøre sorenskriveri, skifteprotokoll 15B, 3A 22, fol. 736b-737; image 24563/277). On 14 August 1766 a registration (inventory) was held at Diub after the late Siur Ingebrigtsen. The parties were his surviving widow Inger Rasmusdatter, with her legal guardian (lauværge) Peder Jørgensen, apparently a court juror (lagrettemann), whose farm is illegible, and his children: son Nils Siursen of Valderøy, of age and present; son Hans Siursen of Hestenesøy (residence uncertain), of age and present; a daughter, perhaps Synneve (the name is uncertain), married to Ole Olsen; daughter Elisabeth Siursdatter, unmarried, with a guardian; and daughter Ingeborg Siursdatter, unmarried, with guardian (formynder) Rasmus Olsen of Diub. The entry gives no ages. It mentions no earlier marriage, but it calls the heirs only 'den afdødes efterladte Børn' (the deceased's surviving children), not the widow's or their joint children, so it does not say whether any child was Inger's. The children baptized in 1747 and 1749, and Marthe (1738), receive no share and were presumably dead by 1766. ¶5

He is the father of Ingeborg Siversdatter, who married Jørgen Paulsen of Store Kalvøy in 1768 and whose son John Jørgensen moved to Valderhaug - so he stands in the direct line to Rev. Peter Valder. ¶3
