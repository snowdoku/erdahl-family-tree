About the family

Her father Erik Johnsen Eidem emigrated from Selbu on 12 May 1868 with his wife Anne Larsdatter and her sister Ingeborg - two years after her wedding - so she probably crossed with or just before her own parents. ¶9

Life story

This settles the question the census and land agents raised. The American 'Brynhild J. Eidem, b. June 1843', enumerated in Union County as a widow with children GILBERT J. and MINNIE J., is this woman: the birth year agrees to the year, the Eidem surname and the county are right, her children's patronymic middle initial J is Johnsen/Johnsdatter, children of JOHN, and her own 'J.' is the ordinary American rendering of a married woman under her husband's name. Her true Norwegian patronymic is ERIKSDATTER. ¶6

It follows that the Union County homestead patent to 'BRYNEL JOHNSON EIDEM', document 260 - issued the same day as John Sivertson Eidem's document 259, for an interlocking parcel of the same section 1 - is HERS, the widow proving up her late husband's claim, and not her mother-in-law Brynhild Jonsdatter Langli's. The two women's names collide in American spelling and only the Norwegian patronymics separate them. ¶7

1865 census: 'Brynild Eriksdatter', b. 1843 (calculated), birthplace Selbo Prgj., at Selbo: EIDEM (NEGAARD), unmarried, 'Tjenestejente' - a servant girl on another holding of the same farm, three months before her wedding. ¶5

1870 census, probable: the wife in John Severson's household at Brule Creek, Union County, 23 August 1870 (family 91), is written 'Betsey', 27, keeping house, born Norway, with a daughter Bidrine, 2, born Dakota. The age fits a birth about 1843. The name does not match Brynild, so the identification rests on her husband (see the note on John Sivertson Eidem). ¶10

1900 federal census: Brule township, Union County, South Dakota, Ancestry image 4120605_00156 - 'Brynila J Eidem, HEAD, b. June 1843, Norway', a widow, with her son Gilbert J. (b. July 1877, South Dakota) and daughter Minnie (b. June 1879, South Dakota). Her household is on the same sheet as Ole S. Eidem's and two sheets from Severt's. ¶1

1910 federal census: she is 'Brynhild Eidem, MOTHER, b. 1844 Norway' in her son Gilbert's household in the same township. ¶2

Inference, flagged as such: her son's middle initial is J, and in this colony the children's middle initials are strict patronymics - Severt's children are all 'S', Ole's are all 'O'. A Gilbert J. Eidem of Brule Creek therefore had a father named John. The only John Eidem of that generation in this family is John Sivertson Eidem (b. 30 Jan 1842, `John Sivertson Eidem`), Severt's and Ole's eldest brother. If that holds, Brynhild is his widow and Gilbert and Minnie are Mayme's first cousins. I have NOT set the parent links, because the argument is onomastic and not documentary; it is a strong lead for the agents who own the Selbu and Elk Point vitals. ¶3

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade · 1 on the identification · 1 alternative set aside

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Rated Probable: the Selbu marriage is documentary and unambiguous, but the chain from it to the Union County widow rests on agreeing particulars rather than on one record naming both. The land-entry case file for document 260 would name her outright. ¶8

The case for this identification 1

Identified in Norway: she is BRYNILD ERIKSDATTER of Eidem, Selbu, and she married John Sivertson Eidem in Selbu church on 3 APRIL 1866. The entry (Selbu parish register 1860-1877, marriages, page 18 serial 9, with the duplicate at 1859-1868 page 149 serial 7) gives her birth year as 1843 (calculated), her birthplace as Eidem, and her father as 'E. Johnsen' - Erik Johnsen Eidem. ¶4

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Possible burial, not proved: the DT58 register of Union County lists BRENHILD EIDEM, 1913 (year only), SOUTH ST PAULS LUTHERAN cemetery, lot 23, block 1, the same lot as JOHN S EIDEM, 1881. A shared lot suggests she may be John's widow, but the register gives no relationships, and no death record for her has been read. Her death record or stone would settle it. ¶11
