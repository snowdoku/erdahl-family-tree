Life story

Record naming him as a parent: the Murray Harbour Bible Christian register records the adult baptism on 20 April 1877 of Mary Ann Moore, born 20 September 1845, daughter of Richard and Sarah (PEI baptisms index, record 72100). It is the only record found that names Richard and Sarah together as parents, and it matches the 1861 household: Richard over 60, a wife aged 45-60, and a daughter counted as 16-21. ¶2

Martock, Somerset lead unresolved: the only Moore baptism to a Richard and Sarah at Martock is Richard, 4 Jun 1826, in the Bower Hinton Independent chapel register (TNA RG 4, page 53; FamilySearch RG4-8 index ark 1:1:F4HV-RNG; no image online). No other child of Richard and Sarah Moore is indexed at Martock in 1810-1845, in either the Independent register or the parish registers (FamilySearch, Moore exact at Martock, 53 records: only William x Rebecca Mander 1820, Thomas x Mary Lye 1825, Thomas x Elizabeth Chaffey 1829 and their families). A single baptism with no sibling set cannot be matched to the PEI family. It stays a lead only. The register image (TNA RG 4, Bower Hinton Independent; on The Genealogist / BMD Registers) would give the child's birth date and the father's abode or trade. ¶23

1841 census: the 1841 census of Prince Edward Island (Kings County, District 15, Lot 64, image 531) lists Richard Moore, with no occupation entered, as head of a household of ten. It comprises himself (the only male aged 45-60), five boys under 16, one woman aged 16-45 and three girls under 16. On the religion and land page (image 532) all ten are entered as Baptists, and no land, lease or rent is entered against him. On the produce page (image 533) the household had paid its own passage (6 persons), kept 4 hogs and recorded no crops. Six members were natives of England and four were natives of the Island. ¶15

Birth range: he was in the 45-60 band in 1841 and over 60 in 1861, which together place his birth between about 1781 and 1796. The birth date is narrowed from BEF 1801 to BET 1781 AND 1796. This is an inference from the two age bands. ¶16

Emigration (inference from the 1841 census): six English-born members (presumably he, his wife and four children) and four Island-born children by mid-1841 mean the family came after the birth of its youngest English-born child and before the birth of its eldest Island-born child. If John Moore (born 10 April 1829, England) is one of the English-born sons, they left after April 1829. The Island-born four probably include Grace (January 1838), Humphrey (about 1839) and Benjamin (January 1841), plus one more child not yet identified. At ordinary spacing that fourth child was born by about 1836-37, so the family arrived about 1829-37. They paid their own passages, so this was not a proprietor-assisted settlement. ¶17

Religion, 1841: the whole household was entered as Baptist, two years before the 1843 Bible Christian revival at Murray Harbour. In 1861 the household was two Baptists and six Bible Christians, and the family was Baptist again by 1881. ¶18

Another Richard Moore: a different, younger Richard Moore and his wife (Catherine) Martha baptised daughters in the same register: Sarah (b. 14 January 1848), Catherine (b. 19 January 1848, perhaps the same child), Harriet Ann (b. 7 May 1850) and Maria (b. 6 April 1853). They were not in Lot 64 in 1861. He may be an English-born elder son of this Richard, but no record links them. ¶13

1861 census, Lot 64, Kings County: Richard Moore, farmer, head of a family of eight - himself over 60 (so born before 1801), a woman aged 45-60, two daughters (16-21 and 21-45) and four sons (two 16-21, two 5-16); two married persons and six single. Two members were Baptists and six Bible Christians. Two persons in the family were natives of England and six natives of PEI, which makes Richard and his wife the immigrants. ¶3

Land, 1861: Richard held 80 acres by leasehold on a 999-year lease on the Cunard estate (the proprietor of the page, Samuel Cunard), third-quality land, of which only 3 acres were arable. He raised 5 bushels of oats and 35 of potatoes and kept 2 neat cattle and 2 hogs, but no horse or sheep - the farm of a poor tenant still clearing his land. ¶4

Death: not in the 1881 census, when his probable widow Sarah lived with their son William; so he died between 1861 and 1881. No death or burial record has been found (the PEI death card index has no Richard Moore). ¶5

Origin: England (1861 census). The Island Register lineage of Richard Moore and Jane Wilt of Stourpaine and Tarrant Rushton, Dorset, whose descendants settled at York River, PEI, includes a Richard Moore baptised at Tarrant Rushton on 7 January 1759; he is too old to be this Richard and no link has been shown - noted only as a lead. ¶6

Religion: in 1861 two members of his household were Baptists and six Bible Christians; the Bible Christian revival of 1843 under Francis Metherall swept the Murray Harbour district (Metherall's history of the Bible Christian Church in PEI, context only). By 1881 his widow and sons were all Baptists. ¶9

1863 map: Lake's Topographical Map of PEI (1863) marks 'R. Moore' at Toronto, an inland settlement in Lot 64, with 'T. Hayter' on the adjoining farm to the west and J. Hooper, T. Giddings and Jas. Bull to the east - the same neighbours who flank him on the 1861 census page. His daughter Grace married the Hayter son next door in December 1862. ¶7

1880 atlas: by 1880 the Toronto land was divided into a row of adjoining strip farms held by Humphrey, Chas., Wm. and Benj. Moore (Meacham's atlas), the pattern of a leasehold split among sons after the father's death. This supports both his death before 1880 and the four men's being his sons. ¶8

Place: the 'Toronto' of the 1863 map is the settlement later called Abney, Lot 64, where his sons Benjamin and William and the younger Albert Moore lived and died. ¶12

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence (unchanged, Probable) as Grace's father: the 1841 household has three girls under 16 and four Island-born members, which fits Grace (aged 3, born on the Island). But the census names only the head, so it adds consistency, not proof. ¶19

The case for this identification 1

Identification: the probable father of Grace Amelia Moore (Mrs Thomas Hayter). Rated Probable from the composition of his 1861 household, which matches Grace and her four brothers exactly, and from his being the only Moore head of household in Lot 64 in 1861. ¶1

Alternatives considered and set aside 2

Possible elder son (unproved): John Moore, born 10 April 1829 (1901 census; England per 1881 and 1891, PEI per 1901), Bible Christian then Baptist, farmed the strip next to Benjamin's at Toronto in 1880. If he was Richard's son, the family emigrated from England between 1829 and 1838. He was not in Richard's 1861 household. ¶10

Woodbury, Devon family ruled out (2 Nov 2026): FamilySearch's indexes of the Woodbury parish registers and bishop's transcripts (England, Devon, Parish Registers 1529-1974; Devon Bishop's Transcripts; England Births and Christenings) give Richard Moore (also Moor) and Sarah a continuous run of baptisms at Woodbury: Walter Thomas (28 Nov 1825), Manuel Horatio (24 Jun 1827), Frederick Henry (10 Feb 1829), Mary Ann Evelina and Francis Jesse (21 Sep 1831), Eliza Catherine (26 Sep 1833), Sarah Ann (5 Sep 1835), Emma Maria (20 Mar 1838) and Elizabeth Ann (23 May 1844). Richard Moore married Sarah FOSTER at Woodbury on 28 Apr 1825 (England Marriages index). The 1851 census still has Richard Moore's daughters Mary (b. 1831), Eliza (1833) and Emma (1837) at Woodbury. This family was therefore baptising children in Devon in 1838 and 1844, when Richard and Sarah of Lot 64 were on Prince Edward Island (Grace born there 13 Jan 1838, Humphrey 1839, Benjamin 1841, all in the 1841 census), and none of the names matches the PEI children. It is not the Lot 64 family. ¶22

Searches that found nothing 4

Searches that found nothing: no Richard Moore in the PEI marriage registers 1832-88, the PEI death card index 1810-1913, the PEI House of Assembly journals (poor relief and road lists, 1830s-1880s), Find a Grave, or the Island Register's Moore lineages (the Dorset Moores of York River, the Portsea Moores of Albion, the London Moores of Charlottetown - none has a Richard who fits). ¶11

English origin not found: the Moores' Bible Christian membership points to the West Country (Devon or Cornwall), where that denomination began, but a FreeREG sweep of all 267 Moore baptisms in Devon and Cornwall from 1815 to 1838 found no child of a Richard and Sarah Moore. A FreeREG search of all counties for a Richard Moore marrying a Sarah between 1815 and 1837 gives only scattered candidates with nothing to link them. FreeREG's coverage of these counties is incomplete. ¶14

English origin, further free searches (2 Nov 2026; leads only, nothing proved): (1) The England-wide christening index (Ancestry 9841) holds 59 baptisms 1819-1839 of Moore children whose parents are given exactly as Richard and Sarah. Only three are in the South-West: Richard, baptised 4 Jun 1826 at the Bower Hinton Independent chapel, Martock, Somerset; and Mary Ann Evelina and Francis Jesse, both baptised 21 Sep 1831 at Woodbury, Devon, where a Richard Moore had married a Sarah on 28 Apr 1825 (Ancestry 9852; her surname is hidden on the free view). The Martock entry is of interest because it is a Dissenting baptism and the PEI family was entirely Baptist in 1841, and because a younger Richard Moore with a wife (Catherine) Martha was baptising children in the Murray Harbour Bible Christian register in 1848-53, who would have been about 22 in 1848 if he were the Martock child. Nothing links either family to Lot 64; both are leads to test, not findings. The Woodbury family's ornate names (Evelina, Jesse) fit a better-off household than a poor Lot 64 tenant. (2) No John Moore baptised 1828-1830 to a Richard and Sarah appears in the christening index (16 John Moore baptisms of those years with a mother Sarah; fathers William, John, Henry, Thomas, Joseph, Robert, James, Benjamin), so the possible elder son John (born 10 Apr 1829) is not found. (3) FreeREG marriages of a Richard Moore 1812-1835 in Somerset, Devon, Cornwall and Dorset include no bride Sarah except Sarah Harding at Walcot (Bath), 26 Jan 1814. (4) 'Wake' test: the middle name Wake given to two grandsons (Humphrey Wake Moore, b. 1874, and Joseph Wake Hayter, b. 1883, both grandsons of Richard and Sarah through their children Humphrey and Grace) suggested Sarah's maiden name might be Wake. No marriage of a Sarah Wake to a Richard Moore is in FreeREG (14 Sarah Wake marriages 1812-1838, none to a Moore) or in the England marriage index (Ancestry 9852, 27 Sarah Wake marriages 1814-1834, none to a Moore). ¶20a

Murray Harbour Bible Christian register (negative): images 135-136 of FamilySearch DGS 005240355 (baptisms nos. 121-152, March 1850 to July 1852, by John Butcher and Jesse Whitlock, B.C. ministers) contain no child of Richard and Sarah Moore. The Moore entries there are Charlotte Hannah (George and Ann Moore, b. Oct 1845, bapt. 11 July 1851) and Charles (William and Mary Moore, b. Jan 1847, bapt. 11 July 1851). The same page has the Hayter baptisms of 9 April 1851 (nos. 129-131: James, Jane Eliza, Sarah Catharine White, all of Thomas and Martha Elizabeth Hayter); no sponsors are recorded in this register. ¶21
