About the family

His land is in the same section 1 as the patents of Brynhild Jonsdatter Eidem, John Sivertson Eidem and, in 1891, Severt S. Eidem. Four Eidem patentees held parts of one 640-acre section. His own patronymic is Erickson and the patent immediately after his, document 666, is Erick Johnsen Eidem's, so he is very probably that man's son. His relationship, if any, to the Sivert Johnson Eidem family is unproved; rated Speculative and no link asserted. ¶2

Life story

Tentatively placed as a son of Erik Johnsen Eidem, b. 1817 at Eidem in Selbu, the Union County patentee whose Norwegian identity is now established. The patronymic Erickson fits exactly, the county is right, and Erik's known children run Brynild b. 1843, Lars b. 1845, Ingeborg b. 1848 and Johana b. 1853, so an elder son John born about 1838-1842 and gone out ahead of the family is entirely possible. ¶3

Land patent, 1874: patent of 10 DECEMBER 1874, document number 665, accession STA-SD1060__.101, for the NORTH-WEST QUARTER OF THE SOUTH-EAST QUARTER, the NORTH HALF OF THE SOUTH-WEST QUARTER, and the SOUTH-WEST QUARTER OF THE SOUTH-WEST QUARTER OF SECTION 1, TOWNSHIP 92 NORTH, RANGE 50 WEST OF THE 5TH PRINCIPAL MERIDIAN, Union County, Dakota Territory. ¶1

How sure are we?

Speculative. A plausible candidate only. Treat this link as a question, not a fact.

1 on the grade

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Rated Speculative: no Selbu baptism has yet been matched to him, and the 1865 census household at Pladsen does not contain him - which is consistent with a son already in America by 1865, and equally consistent with his being somebody else's son altogether. The record that would settle it is a Selbu baptism to Erik Johnsen and Anne Larsdatter about 1838-1845. ¶4
