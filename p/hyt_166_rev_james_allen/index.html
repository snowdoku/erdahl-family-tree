About the family

Family clue: the 18-acre farm 'was devised to me by my Uncle Penn dec[ease]d' (vol. 17 p. 101, image 67). This is James Penn, ruling elder of the First Church of Boston (d. 1671); the annotated Book of Possessions (Boston Town Records, FamilySearch full-text snippet) says that in 1671 the lot 'fell by his will to James Allen, his nephew'. 'Uncle' could be through either parent (or by marriage), so no parent is entered. ¶5a

Parents, leads only: (1) Foster, Alumni Oxonienses: 'Allen, James, "cler. fil." Magdalen Hall, matric. 16 March, 1649-50; chaplain New Coll. (by the parl. visitors) 1649, and fellow 1650, B.A. 28 April, 1652, M.A. 30 May, 1654'. The matriculation gives only 'clergyman's son', with no father's name or county, so it confirms Weis's 'son of a Hampshire minister' only as to the father's calling. (2) His 'Uncle Penn' (will of 1710) calls him only 'kinsman': the will of Elder James Penn (1671) 'mentions kinsmen James Allen and Penn Townsend, sister Hannah Townsend and her sons' (Waters, Notes on the Townsend Family, 1882), and leaves Allen 'an enlargement of his ground'. Penn names no Allen sister. (3) The will of Penn's widow Katherine, 25 Oct 1679, 'names only her kinsmen James Allen & his sons James, John & Jeremiah', and is sealed with arms (party per chevron argent and ermine, in chief two leopards' heads erased) that the Heraldic Journal (IV: 110) identified as Alleyne arms (Lea, Penn Gleanings, p. 35). Katherine using Allen arms suggests she may have been an Allen herself, which would make her James's paternal aunt and James Penn his uncle by marriage. That is a hypothesis only; no parent is entered. ¶6

Life story

Origin: Weis, Colonial Clergy (read): 'b. Hampshire, Eng., June 24, 1632, son of a Hampshire minister; matric. Magdalen Hall, Oxford, Mar. 16, 1649/50; Chaplain, New Coll.... Fellow... ejected for non-conformity, 1662; came to N.E., June 10, 1662... d. Boston, Sept. 22, 1710, a. 78'. Foster's Alumni Oxonienses describes him as 'cler. fil.' (a clergyman's son) but does not name the father. The Background of Mary Alice (Sharpe) Yalden Thomson (compiled) repeats 'son of a minister in Hampshire'. No father entered. ¶3a

Rev. James Allen, an ejected English minister who arrived at Boston 10 June 1662 and from 1668 was teacher of the First Church; husband of Elizabeth (Houchin) (Waldo Genealogy i:118, compiled). He acquired some 36 acres at West Boston, and by his deed of settlement of 1706 (Suffolk Deeds 23:8) and his will of 1710 he vested the twenty-acre farm north of Cambridge Street in 'his son, John Allen', and the northern ten acres of his pasture south of Cambridge Street in his son Jeremiah (Gleaner XI and XIX, abstracts of the deeds). ¶1

James Penn's will (1671), as quoted in the 'Gleaner' articles on the Boston land titles (Record Commissioners' Fifth Report, 1880): 'I give, etc. to Mr. James Allen all my pasture, being eighteen acres, more or less, lying between Major Leverett and Captain Davis, to enjoy after my wife's decease forever', and to Allen 'an enlargement of his ground to the pear tree'; Penn's dwelling house went to 'his kinsman, Col. Penn Townsend'. The quoted words give Allen no relationship to Penn; the relationship comes from Allen's own will ('my Uncle Penn'). So no parent can be entered from the printed quotations. Test record unchanged: the full text of James Penn's will (Suffolk Probate, 1671; dated 1671, Penn d. 30 Oct. 1671 per the First Church records, Col. Soc. Mass. 39). ¶8

Penn wills read on the images, for Rev. James Allen's parentage: NEGATIVE. (1) Katharine Penn's original will, Suffolk probate case 1270 (FamilySearch 'Massachusetts, Suffolk County, probate & family court records', DGS 102889990, images 172-174; folder 'No. 1270 Pen - Katharine 1682/3 Feb 7 Will'): 'I Katharine Pen widdow & Relict of Mr James Pen late Elder of the first Church of Christ in Boston'... 'I give to ye Children of my Kinsman Mr James Allen the One hundred pounds' in Jno. Man's hands, then 'to his son James my great silver cup', 'to his daughter Hannah my silver tankerd', 'to his Son John a bed', 'to his son Jeremiah & his daughter Sarah' two silver spoons each, and 'my aforesaid Kinsman Mr James Allen to be my Executor'. Dated 25 Oct. 1679; signed 'The marke of Katharine K P Pen' with a wax seal; witnesses Humphry Davie, John Fayerweather, Richard Raymond; sworn 7 Feb. 1682/3 before Gov. Simon Bradstreet, Wm Stoughton and Samuel Nowell. She calls him only 'Kinsman'; there is no 'brother', 'nephew' or maiden name. (2) James Penn's will, in a later Suffolk probate record-book copy, pp. 567-568 (FamilySearch 'Massachusetts. Probate Estate Case Files 1671-1697, 1697-1742', images 1035-1036): 'I give to my Kinsman Mr James Allen all my Pasture being Eighteen Acres', 'my Kinsman Penn Townsend' (a sister's son) the Pullin Point farm, 'my Sister Hannah Townsend' and her children Peter, James and Deborah, 'the Children of [Richard?] Hull namely Thomas Mary & Hannah Hull', 'my dear Wife my sole Executrix', overseers Elder John Wiswall and Mr Anthony Stoddard, dated 29th of the 7th month 1671; and a codicil 'to my Sister Mrs Mary M[innin?] ten pounds'. Again only 'Kinsman' for Allen, the same word he uses for his nephew Penn Townsend. Neither will names Katharine's maiden name or Allen's father, so the father cannot be added and the Allen-born hypothesis stays untested. Crops: (and part2, part3), and...-p568-....png. Note: the Suffolk index (vol. 3 p. 22) gives Katharine's will as case 1270 (1683) and James Penn's (1671) as 'misc'l docket'; the original vol. 7 ff. 153-154 entry was not browsed. ¶10

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Graded Probable on his link to John Allen: the Boston register names 'John of James & Elizabeth Allen', born 29 Feb. 1671/2, and the First Church baptised 'John Allen of our Teacher' in 1672; the Gleaner's abstract of the 1706 settlement calls the farm's grantee 'his son, John Allen'. That this John is the goldsmith and the father of Jeremiah (b. 1699) is taken from compiled works (see John's notes). ¶2

Upgrade: graded on his link to John Allen. His own will, recorded in Suffolk Probate Records vol. 17 pp. 101-102 (docket 3272; DGS 007703076 images 67-68, image read): 'I James Allen of Boston in New Eng[lan]d Clerk, being now in the Seventy fourth year of my Age', dated 'the Twelfth day of March 1705/6 In the Fifth Year of the Reign of Queen Anne', witnesses Tho. Palmer, B. Walker, Samuel Lillie. He leaves 'unto my son John Allen his heires & assignes forever All that my Farm... in his present Tenure and Occupation being, w[hi]ch was devised to me by my Uncle Penn dec[ease]d Containing by Estimation Eighteen acres', with two acres of meadow bought of John Dassett, and 'my Negro man Richard now living with him'; his mansion house to 'my Son Jeremiah Allen', who is to pay 'my Daughter Hannah Danforth' (wife of the Rev. Samuel Danforth of Taunton) £100; land to Jeremiah and 'his Daughter Mary Allen' (my granddaughter); his books to 'my sd Two Sons John Allen & Jeremiah Allen'; his reserved movables to 'my Daughter in Law Mary Allen, Wife of my sd Son Jeremiah'; residue of money to 'my sd Three Children Viz[t] Hannah Danforth, John Allen & Jeremiah Allen'; executors 'my aforesd Two Sons John Allen & Jeremiah Allen'. Probate 18 Oct. 1710 before Isaac Addington: the will of 'James Allen late of Boston aforesd Cler[k] dec[ease]d' proved and administration committed 'unto his two sons John Allen & Jeremiah Allen Exec[utors] in the same will named'. Continuity to the John in the tree: the Boston register's 'John of James & Elizabeth Allen', b. 29 Feb. 1671/2 (First Church: 'John Allen of our Teacher'), is the only son John recorded for Rev. James; the will's son John holds the 18-acre farm 'in his present Tenure' that the 1706 settlement (Suffolk Deeds 23:8) and John Allen the goldsmith's own estate (Probate 55:293, 64:465) carry down; no rival John Allen son of James. The earlier 'Graded Probable' note is superseded: the will names the relationship. ¶4

Searches that found nothing 1

Parents, search of 2 Nov 2026: no father found, and no parent entered. (1) Matthews, Calamy Revised (1934), read in archive.org full-text snippets: 'ALLEN, James. Fellow of New College, Oxford. 1649-60. Congregational. (Al. Oxon.: Cal. 75.) Son of a Hampshire minister. Born 24 June 1632. Magdalen Hall, Oxf., mc. 1650: B.A. 1652: M.A. 1654. Went out — "to his relations" in New England: reached Boston 10 June 1662 (Savage...)', ord. teacher of 1st Church, Boston, 19 Dec. 1668, 'Died 22 Sept. 1710. Married (1) 18 Aug. 1663, Hannah (d. 1668), daughter of Ric. Dummer. (2) Elizabeth (d. 1673), daughter of Jer. Houchin, and widow of John Endicott...'. Matthews does NOT name the father; his only authority for the father is Calamy's Account (1713) p. 75. Weis, Savage, Ellis (History of the First Church, 1881, p. 118: 'His father was a minister in Hampshire'), Putnam's Monthly (1893-4) and Knoff, Thirty-one English Emigrants (1989) all repeat 'son of a minister in Hampshire' with no name. (2) Foster's 'cler. fil.' entry cites only Burrows, Register of the Visitors (Camden 1881) p. 170; the Visitors' register lists 'Allen, James, Fellow, 1650' and a 'Mr. Allen' admitted 4 Sept. 1649 among the chaplains, with no father or county. (3) He was NOT a Winchester College scholar: Kirby, Winchester Scholars (1888) has only 'Allen, James (13), St. Mary Overy', admitted 1630 (so born c.1617, of Southwark) — a different man; our James came in through Magdalen Hall and a Visitors' appointment, so no Winchester entry giving a birthplace exists. (4) No Hampshire clergyman named Allen/Alleyn beneficed c.1620-1645 appears in Foster (Oxford) or Venn (Cambridge) full text; the only Hampshire Alleyn is Abraham Alleyn, D.D., rector of West Meon from 1660 (too late, and his sons were born 1651-67). So the father was probably a non-graduate curate or lecturer, or a graduate whose benefice Foster/Venn omit. CCEd (theclergydatabase.org.uk) returned HTTP 503 all session and could not be searched. (5) FreeREG: no baptism of a James Allen/Allin 1628-1636 in Hampshire, Wiltshire, Dorset, Berkshire, Surrey, Isle of Wight or Oxfordshire (the 1632 Hampshire registers are hardly transcribed there). (6) The 'relations' in New England: Calamy's 'to his relations' fits his 'Uncle Penn'. Lea's Penn Gleanings p. 35 notes that the Heraldic Journal identified Katherine Penn's seal (1679) as the arms of 'the Alleynes, Baronets, formerly of Barbadoes', and that her will names only Allen kin, not the Townsends who were James Penn's blood kin (Penn's sister Hannah Townsend and her children). That pattern favours Katherine Penn being born an Allen, the sister of James's father, rather than James's mother being a Penn. Hypothesis only. ¶9a
