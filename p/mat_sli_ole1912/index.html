Life story

Second child of Ole Gundersen Slind and Ingeborg Olsdatter, Oline Slind's nephew. Born 30 March 1912 in Selbu, in the 1920 census of his father's farm at Kjosnes sondre, and emigrated with the family on the Stavangerfjord on 7 June 1922 aged ten. ¶2

Find a Grave records 'Dr Ole Slind, 30 Mar 1912 - 23 Sep 2008' at Selbu Cemetery, La Crosse, Whitman County, Washington - the same cemetery as his father, and an exact match on the day of birth. Rated Probable rather than Confirmed only because the memorial carries no parents, no inscription and no obituary; the date agreement with the Norwegian census, in the same small cemetery as his father, is nonetheless very strong. The title 'Dr' suggests the boy who left Selbu at ten took a degree in America. ¶3

Emigrated with his parents and five siblings, Trondheim 7 June 1922 on the Stavangerfjord: emigrant protocol no. 15, page 140, line 29, group 00883, 'barn', residence Selbu, destination 'U.S. Amerika'. ¶1

Marriage: BERTHA THERESSA TJERANDSEN, 15 JUNE 1940, King County, Washington - King County Marriage Records 1855-2017, reference kingcoarchmcvol84_414, groom entered as "Ole Slind Jr", which is how the second Ole in the family was distinguished from his father. The same licence is filed in Pend Oreille County with the bride's surname mangled four different ways in four separate index rows - Tjerandsen, Tjersandsen, Fjerandsen, Yerandsen and Tyesaduch - a good illustration of why a surname must be searched as a prefix in the Washington index. ¶4

Washington DOH Death Index records "SLIND, OLE, 2008, Skagit County" and "SLIND, BERTHA T, 2008, Skagit County" - husband and wife dying in the same year in the same county. Note a discrepancy with what the tree already carries: the existing entry gives his death as 23 September 2008 in WHITMAN County, while the Washington state death index says Skagit. I have not overwritten the date; the county should be checked against the certificate, and Skagit is where his brother Gilbert also died (1993) and where the family's north-western branch lived. ¶5

The "Jr" is not strictly correct - he was his father's son, not his namesake's - but it is the usual American way of handling two Oles in one household, and the family had two Oles in two generations as well as two Oles among Gunder Olsen Slind's own sons. ¶6
