Life story

Robert Barnes, M.D., sometime fellow of Merton College, Oxford. He made his will on 1 Jan 1603/4; his wife Anne survived him. The will names his daughters Elizabeth (wife of Michael Hugganson), Anne (late wife of Godfrey Barton), Mary the elder (wife of William Bowne), Eleanor (wife of Thomas Yaiden), Mary the younger (wife of Henry Clark of Worton) and 'yongest, Prudence, wife of John Eveleigh, M.A. and principall of Hart hall' (Anthony Wood's abstract, Survey of Oxford, vol. 3, p. 248). Added. ¶1

Foster (Alumni Oxonienses) adds that his will was proved at Oxford in 1604 (day and month partly illegible in the OCR: '12...'). He held a Magdalen College lease of a house on Canditch from 28 Jun 1588 (Salter, Cartulary of St John's Hospital, vol. 2, p. 315; the rental of about 1591 has 'Mr Barnes [Dr Barnes, 1591]') and Christ Church's 'farme place once of Ruley' in St Giles, both of which passed to his daughter Prudence Eveleigh (Cartulary of Oseney, vol. 2, p. 199). His parents were still not found: Wood's Athenae and Fasti give none, and Griffiths's index of Chancellor's Court wills is not on archive.org. His wife Anne is now entered (see her page) as Prudence's mother at Speculative only. Added. ¶4

His widow: the St Giles, Oxford, burials copied by Wood include 'Ann Barnes, widdow of Dr. Robert Barnes a phisitian, 21 Dec. 1612'. She is the wife Anne named in his 1603/4 will. She is NOT added as Prudence's mother. The will names two living daughters called Mary ('Mary the elder' and 'Marye the yonger'), which usually means children of two different wives. Barnes was B.A. by 1534 and so born about 1515, while Prudence, the youngest daughter, was still having children in 1603-04. So Anne may well have been a second wife. His own parents were not found. Foster (Barnes, Robert, B.A. 1534, fellow of Merton 1538, D.Med. 1566, Linacre lecturer 1558) points to Wood's Athenae and Fasti, which give no parentage. Test record for Prudence's mother: the original will of Robert Barnes, proved in the Oxford University Chancellor's Court, 1604 (Oxford University Archives, Hyp/B), which may say which daughters were Anne's; St Giles or St Mary Magdalen, Oxford, registers for his marriage(s). Added. ¶3
