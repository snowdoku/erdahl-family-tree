About the family

Siblings: Paul (1881), Anna Therese Halvorson (1882-1935), Carl P. O. (1883-1979), Gustav Andreas (1885-1943), Anton Johannes (1887-1965), Marie Lovise (1888), Rev. Erling L. Tungseth (1893-1968; mayor of Thief River Falls 1931-34, Minnesota state senator 1935-42). ¶4

Her father had gone ahead alone on 11 Oct 1893, bound for Manitoba, five weeks after her brother Erling was born. Her Norwegian grandmother Tørri Jakobsdatter died at Tungset on 20 Jun 1893 and her grandfather Paul Ellingsen on 14 Jan 1894, so both of her father's parents died in the months between his departure and the family's. ¶7

The family's route after China, from Palmer Valder's obituary: from China back to Norway, then to Mayville, North Dakota, and finally to Ferndale, Washington, where other missionaries they knew had settled - which is how the Valders came to be in the same Ferndale Lutheran Brethren community as the Erdahls. Peter Valder went back to China and later to Taiwan on several further missionary trips. Their youngest son Fred was born in Ferndale on 28 Dec 1928, so the family was in Washington by the end of 1928. ¶10

Her brother and sisters, with the dates now established from Norwegian records and American gravestones: Paul P., 1881-1934; Anna Therese (married successively Awrey, Bodwell and Halvorson), 1882-1935; Karl Petter Olai 'Carl P. O.', 3 Sep 1883 - 23 Mar 1979; Gustav Andreas, 12 Jun 1885 - 23 Jun 1943; Anton Johannes, 3 Jan 1887 - 11 Nov 1965; Marie Lovise, born 1888; and Rev. Erling Ludvig 'E. L.', 9 Sep 1893 - 29 Jun 1968, mayor of Thief River Falls and a Minnesota state senator. ¶11

Eight children with Peter Valder: Myrtle Pearl (1915, born in Boone County, Iowa), Gladys Margie (1916), Palmer M. (1919, Honan province, China), Frances Esther (1921, Siangyang in Hupeh, China), Otto Trygve (1922, Siangyang), Marie Rose (1925), Carol David (1926) and Fred Martin (1928). Her Find a Grave memorial links all eight. ¶13

Life story

Lutheran Brethren missionary in Honan, China. ¶1

Death: the Minnesota death index records Olga Johanna Valder, born 23 January 1890 outside Minnesota, died 16 February 1980 in Hennepin County, certificate 1980-MN-004950, mother's maiden name SATNESS (Sætnes). The index is the state's abstract of the death certificate; it names the county only, and no certificate image is online, so the town is not known. The mother's maiden name agrees with her mother Caroline Sætnes. ¶19

1891 Norwegian census: born Jan 1890, Kristiansund. Emigrated with her mother and siblings from Kristiansund 2 May 1894 to Minnesota; grew up on the family farm near Hawley, Clay County, MN. ¶3

Her Norwegian name in full, as written in the 1891 census of Kristiansund, was Olga Johanne Petrine Tungsæt, born January 1890 in Kristiansund, daughter of the head of household. The family lived at 47 Toldbodsmujet, property no. 45, in census district 006 Hammersmaget - one of seven households sharing a building that held forty people. Her father was a shopkeeper (handelsmand). ¶5

She was four years and three months old when she emigrated. The Nordmøre police emigrant protocol records her leaving Kristiansund on 2 May 1894 with her mother and seven brothers and sisters, by the Dominion Line, destination Minnesota, household position 'Barn' (child), the ticket bought in Kristiansund. The clerk entered her age as 2, which is wrong by two years; her birth year is calculated from that as 1892 in the index. ¶6

She is buried at Oak Grove Cemetery in Fergus Falls, Otter Tail County, Minnesota, with her husband Peter Valder, whose dates are 13 Apr 1887 to 14 May 1967. Find a Grave gives her own dates as 23 Jan 1890 to 16 Feb 1980 and records the marriage as 1913. ¶8

1895 Minnesota census: Olga Tungseth, 4, born in Norway, is in her father Peter's household in Skree Township, Clay County, with her mother and seven brothers and sisters (p. 6, read from the image; the census index misreads her name as 'Olya'). By her birth on 23 Jan 1890 she was five; the age is a year low. ¶20

1900 census: Olga Tungseth, daughter, born January 1890 in Norway, aged 10, single, immigrated 1894, at school, in her mother Caroline's household in Skree Township, Clay County, on 14 Jun 1900; her father was not at home (ED 316, sheet 185A, line 6, read from the image). ¶21

1905 Minnesota census: Olga Tungseth, 16, born in Norway, 11 years in Minnesota, at home in Skree Township with her parents and her brothers Carl, Gustav, Anton and Erling; the occupation entry is struck through and illegible (Enumeration District 10, schedule sheet III, line 35, read from the image). She was in fact fifteen. ¶22

1910 census: Olga K. Tungseth, daughter, 20, single, born in Norway, immigrated 1894, speaks English, no occupation, at home in Skree Township with her parents Peter and Karolina and her brothers Carl, Gustav, Anton and Erling (ED 59, sheet 187A, line 39, read from the image). Three years later, of Hawley, she married Peter Valder. The initial K is unexplained; her Norwegian names were Olga Johanne Petrine. ¶23

Of Hawley, MN when she married Peter Valder in 1913. ¶2

Her eight children, as listed on her Find a Grave memorial: Myrtle Pearl (Thompson), 1915-1976; Gladys Margie (Behnke), 1916-1991; Palmer M., 1919-2014, born 16 Apr 1919 in Honan (Henan), China; Frances Esther (Walla), 1921-2013; Rev. Otto Trygve, 1922-2013; Marie Rose (Nelson), 1925-2009; Carol David, 1926-1997; and Fred Martin, 1928-2015. The first two were born before the China years and the last two after; Palmer's 2014 obituary confirms that he was born at the Honan mission station where his parents served as Church of the Lutheran Brethren missionaries. ¶9

That her eldest child was born in Boone County, Iowa in February 1915 shows the couple's first home after their 1913 marriage was in Iowa, not Minnesota (inferred from the birthplace on Myrtle's memorial). ¶14

1930 census: on 5 April 1930 the family lived in Ferndale town, Whatcom County, Washington (enumeration district 37-31, sheet 2B, dwelling 50), in a house Peter owned, valued at $2,500. The household was her husband Peter M., 42, head, a foreign missionary; Olga herself, 40; and their children Myrtle P. 15 (born Iowa), Gladys H. 13, Palmer M. 10, Frances E. 9 and Otto T. 7 (all four born in China and marked 'Am Cit', American citizens by birth abroad), Marie R. 5 and Carol D. 4 (both born North Dakota), and Gotfried M. 1 (born Washington). Read from the census image. ¶15

1930 census (her own entry): Olga J. Valder, 40, married first at 23 (that is, in 1913), born in Norway of Norwegian parents, mother tongue Norwegian, immigrated in 1893 and naturalized; no occupation. The year 1893 is a year early: the Kristiansund emigrant protocol shows her leaving with her mother on 2 May 1894. Her father had left in October 1893, which may be the source of the date. ¶16

1940 census: the 1940 census index shows Olga Valder, wife, born about 1890 in Norway, in Peter Valder's household at Ferndale, Washington. Index only; the image was not read. ¶17

1940 census (read from the image, replacing the earlier index-only entry): Olga Valder, wife, 50, married, had completed the 8th grade, was born in Norway and was naturalized ('Na'); she was doing housework, with no income, and had lived in the same house in Ferndale town, Whatcom County, Washington on 1 Apr 1935 (dwelling 149, Alder Street, owned by her husband Peter, a dairy farmer). ¶24

1950 census: on 1 April 1950 Olga J. Valder, 60, born Norway, lived with her husband Peter and son Fred in Otter Tail County, Minnesota, and worked 40 hours a week in dining-room work at a general hospital. Read from the census image (ED 56-35). ¶18

Death (Social Security index): the Social Security Death Index records Olga Valder's death in February 1980 with her last residence at Minneapolis, which places her in the city within Hennepin County that the Minnesota death index names (last residence, not a stated place of death). ¶26
