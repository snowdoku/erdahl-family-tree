Life story

Primary records, user only (no relationship named): the 1624 leidang roll for Selbu tinglag lists 'Pouell Sandvigh' (39890 view 76 right, 7th entry), so a Pål held Sandvik in 1624; with a son born about 1610 (1665 manntall per the bygdebok), he was born before about 1590. The 1646-47 kongsjordebok lists 'Poulle Sandvigge' among the diocese tenants ('Stichtens Godz') in Selbu (51306 view 181 right), so the name Pål still stood on the Sandvik holding in 1646, nine years after Peder Pålsen's lease - consistent with the book's father-and-son joint use. Farm succession plus patronymic is Probable, not Confirmed. ¶2

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade · 2 open questions

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Graded Probable on his link to his son Peder Pålsen Sandvik (see his page). The bygdebok (Selbu i fortid og nåtid vol. 3, Sandvik bruk 21, canvas 373) says that a 'Pål Sandvik' is named in 1592 and about 1600, that in 1627 'Pål Pålsen' is named on the farm, and that in 1637 'Peder Pålsen bygslet' (took the lease) while '[...] faren Pål [...] bruker [...] sønnen' - the father Pål was still a user with the son. Peder's own patronymic Pålsen (1647 and the 1665-66 manntall) fits. ¶1

Open questions and the next record to read 2

Unresolved: the book also names a 'Pål Sandvik' in 1592 and 1600. Whether that is this man or his father (so that he is 'Pål Pålsen' as son of an older Pål) is not clear from the rebuilt text; the patronymic Pålsen in 1627 suggests a father Pål. Not added. ¶3

Test record: the 1645 poll tax (koppskatt) for Selbu (Digitalarkivet 77038, full-farm men not yet located; the half-farm men are views 269-270), which would show whether Pål and Peder were separate taxpayers at Sandvik; and the Trondheim len accounts (lensregnskap) for 1637, which should record Peder Pålsen's bygsel fee and may say 'efter sin fader'. ¶4
