Life story

The panteregister I 1 abstract for Midbø (fol. 21) records the 1707 sale to Ole Toresen, which agrees with the odelsgods paragraph of the probate. Born before about 1686 (he was buying land in 1707; a 1693 deed is also cited, which may be his own or a predecessor's title). ¶3

How he came to Midbø (re-reading panteregister I 1 fol. 21, view 63): in 1707 'Tallef [..]søn, Aslach Grovum og Even Tug[?]' sold land in Midbø 'til Ole Toresøn' by deed dated at the parsonage 15 Oct 1707, tingl. 5 Mar 1708. The sellers are not called his kin, so he seems to have bought into the farm; his own parents and farm of origin are unknown. ¶4a

Probate: 'Anno 1740 den 9de Aprilis' at 'Midboen i Nissedals Annex' the estate of the couple 'Ole Torsøn og Aasild Torsdatter' (both dead) was registered and divided among their children: 'Sønnen Jon Olsøn', a daughter (read Turi, possibly Kari; the 1753 deed calls her Kari) 'gift med Ole Jonsøn Midboe', 'Torbior Olsdaatter gift med Torgrim Olsøn', 'Inger Olsdaatter' and 'Ragnild Olsdaatter', together with Aasild Torsdatter's two sons by an earlier husband, 'Ansten og Targier Halvorssønner'. The odelsgods is the farm Midbø, which 'Ole Thoresøn' had acquired (deeds dated 16 Feb 1693 and 15 Oct 1707, tingl. 5 Mar 1708). Guardians: Ansten Halvorsen Næss for Inger and Targier Halvorsen for Ragnild, i.e. their half-brothers. Vest-Telemark skifteprotokoll 1735-40 (source 25669), fol. 539, views 359-361. ¶1

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence: Confirmed, graded on his link to his son Jon (Joen) Olsen Midbø (see his page). The probate names 'Sønnen Jon Olsøn' as his heir. Continuity: the heir 'Sønnen Jon Olsøn' of 1740 is the Jon Olsen of Midbø who on 28 Jun 1753 bought out the shares that his half-brothers Anstan and Targei Halvorsen and his sisters' husbands (Ole Jonsen Midbø, Torgrim Olsen and others) had received at the skifte of 9 Apr 1740 (pantebok I 5, No 311), and the same Joen Olsen of Midbø was the widower in the 13 May 1756 probate after Turi Jørgensdatter that names his son Tore: same farm, gaps of 13 and 3 years, the same sibling group in 1740 and 1753, and no rival Jon Olsen at Midbø. His children's names fit: the youngest daughter in 1756 was Aasild, after his mother, and the eldest son Tore, after his paternal grandfather Tor (Ole Torsen's father). ¶2
