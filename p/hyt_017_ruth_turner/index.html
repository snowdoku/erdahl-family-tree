About the family

Father's will: John Turner Senior of Scituate, in his will of 4 March 1695[/6], left his household goods after his widow Mary's death to 'my four Daughters; That is to say Lydia Mary Ruth & Grace' and named a grandson Thomas Prince among six grandchildren (The Mayflower Descendant, vol. 5, pp. 41-45, from the original will, Plymouth County Probate Records vol. 1, pp. 266-270). This is the record that moves her father, John Turner, to Probable. ¶7

Life story

Baptism: 'Ruth, d. John Sr., bp. May 17, 1662', from the Second Church of Scituate records (Vital Records of Scituate, vol. 1, p. 381, Turner, C.R.2; page image read). She is the only Ruth Turner of a suitable age in the Scituate records, and would have been about 23 at a marriage about 1685. The 1902 Prince Genealogy (p. 32) calls Thomas Prince's wife 'Ruth Turner, dau. of John Turner Sr. of Scituate', whose sister Mary married Thomas's brother Isaac; Mary, d. John Sr., was born at Scituate on 10 Dec 1658 (same volume, p. 378, OCR). The identification of this Ruth with Thomas's wife rests on that compiled statement plus the baptism; it is not proved. Her birth is therefore not entered as a fact: baptism 17 May 1662 at Scituate applies only if this identification holds. ¶2

Birth: baptised at Scituate on 17 May 1662 as 'Ruth, d. John Sr.' (Second Church records, Scituate VR vol. 1, p. 381, page image read). The birth field is restored as 'before 17 May 1662' now that her father's identification rests on his will; it was removed in the audit while he was Speculative. Her mother is entered as Mary Brewster, Speculative, because the will names his wife as Mary in 1696 and her father Jonathan Brewster's family record gives the marriage of Mary Brewster to John Turner of Scituate the Elder on 10 November 1645 (Mayflower Descendant 1:8). No record read names Ruth's mother, so a second wife named Mary is not excluded. ¶8

Boston births: 'James of Thomas & Ruth Prince born Nov. 23' in the 1687 list and 'Benjamin of Thomas & Ruth Prince born Feb. 28' in the 1693 list, that is 28 February 1693/4 (Boston Record Commissioners vol. 9, pp. 175 and 208, page images read). The OCR also gives 'Ruth of Thomas & Ruth Prince born Mar. 6' in the 1690 list (p. 189) and her death on 12 September (p. 193); those pages were not read on the image. Benjamin's Boston date is the same day as 'Benjamin, s. Thomas, Feb. 28, 1693-4' in the Scituate town record (Scituate VR vol. 1, p. 300), so the Thomas Prince of Scituate, father of Job, had a wife named Ruth. ¶6

Record: named only as the mother of Job Prince (b. 1695), 'Thomas and Ruth Turner', in the Kingston gravestone record (Vital Records of Kingston, p. 116, G.R.1). Nothing else about her has been searched; Speculative for the same reason as her husband. ¶1

Her Turner parentage stays as Probable on her father's record. Her father's will of 1695/6 names a daughter Ruth and a grandson Thomas Prince, and this account shows that Ruth Prince's eldest son was Thomas, so the two records fit together. Neither record, though, calls her 'Ruth Prince, daughter of John Turner' in so many words. ¶14

Second marriage: as 'Ruth Prince' she married Israell Silvester at Scituate on 3 October 1701 (Vital Records of Scituate, vol. 2, p. 258, town record, page image read; the record does not call her a widow). Children of Israel and Ruth Sylvester recorded at Duxbury: Ruth, 26 June 1701; Israel, 5 May 1705; Grace, Nov. 1706 (Vital Records of Duxbury, births pp. 150-151, page images read). Grace married Isaac Partridge at Duxbury on 10 March 1729[/30] (Duxbury marriages, OCR). ¶3

Second family (Mayflower Descendant): the Scituate marriage 'Israell Silvester and Ruth prince', 3 October 1701, is printed in MD 5:117 (image read). The Duxbury birth of her son Israel Silvester on 5 May 1705 is in MD 8:234 (image read); a date of 17 August 1704 sometimes attached to him belongs to Abia Delano, the entry above. MD 9 (Duxbury records, OCR only) also lists Ruth, 26 June 1701, and Grace, November 1706. An editorial note in MD 8:79-80 (compiled) calls Ruth (Silvester) Cooke Ring a daughter of Israel by Ruth (Turner) Prince. ¶11

Widow and administratrix: in 1703-04, as 'Ruth Silvester late Relict widdow of Thomas Prince … now wife of Israel Silvester', she administered her first husband's estate with Israel and accounted for bringing up the 'three sons he had by her': Thomas, Benjamin and Job (Plymouth County Probate Book 2, pp. 25, 47). Confidence raised to Confirmed as the wife of Thomas Prince and the mother of Job. ¶13

Israel Sylvester's death: an Israel Silvester witnessed a will in 1721, was paid in a 1752 Duxbury estate account and served as a dower commissioner in 1759 (MD 25, 19-20, 23-24; OCR only), so the 'c. 13 February 1716/7' date from a 1970 register snippet is doubtful, though the later entries may be his son Israel (b. 1705). No estate of Israel or Ruth Sylvester was found in Mayflower Descendant vols. 1-25. ¶12

Death and estate: she died intestate at Duxbury before 24 October 1729. On that day the Plymouth County probate court granted administration to 'Mr. Thomas Prince of Duxborough', addressing him as son of 'your Mother Ruth Silvester late of Duxborough' (Plymouth County Probate Book 5 p. 588). Her inventory, styled 'ye Widow Ruth Silvester', was taken by Edward Arnold Esq., Capt. John Alden and Joseph Brewster of Duxbury. It came to £163 10s in movables only, among them a silver tankard, two cows and thirteen chairs, and her son Thomas swore to it on 4 November 1729 (Book 5 pp. 626-627). The index lists her as 'Silvester, Ruth, widow, of Duxborough, 1729' (index p. ~33). The record does not name her husband, but 'widow' shows that Israel Silvester had died before her. ¶15

(FamilySearch signed in, 2 Nov 2026; no grade change; graded on her link to Job Prince, which stays Confirmed). (1) Her estate is old-series Plymouth case no. 20019, 'Sylvester, Ruth, Duxbury', in the Index and Docket Sta-Wat (film 549788, image 130). It lists only the letter of administration (vol. 5 p. 588) and the warrant and inventory (vol. 5 p. 626), with 'Thomas Prince' as administrator. The year as written could be read 1727 or 1729; the letter itself is dated 24 October 1729 (Book 5 p. 588). No division, settlement, account or receipt is docketed, so the record books hold no list of her heirs. (2) The case papers are not online. The FamilySearch image set of Plymouth case files ('probate estate files', catalog 4092105) holds the old series only for parts of A and H (nos. 147-186 and 9290-9876); the rest of that catalog is the second series after 1881. Nos. 20019 and 21382 (her father's case) were not found. (3) Docket no. 19976, 'Sylvester, Israel, Ruth & Grace, Duxbury', 1717: bonds of guardian and letter, 'Ruth Sylvester' guardian, vol. 4 pp. 26-27 (image 125). So in 1717 she was made guardian of her Silvester children Israel, Ruth and Grace. That fits the death of her husband Israel Silvester about 1716/7, the date that an earlier note called doubtful. The record-book entry (vol. 4 pp. 26-27) was not read. (4) The Partridge suit noted (Court Records 1727-1736, image 504, machine transcript; the image itself was not deciphered) is a claim for wages, not for an heir's share. Isaac Partridge of Duxbury, husbandman, and his wife Grace sued Thomas Prince of Duxbury, administrator of 'Ruth Silvester late of Duxborough deceased', for £13 15s. They said that Grace, 'then a single woman & known by the name of Grace Silvester', went into Ruth's service at Ruth's request on 8 March 1728[/9] and stayed until 8 October, at nine shillings a week, and that Ruth was never paid in her lifetime. The suit names no heirs and does not mention John Turner. It does show that Ruth was alive in March 1728/9 and probably needed care until October 1729, which agrees with administration being granted on 24 October 1729. Her Turner parentage is unchanged: no record read here calls her the daughter of John Turner or names her mother. ¶19

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence raised from Speculative to Probable together with her husband Thomas Prince, by the Scituate births (vol. 1, p. 300) naming Thomas as Job's father, and the 1701 Scituate marriage of Ruth Prince to Israel Silvester. Her own name as Job's mother still rests on the Kingston gravestone record. What would confirm: a Plymouth County probate or deed naming Ruth Silvester as former widow of Thomas Prince or as daughter of John Turner (queued as S0119). ¶5

Records that disagree 1

Conflict: the Duxbury birth of Ruth Sylvester (26 June 1701) falls before the Scituate marriage (3 October 1701). One of the two dates may be mis-recorded, or the child was born before the marriage. Not resolved. The Prince Genealogy says the marriage was at Duxbury; the record is at Scituate. ¶4

Corrections 2

Correction: the earlier death search assumed she might have lived to 1760; the 1729 administration shows she died by October 1729. The Israel Silvester of 1752 and 1759 noted earlier was therefore her son Israel (b. 1705), not her husband. ¶16

Correction: the earlier notes calling her 'Speculative' (Kingston gravestone only) and 'Probable' are superseded. She is Confirmed as Thomas Prince's wife and Job Prince's mother by the 1703-04 Plymouth probate (Book 2 pp. 25, 47). Two links above her are separate, and each is Probable, not Speculative: her father John Turner the Elder (his will names his daughter Ruth and his grandson Thomas Prince) and her mother Mary Brewster. The note that calls Mary Brewster 'Speculative' as her mother predates the orchestrator's 13 Oct 2026 review, which raised Mary to Probable. ¶17

Searches that found nothing 2

Death search (negative): no death or burial for Ruth Sylvester (any spelling, as wife or widow of Israel Sylvester) was found in the printed vital records of Duxbury, Pembroke or Scituate (vol. 2, marriages and deaths). The Duxbury entry of 22 July 1775 for 'Abigail, wife of Israel' Sylvester, aged 72, is Israel's later wife, not Ruth (search of the OCR text; not repeated on images). ¶9

Death search (negative, continued): no death or burial of Ruth Sylvester, and no estate of Israel Sylvester naming her, in archive.org full text, the printed Plymouth vital records, or Deane's History of Scituate (1831). The printed vital records of Hanover, Marshfield and Hingham are not on archive.org. A 1970 Mayflower Society register snippet suggests Israel Sylvester died about 13 February 1716/7 (unread, lead only). ¶10a

Open questions and the next record to read 3

Next: Plymouth County probate files for Israel Sylvester. ¶10b

Lead (FamilySearch full text, 2 Nov 2026; not graded): Plymouth County Court of Common Pleas records 1727-1736 (image ark 3Q9M-CSVR-V75D-4, transcript only, image not studied) contain a suit by Isaac Partridge of Duxbury, husbandman, and his wife against 'Thomas Prince of Duxborough in the County of Plymouth', administrator of 'Ruth Silvester late of Duxborough deceased', for a share of her estate held by him as administrator. This places Ruth Silvester's death at Duxbury before about 1736 and shows her son Thomas Prince of Duxbury as her administrator. The wife's name is garbled in the transcript; she is presumably a daughter of Ruth (by Prince or Silvester). The administration itself (Plymouth Probate, Silvester, Ruth, Duxbury) would list her heirs, and might mention the Turner household goods. ¶18a

Next: Plymouth probate docket for Ruth Silvester (Index and Docket Sta-Wat, film 549788) and the case papers. ¶18b
