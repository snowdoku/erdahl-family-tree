About the family

Father of Erna Knodel, named on her 1939 marriage registration at Penticton as Gottlieb Knodel, born Germany. He is not to be assumed to be the Gottlieb Knodel who died at Chilliwack in 1985 aged 79: that man was born about 1906 and was more probably Erna's brother or cousin. ¶1

Family: the nine children of Gottlieb Knodel (1879-1958) and Anna Karolina Schmidt (1881-1974) are Jacob (1902), Wilhelm/William (1904), Gottlieb (1905), Anna (1906), Sophie (about 1908-09), Ludwig (1910), Frieda (about 1911-12), Erna (1915) and Otto (1918). Seven of them are proved by British Columbia registrations that name both parents. Anna is proved by her BC death registration, and Otto rests on Find a Grave family links and the shared family gravestone at Eatonia. ¶6

Children registered at Pfeilsdorf (Standesamt Pfeilsdorf, all images read): Gottlieb 2 Nov 1905, Anna 24 Dec 1906, Sophie 30 Sep 1908, Ludwig 21 May 1910, Frieda 4 Mar 1912, Erna 8 Oct 1915, Otto 11 Jan 1918. He signed the acts of 1905-1912 himself. In the 1918 act he is called 'Besitzer' (owner) instead of 'Ansiedler' (settler), so by then he held his settler farm as its proprietor. No Knodel birth was found at Pfeilsdorf in 1913, 1916 or 1917; the 1914 birth register is not online. ¶12

Life story

Death and burial: Find a Grave memorial 229408869 gives his birth as 15 April 1879 in 'Ukraine' and his death as 24 February 1958 at Eatonia, Saskatchewan, with burial in Eatonia Cemetery. The family gravestone photograph shows only the years: a black granite double tablet reading 'KAROLINA 1881-1974 | GOTTLIEB 1879-1958', with wheat-sheaf ornaments, beside a matching tablet for his son Otto (1918-1973) and Otto's wife Mary (1926-1992), on one base with a centre block lettered 'KNODEL'. The day and month come from the memorial, not the stone, and are not yet verified against a Saskatchewan death registration. ¶2

Birth (probable): The St Petersburg Lutheran Consistory extracts record a Gottlieb Knodel born 3 April 1879 at Friedenstal, Bessarabia, son of Ludwig Knodel and Wilhelmine Tarnowsky. 3 April in the Julian (Old Style) calendar used in the Russian Empire is 15 April in the Gregorian calendar - exactly the birth date on Gottlieb's grave at Eatonia, Saskatchewan - and Friedenstal lies in what is now Ukraine, the birthplace Find a Grave gives. No other Gottlieb Knodel in the Bessarabian extracts fits, and no death of this child appears in the Friedenstal death extracts to 1885. Rated probable, not confirmed: the identification rests on the calendar-converted date match, not on a record naming his parents from the Canadian side. ¶8

Migration (outline): born Bessarabia 1879; married about 1900 (per Find a Grave); his sons Jacob (1902) and Wilhelm (1904) were born in Romania - probably the Dobruja, where many Bessarabian Germans settled after 1878; an unsourced online biography gives Wilhelm's birthplace as 'Cogelea', i.e. Cogealac (German Kodschalak). Settled at Pfeilsdorf, West Prussia, by 1905; still there in 1915; after Pfeilsdorf became Polish in 1920 the family emigrated to Canada about 1929-30 and farmed at Eatonia, Saskatchewan, where he died in 1958. ¶10

Bessarabian birth (still Probable): the only baptism index entry found is the St Petersburg Consistory extract of 3 April 1879 (Old Style; 15 April New Style) at Friedenstal, son of Ludwig Knodel and Wilhelmine Tarnowsky (FHL 1895629/1 p.300 no.12). A 2026 search for a second source found none online. There is no Knodel death matching him in the Bessarabian extracts to 1885. His father's household is missing from the indexed Friedenstal family book of 1881-1900. Gottlieb's confirmation (about 1893-94) and the 1861-80 family book page would be in the Arzis/Friedenstal registers (FHL films 1767875 items 1-2), which need a FamilySearch login. ¶15

Ancestral homeland (probable, via the Friedenstal Knodel clan): the Knodels of Friedenstal came there about 1841 from the Swabian colony of Leonberg (Polish Lwowek) in Sanniki parish, west of Warsaw. That colony had been settled from Wuerttemberg about 1800-1806. The family's Wuerttemberg roots were at Ensingen (Oberamt Vaihingen) and Ochsenburg (Oberamt Brackenheim). Gottlieb's own descent from them, through his father Ludwig, is not proved. ¶16

Dobruja check (2026): the Knodels named in the Dobruja German literature (Jahrbuch der Dobrudschadeutschen 1956-77; Traeger 1922; DAI and EWZ resettlement indexes of 1940) belong to the Fachria and Cogealac families. Those families descend from the Alt Elft and Teplitz Knodels. No Gottlieb Knodel born in 1879 and no Ludwig Knodel appears in them. This neither confirms nor refutes the Friedenstal birth. A Fachria-family Gottlieb born in 1879 would probably have been born in the Dobruja, where those families were living from 1877 to 1884. That conflicts with the 'Russia' and 'Ukraine' birthplaces in his Canadian records, so Friedenstal remains the best fit. ¶17

Emigration: Gottlieb Knodel (index age implies born about 1879), his wife Karolina and their children Frieda, Erna and Otto landed at Quebec on 22 July 1928 from the White Star-Dominion liner Megantic, which had sailed from Southampton; their stated destination was Winnipeg, Manitoba. His son Wilhelm followed four days later on the Canadian Pacific liner Montrose (Southampton to Quebec, 26 July 1928), bound for Eatonia, Saskatchewan. Source: Ancestry's index to Library and Archives Canada's passenger lists 1925-1935 (dbId 1263), ship and ports established by exact-keyword searches of the index; the manifest image itself has not yet been read. ¶20

Baptism: The Friedenstal birth register of 1879, in the St Petersburg Consistory duplicate (FHL film 1895629 item 1, p.300, entry 12; image read in 2026), records Gottlieb Knodel, born 3 April 1879 (Old Style; 15 April New Style) and baptised 8 April 1879, legitimate, son of Ludwig Knodel and Wilhelmine née Turowsky (first letter uncertain; possibly Furowsky), both Evangelical Lutheran. The father's occupation word is uncertain (perhaps 'Ackerbauer', farmer). The godparents (Pathen) were Gottlieb Knodel, Andreas Gall and Friedrike Engminger (reading uncertain; perhaps Enzminger). The godfather Gottlieb Knodel was probably a relative; he is not identified. ¶34

Emigration (manifest read): Gottlieb Knodel, head, aged 49, married, farmer, born 'Roumania, Friedenstal', a Polish citizen of German race, landed at Quebec on 22 July 1928 from the Megantic (sailed Southampton 14 July 1928) with his wife Karolina (47) and children Frieda (16), Erna (13) and Otto (10), all bound for Winnipeg by the Canadian National Railways. They held Polish passports issued at Warsaw on 20 June 1928. His nearest relative left behind is typed as 'Brother Jakob Knodel, Pliznica, Poland' (Płużnica, i.e. Pfeilsdorf); his wife gives the same man as 'Brother in Law' and the children as 'Uncle' (Megantic manifest, LAC T-14745 frame 00366, vol 15 p 110). ¶36

Pfeilsdorf: the village was the former manor (Gutsbezirk) of Plusnitz, bought by the Royal Prussian Settlement Commission on 25 July 1899, parcelled into settler farms and made the rural commune of Pfeilsdorf in 1904 (GOV gazetteer). Its settlers were German families from Bessarabia, many of whom had lived for a time in the Dobruja (marriage acts of fellow settlers name Friedensfeld, Neu-Sarata and Hoffnungsdorf in Bessarabia). The district passed to Poland on 10 January 1920. ¶28

Dobruja years (about 1900-1904): his sons Jacob (1902) and Wilhelm (1904) were born in Romania. Pfeilsdorf, where he settled by November 1905, was described in 1922 as an almost pure colony of Dobruja peasants (Traeger, pp.137-138), so the family's Romanian home was almost certainly the Northern Dobruja. Family tradition names Wilhelm's birthplace 'Cogelea', probably Cogealia (Lumina) or possibly Cogealac, in Constanța county. No Romanian record of the family has yet been seen. ¶13

Context, the return to Prussia: from about 1900 the Royal Prussian Settlement Commission (Ansiedlungskommission, Posen) settled German 'return migrants' (Rückwanderer) from Russia and Romania on estates it had divided into farms in Posen and West Prussia. Traeger (1922) records that letters from the first settlers drew followers from village after village in the Dobruja. The colony of Sarighiol lost 11 families in 1904, nine of them to Settlement Commission colonies in West Prussia; Cogeala (Cogealia) sent eight families to 'the area of Pillau [probably Bilau, Kreis Culm] and Kamin in West Prussia'. The German consul at Constanța wrote in 1918 of the experience of the 'return migration of Dobruja German peasants into the Prussian settlements in the East' from 1905 onward (Jahrbuch der Dobrudschadeutschen 1962, pp.15-16). Push factors named by Traeger were insecure land titles, tax-farmers and the 1904 Romanian land survey, which gave settlers 5 ha instead of the promised 15. ¶14

Birthplace evidence for the family: the father is recorded as born in Russia and the mother in Romania. The children are recorded as born in Romania (Jacob 1902, William 1904), at 'Pfeilsdorf' (William 1904, Anna 1906, Frieda about 1912 - three independent informants), in Poland (Ludwig 1910) and in 'Germany' (Gottlieb 1905, Sophie, Erna). A Geni-derived Find a Grave biography gives William's birthplace as 'Cogelea, Romania', probably Cogealac (German Kodschalak) in the Dobruja. 'Pfeilsdorf' has not yet been identified on any gazetteer. ¶7

Settler at Pfeilsdorf: By November 1905 Gottlieb was an 'Ansiedler' (settler placed by the Prussian Settlement Commission) at Pfeilsdorf (Płużnica), Kreis Briesen, West Prussia. He registered there in person, and signed, the births of his children Gottlieb (2 Nov 1905), Anna (24 Dec 1906; the 1949 Polish copy renders his name 'Bogumił', the Polish form of Gottlieb), Sophie (30 Sep 1908) and Ludwig (21 May 1910), each time described as an Evangelical settler living at Płużnica with his wife Karoline née Schmidt. Erna's birth on 8 Oct 1915 was registered by the midwife, Gottlieb not being the declarant (wartime; the act gives no reason). ¶9

A second Knodel settler family lived at Pfeilsdorf at the same time: Jakob Knodel and his wife Katharina née Reister, with children Elisabeth (b. 20 Jan 1905), Gottlieb (b. 25 Oct 1906) and Wilhelm (b. 4 Oct 1908) registered there, and another child in 1911. Jakob was very probably a brother or cousin of Gottlieb; the link is not proved. ¶11

Land: the land-register files (Grundakten) of the Pfeilsdorf settler holdings, 1907 onward, survive at the State Archive in Toruń (fond 69/1332, one file per holding, not scanned), and the commune's land-tax roll 1909-1932 at the State Archive in Poznań (53/434/0/-/454). Which file is Gottlieb's has not yet been identified. ¶29

Pfeilsdorf kin, the Eberle burial (index only): the Villisaß parish burials index has a burial of Gottlieb Eberle on 13 Oct 1908, relative 'Friedr.' (Ancestry dbId 61250, image 0245316-00425, not read). This agrees with the civil death of Gottlieb Eberle, son of Friedrich Eberle and Friederike née Knodel, at Płużnica on 11 Oct 1908 aged 4 1/2, noted above. The image would give the burial details and may name the parents. ¶40

Passenger-list index cross-check (Oct 2026): a keyword search of Ancestry's 1925-1935 Canadian passenger index (dbId 1263) for 'Friedenstal' returns Gottlieb's record 2638379, so the hidden birthplace field of the index agrees with the manifest reading 'Roumania, Friedenstal'; this is index agreement, not a separate source. ¶41

Witness, 1912: on 2 Nov 1912 the settler Gottlieb Knodel, 33, of Pfeilsdorf witnessed the marriage at Pfeilsdorf of Michael Schlenker (born at Cogealia, Romania) and Roberta Schmidt (born 9 Apr 1893 in Romania), daughter of Karoline Stürzeberger née Schmidt of 'Widau' (reading uncertain), Kreis Gnesen (Pfeilsdorf marriages 1912 no. 13; image read). His age fits his birth in April 1879. Roberta may be a relative of his wife Karolina née Schmidt, for example a daughter of Karolina's aunt Karoline (b. 1860); that is a lead, not a finding. ¶31

Church: the family belonged to the Evangelical (Lutheran) parish of Villisaß, Kreis Culm, which served the Protestant settlers of Pfeilsdorf (Meyers Gazetteer lists only a Catholic parish church at Pfeilsdorf itself). The Ancestry index of German Lutheran registers (dbId 61250) has Villisaß baptisms of five of the couple's children, each with the relatives indexed as Gottlieb and Karoline (or Caroline): Gottlieb on 24 Dec 1905, Anna on 6 Jan 1907, Sophia on 25 Oct 1908, Ludwig on 21 May 1910 and Frida on 30 Mar 1912. Each follows the child's civil birth at Pfeilsdorf by 0 to 52 days (Ludwig's indexed date is the same as his birth date, so the index may give his birth rather than his baptism). Erna (1915) and Otto (1918) are not in the index. These are index entries only; the register images, which would name the godparents, have not been read. The same parish baptised the children of Jakob Knodel and Katharina Reister (1905-1911) and of Friedrich Eberle and Friederike née Knodel (1904-1910). ¶32

Pfeilsdorf kin, the Eberle household (continues the note on Friederike Eberle née Knodel): the Pfeilsdorf birth register names Friederike née Knodel ('Fryderyka z domu Knodel') as the mother of three children of the settler Friedrich Eberle, all born at Płużnica: Gottlieb on 23 Jul 1904 (1904 no. 42), Gustav on 21 Nov 1906 (1906 no. 51; the month is partly conjectural) and Frieda Friederike in Oct 1908, about the 17th, a few days after her brother Gottlieb's death (1908 no. 44). None of the acts gives her parents or birthplace. The Villisaß Evangelical register, as indexed by Ancestry (dbId 61250, index only), has baptisms of Gottlieb (2 Oct 1904), Gustav (30 Dec 1906), Frieda Friederike (25 Oct 1908, the same day as Gottlieb Knodel's daughter Sophia) and a fourth child, Gertrud (14 Aug 1910), each with the relatives Friedrich and Friederike, and the burial of the boy Gottlieb Eberle on 13 Oct 1908 (relative 'Friedr.'); the index does not give Friederike's maiden name. Friedrich Eberle witnessed Pfeilsdorf marriages aged 35 in Jan 1907 (1907 no. 5) and 38 on 2 Apr 1910 (1910 no. 3), so he was born about 1871-72. Friederike's marriage, not at Pfeilsdorf in 1901-1910, would name her parents. ¶33

Chain migration: The family came to Saskatchewan in stages. The eldest son Jacob landed in 1924 (Megantic; Form 30A index). Gottlieb jr followed in 1925 or early 1926, and by June 1926 he was a farm servant in the RM of Newcombe; Anna landed at Halifax on 13 June 1926 from the Red Star liner Pennland. Ludwig and Sophie ('Ludwik' and 'Zofia') landed at Halifax on 28 March 1927 from the White Star liner Doric out of Liverpool. In the 1926 Prairie census Jacob (a labourer) and Anna (a domestic) were hired hands on Lucas Seefried's farm in the RM of Newcombe. The parents came last, in July 1928. Their pioneer in the district was Simon Eberle, a fellow settler from Pfeilsdorf who had come in 1910. ¶22

Emigration, the chain: the children went ahead - Jacob in 1924 (Megantic), Gottlieb jr and Anna 1925-26 (Anna landed at Halifax 13 Jun 1926), Ludwig and Sophie 1927 (Halifax 28 Mar 1927, from Liverpool) - and the parents followed with Wilhelm, Frieda, Erna and Otto in July 1928. The family's neighbours from Pfeilsdorf, the Eberles, had gone to Canada in 1910 and were farming near Eatonia, Saskatchewan, where the Knodels settled. ¶27

Passenger-list index details (inferred, not read from the image): exact-keyword tests of Gottlieb's 1928 index entry return 'Friedenstal' and 'Roumania' (evidently his place of birth, Bessarabia having been Romanian since 1918), 'farmer', 'Polish' (nationality), 'German' (race), 'married', and a relative 'brother Jakob' [Knodel]. The children's entries give last residence 'Pluznica' (Płużnica, Poland) and the same relative as 'uncle Jakob'. On the 1925-1935 Canadian form this is the 'nearest relative in the country from which you came'; no Jakob Knodel of that generation was in Canada in 1931. This is the first record naming Gottlieb's own birthplace in his lifetime, and it agrees with the Friedenstal, Bessarabia birth of 1879 found in the St Petersburg Consistory extracts. It also shows that the Jakob Knodel settled at Pfeilsdorf with his wife Katharina née Reister was Gottlieb's brother. ¶21

1931 census: On 1 June 1931 Gottlieb Knodel ('Gottleb'), 52, farmer, headed a household in the Rural Municipality of Newcombe No. 260, Kindersley electoral district, Saskatchewan (sub-district 42, page 10, lines 44-49), living on the north-west quarter of section 27, township 25, range 24, west of the 3rd meridian. He owned the house: a wooden single house of 3 rooms valued at $500, with no radio. He gave his birthplace and both parents' birthplaces as Rumania, his year of immigration as 1928, and his status as an alien (not naturalised). Nationality and 'racial origin' were both entered as Rumania; mother tongue German; Lutheran; he did not speak English. With him were his wife 'Cataline' (50), and their children William (27, labourer on his father's farm, unpaid), Erna (15, at home), Otto (12, at school 8 months) and Freda (18, noted 'abt Oliver BC', i.e. away in British Columbia). ¶18

Arrival in Canada: The 1931 census dates the parents' immigration to 1928, not 1929-30 as had been inferred from his widow's '44 years in Canada' in 1974. The children came in stages: Jacob in 1924, Gottlieb jr and Anna in 1926, Sophie and Ludwig in 1927, and the parents with William, Frieda, Erna and Otto in 1928 (each child's own 1931 census line). They settled beside the Eberle family, fellow settlers from Pfeilsdorf who had come in 1910 and farmed at 21-26-24-W3 near Eatonia; two Knodel sons married Eberle daughters. ¶19

Farm: In June 1931 the family owned and lived on the north-west quarter of section 27, township 25, range 24, west of the 3rd meridian, in the Rural Municipality of Newcombe No. 260. That is about 10 km south-east of Eatonia village, whose cemetery is on NE 14-26-25-W3. Their nearest Lutheran burial ground was the Cornfeld Lutheran Cemetery on NE 14-25-25-W3, used 1914-1936 (Saskatchewan Genealogical Society cemetery index). It was probably the rural congregation the family first attended. ¶23

Saskatchewan: in 1944 his son Wilhelm was a farmer at Eatonia, and his son Otto married there in 1945. The family farm was evidently in the Eatonia district, about 45 km south-west of Kindersley. ¶3

Arrival: his wife's 1974 BC death registration says she had been 44 years in Canada, so the couple came about 1929-30. Their daughter Anna married in Saskatchewan in 1929 a man born at Melville, Saskatchewan. The passenger list has not yet been found. ¶4

Later Saskatchewan years: The federal voters list of 1949 for Kindersley electoral district lists 'G. Knodel', farmer, and 'Mrs G. Knodel', housewife, at Eatonia (keyword tests of the Ancestry index, dbId 2983). Only British subjects were listed, so he and Karolina had been naturalised by 1949. His son Otto kept the farm at Eatonia and appears on the lists of 1957-1965. Gottlieb died at Eatonia on 24 February 1958 and was buried in Eatonia Cemetery. ¶24

Emigration: Canadian passenger-list index (Ancestry 'Canada, Incoming Passenger Lists, 1865-1935', anonymous search) lists Gottlieb Knodel (b. abt 1879), Karolina (abt 1881), Frieda (abt 1912), Erna (abt 1915) and Otto (abt 1918) on one manifest page, arriving 22 July 1928, destination Winnipeg, Man. Index keywords that match this record (tested one by one) are Quebec, Southampton, Megantic, Polish, German, Farmer and Married: i.e. the White Star-Dominion liner Megantic, Southampton to Quebec, the family travelling as Polish nationals of German 'race'. The manifest image (Quebec reel, 1925-35 series) has not been seen; it should give their last residence (expected Plużnica) and the relative they were joining. ¶25

The case for this identification 2

Identity, reassessed (Oct 2026; stays Probable): the Canadian settler Gottlieb Knodel (1879-1958) is very probably the child baptised at Friedenstal in April 1879. The baptism image (FHL 1895629/1 p.300 no.12, read in 2026) now verifies the parents' names that the odessa3 extract gave, but it does not itself connect the child to the Canadian man. That bridge rests on four agreeing points. (1) His 1928 Canadian passenger-list index entry returns 'Friedenstal' as a keyword (an index inference; the manifest image has not been read). (2) His birth date of 15 April 1879 New Style is exactly 3 April Old Style; but the day and month come from his Find a Grave memorial, not the stone (which gives only the year), and that memorial may itself draw on the Consistory extract, so this point may not be independent. (3) His stated ages agree: 33 in November 1912 and 52 in June 1931. (4) Canadian records give his birthplace as Russia. The Consistory extracts record no other Gottlieb Knodel born at Friedenstal in the late 1870s, and he named a son Ludwig (b. 1910). The birth place therefore keeps its qualifier 'probable'. Reading his line on the 22 July 1928 Megantic manifest (free LAC 'Passenger Lists, 1925-1935') would allow Confirmed. ¶35

Identity, reassessed (Oct 2026; now Confirmed): the Canadian settler is the child baptised at Friedenstal in 1879. His own 1928 manifest, a primary record now read, gives his birthplace as 'Roumania, Friedenstal' (Bessarabia was Romanian from 1918) and his age as 49, which places his birth between July 1878 and July 1879. The Friedenstal baptism (FHL 1895629/1 p.300 no.12, image read) records a Gottlieb Knodel born there on 3 April 1879 Old Style (15 April New Style), son of Ludwig Knodel and Wilhelmine née Turowsky, and no other Gottlieb Knodel born at Friedenstal in 1878-79 has been found in the Consistory extracts. (Audit check,: the 1870s and 1880s birth extracts list six other Gottlieb Knodel births, at Eigenheim 1870, Alt Elft 1871 and 1881, Friedensthal Aug 1883 and Nov 1884, and Alt Arzis 1885; none fits a man aged 49 in July 1928 and born at Friedenstal. The extracts are not complete, so a namesake cannot be excluded absolutely, but the brother Jakob of Płużnica and his Pfeilsdorf ages corroborate this identification.) His ages at Pfeilsdorf (33 on 2 Nov 1912) and in the 1931 census (52 on 1 Jun 1931) also fit April 1879. The manifest also names a brother Jakob at Płużnica, which fits Ludwig's family (see Jakob Knodel). The birthplace therefore loses its 'probable' qualifier. The exact day still rests on the baptism, not on his own records. ¶37

Alternatives considered and set aside 2

Geni (lead only, not evidence): Geni's public search listing makes him the son of a Wilhelmina Knodel and gives him eight siblings. ¶5

Pfeilsdorf kin, a third Knodel household (relationship unproved): Friederike Eberle née Knodel, wife of the settler (osadnik) Friedrich Eberle, lived at Płużnica (Pfeilsdorf) by July 1904, before Gottlieb's own arrival there in 1905. Their son Gottlieb Eberle was born at Płużnica on 23 Jul 1904 and died there on 11 Oct 1908 aged 4 1/2 (Pfeilsdorf deaths 1908 no. 26, declared and signed by Friedrich Eberle; the mother is written 'Fryderyka urodzona Knodel'). No record yet read names her parents, so she could be Gottlieb's sister, a cousin or a Knodel from another family; she is not linked in the tree. Her marriage act or her own death act would decide it. Friedrich Eberle is distinct from the settler Simon Eberle (husband of Maria Hildebrandt), whose family was later the Knodels' neighbour near Eatonia, Saskatchewan. ¶30
