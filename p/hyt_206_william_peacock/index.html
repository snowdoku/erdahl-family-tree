Life story

William Peacock, mercer of London, named by Lodge (Peerage of Ireland, 1789, vol. 1, p. 144) as the father of Jane, 'daughter and coheir to William Peacock, mercer of London', who married Michael Boyle, merchant of London. 'Coheir' means he left no son, and at least one other daughter shared his estate. The birth year is a rough estimate from his daughter's children (born about 1570-90). Added. ¶1

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade · 1 on the identification · 1 search with no result · 3 open questions

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Probable, graded on his link to his daughter Jane Peacock. This rests on Lodge alone, the same sentence that puts Jane herself in the tree. The phrase 'daughter and coheir' is heraldic in style, which suggests Lodge drew on a family pedigree. No corroboration was found. The London visitations of 1568 and 1633-35 have no William Peacock, mercer. The PCC will catalogue (TNA Discovery) has no William Peacock, mercer, before 1620; there are William Peacock, leather seller (1570, PROB 11/52/317) and William Peacocke, skinner (1589, PROB 11/73/416). ¶2a

The case for this identification 1

Test record read, and it does not fit: the PCC will of William Pecoke (PROB 11/33/60, proved Jan 1549/50; image read). He was not a London mercer with coheiresses. The will is a short soldier's or settler's will: 'That I William Pecoke make my testament... I make my brother John and Joane my wife my full executours... I geve unto John my brother my whole geldyng [?]... And to Angell my sonne tenne poundes and to [Sabirge ?] my doughter fyve poundes. And to Joane my servant ffoure poundes and the one halfe of my houshold stuffe... And the residue of my stuffe here in Haddyngton I geve unto John Haxall [?] and John Johnson, maister carpenter of the said towne', the residue of goods and money 'unto my wife both here and at home'. A later clerk's marginal note reads 'died in Scotland - see Act book'. He had a living son, Angell, so his daughters were not coheiresses, and no Jane is named. This candidate is ruled out. Grade unchanged (Probable, on Lodge alone, on his link to Jane Peacock). ¶4a

Searches that found nothing 1

Searched again; nothing found. The TNA catalogue of PCC wills before 1610 has no Peacock described as a mercer. The William Peacocks there are William Pecoke or Pecock (PROB 11/33/60, proved 16 January 1550, no place or trade given), William Peacock, leather seller of London (PROB 11/52/317, 1570), and William Peacocke, skinner of London (PROB 11/73/416, 1589). Jane, wife of Michael Boyle, was born about 1540-55, so the 1550 will fits a father who died while his coheiresses were young. It is the first to read. ¶3a
