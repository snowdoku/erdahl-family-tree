Life story

Note only (this person belongs to another branch's worker): Morant's deed-based account of Langleys in Great Waltham makes the Everard coheiress a daughter of John Cornish (and Idonea?), not of William as in the 1552 visitation, and the 1515/16 recovery of the Cornish moiety (ERO D/DTu 85A) names only John Cornyssh son of John. See the Correction on William Cornish. Her Cornish surname still rests on the visitation alone. ¶4

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

2 on the grade · 1 record that disagrees · 2 open questions

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Graded on her link to her son Ralph Josselyn. 'My wife Elizabeth' is life tenant and executrix in the will of Ralph Josselyn of Great Canfield, 31 May 1525 (abstract, NEHGR 71:20-21); she was taxed on lands worth 40s. a year 1542-1546 and was a legatee, with her daughter Agnes, of Philippa Josselyn of High Roding in 1530. Her surname Cornish ('daughter and coheiress of William') comes from the Josselyn pedigree in the Visitation of Essex (Harleian Society 13, as cited by French no. 20 and Zubrinsky n. 6), a compiled heraldic source. The will does not say she was the mother of the son Ralph (born about 1503); the eldest child Agnes (Lady Wiseman) was born about 1499, so a single marriage from about 1497 is likely but not shown. Speculative. ¶1a

Upgrade, Speculative to Probable, graded on her link to her son Ralph Josselyn: Ralph Josselyn's will of 31 May 1525 names one wife, Elizabeth, as life tenant and executrix, and the children Agnes, Philip, Ralph, Nicholas (under 21) and Denys; Philippa Josselyn's will of 1530 calls her 'Elizabeth, late the wife of Ralph Josselyn' and Agnes 'the wife of John Wyseman'. Hawley's Visitation of Essex of 1552, made while Agnes was alive, gives 'Raffe Josselyn = Elizabeth da. & coh. of Will. Cornishe' with their daughter Agnes, wife of John Wiseman of Canfield, and the 1634 Visitation of London has 'Rafe Joscelin = Elizabeth'. No other wife of Ralph appears in any record or pedigree. So she was the mother of the eldest child Agnes and was his wife when he died with a son still under 21. That Ralph the son was hers is inferred (no record names his mother), so Probable. This supersedes the earlier note that the Cornish surname comes only from the 1612 visitation: it is in the 1552 visitation. Her father William Cornish is entered as Speculative (visitation only). ¶3

Records that disagree 1

Great Canfield register (FamilySearch Essex index, two index copies, image not read): 'Elizabeth Joslyn' buried at Great Canfield in August 1546; one copy gives the spouse as 'Rafe Joslyn', the other gives 14 Aug. 1546 with spouse 'John Joslyn'. If the 'Rafe' reading is right, this is very probably the widow of Ralph of Great Canfield (will 1525), whose taxing ended in 1546, and her death would be 14 Aug. 1546. Because the two copies disagree on the husband, the death date is not changed. ¶2a
