About the family

Children in Boston (this man is the father, married 1701, birth unknown; his son of the same name, born 1702, is the separate person Christopher Kimball): 'Christopher Son of Christopher Kemball and Sarah his Wife', born 2 September 1702 (Boston Births, p. 15), and 'Abigall daughter of Christophr. Kimbal and Sarah his Wife', born 28 January 1703/4 (p. 22). Both page images were read. The 1897 Kimball genealogy gives the son's birth as 'Dec. 2, 1702'. The record says 2 September, so the book is wrong on this date. No later Boston births were found for this couple in the OCR of the volume. ¶2

Life story

Origin tested, no change: the 1676 settlement of Richard Kemball of Wenham's estate (Ipswich court, 26 Sep 1676; Essex probate docket 15,724, printed in Probate Records of Essex County 3:72-74) names only the administrators Samuel and Thomas, 'John the eldest son', and the widow Mary, their stepmother, and divides £800 among 'the eight children of the deceased Richard'. It does not name the other five. The 1897 Kimball genealogy fills its list of Richard's children with 'vi. Christopher, b.; d..' (no dates) and 'viii.' left blank, so its placing of Christopher of Boston in that family is a compiler's guess fitted to the 'eight children', not a record. A full-text search of archive.org for Christopher Kimball, Kemball, Kimbal, Kembal and Kimbol found him only in the Boston records of 1701-1704. Parents still not entered. ¶10a

The Prince Genealogy, the other printed source for his origin, is explicit that it is guessing: it calls him 'Capt.' Christopher Kimball, 'from Ipswich, probably descended from Richard Kembal', and later names 'son Richard of Ipswich, who I assume to be the father of Capt. Christopher Kimball who was born in Ipswich'. It adds that he 'd. on the Spanish Main about 1705' (no source given; consistent with his last Boston child in Jan 1703/4 and his widow's remarriage in 1713). Compiler's claims only. ¶11

No grade change; graded on his link to Abigail Kimball: the Bridgewater lead for his widow and daughter was tested and found no record (Bridgewater, East and West Bridgewater VRs and Mitchell's History searched; no Kimball or Jolls before the 19th century). The 1723 Suffolk guardianship once read as 'David Kemball' is a Kendall (index vol. 2 p. 201, page image). A compiled source, Blinn-Pollard-Winkley and Allied Families (pp. 47-48), calls Abigail, born 28 Jan 1703/04, daughter of Christopher and Sarah (Jolls) Kimball, and the wife of Ephraim Everson; it cites no record and adds nothing beyond the 1897 Kimball genealogy. Open test record: the 1740 Bristol County (Taunton) probate of Sarah's brother Robert Jolls (S0253). ¶13

No grade change: the will of Robert Jolls of Bristol (14 Jan 1739/40, Bristol County Probate vol. 9 p. 337 1/2) names only his wife Experience, daughter Mary Munroe and son Thomas; it has no bequest to a sister Kimball or a niece Abigail, so it gives no support to Christopher Kimball and Sarah Jolls as Abigail's parents. See the full note on Sarah Joles. ¶14

Marriage: 'Christopher Kimball & Sarah Joles' were married in Boston by Mr. Cotton Mather on 3 April 1701 (Boston Marriages 1700-1751, p. 4, page image read). No residence is given. ¶1

Origin: the 1897 Kimball Family in America (vol. 1 p. 53, entry 17) calls him 'Christopher Kimball (Richard, Richard) of Ipswich' and lists him among the children of Richard Kimball with no birth date. No Christopher Kimball birth or baptism was found in an OCR search of the printed Vital Records of Ipswich (both volumes). His parentage is the compiler's claim only. ¶3

Probate: no estate for any Christopher Kimball before 1870 appears in the Suffolk County probate index, 1636-1893 (vol. 2, p. 211, page image read). His death and later residence are unknown. ¶4

Plympton searched: Kimball appears in the Plympton vital records before 1800 only in the marriages of Sarah Kimball and Thomas Shirtlef (21 May 1713, not called a widow) and of Abigail Kimball and Jobe Prince (24 Dec 1719); no Kimball birth or early death is entered (Vital Records of Plympton, page image p. 391 and OCR search). Nothing places Christopher Kimball or a widow of his in Plympton, so he stays Speculative. Lead only, not adopted: the 1897 Kimball genealogy makes him a son of Richard Kimball of Wenham; no record has been read for this. ¶6

Death: he died before April 1713, when his widow (as identified above) intended marriage with Thomas Shurtleff; inferred, no death record found. ¶9

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade · 1 on the identification · 1 alternative set aside · 2 searches with no result · 1 open question

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Upgrade: Speculative to Probable, on the Boston marriage intention of 1713 described here. Plympton link found: the Boston marriage intentions record 'Thomas Shotley of Plymouth & Sarah Kemball of Boston' in April 1713 (Boston Marriages 1700-1751, Record Commissioners vol. 28, p. 92; page image read; the printed date is 'Apr. 5, 1713' but it falls between entries of 23 April and 6 May, so it is probably the 25th). This is the Boston side of the marriage of Sarah Kimball and Thomas Shirtlef at Plympton on 21 May 1713. The bride was 'of Boston'. The Boston birth registers have no Sarah Kemball/Kimball born 1667-1700 (vol. 9 has only Sarah of Thomas and Elizabeth Kemble, born 19 Apr 1666, who would have been 47), and the only Sarah Kimball in the Boston marriages before 1713 is Sarah Joles, who married Christopher Kimball in 1701. So the 1713 bride is very probably Christopher's widow, Sarah (Joles) Kimball. Her remarriage to a Plymouth/Plympton man explains why their daughter Abigail, born in Boston on 28 Jan 1703/4, married Job Prince at Plympton on 24 Dec 1719, at fifteen. Earlier passes rejected the Shurtleff bride as 'more likely a young bride' because Thomas Shurtleff left eight minor children about 1730; a widow born about 1680 could have borne children from 1714 to the mid-1720s, so the objection is not decisive. With the Boston birth, the age at death, the name Christopher among Abigail's children, and now a documented move of a Sarah Kimball of Boston to Plympton, the parentage is raised from Speculative to Probable. ¶8a

The case for this identification 1

Identity (Speculative): proposed as the father of Abigail Kimball who married Job Prince at Plympton in 1719. The grounds are her Boston birth date, which matches her age at death, and the name Christopher among her children. No record yet places him or his widow in Plympton. What is missing, and would raise it: a Plymouth County deed or probate record, or a Plympton church record, for Christopher or Sarah Kimball about 1704-1725. ¶5

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Not Confirmed: no record calls the 1713 bride a widow or names Abigail as her daughter. Records that would confirm it: the Suffolk guardianship of David Kemball, 1723 (docket 4740, queued S0208), and the Plymouth probate of Thomas Shurtleff of Plympton (c.1730, Book 5 pp. 706-711), which may name a stepdaughter Abigail Prince. ¶8b

Searches that found nothing 2

Plymouth probate (negative): the Plymouth County probate index 1686-1820 has no Kimball, Kimbal or Cimbol entry at all, so there is no Plymouth guardianship for his daughter Abigail; if one exists it is more likely in Suffolk. A Sarah Kimball married Thomas Shirtlef at Plympton on 21 May 1713 (Vital Records of Plympton). The Shurtleff genealogy (1912, vol. 1 p. 4) gives no parentage for her, and the Plympton Thomas Shurtleff who died about 1730 left eight minor children (Plymouth Probate Book 5 pp. 706-711), so she was more likely a young bride than Christopher's widow. This lead does not link him to Abigail. ¶7

Essex probate (S0221, negative): the original file papers for docket 15724 (Richard Kemball of Wenham, 1676) are not on FamilySearch. The bound Essex probate volume for 1672-1691 (FamilySearch DGS 7704881) has a manuscript abstract index (image 33, read). Its Kimball & Kemball entries are Caleb, Henry, John ('in account with Thomas Kimball'), Richard of Wenham (inventory 17 June 1676, administered by his sons Samuel and Thomas, widow Mary) and Thomas of Bradford (1676, widow Mary, eight children). No Christopher Kimball appears, so the volume gives no support to the 1897 claim that he was Richard's son. His parents stay un-entered. The original file is on AmericanAncestors (Essex County Probate File Papers), outside FamilySearch. ¶12
