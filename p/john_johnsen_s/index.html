About the family

Parentage (Speculative): in the 1801 census at Sætnes, Jon Jonsen, age 8, was a son of the landless cottar Jon Tronsen (52) and Olava Knudsdatter (50); his brother Søren was 10. ¶3

His wife's family is now identified: Anne Pedersdatter was born 18 Oct 1791 at the neighbouring farm Brækken (Sogge-Brækken), daughter of Peder Amundsen and Eli Pedersdatter. Their marriage about 1816 is still not found in any indexed register. ¶7

Parentage lowered to Speculative (audit, 13 Oct 2026): the link to Jon Tronsen and Olava Knudsdatter rested on his being the Joen Jonsen baptised at Sætnes-Ødegården in 1792 (farm continuity and burial age). Two records now weaken it: Kari Johnsdatter's Grytten confirmation of 19 Oct 1834 (1539P 1818-1853, p. 495, no. 5, read on the scan: mother 'Anne Pedersd. fra Lesjøe') and the Lesja marriage of 13 Mar 1816 (0512P 1777-1819, pp. 834-835, index: groom Joen Joensen of Klevstuen, bride Anne Pedersdatter, maiden of Kloppen) show that in 1816-1819 he lived in Lesja, not at Setnes; and a second Jon Jonsen of Setnes, bachelor and soldier, married Aleth Rasmusdatter in Grytten church on 15 Oct 1816 (1539P 1807-1817, p. 613) with a cautioner 'Jon Setnes'. That soldier is at least as good a fit for the 1792 son of Jon Tronsen, whose elder son Søren was likewise a soldier of Setnes. Until the Lesja marriage entry is read for the groom's age and birthplace, the 1792 identification, and the birth date 6 Sep 1792 at Sætnes-Ødegården taken from it, are Speculative. ¶13

Life story

Lesja alternative (Speculative): Joen, born 12 Nov 1784 and baptised 28 Nov 1784 at the Verket (Lesjaverk) annex church, son of Jon Alvsen of Raannaahougen (Rånåhaugen) and Kari Rolvsdatter. Read on the scan (Lesja ministerialbok 1777-1819, 0512P, source 9237, canvas 96, p. 192): 'Jon Alvsen Raannaahougen og Kari Rolvsdatter Barn Joen, fød d. 12te Novb. Test[es]: Ole Knudsen Raannaae, Hendrich Qvarn, Ole Alvsen Nyestuen, Elie Larsdatter Raannaae, Jøda Ericsdatter Raannaae.' In the 1801 census (Lesja, Raanaae, household 3) he is Joen Joensen, 16, unmarried servant, with his parents Joen Alvsen, 55, cottager with land, and Kari Rolvsdatter, 50, and siblings Aaste (23), Alv (20, tailor) and Anne (14). ¶20

Godparents of son Joen, read on the scan: Lesja ministerialbok 1777-1819, p. 844, 1816: 'Joen Joensen Stuefloten og Anne Peders D: B: Joen fød d. 1 Juni. Fadere Peder Klopen, Syver Enneboe, Knud Slettum, Mari Nystuen, Brigit Sør-Enneboe. Lægd No 1.' ¶22

Baptism (Probable, upgraded from Speculative): 'Joen Joensen', born 6 Sep 1792 at Sætnæss-Ødegaarden and baptized 8 Sep 1792 at Grytten, son of Joen Tronsen and Olava Knudsdatter. He was confirmed as Joen Jonsen of 'Setnes Øgaard' 17 Jul 1808. He was the Jon Jonsen, 8, in their household in 1801. The farm continuity (his wife Anne died at Sætnes-Ødegård in 1850, he at Sætnes in 1851) and the age (60 at death, born about 1791) fit. His brother Søren Jonsen of Setnes (born 14 Feb 1790) married Eli Pedersdatter of Hagen in 1814. ¶5

His marriage to Anne Pedersdatter (about 1816) is not indexed in Grytten, Veøy, Hen or neighbouring parishes for 1813-1817. ¶6

Occupation across his life: he is 'inderst' (a lodger who rented house-room, with no land of his own) at Sætnes in the records of the 1810s and 1840s, and 'husmann' (a cottager holding a small plot from the main farm) at his death in 1851. His son Peder is likewise described as 'Ungkarl og Inderste' - bachelor and lodger - at his 1842 marriage, so the family held no land of its own in two generations. ¶8

Marriage: Joen Joensen, bachelor (ungkarl) of Klevstuen, married Anne Pedersdatter, maiden (pige) of Klaappen (Kloppen), on 13 Mar 1816 at the Lesjaverk (Verket) annex church in Lesja parish (index only, pp. 834-835; the scan has not been read). This is the marriage that earlier searches failed to find in Grytten and its neighbours. Their first son Joen was baptised in Lesja on 4 Jul 1816 (father of Stuefloten), Peder on 26 Oct 1817 and Kari on 28 Nov 1819 (both at Kloppen). By 1823 the family was at Isterdal in Grytten, by 1826-29 at Aag, and by 1831 at Setnæs. ¶10

Disambiguation: do not confuse him with his namesake Jon Jonsen Setnes, bachelor and soldier, who married Aleth Rasmusdatter of Sauseth in Grytten church on 15 Oct 1816 (p. 613; the index misreads the groom as 'Jon Larsen' and the place as Heen). That Jon Jonsen was a lodger and then crofter under Sogge (Sogge-Megaard), with children Joen (1819, 'Sogge Setnes'), Marie (1822) and Johnette (1825). Our John Johnsen was married to Anne Pedersdatter and was in Lesja in 1816-1819. Any 'John Johnsen Setnes' record of 1816-1851 must be sorted by the wife's name. A search of all Grytten church records 1815-1840 for a mother named Anne with a father surnamed Johnsen found only our one couple. ¶12

Lesja marriage entry read on the scan (13 Oct 2026): 'd. 13de Martii, Ungkarl Joen Joensen Klevstuen og Pige Anne Pedersdatter Klaappen. Cautionister: Under Corporal[?] Tore Mølmen og Henrik Maarsen' - bachelor Joen Joensen of Klevstuen and maiden Anne Pedersdatter of Klaappen (Kloppen); the sureties (cautionister, marriage witnesses) were Tore Mølmen, apparently an under-corporal, and Henrik Maarsen. The entry gives no age, no birthplace and no father, so it neither proves nor refutes the 1792 Sætnes-Ødegården identification; that parentage stays Speculative. On the same page, in a baptism of Jan 1816 (Ingebret, son of Poul Joensen Ovram and Guri Pedersdatter), Joen Joensen Klevstuen stood godfather beside Peder Pedersen Klaappen and Ingebret Pedersen Klaappen. ¶14

Grytten in-migration list 1822, read on the scan: Grytten ministerialbok 1818-1853 (1539P), Tilgangs-Lister (arrivals), p. 608, no. 26: 'John Johnsen Kloppen og Anne Pedersd:', ages '38 aar' and '32 aar' (the second digit of each is faint; 38 and 32 are the best readings, matching the Digitalarkivet transcription 'Age: 38 år'), occupation apparently 'Dagarbeidere med deres Barn' (day-labourers with their children), 'fra Læsjøe' (from Lesja). This dates the family's move from Kloppen in Lesja to Grytten to 1822, just before son Anders's baptism at Isterdal in Aug 1823. ¶16

Lesja out-migration list 1822, read on the scan: Lesja ministerialbok 1820-1829 (0512P, Digitalarkivet source 9238), Afgangs-Lister, p. 560, 1822 no. 3: '[Huus]folkene: John Johnsen Kloppen og h[ustru] Anne Pedersdatter', ages about 38 and 32 (faint), 'Huusmandsfolk' (cottar folk), 'til Grytten', with a certificate (attest) dated about 2[?] June 1822. This is the Lesja side of the Grytten 1822 arrival entry and gives the same ages; the two lists are probably not independent, since Grytten copied the Lesja certificate. It names no birthplace or father. Same page, 1821 no. 18: Marit Larsdatter Eneboe, 22, farmer's daughter, moved to Grytten - from the farm where Anne Pedersdatter's parents lived in 1801; a possible relative, not yet traced. ¶18

Possible later children of the same couple (Speculative): Anders (born 1823, Isterdal), Iver (born 1826, Aag) and Ingebrigt (born 1828, Aag), all with parents John Johnsen and Anne Pedersdatter in Grytten. ¶4

Death: Probable. John Johnsen Setnæs, cottar (husmann), died 22 May 1851, age 60 (born about 1791). He was killed by a stone that fell from the Setnes mountain, and was buried 28 May 1851 at Grytten. ¶2

His death was violent and is worth recording plainly: on 22 May 1851 he was killed by a stone that fell from the Setnes mountain, the steep scree slope above the farm, and was buried at Grytten on 28 May 1851 at the age of 60. ¶9

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Confidence adjusted: Probable to Speculative (13 Oct 2026). In this tree a person's own confidence carries the evidence for his link to his parents. His link to Jon Tronsen and Olava Knudsdatter rests on his being the Joen Jonsen baptised at Sætnes-Ødegården, Grytten, on 8 Sep 1792, and that identification is Speculative: Kari Johnsdatter's Grytten confirmation of 19 Oct 1834 (p. 495, no. 5, mother 'Anne Pedersd. fra Lesjøe') and the Lesja marriage of 13 Mar 1816 (0512P 1777-1819, pp. 834-835, read on the scan, no age, birthplace or father given) put him in Lesja in 1816-1819, and the soldier Jon Jonsen Setnes who married Aleth Rasmusdatter in Grytten on 15 Oct 1816 (p. 613) fits the 1792 baptism at least as well. The doubt therefore belongs on him, not on Jon Tronsen and Olava Knudsdatter, whose own levels are restored. His identity as the father of Peder Johnsen Sætnes (1817 Lesja baptism and 1833 Grytten confirmation) is not in question. ¶15a

Upgrade: Confirmed as the father of Peder Johnsen Sætnes (see his page), under the grading rule that a person's grade covers the link to the child in the tree. Peder's confirmation, Grytten ministerialbok 1818-1853 (1539P), confirmations 1833, p. 492 no. 4, 15 Sep 1833, read on the Digitalarkivet index (and earlier on the scan, canvas 149), gives 'Peder Johnsen', of 'Setnæs', born 1817, father 'John Johnsen', 'Indst:' (lodger), mother 'Anne Pedersd', 'Hustru'. Peder's 1817 Lesja baptism (Jon Jonsen and Ane Pedersdatter of Kloppen) names the same parents, and the Lesja and Grytten records share the children Joen 1816, Peder 1817 and Kari 1819. Two primary records therefore name John Johnsen as Peder's father. This grade covers only that link: his own birth and parentage (Jon Alvsen Rånåhaugen and Kari Rolvsdatter) keep their earlier grade of Speculative, and his identification with any baptism is still open. ¶23

The case for this identification 2

Identity caution: the groom of 1816 was living at Klevstuen in Lesja. Nothing yet shows that he is the Joen Jonsen baptised at Sætnes-Ødegården, Grytten, in 1792, the identification behind this record's birth and parents. A Grytten man working on the Romsdal road in Lesja (Stuguflaten and Lesjaverk lie on it) would fit, and his burial age of 60 in 1851 agrees, but the Lesja marriage entry should be read on the scan for his age, birthplace and forlovere before the 1792 link is relied on. ¶11

Identity caution: the 1822 age of 38 points to a birth about 1783-84, not 1792. It disagrees with his burial age of 60 in 1851 (born about 1791) and with the 6 Sep 1792 Sætnes-Ødegården baptism on which his Grytten parentage rests. The entry also does not say he was born in Grytten. Lesja itself had boys named Joen Joensen in the 1801 census (born about 1790 at Bjørlien, and born about 1795 at Lesja Jernverk, the ironworks at Lesjaverk where he married in 1816), so a Lesja birth is a live alternative. The parentage stays Speculative and, if anything, is weaker. ¶17a

Corrections 1

Correction: the identification with Joen Joensen baptised 8 Sep 1792 at Sætnes-Ødegården, Grytten, son of Jon Tronsen and Olava Knudsdatter, is now unlikely and his parents are changed. (1) Grytten had its own Jon Jonsen Setnes, bachelor and soldier, who married Aleth Rasmusdatter of Sauseth in Grytten church on 15 Oct 1816 with 'Jon Setnes' as a cautioner (Grytten ministerialbok 1807-1817, p. 613); a local soldier of that age is the natural fit for the 1792 Sætnes-Ødegården baptism and the 1808 'Setnes Øgaard' confirmation. (2) Our John was in Lesja from at least 1816 to 1822, married there as a bachelor of Klevstuen, and both the Lesja out-migration list and the Grytten arrival list of 1822 give his age as 38 (born about 1784), not 29-30. ¶19

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Why the Lesja candidate fits better (inference): his age (12 Nov 1784) matches the age 38 given in both 1822 migration lists exactly; he was baptised at the same Verket annex church where John married in 1816 and had his children baptised in 1816-1819; John's first son was named Joen and his first daughter Kari, the names of this Jon Alvsen and Kari Rolvsdatter, following the usual naming pattern (his second son Peder was named after his wife's father); and a godmother of John's son Joen in 1816 was 'Mari Nystuen', while Ole Alvsen of Nystuen (probably Jon Alvsen's brother) stood godfather to the 1784 Joen. The godparents of John's sons in 1816-1817 also include four people of Slettum (Knud, Rolv, Erland and Rønnog), the household of Peder Rolvsen (b. about 1745) in 1801; Peder Rolvsen may be a brother of Kari Rolvsdatter (b. about 1751), which would make them John's cousins, but that is not proved. Against it: John's burial age of 60 in 1851 points to about 1791. The Lesja marriage register 1800-1819 has only one Joen Joensen groom (ours, 1816), and no Lesja burial of a Joen Joensen of the right age was found for 1801-1819, so the 1784 Joen is not otherwise accounted for. The link stays Speculative until a record names him with his origin, such as a probate (skifte) of Jon Alvsen or Kari Rolvsdatter in the Nord-Gudbrandsdal sorenskriveri protocols, which are not person-indexed. ¶21

Open questions and the next record to read 2

What would settle it: a Grytten or Lesja record giving his age, birthplace or father, such as a Lesja moving-out list (utflytting) of about 1820 or the 1820s Grytten moving-in entries. ¶15b

What would settle it: the Lesja out-migration list for 1822 (Lesja ministerialbok 1820-1829/1820-1831, Avgangslister) for his stated age and destination, and the Lesja baptism register about 1783-92 for a Joen son of Joen. ¶17b
