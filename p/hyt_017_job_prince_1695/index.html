About the family

Children (Kingston VR): Thomas (1720), Ruth Leach (1721), Job (1723), Kimball (28 April 1726), James (1729) and Christopher (8 March 1729/30). ¶3

Parents (primary record): his mother's probate account of July 1704 names him as one of the three sons that Thomas Prince, late of Boston, mariner, 'had by' his wife Ruth. Their uncle Samuel Prince of Sandwich was appointed guardian of Thomas, Benjamin and Job on 24 June 1704 (Plymouth County Probate Book 2, p. 47). His own estate, as Job Prince of Kingston, was administered from 1731 (Plymouth Probate Book 6 pp. 55-157, not yet read). Guardians were appointed for his sons Kimball and James in 1744-45 (Book 9 p. 388; Book 10 p. 86). ¶9

Heirs (Plymouth probate): on 24 December 1744 'Kimbal Prince of Kingston' chose Ephraim Everson of Kingston, his stepfather, as guardian for his share 'in right of his Father Job Prince late deceased' (Book 9 p. 388). The Plymouth probate index (pp. 27-28) shows no division or settlement of Job's real estate and no guardianship for his son Job, who came of age in 1744. So no probate record names the son Job. Plymouth County deeds for the Kingston land, about 1744-60, are the likeliest place to find 'Job Prince of Boston' acting as an heir. ¶11

Life story

Birth: Kingston's gravestone record calls him 'first of the family in Kingston,' son of Thomas and Ruth Turner, born 1695 (Vital Records of Kingston, p. 116, G.R.1). ¶1

Birth: the Scituate town register records 'Job, s. Thomas, Apr. 14, 1695' under Prince, with brothers Thomas (10 July 1686) and Benjamin (28 Feb 1693/4) (Vital Records of Scituate, vol. 1, p. 300, page image read). This agrees with the Kingston gravestone's birth year 1695 and father Thomas; the 1902 Prince Genealogy's 'Aug. 1695' is not supported. The Kingston and Scituate Jobs are taken to be the same man: no other Job Prince of this age is known, and his father's widow remarried at Scituate in 1701. ¶8

Marriage: 'Job PRINCE of Plimouth published to Abigail CIMBOL of Plimptown' appears in the Plymouth marriage intentions of 1719/20-1720 (Vital Records of Plymouth, town records p. 263; OCR only, the exact day not aligned and the page image not read). ¶2

Marriage: The printed Vital Records of Plympton, Massachusetts, record under Prince the marriage of 'Jobe and Abigail Kimball, Dec. 24, 1719' (marriages p. 367, page image read). This fixes the place and date of the marriage whose intention was entered at Plymouth in 1719/20. ¶7

Death: died in April 1731 in Jamaica, per the Kingston gravestone record (Vital Records of Kingston, p. 371). His widow Abigail married Ephraim Everson in Kingston on 15 March 1731/2. ¶4

Estate (Plymouth County Probate): on 11 August 1731 Judge Isaac Winslow granted administration to 'Abigail Prince of Kingston, Widow', whose 'husband Job Prince late of Kingston' had died intestate (Book 6 p. 55). Her account, allowed on 4 April 1732, lists only creditors, among them Ephraim Everson, whom she married that spring (Book 6 p. 146). Household goods were set off to 'Abigail Prince Widow Relict of Job Prince' on 10 April 1732 (Book 6 p. 157). About 30 acres in Kingston were appraised at £180 old tenor in September 1743 (Book 9 p. 138). None of these entries names his children, so his son Job (b. 1723) is not yet tied to him by a probate record. A division among the heirs, if one was recorded, has not been found. ¶10

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Upgrade: to Confirmed as father of Job Prince of Boston (see his page). The record is the diary of Hezekiah Prince of Kingston and Thomaston (son of Kimball Prince of Kingston, aged 21 in April 1792, d. 1840), entry for Monday 18 Nov 1793 on arriving in Boston, printed in the New England Magazine n.s. 9 (Feb. 1894): 727-728 from the original journal (editor's notes in brackets, kept apart): 'I stopped with my cousin, James Prince, who married Miss Gordon. He is U.S. marshal, the son of my uncle Job Prince. My father, Kimball Prince of Kingston, Mass., together with four brothers and one sister, were all born at Rocky Nook, Kingston. Their father was Capt. Job Prince, who married Abigail Kimball of Bridgewater, 1722. His father was Capt. Thomas Prince of Boston, who married Ruth Turner of Scituate... My grandfather, Capt. Job Prince of Rocky Nook, Kingston, died at Jamaica, W.I., of the smallpox in 1734. Three of his sons were sea captains residing in Boston. Capt. Job married Elisebeth Allen, died 1790. His mansion house was between McLean Street and Allen Street, West End... I have often been at my uncle Job's mansion, once with my father in 1785. He had eight children, Hezekiah, for whom I was named, Capt. Job, Jr., master of the big ship Massachusetts, Thomas, James, and Samuel; also three daughters, Sarah, Elisebeth, and Abigal.' A grandson, writing in 1793 of people he knew (he had visited his uncle Job's house in 1785 and was staying with Job's son James), names Job of Boston, husband of Elizabeth Allen, who died in 1790, as a son of Capt. Job Prince of Rocky Nook, Kingston, and Abigail Kimball. His list of his uncle's eight children (Hezekiah, Job, Thomas, James, Samuel, Sarah, Elizabeth, Abigail) matches the Boston birth register for Job and Elizabeth Prince, 1749-1767, which proves the uncle is the man in the tree. The Kingston gravestone entry 'Job, ch. Job and Abigail (Kimball), 1790, in Boston' (VR Kingston p. 371) agrees. This settles the step that earlier passes left Probable (no probate or deed had named Job of Boston as heir of Job of Kingston). The diary's small slips (marriage '1722' for 24 Dec 1719; death '1734' for April 1731) do not affect the relationships. ¶12a

Audit 12 (2 Nov 2026): Confirmed KEPT, graded on his link to his son Job Prince of Boston (see his page). This rules on the audit 11 carry-over flag. The diary's statement that matters for HIM is not the family history but first-hand, current knowledge: the diarist, son of Kimball Prince (whose parentage, 's. Job and Abigail', 1726, is in the Kingston town register), writes in 1793 of 'my uncle Job Prince', whose house he had visited with his father in 1785, and is staying with that uncle's son. A father's brother of the same surname is a son of the diarist's grandfather Job, which makes this a chain of relationship words (register 's. Job' + diary 'my uncle') rather than a late statement about a distant event. That is the distinction from audit 11 pattern 1. The diary's errors about dates (1722, 1734) concern his marriage and death, not the uncle relation. Residual: 'uncle' could in principle mean an uncle by marriage; no record makes Job of Boston the husband of a sister of Kimball or of Kimball's wife (Ruth Prince married Leach). His wife's link is lowered separately (see Abigail Kimball). ¶13

The case for this identification 1

Identity (Probable): he is the father of Job Prince of Boston by the reasoning set out on that person (the 1790 Boston death of his son Job and the family naming). The Kingston town register itself records him as a father: 'Kimball, s. Job and Abigail, Apr. 28, 1726' and 'Cristifer (Princ), s. Job and Abigail, Mar. 8, 1729-30' (Vital Records of Kingston, pp. 116-117, unbracketed town entries), and it calls his widow's later Everson children those of 'Abigail (Kimball) (formerly w. Job Prince)'. His birth year (1695), his death in Jamaica, his parents, and the 1723 birth of his son Job come only from the later family gravestone record (G.R.1). ¶5

Alternatives considered and set aside 2

Not the same person as Job Prince: that is his son Job (born 1723, died 1790 in Boston), who married Elizabeth Allen in Boston in 1748. Do not merge them. ¶6

Caveat: read in the 1894 printed edition, not the manuscript. ¶12b
