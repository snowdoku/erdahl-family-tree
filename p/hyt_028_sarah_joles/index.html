About the family

Children: Christopher, born 2 September 1702, and Abigail, born 28 January 1703/4, in Boston (Boston Births 1700-1800, pp. 15 and 22). ¶2

Father (Probable): Capt. Thomas Jolls of Boston, ship-master and merchant (d. about 1686), per the Prince Genealogy (c.1902), pp. 23-24, which cites the will of her brother Thomas Jolls, shipwright, of New York (d. 1696; Suffolk Probate vol. 11 p. 213), leaving her his property, and her 1706 deed to her brother Robert Jolls of Bridgewater. Supported by the Suffolk Inferior Court of Pleas, 2 April 1695: 'Sarah Jolls, Boston, spinster' and 'Thomas Jolls, Boston, shipwright' each sued 'Hannah Jolls, widow, admx.' (the widow of Capt. Thomas Jolls, Suffolk Deeds XIV:321) for money due; Sarah was awarded £49 4s 6d. Her baptism or birth is not recorded in the Boston registers. Mother not entered (Rebecca or Abigail, wives of Thomas Jolls before 1677). ¶9

Mother (Speculative): Abigail, wife of Capt. Thomas Jolls by 1672-1674 (Boston births of Thomas and Jonathan), named as Sarah's mother by the Prince Genealogy; see Abigail. Her half-brother John Jolls, son of Thomas and his fourth wife Hannah (Briggs) Winslow, is named in Richard Winslow's will of 1697 (Suffolk Probate 16:413). ¶10

Brother's will read: Suffolk Probate 11:213-214. Thomas Jolls, shipwright of Boston, made his sister 'Sarah Jolls of Boston Spinster' his sole heir and executrix (will 13 Oct 1693, proved 3 Oct 1696); he also names his brother John Jolls and 'my Fathers Estate'. This places an unmarried Sarah Jolls in Boston in 1693-96, five years before 'Sarah Joles' married Christopher Kimball there, and makes her a daughter of Capt. Thomas Jolls by the chain set out on Thomas Jolls. Her parents' confidence is graded on their records. ¶14

Life story

Marriage: 'Christopher Kimball & Sarah Joles' were married in Boston by Mr. Cotton Mather on 3 April 1701 (Boston Marriages 1700-1751, p. 4, page image read). The 1897 Kimball genealogy spells her name 'Sarah Jolls of Boston'. Her own birth and parents have not been searched. ¶1

Her own deed: Suffolk Deeds 23:215v-216, 15 Jan 1706/7. As 'Sarah Kimball of Boston... Widow only Daughter of Thomas Jolls late of Boston Marriner' she sold her Black Horse Lane share to 'Robert Jolls of Bridgewater... Yeoman Only Surviving Son of my said Father'. So Christopher Kimball was dead by 15 Jan 1706/7. The deed does not name her husband or children; her link to Abigail (Kimball) Prince is graded separately and is unchanged. ¶12

Plympton 1713 marriage read: the page image reads 'SHIRTLEF... Thomas and Sarah Kimball, May 21, 1713.*', the asterisk meaning no intention was recorded (Vital Records of Plympton, marriages p. 391). She is not called a widow and no residence is given. The same page has 'Thomas and Feby Shaw, July 29, 1708', and children of Thomas and Phebe Shurtleff were born at Plympton from 1709 to 1728, so the 1713 groom was another Thomas or the entry is unexplained. This record does not link Sarah (Joles) Kimball to Plympton; the lead remains unproved. ¶5

Second marriage (Probable identification): as 'Sarah Kemball of Boston' she intended marriage with 'Thomas Shotley [Shurtleff] of Plymouth' in Boston in April 1713, and as Sarah Kimball married Thomas Shirtlef at Plympton on 21 May 1713. ¶8

Second record of the marriage: Cotton Mather's own list of the marriages he performed, printed in 'Marriages in Boston', NEHGR 34 (1880), gives the April 1701 couple as 'Christopher Kimbal' and 'Sarah Jolls', with the day as 3. So the minister spelled her surname Jolls, the spelling of Capt. Thomas Jolls's family, where the town copy has 'Joles'. This supports, but does not prove, the Jolls parentage queued at S0217 and S0218. ¶11

Bridgewater lead tested (2 Nov 2026), no grade change; graded on her link to Abigail Kimball. (1) Her brother Robert Jolls: the Bristol town register records 'Thomas, of Robert and Experience' Jolls '(b. Bridgewater), Nov. 9, 1703', John '(b. Scituate), Oct. 2, 1705' and Mary '(b. Bristol), Sept. 8, 1715', and the death of 'JOLLS Robert, 63y., Jan. 17, 1739-40' (Arnold, Vital Record of Rhode Island vol. 6, Bristol; OCR). He was at Bristol by April 1713, when he sold land there (deed cited in the Bosworth Genealogy from Taunton Deeds). (2) The printed Vital Records of Bridgewater (vols. 1-2), the West and East Bridgewater VRs and Mitchell's History of Bridgewater have no Jolls and no Kimball entry before the 19th century (OCR searches), so no Bridgewater record places Sarah or her daughter Abigail there. The Prince Genealogy's statements that Sarah 'moved there [Bridgewater] with her two children' and that Abigail was 'of Bridgewater' are the compiler's, not a record; the 1719/20 Plymouth intention calls Abigail 'of Plimptown'. (3) Bristol was part of Bristol County, Massachusetts, until 1747, so Robert Jolls's 1740 estate is in the Bristol County probate records at Taunton; a bequest to a niece Abigail (then wife of Ephraim Everson of Kingston) would settle the link. Queued as S0253. ¶15

Robert Jolls's will read (negative, FamilySearch signed in, 2 Nov 2026). The Bristol County probate index (A-Owen, image 421) lists 'Jolls, Robert': will vol. 9 p. 337 1/2, probate p. 337, inventory p. 375, receipt p. 388. The will (vol. 9 pp. 337 1/2-338, images 496-497 of 'Bristol. Probate Records 1733-1740') is that of 'Robert Joles of Bristol in the County of Bristol in New England, yeoman', dated 14 January 1739/40 and proved at Bristol on 19 February 1739/40, when probate was granted to 'Thomas Joles of Bristol, yeoman', named in the certificate as the son of the deceased. Its legatees are only his 'beloved daughter Mary Munroe' (land near Deacon Throop's, three slaves, a bed, a chest of drawers and a looking glass), his 'beloved wife Experien[ce]' (use of half his real estate and of the indoor movables for life, with a slave woman, a boy, two cows and ten sheep; image read for the wife's name), and his 'beloved son Thomas Joles' (the residue and the other half of the real estate after the widow's death; sole executor). It names no sister, no Kimball, no Everson, no niece and no Abigail, and no other kin. So the will does not test the proposed sister Sarah (Jolls) Kimball either way: a testator with a son and daughter living need not mention a sister's child. The receipt (p. 388) and inventory (p. 375) were not read; the receipt is presumably Mary Munroe's. Robert's son John (born Scituate 1705) is not named, so was probably dead. Other 'Robert Jolls' full-text hits in the Bristol, R.I. 'Deeds, wills, inventories' volumes are typed 20th-century deed abstracts (Books 5-13, 1710-1723: Grace Hayman to Robert Jolls 1710, Andrew Willett to Robert Jolls of the Boundfield farm 1710, division with John Throope 1714, sale to James Pinnio) and the Bristol (Mass.) probate hits are Robert acting as a divider, creditor or witness (Jabez Howland 1712, Jonah Lincoln account, Thomas Martin receipt 1733/4, Winslow division 1733, a 1739/40 partition). None mentions a Kimball or an Abigail. The later Bristol town-council probate of Thomas Jolls (will proved after 1747; Bristol R.I. probate and town records, image 19) and a 1746-93 will leaving legacies to daughters Lydia and Hannah Jolls belong to the son's generation and were not followed. Grade unchanged (Probable, compiled genealogy only); the link of Sarah to Abigail Kimball still lacks a record. ¶17

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade · 1 on the identification · 2 alternatives set aside · 2 searches with no result · 1 open question

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Upgrade: Speculative to Probable, on the Boston marriage intention of 1713 described here. Plympton link found: the Boston marriage intentions record 'Thomas Shotley of Plymouth & Sarah Kemball of Boston' in April 1713 (Boston Marriages 1700-1751, Record Commissioners vol. 28, p. 92; page image read; the printed date is 'Apr. 5, 1713' but it falls between entries of 23 April and 6 May, so it is probably the 25th). This is the Boston side of the marriage of Sarah Kimball and Thomas Shirtlef at Plympton on 21 May 1713. The bride was 'of Boston'. The Boston birth registers have no Sarah Kemball/Kimball born 1667-1700 (vol. 9 has only Sarah of Thomas and Elizabeth Kemble, born 19 Apr 1666, who would have been 47), and the only Sarah Kimball in the Boston marriages before 1713 is Sarah Joles, who married Christopher Kimball in 1701. So the 1713 bride is very probably Christopher's widow, Sarah (Joles) Kimball. Her remarriage to a Plymouth/Plympton man explains why their daughter Abigail, born in Boston on 28 Jan 1703/4, married Job Prince at Plympton on 24 Dec 1719, at fifteen. Earlier passes rejected the Shurtleff bride as 'more likely a young bride' because Thomas Shurtleff left eight minor children about 1730; a widow born about 1680 could have borne children from 1714 to the mid-1720s, so the objection is not decisive. With the Boston birth, the age at death, the name Christopher among Abigail's children, and now a documented move of a Sarah Kimball of Boston to Plympton, the parentage is raised from Speculative to Probable. ¶7a

The case for this identification 1

Identity (Speculative): proposed as the mother of Abigail Kimball (married Job Prince at Plympton in 1719), on the same grounds as Christopher Kimball. Plausible because of Abigail's exact age fit and the Christopher name among her sons; missing is any record placing this couple in Plymouth County. What would raise it: a Plymouth County deed or probate record, or a Plympton record of Sarah Kimball or of her remarriage, about 1704-1725. ¶4

Alternatives considered and set aside 2

Not Confirmed: no record calls the 1713 bride a widow or names Abigail as her daughter. Records that would confirm it: the Suffolk guardianship of David Kemball, 1723 (docket 4740, queued S0208), and the Plymouth probate of Thomas Shurtleff of Plympton (c.1730, Book 5 pp. 706-711), which may name a stepdaughter Abigail Prince. ¶7b

Lead closed: the 'David Kemball, guardianship 1723, docket 4740' reading of the printed Suffolk probate index came from OCR columns. On the page image (vol. 2 p. 201) the left column has only the cross-reference 'Kemball, Kemble, see Kimball'; the 1723 guardianships of Abigail (4748), David (4749) and Hannah (4750) on that page are KENDALL children in the right column. This confirms the finding: there is no 1723 Kimball guardianship in Suffolk. ¶16

Searches that found nothing 2

Birth searched, not found: no Sarah Jolls or Joles is entered in Boston births to 1699 (Record Commissioners vol. 9). The entry earlier read from OCR as 'Sarah of John & Elizabeth Walley Jolls' is, on the page image, 'Sarah of Jo[h]n & Elizabeth Walley born Aug. 25' (1684, p. 164): a Walley child, not a Jolls. The Jolls births in that volume are children of Thomas and Abigail (c.1672, 1674) and of Thomas and Susanna (c.1677). Her parents remain unknown. Confidence stays Speculative. ¶6

Plympton probate checked, negative: Plymouth Probate Book 5 pp. 706-711 (23 May 1730; page images read). Administration of 'Thomas Shurtleff Late of Plimton... deceased... Intestate' went to 'Your Brother' Barnabas Shurtleff and to Jonathan Shaw. Guardians were appointed for his children Thomas (14-21), Jonathan, Mehitabel, Phebe, William, Barnabas, Ichabod and Elizabeth (under 14). No widow is named, and neither Sarah nor Abigail Prince appears. The daughter Phebe and the Shaw guardian fit the Thomas Shurtleff who married Phebe Shaw in 1708, so these records do not show that the 1713 bride Sarah Kimball belonged to this household. Separately, the lead 'David Kemball guardianship 1723, docket 4740' is a misreading: docket 4740 is Joseph Muckamoy of Milton or Roxbury, 1723, and the printed Suffolk index has no 1723 David Kimball. ¶13

Open questions and the next record to read 1

Lead, unresolved: a 'Sarah Kimball' married Thomas Shurtleff at Plympton on 21 May 1713 (Plympton VR, marriages p. 391, OCR text only). If she were the widow of Christopher Kimball, that would explain how Abigail came to be 'of Plimptown' by 1719. But the Plympton births list a Thomas Shurtleff with a wife Phebe having children in 1712-1728, so this bride's identity is unclear. Not adopted. ¶3
