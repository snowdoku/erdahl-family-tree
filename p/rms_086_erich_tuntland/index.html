About the family

Father of Peder Erichsen of Tuntland (see his page), graded Probable on that link: the 1666 census names Peder as his son on Tuntland; Peder Erichsen, aged 52, was opsidder on Tuntland in 1701 (manntall 1701, Ryfylke, p. 177) and died there before 27 Sep 1725. The identification of the 1666 son Peder with the 1701 Peder Erichsen rests on name, patronymic, farm and age (21 in 1666 against 52 in 1701, a four-year difference), across 35 years, so Probable, not Confirmed. No other Peder Erichsen is on Tuntland in either list. ¶2a

Life story

Opsidder (farmer) on Tuntland, Hjelmeland. The vicar's census of 1664-1666 (Ryfylke prosti, DA source 35515) lists him in 1666 as 'Erich', opsidder, aged 80 (born about 1586), on Tuntland, a full farm of 4 løber, his own holding 2 løber, with his sons ('Sønner') 'Ole', aged 40 (born about 1626), and 'Peder', aged 21 (born about 1645). The 1664 list for Tuntland names Erich, Lauridtz, Peder and Ole. His patronymic is not given in the index. ¶1

Patronymic found: the bailiff's census of 10 May 1664 for Ryfylke (Fogdenes manntall 1664-1666 no. 12, DA source 35571, Hjelmeland skipreide, p. 59, viewer 37) lists on 'Thundtland' (Tuntland) first 'Erich Bundersen' aged 78, with 'Olle Erichsen Søn' aged 30 in the sons' column; then Lauridtz Halfuorsen 70 and Peder Halfuorsen 69. The patronymic is written 'Bunder-sen', the same spelling the clerk uses for the given name 'Bunder' (Bonde) of Bunder Osmundsen on Søndre Fister and Bunder Olsen on Kleppe on the same opening, so it is read Bondesen, son of Bonde. Age 78 in May 1664 agrees with 80 in the vicar's 1666 list (born about 1586), and the son Ole is the Ole (40 in 1666) of the vicar's census, so this is the same Erich. The ages of Ole differ (30 in 1664 against 40 in 1666); one of the two is a slip. Peder (21 in 1666) is not in the bailiff's 1664 list, which named only one son; this does not affect his link, which rests on the vicar's census. Name changed from 'Erich //' to 'Erich /Bondesen/'. ¶4

Age check: the implied gap of about 59-61 years between Erich (80 in 1666) and his son Peder (21 in 1666; 52 in 1701) is large but possible; ages of old men in the 1660s lists are often rounded up, and the 19-year gap between the sons Ole (40) and Peder (21) suggests two marriages. This is a reason for Probable rather than Confirmed. ¶3

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.
