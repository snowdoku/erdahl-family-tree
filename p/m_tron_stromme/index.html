Life story

Context: in the 1701 census (manntall) for Nordmøre and Romsdal the sons listed at Strømme are Anders Sørensen (born about 1695), Olle (1698) and Peder (1699), which means the household head there in 1701 was a Søren, not a Jon. Tron Jonsen therefore most likely married or moved into Strømme rather than inheriting it. An Ingebor Joensdatter of Strømme, born about 1672, was buried there in 1752 and may have been his kinswoman, but this is unproven. ¶5

Probable baptism (scan read): Grytten ministerialbok p. 26, 18th Sunday after Trinity (16 Oct 1729), Eid church: 'Döbt Jon Jeldens barn kaldet Troen. Testes: Peder Lerem, Huusmand Peder ibid, Peder Ströme, Ingebor Ora, Birgitthe Lerem' - baptised Jon Jelden's child called Troen; godparents (faddere) Peder Lerem, the cottager (husmann) Peder of Lerem, Peder Strømme, Ingebor Ora and Birgitthe Lerem. The farm Jelden, the father's name Jon and a godfather from Strømme, where Tron later lived, fit Tron Jonsen; the entry gives no other link. The mother is not named - the clerk names only fathers on this page - so her name remains unknown. ¶6

Of 'Jelden eller Strømme', Grytten. Father of Joen Thronsen, baptized 28 Dec 1751; the sponsors were from Mættit, Dahle, Hamre and Strømme. His patronymic is not given in the index. ¶1

Patronymic now proven. The Grytten parish register records 'Thron Joensøn' of Jelden twice: as a betrothed man ('trulovar', with the note 'Uenroullerede', meaning not enrolled for naval service) in 1751, and as the groom who married Birgitte Bjørnsdatter of Eide on 4 Apr 1752 at Eid church. The baptism of his son Joen on 28 Dec 1751 at Eid church names the father as 'Thron' of 'Jelden el. Strømme' (Jelden or Strømme) and carries the clerk's note 'NB. Forældrene vare Trolovede og skulde samme Dag blevet ægteviede' - the parents were betrothed and should have been married that same day. The wedding in fact took place the following April. The farm, the dates and the betrothal note tie the three entries to one man, so his full name was Tron Jonsen and his own father was named Jon. ¶2

The 4 Apr 1752 marriage entry (scan read, p. 190) reads 'Thron Joensøn Jelden og af ham beswangrede Birrite Bjørns D. Eide' - Birgitte Bjørnsdatter of Eide, got with child by him. It gives no ages, sureties (forlovere) or fathers. ¶7

He appears later at Strømme in the Grytten register as the father of a child baptized 2 Feb 1762 and as 'Thron Joensøn' of Strømme, father of a child baptized 2 Mar 1766, and as a godfather at Strømme on 29 Sep 1764. Jelden and Strømme are neighbouring places in the Eid part of Grytten parish, and the register uses the two names for the same household. ¶3

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence adjusted: Confirmed to Probable (13 Oct 2026). In this tree a person's own confidence carries the evidence for his link to his parents. The records that make him Jon Tronsen's father (the 1751 baptism of Joen at Eid church, the 1751 betrothal and 4 Apr 1752 marriage as 'Thron Joensøn' of Jelden) are primary, but that link is carried by Jon Tronsen Sætnes. His own link to his father Jon (Joen) rests on the Grytten baptism of 16 Oct 1729 ('Döbt Jon Jeldens barn kaldet Troen', p. 26), which his own note calls a Probable baptism: the patronymic Joensøn and the farm Jelden fit, but no record names his father together with his later life. ¶8a

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Caution recorded for anyone following this line: a separate 'Thron Halvorsen' of Strømme stood godfather at a Grytten baptism on 8 Mar 1761, so not every Tron at Strømme in these years is this man. ¶4

Open questions and the next record to read 1

What would settle it: a probate after Jon Jelden naming Tron as heir, or Tron's confirmation entry of about 1744-47. ¶8b
