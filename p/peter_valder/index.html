Life story

Lutheran Brethren missionary in Honan, China. ¶1

Baptized Peder Johan; in Norway he went by Peder Johan Martinussen (Valderhaug). 'Valder' is shortened from the farm name Valderhaug. ¶2

World War I draft: no WWI draft registration card for him was found on FamilySearch (searched as Peter Valder, born 1886-1888). This is consistent with his being in China as a missionary from 1916, since men abroad did not register in the U.S. draft. Variants Valderhaug and Martinussen were not tried. ¶34

Baptized at Borgund as 'Peder Johan', born 13 April 1887. The register names his father as Martinus Eliassen, farmer (gaardbruger) at Valderhaug, born 1853, and his mother as Hansine Pedersdatter. The Digitalarkivet transcription of the Borgund 1880-1920 book records no godparents; the klokkerbok (parish clerk's duplicate) copy of the same birth carries the date 5 June 1887, which should be his baptism date. ¶8

Death: the Minnesota death index records Peter M. Valder, born 13 April 1887 outside Minnesota, died 14 May 1967 in Hennepin County, certificate 1967-MN-013101, mother's maiden name ROSEVOLD. The index is the state's abstract of the death certificate and agrees with his baptism date; no certificate image is online (MNHS has none for this certificate), so the city, cause and informant were not read. 'Rosevold' is the Minnesota clerk's form of a Norwegian farm name such as Røssevold; his mother is recorded in the Borgund register as Hansine Pedersdatter, and the farm she came from is not yet traced. ¶27

1891 census (1 Jan 1891): Peder Martinussen, aged 4, son, at Valderhaug (holding 135aab), Valderøy district. The household was his father Martinus (1851), his mother Hansine (1856), his brother Elias (1885), his sister Marie (born April 1890), and two servants, Johan Hanssen (1872) and Karn Hansdatter (1872) of Hareid. A second household on the same farm held his grandparents Elias Johnsen (1816) and Marie Knutsdatter (1814) with their son Karl (1859). ¶9

No confirmation record for him is indexed on Digitalarkivet. Valderøy belonged to the Giske sub-parish (annex) of Borgund, and the Giske church book 1899-1941 has its baptisms transcribed but not its confirmations, so his confirmation (about 1901-1902, at Giske church) still has to be read from the page images. ¶12

1900 census (3 Dec 1900): Peder Martinussen, 13, son, at Valderhaug, farm number (gnr) 183, holding (bnr) 29, in Borgund. The household of ten was his parents; his siblings Marie (1890), Karoline (1892), Ole (1895), Elias (1897) and Martin (born 19 Aug 1900); a maid, Josefine Aagnesdatter (1868); and his widowed grandmother Marie Knudsdatter (1814), who lived on the farm as a 'føderaadsenke' - a widow kept on a retirement pension charged on the farm. ¶10

Context: the Church of the Lutheran Brethren was organised at a conference in Milwaukee in December 1900 and recommended China as its first foreign field on 18 December 1900; its first missionaries sailed in 1902. The China field was centred on Tsaoyang (Zaoyang) in northern Hupeh and the adjoining districts of western Honan, and its American headquarters in these years was the Lutheran Brethren church and Bible school at Wahpeton, North Dakota. The work continued until the communist takeover of 1949 closed China to foreign missionaries; the denomination's Asian work then continued in Taiwan and Japan, which is the context of Peter Valder's move to Formosa in 1952 and of his son Otto's 35 years there. ¶18

The family's route home from China, as his son Palmer's obituary describes it, was China to Norway, then to Mayville, North Dakota, and finally to Ferndale, Washington, where other missionary families they knew had also settled. ¶19

Emigrated from Ålesund 6 May 1905 as 'Peder J. Martinussen', 18, fisherman, travelling alone on a ticket paid with money sent from America (link probable). ¶3

Emigration: entered in the Ålesund police emigration protocol on 6 May 1905 as 'Peder J. Martinussen' of Borgund, fisherman, unmarried, bound for 'De Forenede Stater' (the United States). His stated reason for leaving was 'haab om bedre fortjeneste' (hope of better earnings); his intended occupation was 'ubestemt' (undetermined); his ticket had been paid with 'penger tilsendt fra utlandet' (money sent from abroad), which means a relative or friend already in America prepaid his passage; his travel document was a 'rodeseddel', the local parish permit. Group 01109, page 87, line 13. The protocol names no ship or port of landing. ¶11

Graduated Lutheran Brethren Bible School, Wahpeton ND, 1910. Married Olga Johanna Tungseth of Hawley, MN in 1913. ¶4

Sailed for China 1916 with Olga and first daughter; about 15 years with the Lutheran Brethren Mission on the Honan/Hupeh border. A 1930 U.S. consular despatch names 'the Reverend Peter Valder of the Lutheran Brethren Mission at Tsaoyang, Hupeh'. ¶5

Where he worked in China: his eldest son Palmer was born in Honan (Henan) province in 1919, his daughter Frances and his son Otto were both born at Siangyang (Xiangyang) in Hupeh in 1921 and 1922, and a United States consular despatch of 1930 places 'the Reverend Peter Valder of the Lutheran Brethren Mission at Tsaoyang, Hupeh'. The family therefore served on both sides of the Honan-Hupeh provincial border over about fifteen years. ¶17

Seattle arrival 1927: the FamilySearch index of Seattle passenger lists 1890-1957 (NARA M1383 roll 125) lists Peter Valder, 40, born about 1887, arriving at Seattle in 1927 on the Japanese liner Africa Maru, with his birthplace entered as 'United States', which is how the index renders a passenger listed as a U.S. citizen. The manifest image was not viewed. Arriving as a citizen in 1927 implies he was naturalized by then, consistent with 'Na' in 1930 and 1940 (inference). Since his children Marie and Carol were born in North Dakota in 1925 and 1926, this 1927 arrival is probably a return from one of the later missionary journeys mentioned in Palmer's obituary (inference). A second index entry, a Peter Valder arriving at Seattle in 1948, was not opened. ¶29

1930 census: on 5 April 1930 the family lived in Ferndale town, Whatcom County, Washington (enumeration district 37-31, sheet 2B, dwelling 50), in a house Peter owned, valued at $2,500. The household was Peter M., 42, head; his wife Olga J., 40; and their children Myrtle P. 15 (born Iowa), Gladys H. 13, Palmer M. 10, Frances E. 9 and Otto T. 7 (all four born in China and marked 'Am Cit', American citizens by birth abroad), Marie R. 5 and Carol D. 4 (both born North Dakota), and Gotfried M. 1 (born Washington). Read from the census image. ¶22

1930 census (his own entry): Peter M. Valder, 42, married first at 25, born in Norway of Norwegian parents, mother tongue Norwegian, immigrated in 1906 (the last digit could be a 5) and naturalized; he could read and write and spoke English. His occupation was 'Missionary', industry 'Foreign', a wage worker at work on census day; not a veteran. The 1906 arrival year is his or the informant's recollection; the Ålesund emigrant protocol dates his departure to 6 May 1905. ¶23

1940 census: the index of the 1940 census (Ancestry, from the NARA T627 image) shows Peter Valder, head, born about 1887 in Norway, at Ferndale, Whatcom County, Washington, with his wife Olga and the children Palmer M., Otto T., Marie R. and Carol D. Index only; the image was not read, so occupation and the other columns are not recorded here. ¶24

1940 census (read from the image, replacing the earlier index-only entry): in April 1940 Peter Valder, 53, head and the informant, owned his house on Alder Street in Ferndale town, Whatcom County, Washington (dwelling 149, value $3,000, not a farm residence), and had lived in the same house on 1 Apr 1935. He had completed the 8th grade, was born in Norway and was naturalized ('Na'). In the week of 24-30 Mar 1940 he worked 60 hours as a farmer on a dairy farm, working on his own account; he worked 52 weeks in 1939 (the figure is not perfectly clear), had no wage or salary income, and had other income of more than $50. With him were his wife Olga and six children: Palmer M., Francis (Frances), Otto T., Marie R., Carol D. and Gotfred M. (Fred). This agrees with the obituary's 20 years of dairy farming at Ferndale. ¶28

Draft registration 1942: in the April 1942 'old man's' registration (men born 1877-1897) he registered as Peter Valder, born 13 April 1887 at Aalesund, Norway, living at Ferndale, Washington. Index entry only (Ancestry collection 1002); the card was not read. The birth date agrees exactly with the Borgund baptism register. ¶25

Draft registration 1942 (read from the card image, replacing the earlier index-only entry): in the 1942 'old man's' registration (men born 28 Apr 1877 to 16 Feb 1897) he registered as Peter Valder, serial number U 930, of Ferndale, Whatcom County, Washington, age 55, born 13 Apr 1887 at 'Aalesund, Sonmor Co., Norway' (Sunnmøre). He gave his employer as himself, a dairyman, at Ferndale, and named Olga Valder of Ferndale as the person who would always know his address. The card back was not viewed. The birth date agrees exactly with the Borgund baptism register. ¶33

1950 census: on 1 April 1950 Peter M. Valder, 62, born Norway, was living in Otter Tail County, Minnesota (enumeration district 56-35), with his wife Olga J., 60, and their son 'Gotfred M.', 21, born Washington (Fred). He worked 40 hours a week as a psychiatric aide at the State Hospital - the Fergus Falls State Hospital is the state hospital in Otter Tail County (context, inferred). His son Palmer, Palmer's wife Wava and their two small daughters were enumerated on the same sheet as a separate household. Read from the census image. ¶26

About 20 years a dairy farmer at Ferndale, WA. Went to Formosa (Taiwan) as a missionary in 1952 for about 10 years. ¶6

His 1967 obituary, transcribed on his Find a Grave memorial, calls him 'Rev. Peter M. Valder, 80, a pioneer missionary to China under the Lutheran Brethren Church of America.' It says he was born in 1887, emigrated alone to the United States at 17, graduated from the Lutheran Brethren Bible School at Wahpeton, North Dakota in 1910, married Olga Tungseth of Hawley in 1913, sailed with her for China in 1916 and worked there as a missionary about 15 years 'until the political climate changed', then spent about 20 years as a dairy farmer at Ferndale, Washington, went to Formosa (Taiwan) in 1952 for about ten years of work among the local people, and returned to the United States after a stroke. He died at his home in Minneapolis. ¶13

Funeral: services at Bethel Lutheran Church of Fergus Falls, Minnesota, the Rev. Joseph A. Rangen officiating, with visitation at the Glende Johnson Funeral Home; buried in Oak Grove Cemetery, Fergus Falls, where his wife Olga was buried in 1980. ¶14

Survivors named in the obituary: his wife; four daughters and four sons, one of them 'Rev. Otto Valder of Taipeh, Formosa'; a sister, Mrs. Caroline Aadland of Everett, Washington; and four brothers still living in Norway, named as Elias, Ole, John and Martin. The four brothers match the Borgund and Giske records exactly (Ole born 1895, Elias born 1898, Martin born 1899 and Johan born 1902), and the sister matches his sister Karoline, born 2 Oct 1892. ¶15

Death (Social Security index): the Social Security Death Index records Peter Valder's death in May 1967 at Minneapolis, agreeing with the Minnesota death index and the obituary. ¶32

Corrections 2

Correction: Find a Grave and the obituary give his birthplace as 'Aalesund, Norway'. The Borgund church book and the 1891, 1900 and 1910 censuses all place his birth and his whole childhood at the farm Valderhaug (gnr 183, bnr 29) on the island of Valderøy in rural Borgund parish, which became the separate parish of Giske in 1908. Ålesund was only the nearest town, and the port he sailed from in 1905. ¶20

Correction: the obituary says he emigrated 'alone at 17'. He was 18 - born 13 April 1887 and registered as an emigrant at Ålesund on 6 May 1905. ¶21

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Not him: the 'Peter Martinusen' on the Ellis Island list of the Willehad from Helsingborg, 19 May 1904, is not a passenger but a relative named on the line of a Danish passenger, Paul Petersen, and the date is a year before his 6 May 1905 emigration from Ålesund. ¶35
