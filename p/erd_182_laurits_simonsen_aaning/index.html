About the family

Son Truls (Probable): father of Truls Lauritsen Aaning (see his page), born about 1638. For: (1) Aaland names Truls as his son and successor; (2) the 1645 poll tax puts Lauriz with his wife Kristi on Aaning, matching Aaland's couple; (3) the 1664 vicar's census lists the sole farmer of Aaning as 'Truels Lauridtzsøn' 26, so Truls's patronymic is Lauritsen and he held the farm where Laurits had farmed. ¶3a

Life story

Aaland (bygdebok, 1932) on Aaning bruk nr. 2: Laurits Simonsen Brekke was tenant of 1 laup (a share) of Aaning in 1620-33 and later of the whole farm; his wife was Kristi Lassesdatter Brekke, named in 1646; their son ('S.') Truls held the whole farm from 1664. The birth before about 1600 is inferred from his being a tenant in 1620; Aaland's 'Brekke' byname suggests he came from the neighbouring farm Brekke in Oppstryn (inference). ¶1

1645 poll tax: on 'Aaningh', Oppstryn sogn, Innvik prestegjeld, the Nordfjord poll tax lists Lauriz, Kristi and Brite, in that order, and no other household (p. 5b; Nesie before, Breche after). Lauriz and Kristi are the farmer and his wife of Aaland's account; Brite could be a daughter or a maid (the Nordfjord index gives no positions). ¶2

Court book: at the Stryn ting at Tonning on 26 Feb 1703 (Nordfjord tingbok L0016, fol. 29b-30, DA 30924 v30-31) Lars Larsen Lien sued Lars Olsen Tunold over an exchange deed (mageskiftebrev) of 18 Mar 1668 between Lars Larsen, 'then of Brekke', and Ole Carlsen (Tunold's father), copied and confirmed by sorenskriver Peder Raadt on 22 Nov 1687. It recites that Lars Larsen exchanged half a laup in Brekke, 'which his younger brother Ollef Larsen uses', against ½ laup in Bakkelien (Lien), Ole Carlsen's odel, and that Ole Carlsen was to redeem the rest of the Brekke land 'from Lars Aaning'; later Ole Larsen exchanged 21 skilling-rent back and himself 'redeemed in Brekke from Lars Aaning 1 pund' rent. The court gave the Bakkelien half laup to Lars Larsen's children and heirs as odel, and the 1 laup 18 marks in Brekke to the late Ole Larsen's son Jørgen Olsen 'who is odelsmann to all of it', half of it then used by Jørgen's stepfather (name read as Rasmus Tonnesen, uncertain). Reading: the brothers Lars (Lasse) and Ole Lassesen are the 'Lasse Lassesen 42' and 'Oluf Lassesen 40' farming Brekke in the 1664 vicar's census, so the Brekke odel family of the 1660s were sons of a Lasse. A 'Lars Aaning' held Brekke land in pledge in 1668; Lars is the usual spoken form of Laurits, and Laurits Simonsen of Aaning (Aaland's 'Laurits Simonson Brekke', wife Kristi Lassesdatter Brekke) is the obvious candidate. If so, the pledge fits a Brekke inheritance through Kristi Lassesdatter, which would make Lasse and Ole Lassesen her brothers, and also fits Aaland's 1706 odel suit against Truls Aaning over 3½ pund in Brekke. This is inference only: the deed does not call Lars Aaning Laurits, Simonsen, or Kristi's husband, and Truls already held Aaning from 1664. No confidence change. ¶4

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Records that disagree 1

Against or open: Truls (about 7 in 1645) is not in the poll tax, which in Nordfjord omits young children; no primary record states the father-son link; Aaland is a published secondary source. ¶3b
