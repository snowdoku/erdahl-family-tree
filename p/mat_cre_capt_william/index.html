About the family

Family per the compiled genealogy and Murray's study: by Mary Spencer of Providence (who lived with him as his wife for more than seventeen years but was never married to him) William (about 1767-1844), Elizabeth (Mrs Captain James Irving, died 1844 Murray Harbour), George, Mary 'Polly' (Mrs Thomas Searle) and John (1779-1843, Ohio); by Elizabeth Prince, Job Prince (1785-1867), Joseph Bennett (1788-1868) and Samuel Prince (1790-1860, Indiana). ¶7

Wife named: a power of attorney dated at Three Rivers, PEI, 1 Oct 1806 and recorded in the Suffolk County deeds on 28 July 1809 names 'William Creed of Three Rivers in Prince Edward Island Esquire and Elizabeth Creed otherwise Prince his wife', who appoint James Prince Esq. and David Higgins, mariner, of Boston their attorneys; witness and deponent Job Creed of Three Rivers, farmer; attested before J. B. Palmer and certified by Lt-Gov. J. F. W. DesBarres, Charlottetown, 17 Oct 1806 (FamilySearch 'Suffolk. Deeds 1809', images 529-530, image read). This proves his marriage to Elizabeth Prince, but not by itself his paternity of Joseph Bennett Creed, so his grade is unchanged. His widow acted as 'Elizabeth Creed... Widow' on 26 July 1809. ¶19

Life story

Captain William Creed, merchant, ship-owner and master, of Providence, Rhode Island, and Three Rivers, Prince Edward Island; member of the Island House of Assembly, Justice of the Peace for Kings County (1806) and commissioner of highways. 'Captain' refers to his command and ownership of merchant vessels, not to military rank. ¶1

Bennett kinship: the Blennerhassett Pedigree (a manuscript of about 1733-36, printed verbatim in Hickson, Old Kerry Records, 1872, pp. 55-56) shows that Mary Bennett, wife of William Creed of 'Ballindall' (Ballyneal), Co. Limerick, was the eldest daughter of Samuel Bennett junior of Ballincollow and Anne Collis. Her brothers were George, William and Joseph. Joseph is evidently the Rev. Joseph Bennett (born about 1727-28) who, as 'Joseph Bennett of Windsor, Nova Scotia', mortgaged his share of Ballincollow, 'then in the possession of William Bennett' (his brother), to 'William Creed, then late of Limerick city' in 1765. So the 'uncle Bennett' of the family tradition and the source of the name Joseph Bennett Creed can be named: the Rev. Joseph Bennett, brother of Mary (Bennett) Creed. This raises William Creed of Ballyneal and Mary Bennett to Probable as his parents. His own grade, on his link to Joseph Bennett Creed, is unchanged. ¶20

Rev. Joseph Bennett after Nova Scotia: Eaton (1891) guessed that he died at Windsor soon after 1780. But a lease of 18 May 1787 by 'Rev. Joseph Bennett of Cork city, clerk, and Elizabeth (als Hodder) his wife' covers a dwelling house outside the South Gate of Cork, bounded by the holdings of Mr Bartholomew Bready (Registry of Deeds memorial 259686). The 1765 deed had the same couple, Joseph and Elizabeth, owning houses outside the south gate, one tenanted by Bartholomew Brady. So he evidently returned to Cork, and his wife was Elizabeth Hodder. ¶21

Estate file read: the Court of Probate file for his estate survives as 'C - Admin. no. 20, Estate of William Creed' in the PEI Probate Estate Case Files (FamilySearch DGS 008123338, item 2, images 253-260). It holds three papers. (1) The administration bond, dated 11 September 1809: Joseph Bennet Creed of Three Rivers, farmer, Samuel Nelson of Charlotte Town, merchant, and David Higgins (his name written in over a struck-out name), Gentleman, are bound to Robert Gray, Surrogate, in GBP 500; Joseph Bennet Creed is the administrator of the goods of 'William Creed Esquire, late of Three Rivers... deceased' (image 258-259). (2) The inventory, dated Charlottetown 20 February 1810 and received 28 February 1810, of the 'property left by William Creed Esqr deceased Apl 11th 1809'. It lists 160 acres of leased land with buildings, held on a lease for a thousand years; a town and pasture lot at Georgetown; oxen, cows, 23 sheep, eight lambs and eight hogs; household goods worth about GBP 40; and GBP 17 10s due for his services as a Member of the House of Assembly. It is signed 'J. Bennett Creed, Administrator' (image 256). (3) A debtor and creditor account signed J. Bennett Creed (image 254). ¶22

What the file does NOT say: none of the papers names his widow or children, and there is no renunciation by Elizabeth. So the file does not, in words, make Joseph Bennett Creed his son, and the grade stays Probable on his link to Joseph. The death date of 11 April 1809 in the inventory agrees with the Island sources, against the 18 April of the Limerick Chronicle. That David Higgins, Elizabeth Prince's son by her first husband, stood surety for Joseph fits the 1817 Boston deed in which Job, Joseph Bennett and Samuel Creed call David Higgins 'our half brother', but it is corroboration only. ¶23a

Origin: born between 1739 and 1743, by family tradition at Limerick, Ireland, of English parents, and said to have crossed the Atlantic with an uncle surnamed Bennett (hence his son's name Joseph Bennett). The tradition comes from a descendant's typescript (the Cantelo narrative, PEI Genealogical Society library); R. P. Murray, who studied him in depth, states that the birth date and place have not been confirmed. No Irish record has been found. ¶2

Age of the Rev. Joseph Bennett: he was in the thirty-fourth year of his age when the SPG appointed him in 1761, so born about 1727-28 (A. W. H. Eaton, The Church of England in Nova Scotia and the Tory Clergy of the Revolution, 1891, pp. 72-73, from the SPG records; Eaton says he came from England, but the 1765 deed shows Cork and Limerick property). If he was the 'uncle Bennett' of the tradition, he was a younger brother of William's mother, and an uncle born about 1728 fits the tradition's birth of William in 1739-1743 better than the 'aged 80' (born about 1729) of the 1809 Limerick Chronicle notice. A Mary Bennett who had married William Creed of Ballyneal by 1733 could have had a brother born about 1728. This is consistency only, not proof; the birth range BET 1728 AND 1743 is kept. ¶18

Irish deed, 1765: a deed dated 1 November 1765 and registered in Dublin on 23 June 1766 (Registry of Deeds, book 241, page 551, memorial 160893) records that Joseph Bennett of Windsor, Nova Scotia, and Elizabeth his wife, for GBP 300, granted and sold to 'William Creed, then late of Limerick city' four houses outside the south gate of Cork (tenanted by Patrick Allen, Owen Cudmore and Bartholomew Brady), a house in Cork called the Trap House (tenanted by Daves, shoemaker), and their share of Ballincalloe (Ballincollow), Co. Limerick, then in the possession of William Bennett, with a clause of redemption, so in effect a mortgage for money William Creed lent. The witnesses were William Nesbitt, public notary of Halifax, Nova Scotia, and Patrick Dickson, then of Halifax and later of Ballybrinoge, Co. Limerick, so the deed was executed at Halifax. Source: the Registry of Deeds Index Project abstract (irishdeedsindex.net, memorial 160893, indexed from the memorial); the memorial itself was not read. ¶16

(no grade change). The 1765 mortgage by the Rev. Joseph Bennett of Windsor, Nova Scotia, and his wife Elizabeth to 'William Creed then late of Limerick' (Registry of Deeds memorial 160893, read on the image and in the machine transcript) states no kinship between the parties. The 1771/2 Cork memorial in which James Hubatt Creed mortgages to Edward Creed, witnessed by a Joseph Bennett, also has none. So neither gives a record of the uncle tradition. ¶24

PEI: first on the Island about 1767; harbour master at Three Rivers about 1775; settled there permanently by 1788 with his wife Elizabeth (Prince) Higgins, his daughter Elizabeth and son Job Prince, and an enslaved man, Dembo (Benbo) Sickles, whom he bound by indenture of 1 September 1795 to be freed after seven more years. Petitioned on 18 November 1794 for lots in Georgetown Royalty for himself, his stepsons James and David Higgins and his son Job Prince Creed (granted 27 August 1795). The census of Lot 59 in April 1798 lists William Creed with a household of eight. In 1805 he and his sons Job, Joseph Bennett and Samuel signed a petition to Lieutenant Governor Fanning. ¶4

1774 census: The Rhode Island colony census of 1774 lists 'Creed, Capt' (no given name) at Providence, in a household of six whites: two males above 16, two males under 16, one female above 16 and one female under 16, with no Indians or Blacks (Census of the Inhabitants of the Colony of Rhode Island, 1774, printed 1858, Providence continued, p. 41; page image read). He is the only Creed in the census index. The household fits a man living at Providence with a partner and four children, which agrees with the compiled family account, but the census names no one else. ¶9

Privateering (brig Betsy, 1776): On 6 November 1776 Richard Derby Jr., agent for the Massachusetts state brig Tyrannicide, wrote from Boston to Francis Dana that 'Mr [William] Creed' was about to apply to the Council for delivery of the brig Betsey, which had been acquitted at trial in the Maritime Court at Salem; on 8 November 1776 the Council, noting the Betsy 'claimed by Mr. Creed & others', ordered the State's agent not to prosecute the appeal (Naval Documents of the American Revolution, vol. 7, pp. 61-62, citing Massachusetts Archives vol. 166, p. 23; page images read). The editors' note says the Betsy had been taken by the Tyrannicide; the given name William is the editors' bracketed addition. ¶10

Privateering (letter-of-marque bonds, 1780-1781): The Library of Congress calendar of Continental Congress letter-of-marque bonds names William Creed four times (page images read): as a bonder (surety) with master Jonathan Oakes of Malden and William Shattuck of Boston for the Massachusetts brigantine Patty, 6 guns, bond 28 December 1780, 'William Creed [Boston]' (p. 410); as a bonder, 'William Creed, Salem', with master Jeremiah Hegerty and William Shattuck for the Massachusetts schooner Languedock, 4 guns, owned by John Moriarty and others of Salem, 16 March 1781 (p. 368); as a bonder, 'William Creed, Salem', with master John Cathcart and William Shattuck for the Massachusetts ship Essex, 20 guns, owned by Jonathan Jackson, Joseph Lee and others of Newburyport, 14 April 1781 (p. 283); and as an owner, 'William Creed and others, Providence', of the Rhode Island ship Independence, 16 guns, master Joseph Olney, bond 3 June 1781 (p. 350). Each bond was for $20,000 (Naval Records of the American Revolution 1775-1788, Library of Congress, 1906; originals in Papers of the Continental Congress item 196, vol. 11 p. 61, vol. 9 p. 33, vol. 4 p. 86 and vol. 8 p. 38). ¶11

1798 census of Lot 59 (Island Register transcription): William Creed, with 4 males under 16, 2 males 16-60 and 2 females 16-60, a household of 8. The obituary of his son Job Creed, who died at Sturgeon on 30 October 1867 aged 82, says Job was born in the American colonies and 'emigrated with his father to this Colony at a very early age' (The Islander, 15 November 1867). ¶5

Death: died at Charlottetown on 11 April 1809 while the Assembly was sitting, and was buried on 13 April with the Governor, Council and Assembly attending; the government paid his funeral expenses (GBP 30 8s 6d). The PEI death card index records 'William Jp Creed' died 13 April 1809 - evidently the burial date. Letters of administration on his estate were granted on 12 September 1809 to his son Joseph Bennet Creed (PEI Court of Probate minutes, Robert Gray, Surrogate - cited by Murray, not yet read). ¶6

Irish death notice: the Limerick Chronicle of Wednesday 21 June 1809 reported 'Died at Charlottetown, Prince Edward Island on the 18th April, William Creed, aged 80, a native of County Limerick' (abstract in Noel Murphy's Creed family notes, from Rosemary ffolliott's index of Munster newspaper notices 1750-1820; the abstract read is an OCR text and breaks off after these words). This is the first record found that confirms his Limerick birth, which until now rested on family tradition (the Cantelo typescript). Read. ¶13

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Probable rather than Confirmed as Joseph Bennett Creed's father: the evidence is the 1809 administration granted to Joseph on William's estate, the 1817 Boston deed by Job Prince, Joseph Bennett and Samuel Creed 'by our half brother David Higgins' (Elizabeth Prince's son by her first husband), Samuel's 1802 baptism as son of William and Elizabeth (index read), and the 1805 petition - all but the baptism known only from Murray's quotations. ¶8

Audit 11 (2 Nov 2026): considered for a raise to Confirmed (AUDIT-QUEUE raise candidate) and kept at Probable, graded on his link to Joseph Bennett Creed. Joseph's mother is Confirmed through the 1817 'half brother David Higgins' release. Joseph's father is still inferred: no record names him as William's son. The 1809 bond makes Joseph administrator without stating kinship. Samuel Prince Creed's 1802 baptism (index) names William and Elizabeth as Samuel's parents, not Joseph's. The 1817 deed gives Joseph's relationship only to David Higgins. The marriage date of William Creed and Elizabeth (Prince) Higgins has not been found, so the presumption that her son born about 1788 was her husband's rests on surname and household (1798 census: four boys under 16). His notes name the record that would settle it: the PEI Court of Probate minutes of 11-12 Sep 1809 (PARO RG 6), which may say why Joseph was entitled to administer. That record, or one equally direct (the 1805 petition read on the image, if it calls Joseph his son; a Creed deed or estate paper naming 'my son Joseph Bennett Creed'), is needed before a raise (BRIEF grading rule). ¶25

The case for this identification 2

Identity of the privateering records: These entries are taken to be this William Creed (a linking inference, not a record statement). The calendar indexes a single William Creed for all four bonds; the Salem and Boston bonds are tied to one man by his repeated co-surety William Shattuck of Boston; the Providence ownership of 1781 and the 1774 Providence census entry match his known Providence residence; and the Betsy claim at Salem in 1776 matches the claim described in R. P. Murray's study. None of these records names Job Prince, so the Creed-Prince business partnership rests on Murray's study alone, and none names William Creed's parents or birthplace. ¶12

Why the 1765 deed matters: Joseph Bennett of Windsor is the Rev. Joseph Bennett, missionary of the Society for the Propagation of the Gospel at Fort Edward (Windsor), Horton, Cornwallis, Falmouth and Newport from 1761-63 to 1775 and then itinerant missionary on the Nova Scotia coast (Akins, Sketch of the Rise and Progress of the Church of England in the British North American Provinces; Sketch of the Old Parish Burying Ground of Windsor). His share of Ballincollow ties him to the Bennett family of Ballincollow, Co. Limerick (Samuel Bennett the younger, 1713; William Bennett, gent., 1749-1777, per the same deeds index). The deed therefore puts a William Creed, recently of Limerick city, in Nova Scotia in 1765, lending money to a Limerick-born Bennett there. This fits the family tradition exactly: Captain William was born at Limerick, crossed the Atlantic with an uncle named Bennett, and named his son Joseph Bennett Creed; and he is said to have been first on St John's Island about 1767. It is the first contemporary record of the Bennett connection. It gives no relationship between Creed and Bennett, and identifying this William Creed with the captain rests on the names, place and date, so it is evidence of his Limerick origin and of the Bennett link, not proof of either relationship. Grade unchanged (Probable on his link to Joseph Bennett Creed). ¶17

Corrections 1

Correction: the 1809 Limerick Chronicle notice gives his age as 80, which puts his birth about 1729, ten years earlier than the 1739-1743 of the family tradition. Its day of death (18 April) also differs from the 11 April (burial 13 April) of the Island sources; the Island sources are preferred for the date. The birth date is widened to BET 1728 AND 1743 to cover both, pending a baptism. ¶14

Alternatives considered and set aside 2

Not a Loyalist: during the American Revolution he lived at Providence and Salem, appears in the Rhode Island census of 1774 and military census of 1777, and in 1779-1780 bought captured vessels at Salem and owned or bonded American privateers (the schooner True Blue, the sloops Speedwell, Rebecca and Judith, the ship Mary), in partnership with Captain Job Prince of Boston, whose daughter he married. He does not belong on the PEI Loyalist lists (per Murray's study, which cites the Massachusetts and Rhode Island records). ¶3

Possible parents (Speculative): William Creed of Ballyneal (Ballyneale, in Ballingarry parish), Co. Limerick, and his wife Mary Bennett. Faulkner's Dublin Journal of 15 May 1762 reported the death at Limerick of 'Mrs Mary Creed relict of William Creed late of Ballyneal, Co. Limerick'; she was buried in St John's churchyard, Limerick, on 8 May 1762. The Blennerhassett Pedigrees call her husband William Creed of 'Ballindall' (for Ballyneal) and his wife Mary Bennett, and the Ballingarry register has William and Mary Creed baptising Ann (26 July 1733) and Jane (23 September 1735) (all per Noel Murphy's notes; not read in the originals). Why they are proposed: the family tradition says Captain William was born at Limerick and crossed the Atlantic with an uncle surnamed Bennett, after whom his son Joseph Bennett Creed was named. A Bennett uncle would be a brother of a Bennett mother, and this is the only Creed-Bennett marriage found in Co. Limerick in the right generation. Against or unproved: no record names Captain William as their son, and the pedigree as quoted lists only daughters (Anne, Jane and Sarah by 1733). Other Creed households in Co. Limerick at the time (Uregare, Ballygrennan, Maidstown) also had Williams. A baptism of William son of William Creed at Ballingarry, about 1729-1743, would settle it; the Ballingarry Church of Ireland register survives in part (RCB Library, Dublin). ¶15
