About the family

Children: The same biography says Peter P. Eidem 'was the first born in the family of five children'. Two of the five are identified with confidence from their American gravestones and death records — Peder P. (b. 1858) and Mikal Paulsen (b. 1869) — and a third, John P. (b. 1876), is identified as probable. The remaining two are not yet identified. ¶3

Children: five who lived and were baptised, plus one stillborn daughter buried 6 November 1866. A search of the entire Selbu baptismal index for every child indexed to a father Paul/Poul Ingebrigts- returns exactly five, and a search of the entire Selbu burial index for every entry naming him as paaroerande (next of kin) returns exactly one, the 1866 stillbirth. The sibship is therefore closed: Peder 1858, Anne 1860, Ingebrigt 1863, Mikel 1869, John 1876. That is precisely the 'five children' of the 1904 Minnesota biography, and the two the family had never heard of are ANNE and INGEBRIGT. ¶13

Children, four of the five now identified. The 1904 biography says five children. His widow's 1925 obituary names three surviving sons - Peder and John Eidem of Marietta, Minnesota, and JAMES EIDEM OF GREENBUSH, MINNESOTA - and a fourth son, Mikal Paulsen Eidem, had died at Marietta in March 1923. James was unknown to this project before the obituary was read; he is corroborated by the Lac qui Parle County Press delinquent-tax list of 29 December 1893, which prints 'Eidem, James' beside 'Eidem, Mike'. The fifth child remains unidentified and is most likely a daughter who either stayed in Norway or died before 1925. ¶27

Life story

Origin: Paul Eidem was a farmer at Eidem in SELBU, an inland parish south-east of Trondheim in Sør-Trøndelag, Norway. 'Eidem' is the name of the farm he lived on, carried as a byname in the Norwegian manner; his children used it as a fixed surname in America, and the initial 'P.' that most of them carry is his own given name frozen into a middle initial. ¶1

Baptism: born 31 July 1826 and baptised 13 August 1826 at Selbu church, entered as 'Pauel Ingebrits Olsaedt' in the Selbu parish register 1824-1842, page 20 serial no. 73. Father Ingebrigt Johnsen, mother Anne Paulsdatter, residence Alsaett. He is the only Paul born to an Ingebrigt in Selbu in the whole window 1824-1829, which holds only three Pauls, the other two both Paul Olsen. The index mis-keys his gender as 'k' (female); the name and patronymic show it is a boy. ¶7

Inference, flagged as such: he is almost certainly the husband of the Kari Eidem (1834–1925) buried among his sons at Zoar Lutheran Cemetery in Deuel County, South Dakota. She cannot be his daughter, because a daughter born in 1834 would be twenty-four years older than the stated firstborn. ¶4

Confirmation: confirmed at Selbu church on 13 June 1841 as 'Paul Ingebrigtsen Marstad', boys no. 37, aged 15½, born Selbu, parents 'Ingebr. Johns. Anne Pauls d^r' (Ingebrigt Johnsen and Anne Paulsdatter). Selbu ministerialbok 1824-1842, Confirmerede 1841, pp. 280-281. By 1841 the family was living at Marstad, his father's farm at his marriage in 1824 and Paul's own residence at his marriage in 1858; Paul had been born at Alsaet. The age of 15½ is the clerk's round figure - he was in fact 14 years 10 months. ¶30

Confirmation marks: his entry in the column for knowledge of Christianity and diligence reads 'Ligesaa' (the same), and the chain of dittos runs back to no. 26, 'Maadelig oplyst, men skikkelig' - moderately instructed in the catechism, but well-behaved. The words are attributed to Paul through that ditto chain, not written against his own name, though the attribution is near-certain. The vaccination column (also ditto-marked) gives his smallpox vaccination as 9 September 1832. ¶31

Marriage: married at Selbu church 31 May 1858 Karen (Kari) Pedersdatter of Eidem. Selbu parish register 1843-1859, marriages page 341 serial no. 8. He is entered aged 33, birth year 1825 calculated, residence MARSTAD; she birth year 1835 calculated, residence Eidum. Groom's father: Ingebrigt Johnsen. Bride's father: entered as 'Peder Richardsen', which is a transcription error for Peder Bersvendsen (see Kari's notes). ¶9

Marriage: spring 1858, Selbu. His eldest son Peder Paulsen Eidem was born 4 November 1858, so Peder was the first child of the marriage - which is exactly what the 1904 Compendium biography says ('was the first born in the family of five children'). ¶26

1865 census: Selbu, census district 005 'Femte', rural residence 0065 Braendhaugen, a household of six. His widowed mother-in-law Mali Evensdatter, 70, is the Husmoder and Pladsbrugerske - the woman holding the cottage in her own right - and Paul is entered beneath her as 'hendes Svigersoen Husfader', her son-in-law, head of the house, aged 40. Then Kari Pedersdatter 31, his wife; Peder Paulsen 8; Anne Paulsdatter 6; Ingebrigt Paulsen 3. All six born in Selbu parish. ¶10

1875 census: Selbu, rural residence 0062 'Braendhaugen (plads)', loepenr/bnr 63. By now Mali Evensdatter is dead and the residence record names the holder as 'Farmer: Paul Ingebrigtsen', his own occupation entered as Husmand med Jord (cottager with land). The holding's working figures: seed sown barley 1/4, oats 1/2, potatoes 2 barrels; livestock 1 cow, 1 calf, 6 sheep, 2 goats. One cow, six sheep and a quarter-barrel of barley is the standard size of a Selbu cottager's patch - enough to keep a family alive, not enough to live off. Household of five: Paul 1826, Kari 1834, Peder 1858 (Hjelper Faderen, helps his father), Ingebrigt 1863, Mikael 1869. Daughter Anne, 15, is out in service and John is not yet born. ¶11

Death: He died in Norway in 1881, having never emigrated. Source: the printed biography of his eldest son in the Compendium of History and Biography of Central and Northern Minnesota (1904), p. 469 — 'Paul Eidem, the father of Peter P., was born in Norway, and came of a long line of Norwegian ancestors. He died in his native land in 1881.' ¶2

FOR THE NORWAY AGENT: the next records are the Selbu parish register (his burial in 1881, which gives his age and farm) and the 1865 and 1875 censuses of Selbu. ¶5

Death and burial: died 20 December 1881 aged 55 1/2 and was buried 26 December 1881, the second day of Christmas. Selbu parish register 1878-1891, Doede og begravede, year 1881, page 376, serial no. 31, which gives his birth year as 1826. The parish clerk's duplicate (Selbu 1869-1888, burials page 340 serial no. 65) gives his age as '55 1/2 a', his birth year as 1826 calculated, and his date of death as 19 December - a one-day disagreement between the two copies that I record rather than smooth. The 1904 Compendium of History and Biography of Central and Northern Minnesota said only that he 'died in his native land in 1881'; the register now dates it to the day. ¶12

Context: a husmann med jord held a patch of land from a farm in return for a fixed number of labour days on it. He was below a leilending (tenant farmer paying rent) and far below a gaardbruger og selveier (owner-occupier freeholder). The holding was called a plads or husmannsplass and normally carried the farm's name plus a nickname - here Braendhaugen, 'the burnt knoll', under Eidem. Three of his five children emigrated to America; he himself never left Selbu. ¶14

Printed source: the family is written up in the Selbu farm history, Selbu i fortid og naatid, bind 4 (1992), in the chapter BRENNHAUGEN, pages 205-207 - Brennhaugen being the 1992 spelling of the Braendhaugen of the 1865 and 1875 censuses, the husmannsplass (cottager's holding) under the farm Eidem on which the church books place this family. Page 206 carries the given names Paul, Kari, Peder, Anne, Ingebrigt, Mikal and the years 1826 (his own birth year), 1834 (his wife's), 1858, 1860, 1863 and 1869 (four of his five children's), with the patronymic Palsen and the words Amerika and Minnesota; page 207 carries John, Oline, Slind, 1876, 1881, 1901 and Palmer Gilbert. Fifteen independent agreements with the family proved from the parish registers, on two facing pages of the chapter for the one holding in Selbu where that family lived. ¶15

Context - how this page citation was obtained without reading the page: every volume of the Selbu bygdebok is access-restricted to Norwegian IP addresses on nb.no, so neither the page images nor the OCR text can be downloaded from the United States. The National Library's IIIF Content Search service, however, is open to anyone and returns, for any quoted phrase, the exact page and the pixel box of every occurrence, with the surrounding context blanked. A page's contents can therefore be profiled by asking it a long series of yes/no questions - one phrase search per name, per year, per place - and the printed folio number is itself OCR'd in the bottom margin, which fixes the page number exactly. Nothing below is read off a page image; every item is a retrieved search hit. Confidence is accordingly Probable, never Confirmed. ¶17

On the Eidem farm name and what it does and does not mean here: he was a husmann med jord at Braendhaugen, a cottage under Yster-Eidem, and he took the farm's name as a byname in the ordinary Norwegian manner. That name is the only thing he is so far shown to share with Berith Gundersdatter Eidem, who married into the Slind family in 1841 and stands in this tree on the other side of it. She belonged to the freeholders of the same farm; he held a patch of its land in return for labour days. See the reconciliation note on her record. ¶18

IDENTIFIED IN NORWAY, 12 Oct 2026, by the slind-deep agent while testing whether Berith Gundersdatter Eidem belonged to this family. He is PAUL INGEBRIGTSEN, born about 1825 in Selbu, a husmann (cottager) on the Eidem farm. The chain, every link a record read: (1) the Selbu marriage register, page 341 serial 8, records the wedding on 31 MAY 1858 of 'Paul Ingebrigtsen', 33, of MARSTAD, to 'Karen Pedersdatt:', born 1835, of EIDUM, and names the groom's father as INGEBRIGT JOHNSEN and the bride's father as PEDER RICHARDSEN. (2) The 1865 census of Selbu, census district 005, rural residence 0065 BRAENDHAUGEN, household 1, has 'Mali Evensdatter, 70, Husmoder, e, Pladsbrugerske' (widow, holder of a cottar's plot), 'Paul Ingebrigtsen, 40, HENDES SVIGERSON Husfader, g' - HER SON-IN-LAW, head of household - 'Kari Pedersdatter, 31, hans Kone', and their children Peder Paulsen 8, Anne Paulsdatter 6 and Ingebrigt Paulsen 3. (3) The Selbu baptismal registers record FIVE children to Paul/Poul Ingebrigtsen and Kari/Karen Pedersdatter, the mother's residence given as Eidum or Eidem: Peder, baptised 27 Dec 1858; Anne, 27 Dec 1860 (p. 11 no. 161); Ingebrigt, 8 Nov 1863 (p. 48 no. 133); MICHAEL, 29 Mar 1869 (p. 109 no. 13); and JOHN, 29 Oct 1876 (p. 26 no. 122), the father then aged 50. (4) The Selbu burial register records a 'Paul Ingebrigts, b. 1826', buried 26 DECEMBER 1881. ¶19

Three independent things this settles. FIVE CHILDREN - exactly the number the 1904 Compendium biography of his eldest son gives ('Peter P. Eidem was the first born in the family of five children'), and they are Peder 1858, Anne 1860, Ingebrigt 1863, Michael 1869 and John 1876. DEATH IN 1881 - the same biography's 'He died in his native land in 1881', here matched by a burial on 26 December 1881. And MIKAL born 1869 and JOHN born 1876, the two sons already in this tree, appear in the register as Michael and John, baptised in 1869 and 1876, to this couple at Eidem. ¶20

What this gives the next researcher to create: his father INGEBRIGT JOHNSEN, born about the 1790s; his wife's father PEDER RICHARDSEN of Eidem, an unusual patronymic that should be easy to trace; his wife's mother MALI EVENSDATTER, born about 1795, the widowed pladsbrugerske of Braendhaugen in 1865; and two children unknown to this tree, ANNE PAULSDATTER born 1860 and INGEBRIGT PAULSEN born 1863. None of them is entered here, because Paul Eidem's family is another agent's territory and duplicate people are worse than missing ones. ¶22

Name: the 1925 obituary prints him as 'Paul I. Eidem'. The middle initial is new and is most likely a patronymic - a Paul Ingebrigtsen, Iversen, Jonsen or similar of the farm Eidem in Selbu. Recorded as given. ¶25

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence raised from Probable to Confirmed, and the reason: his widow's obituary names him. Minneapolis Tidende, 5 November 1925, p. 7, reporting the death of Mrs. Kari P. Eidem at Marietta, Minnesota, says she was 'Gift med Paul I. Eidem Vaaren 1858. Hendes Mand doede i 1881' - married to Paul I. Eidem in the spring of 1858; her husband died in 1881. Until now his marriage to Kari rested on an inference from her burial among his sons, and his 1881 death on the single printed biography of his eldest son in 1904. Both are now stated by the family's own community newspaper, in his widow's death notice, three weeks after she died and forty-four years after he did. ¶24

The case for this identification 2

Identity: the 'Paul Eidem' of the 1904 printed biography of his son Peter P. Eidem is PAUL INGEBRIGTSEN, husmann med jord (cottager with land) at the holding Braendhaugen under the farm Eidem in Selbu. Eidem is a farm name, not a family name; nobody in Norway used it as his surname, and the Norwegian records call him Paul (or Poul) Ingebrigtsen throughout. His children took Paulsen Eidem in America. His tree name has been changed from 'Paul /Eidem/' to 'Paul /Ingebrigtsen/' to match the records; he is the same man. ¶6

Why the family is called EIDEM and he was not born an Eidem. He was 'Paul Ingebrigtsen of MARSTAD' when he married in 1858; his father was Ingebrigt Johnsen; and he came onto the Eidem farm by marrying Kari Pedersdatter, who WAS born there. His holding was BRAENDHAUGEN, a husmannsplass - a cottager's plot carved out of the Eidem farm - which is why the censuses of 1865 and 1875 put him at Braendhaugen while every church-book entry gives his residence as Eidum or Eidem and the 1866 burial register calls him 'Poul Ingebrigtsen, Eidem, Hsmd.' The farm name his children carried to Minnesota came to them through their mother, not their father. ¶21

Corrections 3

Correction: the 1881 burial index labels 'Eidem' as Paul's BIRTH place. That is his residence, the Braendhaugen cottage under Eidem into which he married in 1858. He was born at Alsaet (written Alsaett, Aldsaedt, Alsaeth or Olsaedt), his mother's farm, and he was living at Marstad, his father's farm, when he married. A researcher who believes Paul was born at Eidem will hunt for his father among the Eidem households and never find him. ¶8

Correction: the death date was carried as '1881' only. The Selbu burial register 1878-1891, 1881, p. 376 no. 31 (re-read 13 Oct 2026), gives date of death 20 December and burial 26 December 1881. The clerk's duplicate (1869-1888, p. 340 no. 65) says 19 December; the minister's 20 December is preferred, as in the Death and burial note. ¶28

Correction: the Paul x Kari marriage date was carried as 'ABT 1858'. The Selbu marriage register 1843-1859, p. 341 no. 8 (re-read 13 Oct 2026), gives the marriage date as 31 May 1858. The bride's father there, 'Peder Richardsen', is an index misreading of Peder Bersvendsen; see Kari's notes. ¶29

Open questions and the next record to read 1

Open question for whoever reads the page: page 206 carries the string '1826-1867' as well as a separate '1826'. Volume 4 writes holder tenures as year-ranges - 1792-1825, 1860-1886, 1836-1880 and 1881-1884 all appear on these same three pages - so '1826-1867' is most probably a tenure. But if the book means that Paul Ingebrigtsen lived 1826-1867, the book is wrong, and demonstrably so: the 1875 census names him as the living holder of Braendhaugen, and the 1876 baptism of his son John gives the father as 'Poul Ingebrigtsen, b. 1826, age 50'. Recorded as a flag, not as a correction, because the page cannot be read from outside Norway. ¶16
