About the family

David Erdahl's maternal great-great-great-grandfather on the Eidem-of-Union-County side, the father of Sivert 'Severt' S. Eidem and so the grandfather of Mayme O. Eidem. Reached 11 Oct 2026. ¶1

Eight children were baptised to him and Brynhild Johnsdatter in Selbu between 1842 and 1859 and exactly five were alive at the 1865 census; the other three - a Sigrie baptised 1844, a John baptised 1847 and a John baptised 1854 - died in infancy and their names were re-used, which is why there is a Sigri in both 1844 and 1849 and a John in 1842, 1847 and 1854. ¶22

His mother is now named: SIGRI BERSVENDSDATTER, born about 1784, living with him in 1865 at eighty-one on a foderaad pension. Since his own patronymic is Jonsen, her husband was a JON of ostre Eidem who had died before 1865. ¶27

Life story

Baptism: born 20 AUGUST 1815 and baptised on the 14th Sunday after Trinity (27 August) 1815 - Selbu parish register 1801-1815, births and baptisms, PAGE 221, SERIAL NO. 2. Read from the scanned page: 'Et Drengebarn kaldet Sivert foedt d. 20de August. Foraeldrene John Siverts: Ejdem og Hustrue Siri Bersvends d:' - so both his parents are named outright, and his mother's patronymic here is the same one the 1865 census gives her fifty years later. ¶10

Godparents (faddere), read from the same scanned page and not carried by any index: OLE SIVERTSEN EJDEM, THOMAS HALVORSEN GARMDAL, PEDER BERSVENDSEN DRAXEN, GURI ANDERSDATTER EJDEM and INGEBOR SIVERTSDATTER [farm abbreviated]. Three of the five are close kin: Ole Sivertsen and Ingebor Sivertsdatter are his father's own brother and sister, both of them children of Sivert Jonssen in the 1801 census of Eidum, and Peder Bersvendsen carries his mother's patronymic and is almost certainly her brother. Selbu godparents were chosen from the two families, and this set confirms the reconstruction of both. ¶11

1825 census: at rural residence 0059* Eidem, Selbu, aged 10, 'Soen' in the household of 'Joh Siverts.' (41, Gdm. = gaardmand, farmer) and 'Sigrid Bersvendsd.' (39, Hustru). His brothers and sisters in the house are Helge Johnsdatter 13, Anders Johnsen 5 and a half, and Marit Johnsdatter 2, with his father's unmarried sister Ingebor Sivertsdatter, 33, as servant. Three more households on the same farm belonged to his father's brothers. ¶15

Marriage: married in Selbu church on 8 NOV 1841 to Brynnild Johnsdatter Langlie. The entry (Selbu parish register, Selbu local parish 1824-1842, marriages, page 339, serial no. 30) enters him as 'Ungk. Sivert Johnsen Eidem', aged 26, birthplace 'Saelboe', and names the groom's father as JOHN SIVERTSEN EIDEM and the bride's father as JOHN NIELSEN LANGLIE. Banns were read on 17, 24 and 31 October 1841. He had been vaccinated on 26 April 1819 and his bride on 3 March 1822. ¶13

The marriage witnesses (forlovere), which no index carries and which I read off the scanned page: OLE THOMASSEN EIDEM and JOHN NIELSEN LANGLIE - the second of the two being the bride's own father. ¶14

Purchase of Eidem, 1854: on 23 February 1854, at the Selbu ting at Sjøvold, his widowed mother Sigrid Bersvendsdatter, his brother Anders Johnsen Eidem, and his sisters' husbands Ole Ingebrigtsen (for Helge) and Erik Torstensen Uglem (for Marit) deeded the farm Eidem (matr. no. 51, løpenr 62) to him as 'Afdødes ældste Søn', the deceased John Sivertsen's eldest son. The price was 450 speciedaler, paid by taking over the farm's 1841 mortgage to Throndhjems Sparebank (370 Spd still owing), a new 70-Spd bond to Sivert Monsen, and 10 Spd in cash. He also took on his mother's retirement maintenance (føderåd). ¶39

Kårbrev, 1854: on the same day he signed a kårbrev (retirement contract) granting 'min Moder Enken Sigrid Bersvendsdatter' lifelong free maintenance from the farm. It gave her a room in the kår house, firewood, a horse for church, care in sickness and old age, fodder for a cow, potatoes, milk, one speciedaler in cash every March and a garden plot. ¶40

1865 census: at rural residence 0058 'oestre Eidem', census district 005 'Femte', loepenummer 62, Selbu, aged 51, Husfader (head of household), married, occupation 'Gaardbruger og Selveier' = working farmer and FREEHOLDER - he owned his land outright. The holding sowed half a barrel of barley, three-eighths of mixed grain, three of oats and four of potatoes, and carried 2 horses, 8 cows, 13 sheep and 4 goats; nine people lived on it in one building. He is the first freeholder found on this maternal line: the Slinds of Slind nordre were leilendinger (tenants) and Paul Eidem's family at Braendhaugen were husmenn (cottagers). ¶16

The farm: loepenummer 62 in 1865 and 1875, and gnr 56 bnr 2 - 'Eidem oestre soendre' - by the 1900 census. The cottage holding Luddumoen was a plass under it. Eidem in Selbu is not one farm but a cluster of five farm numbers, gnr 56 (Eidem oestre), 57 (Eidemsbrennan), 58 (Oestigaard and Eidemsnes), 59 (Eidem oevre) and 60 (Eidem nedre), with a dozen cottages between them. ¶17

The 1865 census ages for this whole household run about one year high - he is entered as 51 against an August 1815 birth, his son John as 24 against a January 1842 birth, his son Sivert as 7 against an August 1859 birth. This is one enumerator's habit of recording the age at next birthday, not five separate errors. Use the register dates in preference to the census ages. ¶18

Death: he did NOT die in Selbu. There is no burial entry for a Sivert Johnsen of Eidem anywhere in the Selbu parish registers between 1866 and 1880. A search of the 1872 burials returns four records which are the duplicate entries of only two men, both named Sivert Mikkelsen of Velve (buried 10 March and 25 August 1872); year-by-year searches of 1866-1873 and a 1866-1880 sweep on the name Sivert Johnsen return nothing for Eidem. A selveier who died on his own farm would be in that book, and the register is complete and indexed for those years. Taken with the family's complete absence from the 1875 census of Selbu and the passing of loepenummer 62 into unrelated hands by 1875, this establishes that he emigrated with his family and died in America on 15 April 1872, and that the Elk Point stone marks a real interment and not a cenotaph. ¶19

Emigration: the crossing falls between 3 APRIL 1866, when his eldest son was married in Selbu church, and 30 APRIL 1867, when the Trondheim police emigrant protocols open - the one season for which no Norwegian emigration list exists. The family is in none of the protocols: searched by his own name and birth year nationwide 1866-1873 (two hits in all Norway, neither his), by domicile Selbo for every emigrant born 1813-1820, by his wife's name and years, by the children's names and years, and by reading all 269 Selbu emigrants of 1866-1869 one by one. No emigration record of any kind survives from Selbu before 1867, and the Selbu parish out-migration lists are not transcribed. ¶20

No Norwegian probate exists or should exist: he died in Dakota Territory. The Norwegian record that would date the departure to the month is the DEED OF SALE of gnr 56 bnr 2, in the panteregister of the Strinda og Selbu sorenskriveri, which is scanned but not transcribed. ¶21

Every name and age in that household matches what this tree already held from the gravestones in Saint Paul Cemetery at Elk Point, Union County, South Dakota: a father Sivert born 1815, a wife Brynhild born 1817-18, and children John 1842, Sigri 1849, Marit 1851, Ole 1857 and Sivert 1859. Five children of the right names in the right years, on a farm called Eidem in Selbu, in one household. The Find a Grave family links that this record formerly rested on are now corroborated by a primary census. ¶25

The farm is identified: OSTRE EIDEM, east Eidem, in Selbu, and he OWNED it - 'Gaardbruger og Selveier', farmer and freeholder, not a tenant. He emigrated from a freehold. ¶26

The farm: his son's obituary places the family at a 'gaard or tract of land called [E]idem' in 'Selbo in old Norway by the side of one of its numerous lakes'. That is ostre Eidem on Selbusjoen. ¶31

Emigration, April-June 1866, with the whole household, by the Quebec route: sold the farm, sailed from Trondheim, landed at Quebec, rail to Chicago and on to the end of track in Iowa, ox team to Sioux City, ferry over the Big Sioux into Dakota Territory, and up Brule Creek to the land the family then held for three generations. ¶32

Sale of Eidem, 1866 (dating the emigration): as 'Sivert Johnsen østre Eidem den Ældre' (the elder) he sold østre Eidem (matr. 51, løpenr 62) to Sivert Johnsen østre Eidem 'den Yngre' (the younger) for 560 speciedaler. The buyer took over the 1841 Sparebank mortgage (now 360 Spd) and the mother's 1854 kår. The deed was signed at Trondheim on 28 February 1866 and read at the Selbu ting on 3 March 1866. The text appears to fix the hand-over for the coming 14 April (1866), although that reading is uncertain. This puts the family's departure after mid-April and before their arrival at Quebec on 9 June 1866. The buyer is a different man of the same name, not his son. ¶41

1870 census: Brule Creek, Union County, Dakota Territory, enumerated 23 August 1870 (post office Elk Point), page 10, lines 20-25, printed numbers 89 and 84 in the dwelling and family columns: 'Johnson, Seivert', 55, male, white, farmer (ditto from the line above), real estate $600, personal estate $100; 'Emma', 52, keeping house; 'Sarah', 21, at home; 'Mary', 19; 'Ole', 14; 'Seivert', 11. This is the 1865 Selbu household of oestre Eidem name for name and in the same order - Sivert 51, Brynild 48, Sigri 17, Marit 15, Ole 10, Sivert 7 - less the married eldest son John, with every age moved on by about five years. 'Sarah' is the usual American rendering of Sigri and 'Mary' of Marit. 'Emma' is the enumerator's rendering of Brynhild, a name an English-speaking census-taker had no spelling for; nothing else in the household or the neighbourhood suggests a second wife, and Brynhild lived until 1890. The father's age is written cramped and has also been read 53; 55 fits his baptism of August 1815 exactly. The birthplace column is a chain of dittos running up the page from the household above and was not resolved, so Norway is not read from this entry. This is the first record of the family in Dakota: they had arrived by August 1870, four years after the 1866 sailing, and he had land worth $600. ¶35

1870 census, neighbours: the household entered just above his (family 88 by the margin numbers) is Erick Johnson, 60, farmer, with Annie 61, Johanna 16 and Hemmer 22. Nothing links them to this family by name or age, and they are not his parents. No second Eidem household (his son John Sivertsen and wife) was found on Brule Creek pages 1-13. ¶36

He died in 1872, four years after the Dakota Territory's Union County was settled by Norwegians, and his widow Brynhild survived him by eighteen years. Their family's churchyard, Saint Paul Lutheran at rural Elk Point, is described in a 2013 family obituary as 'her family's burial ground for three generations and 150 years' - which dates the settlement to about 1863-1870 and makes this man the immigrant. ¶3

Mayme (Eidem) Eidem's paternal grandfather, named as the father of Severt S. Eidem on the latter's Find a Grave memorial. The Norwegian birth place is inferred from his son Severt's recorded birth place, the farm Eidem in Selbu, and from the patronymic Johnson borne by him and used as the middle initial by his son; it has not yet been read in a parish register. Rated Probable for that reason. ¶5

He died in 1872, the year after the Elk Point Norwegian colony's cemetery came into use, and is the earliest Eidem buried there apart from Thomas Olsen Eidem (1813-1889) and Haldor Tomassen Eidem (1830-1874). His sons took the patronymic Sivertson: John Sivertson Eidem (1842-1881), Ole Sivertson Eidem (1857-1948) and Severt S. (1859-1940). ¶6

Context - how this page citation was obtained without reading the page: every volume of the Selbu bygdebok is access-restricted to Norwegian IP addresses on nb.no, so neither the page images nor the OCR text can be downloaded from the United States. The National Library's IIIF Content Search service, however, is open to anyone and returns, for any quoted phrase, the exact page and the pixel box of every occurrence, with the surrounding context blanked. A page's contents can therefore be profiled by asking it a long series of yes/no questions - one phrase search per name, per year, per place - and the printed folio number is itself OCR'd in the bottom margin, which fixes the page number exactly. Nothing below is read off a page image; every item is a retrieved search hit. Confidence is accordingly Probable, never Confirmed. ¶9

Cemetery register, probable match only: the DT58 register of Union County lists SIVERT JOHNSON, 1872 (year only), SOUTH ST PAULS LUTHERAN cemetery, lot 25, block 1. The year, the county and the form 'Johnson' for the patronymic Johnsen fit him, and no Eidem is entered for 1872. It is not proved: the register gives no surname Eidem and no day, and the lot is not one of the Eidem lots (7, 23 and 27 of block 1). The stone in lot 25 would settle it. ¶37

Probate: 'Sønnen Severt Johnsen, 16 1/2 Aar' (the son Severt Johnsen, 16 1/2 years old) is an heir in the skifte after his father John Sivertsen Eidum, closed 8 November 1832 (skifteutlodningsprotokoll 1, fol. 129b-130). The age agrees with his birth on 20 August 1815 when counted from the registration of 8 March 1832. His guardian was his paternal uncle John Sivertsen Eidem. ¶38

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 4

Rated Probable, not Confirmed: the link to his son rests on Find a Grave's family links together with the dates cut on the Saint Paul Cemetery stones at Elk Point, which the house rules treat as a lead rather than evidence. His son's own birthplace, Selbu kommune, Sor-Trondelag, is however given on that son's memorial and is consistent. ¶2

Confidence raised from Probable to Confirmed: his own baptism names both his parents, the 1841 marriage entry names his father and his wife's father, and the 1865 census household names his wife, his five surviving children and his widowed mother. The American sibship from Find a Grave agrees with the 1865 household name for name and order for order. ¶23

FOUND IN NORWAY, 12 Oct 2026, by the slind-deep agent, and the confidence is raised from Probable to Confirmed. The 1865 census of Selbu, census district 005 'Femte', rural residence 0058 OSTRE EIDEM - EAST EIDEM - household 1, in full: 'Sivert Jonsen, 51, Husfader, g, GAARDBRUGER OG SELVEIER' (farmer and freeholder); 'Brynild Jonsdatter, 48, hans Kone'; 'John Sivertsen, 24, Son, ug, Faderens Hjaelp' (helps his father); 'Ole Sivertsen, 10, Son'; 'SIVERT SIVERTSEN, 7, Son'; 'Sigri Sivertsdatter, 17, Deres Datter'; 'Marit Sivertsdatter, 15, Deres Datter'; 'SIGRI BERSVENDSDATTER, 81, HUSFADERENS MODER, e, Foderaadslem' - THE HOUSEHOLDER'S MOTHER, a widow living on a foderaad, the pension in kind reserved out of a farm for its retired holders; and 'Ingeborg Sivertsdatter, 74, ug, Foderaadslem'. ¶24

Confidence raised from Probable to Confirmed. His son's obituary names him in full and twice, and makes him the head of the emigrating party: Leader-Courier (Elk Point, South Dakota), 1 August 1940 - 'His parents were SIVERT JOHNSON EIDEM and BRYN[H]ILD JOHNSDATTER EIDEM. They were honest hardworking people... Among these were Sivert Johnson Eidem and family. He sold his little home and made preparations for this journey.' Until now the link rested on a Find a Grave family listing, which the house rules treat as a lead; this is a narrative statement by his grandchildren at his son's graveside. ¶30

Notes on method and coordination

Note for whoever owns this branch: this family is NOT related to Paul Eidem of Braendhaugen, the other Selbu Eidem in this tree, nor to Gunder Johnsen Eidem, the third. The 1865 census shows three separate Eidem households on the farm - ostre Eidem (Sivert Jonsen, freeholder), Oppigaard (Thomas Olsen, from Stjordal) and Negaard (the district physician Dr Fredrik Erik Holler, with the cottagers Ole Haldorsen and Gunder Gundersen) - plus the cottar's plot Braendhaugen. Eidem is a place, and in this tree it is three unconnected families. ¶28
