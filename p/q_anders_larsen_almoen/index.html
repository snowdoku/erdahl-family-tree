Life story

Sætran searched for his father Lars, with no result. The probate of Einer Jensen and Anne Hågensdatter of Sætran, 11 Nov 1754 (24753 fol. 542a), names only their own children: Haagen 30, Jens, Jon 22, Anders 20, Lasse 16, Hans, Anne and Berli. There is no Lars of the right generation. The Hegra and Stjørdal burial index has no adult Lars at Sætran 1755-1800, only the infant Lars Haagensen (1763). Either his father Lars died before 1755, or 'Setran' in 1761 only names the farm where Anders served. ¶10

Betrothal and marriage found: Stjørdal ministerialbok 1755-1780 (Digitalarkivet 16371), marriage section, canvas 330, left page (p. 328, year heading 1761), 4th entry. On the same day and place as the entry before it (16 Feb 1761, Værnes), these two were betrothed: 'ungkarl og [Rekrut?] Soldat af Hr. Major Sommerschildts Compagnie Anders Larsen Setran', who had the military session's permission to marry 'Pigen Anne Jonsdatter Elmoen' (the maid Anne Jonsdatter of Almoen), and who 'haver bygslet den halve part i gaarden Elmoen' (had taken the lease of half of the farm Almoen). Sureties: Jon Hukkelberg[?] and Erich Fossum[?]. Margin: married at Hegre 9 April [1761]. This gives the marriage date (the tree had only 'before 1764'). It also shows that Anders, a bachelor soldier, came to Almoen by marrying a girl of the farm and taking over half its lease; the infant Lars Andersen of Almoen buried in 1761 was their first child. His byname reads clearly 'Setran' (the initial S is the same letter form as in 'Sessionens' on the next word), which is the Hegra farm Sætran (spelled Setran and Sætran in the 1780s registers; 'Sætran, Hegra' in the Stjør- og Verdal probate registers). So he probably came from Sætran and his father was a Lars there, but that is a lead, not a proof. Sætran held several households; a Guttorm Larsen of Setran, a widower, remarried in 1791 and may have been a brother (unproved). Images:. ¶9

He was married by 1760 at the latest: an infant Lars Andersen of Almoen was buried in 1761, and the name was reused for the son baptized 30 Sep 1764. The first son in a Trøndelag family almost always carries the paternal grandfather's name, so his father was in all likelihood a Lars. A 'Lars of Almoen' stood witness at his son Thomas's baptism in 1770, which fits a grandfather still living on the farm, although the register of that period gives only first names and farm, so the identification cannot be pushed further from this source. ¶2

Thirteen baptisms are recorded at Almoen with 'Anders' as father between 1761 and 1799. The nine from the first marriage, to Ane Jonsdatter, run from 1761 to 1781; the four from the second, to Marit Sørensdatter, run 1793 to 1799 (Anne 1793, Søren 1795 who died the same year, Marit 1796 and Martha 1799). ¶3

Farmer (gårdmann) at Almoen. Children baptized as 'Anders, Almoen': Lars 1764, (Joen ~1768), Thomas 1770, and others in 1766-1781. As a widower he remarried in 1792 to Marie/Marit Sørensdatter. 1801 census: 67, in his 2nd marriage, with young children Anne, Marit and Martha. His first son Lars suggests his father was named Lars. ¶1

Remarried 9 July 1792 as a widower of Almoen to Marie Sørensdatter; the forlovere were Eric Frigaard and Hans Boraasen. ¶4

In the 1801 census he is the 'Huusbonde', 67, 'Bonde og gaardbeboer' - a farmer occupying his own farm, not a tenant - with his second wife Marit Sørensdatter, 36, three young daughters, three servants, and two cottager households of his own grown sons (Thomas and Lars) sitting on the farm's land. He died six years later and was buried 27 Dec 1807. ¶5

The standing of this family is worth noticing: Anders himself farmed Almoen outright, but his sons were only husmenn med jord on it, and his grandson Ole and great-grandson Thomas slid further down to the Beitland crofts. The descent from farmer to cottager over three generations is the ordinary Norwegian story behind the emigration of the 1860s. ¶6

Burial (per index, Hegre parish p. 318): buried 27 Dec 1807, 'Anders Larsen', gaardmand (farmer), of Almoen, aged 74, so born about 1733. ¶7
