About the family

Daughter of Benjamin Hayter and Lydia Hicken. In her father's household at Gaspereaux in 1911 (born January 1904, Baptist). In the 1921 census she was a servant at Sturgeon, Kings County, PEI, aged about 17. ¶1

Husband: Jacob 'Jake' Isaak Sr., son of Peter Jacob Isaak (1873-1938), one of a family of twelve children of a Russian-Mennonite family settled at Aberdeen, Idaho (Find a Grave family links). ¶6

Children: four, named on the back of the family headstone as Jake Jr., Ronald, Gloria and Phyllis. Jacob Lyle Isaak (26 July 1927 - 3 April 2004) and Ronald Rex Isaak (7 April 1930 - 16 December 1989) are buried in Homestead Cemetery; the two daughters are not recorded here. Her eldest son's birth in July 1927 shows she had married by about 1926, within a year of entering the United States in December 1925. ¶7

Life story

Proof of identity (wave 6): the U.S. Social Security Applications and Claims Index, compiled from her own application, has Rosebud Isaak born 29 January 1904 at 'Gasperaux PR, Canada' (Gaspereaux, PEI), died 30 July 1988, and - tested through the index's exact-match filters, since the full entry is behind a login - names her father as Benjamin Hayter and her mother as Lydia Hicken; control names (Benjamin Smith, Lydia Smith) do not match. Rosebud Isaak of Aberdeen, Idaho, is therefore proved to be Benjamin and Lydia's daughter, and her Idaho death and burial are now confirmed as hers. ¶8

Emigration: crossed from Canada into the United States in December 1925 (US border-crossing index: Rosebud Hayter, born about 1904 at Gaspereaux, at Sweet Grass, Montana, 29 December 1925; and Rose Hayter, born about 1903 at Gaspereaux, at Seattle, 24 December 1925). ¶2

Marriage: the Western States Marriage Index (compiled at BYU-Idaho from county registers) records Rosebud Hayter's marriage to Jacob Isaak on 4 January 1926 in Power County, Idaho. She had crossed the border at Sweet Grass, Montana, on 29 December 1925, so she travelled from Canada to Idaho to be married six days later. ¶10

1950 census: in Aberdeen, Bingham County, Idaho (enumeration district 6-3, sheet 10, enumerated 7 April 1950), Rosebud Isaak, 46, born Canada and a naturalized U.S. citizen, kept house for her husband Jacob, 48, born Russia and naturalized, a dragline operator and foreman for the canal company. Their children at home were Jake Jr. (22), a general worker at the grain elevator; Ronald R. (20), a labourer; and two daughters aged 17 and 16, all born in Idaho. These are the four children named on the family headstone. In 1940 the census index has Rose Isaak, born Canada about 1904, wife of Jacob, at Aberdeen. ¶9

Marriage and death: the Island Register says she married ___ Isaacs in Idaho and died about 1990 at Aberdeen, Idaho. The Find a Grave index has Rosebud Isaak, born 29 January 1904, died 30 July 1988, buried at Aberdeen, Bingham County, Idaho - the same birth month as the 1911 census and the same town as her brother Glen's infant son's grave. Identification Probable on name, birth date and place; her husband's name (Isaak/Isaacs) needs an Idaho record. ¶3

Marriage and grave: she is buried as Rosebud Hayter Isaak in Homestead Cemetery, Aberdeen, Idaho, beside her husband Jacob 'Jake' Isaak Sr. (20 April 1901 - 16 November 1989). Their double headstone, read from the Find a Grave photograph, names 'JAKE SR' and 'ROSEBUD H', her death date ending '30 1988'; the reverse reads 'ISAAK / OUR CHILDREN / JAKE JR. RONALD / GLORIA PHYLLIS'. The memorial's creator gives her maiden name as Hayter and her dates as 29 January 1904 - 30 July 1988. ¶4

The case for this identification 1

Identification (wave 6): the Isaak memorial is now tied to Benjamin and Lydia's daughter by four independent points - the birth date 29 January 1904 agrees with the 1911 census entry 'Rosebud, daughter, January 1904'; the middle initial H on the stone and the maiden name Hayter on the memorial; a son named Ronald Rex Isaak, born at American Falls on 7 April 1930, a year after her brother Rex was killed; and the place, the American Falls - Aberdeen district where her brother Glenn farmed and buried his infant son in 1930. Rosebud is a rare given name. No primary record naming her parents has yet been read, so the link stays rated as before but is now very strong. ¶5
