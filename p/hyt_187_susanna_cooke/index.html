Life story

Susanna (als Cooke), wife of Samuel Bennett senior of Coss, Co. Limerick, in 1709. She joined him as a first party to the marriage settlement of his son and heir apparent Samuel Bennett junior with Anne Collis, 13 Feb 1708/9 (Registry of Deeds memorial 420). ¶1

Memorial 420 read in full on the image (FamilySearch signed in, 2 Nov 2026; Registry of Deeds book 2 p. 215, registered 2 May 1709). Lease and release of 13 and 14 February 1708[/9]. The release is made between 'Samuell Bennett Sen[io]r of Cos[s] in the County of Limerick Gent and Susanna his Wife of the First part', 'the said William Collis' [of Liseaige, Co. Kerry, gent.] 'and Francis Bennet' [of Ballykendon, Co. Cork, gent.] 'of the second part', and 'Samuel Bennett Jun[io]r Son and Heir Apparent of the said Samuell Bennett Sen[io]r and Anne Collis Eldest Daughter of the said William Collis of the Third part'. It recites that 'a Marriage is soon to be had and solemnized' between Samuel junior and Anne, and that William Collis paid a portion of £300. The lands are Castlereagh (121 acres), 50 acres in Curroughrosty (parish of Galbally), 96 acres in Cloghasty and 7 acres in Mitchelstown, 'in the Parish of Ballingarry Barrony of Cosler [as transcribed; i.e. Connello] and County of Limerick'. Castlereagh and Curroughrosty, 'whereof the said Samuell Bennett Sen[io]r was hitherto seized in right of his said Wife', go to Samuel junior for life, then to his sons in order of seniority, 'and for want of such Issue then to the Heires Males of the Body of the said Susanna', and then to Susanna's right heirs. Cloghasty and Mitchelstown go to Samuel senior and Susanna for life and then to Samuel junior and his sons. In default of his sons they go to 'William Bennett Second Son of the said Samuell Bennett Sen[io]r', then to James Bennett, third son. After the deaths of Samuel senior and Susanna, those lands are charged with £300 payable to William, James and Charles Bennett and to Prudence Bennett, daughter of Samuel senior. Graded on her link to Samuel Bennett: stays Probable. The deed calls her only Samuel senior's wife, never Samuel junior's mother. But Castlereagh and Curroughrosty were her own inheritance, held by her husband 'in right of his said Wife'. They are settled on Samuel junior and his sons, and after them on 'the Heires Males of the Body of the said Susanna' rather than on the husband's younger sons by name. That pattern implies that Samuel junior was her heir of body, that is, her son. It is an inference from the limitations, not a stated relationship, so the grade stays Probable. The image does not give her maiden name; 'als Cooke' comes from the Index Project abstract. ¶3

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Probable, graded on her link to Samuel Bennett: the deed calls Samuel junior the son of Samuel senior and calls her Samuel senior's wife, but it does not say that she was Samuel junior's mother. She could have been a second wife. ¶2a

Grade unchanged: Probable. ¶4b

Searches that found nothing 1

Searched with no result: her parents were not found. The Registry of Deeds index for 1708-30 has Cookes in Co. Limerick, none tied to Bennett: Stephen Cooke, gent., of Gurtnacreghin (1708) and of Ferrihy (1711); John Cooke, gent., of Co. Limerick (1710-11); John Cooke, baker of Limerick city, deceased by 1720; Henry Cooke, innkeeper of Kilmallock (1722). Stephen Cooke is the likeliest kinsman to test. ¶4a
