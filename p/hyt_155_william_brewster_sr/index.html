About the family

Wife (Probable): married about 1564 Mary (Smythe), widow of John Simkinson of Doncaster, mother of the Elder (Hunt, NEHGR 124 (1970): 250-254; Bunker 2010). His widow in 1590 was Prudence, evidently a later wife. His own parents: the GSMD Silver Book (Merrick 2000, pp. 1-5, as cited by Wikipedia) gives William Brewster (c.1510-1558) and Maud Mann; not entered, because nothing more than the bare citation was seen (lead only; a compiled 'Carpenter Lineage' file on archive.org cites Hunter's collections for a William Brewster of Bentley cum Arksey). ¶6

Wife confirmed in a record: the Chancery bill TNA C 2 Eliz. B31/1 (1558-1579) by 'William Bruester and Mary his wife, the late wife of John Symkinson late of Doncaster', quoted by Hunt, NEHGR 124 (1970): 252 n.2. Hunt also places him at Scrooby by 6 May 1564 (will of Bartholomew Bryan of Scrooby, York P&E 17:389: William Brewster 'dwelling in Scrooby') and suggests he was the 'William Bruister' who witnessed Christopher Man's will at Scrooby on 11 Oct 1558. ¶7

Parents (Speculative): Hunt (NEHGR 124: 250) writes that William Brewster, taxed in 1524 at Bentley cum Arksey next Doncaster (citing Banks, English Ancestry and Homes of the Pilgrim Fathers, pp. 35-39), 'seems to have married long before 1558 Maude, sister of Christopher Man of Scrooby', and that their sons 'seemingly were' William (b. about 1535, d. 1590) and Henry, vicar of Sutton-cum-Lound 1565-1594. The record behind Maude is Christopher Man's will, 11 Oct 1558, proved 13 Jan 1558/9 (York P&E 15 pt 3 fo. 186), quoted by Hunt: 'to my sister Maud Bruister 6/8... to my brother Wm. Man 6/8... Alice my wife', witnessed by 'Mr. Thomas Simkinson, William Bruister and John Simkinson'. The GSMD Silver Book (Merrick 2000) follows Hunt. No record read names William of 1590 as son of William or of Maud, so both are Speculative. ¶8

Life story

Office and residence: a William Brewster was assessed to the 1571 subsidy in Scrooby-cum-Ranskill on goods worth GBP 3 (one of only three so assessed), and from about 1575-76 William Brewster senior was the Archbishop of York's receiver and bailiff of the Scrooby manor, living in the manor house, and postmaster at Scrooby (Dexter pp. 253-254, citing Hunter's Collections). In June 1590 he supplied the Earl of Worcester with post-horses at Scrooby; the earl found him dead on his return some weeks later (Dexter p. 323, citing Stanhope's letter, S.P. Dom. Eliz. 233:48). Death date inferred: after early June and before 24 July 1590. ¶2

Administration: 'Vicesimo quarto die mensis Julii 1590, Administraco... Bonorum Jurium et Creditorum que fuerunt Willmi Brewster nuper de Scrooby defunct Comissa fuit Willmo Brewster filio eiusdem...' - on 24 July 1590 administration of the goods of William Brewster, late of Scrooby, deceased, was granted to William Brewster, 'the son of the said deceased'; 'Prudentia Brewster Vid. Relicta eiusdem' (Prudence Brewster, widow, his relict) renounced; the son exhibited an inventory and five shillings were paid (York, Act Book for the Deanery of Retford cum Laneham, 1590, transcribed with translation by Dexter, England and Holland of the Pilgrims, p. 323 n.3). Dexter notes that the act names no other child. ¶1

The case for this identification 1

Why the son is Elder William Brewster (identity chain): (1) Sir John Stanhope, Postmaster-General from 20 June 1590, wrote to 'Mr. Secretary Daveson' answering his request that young William Brewster be confirmed as post at Scrooby, objecting that the young man had not 'had admyttance and use of the place in his father's tyme'; Davison's endorsed reply said Brewster had held the place by Randolph's gift for a year and a half before his father's death (S.P. Dom. Eliz. 233:48, as quoted and summarised by Dexter pp. 317-318, 324). (2) Bradford's memoir says the Elder 'served... Mr. Davison, diverce years, when he was Secretary of State', then 'lived in ye country', and that the Scrooby congregation 'ordinarily mett at his house... (which was a manor of ye bishops)' (Bradford pp. 488-490). (3) He held the Scrooby post continuously from 1594 records for about seventeen years and was cited in 1607 as 'William Bruster of Scrowbie, gen.' (Dexter pp. 255, 325). The son of the 1590 administration is therefore Davison's former servant, the postmaster living in the archbishop's manor house, i.e. Elder William Brewster (see his page). ¶3

Alternatives considered and set aside 2

Mother of Elder William not entered: Dexter (p. 253) takes Prudence to be the Elder's mother, but the 1590 act calls her only the deceased's widow and relict, and no record read names her as the son's mother. Later compiled work (not read here) treats Prudence as a second wife. She is therefore not linked as a mother. Dexter also rejects the old suggestion that James Brewster, vicar of Sutton-cum-Lound, was the Elder's brother, because the estate settlement implies William junior was the only living child (p. 255). ¶4

Lead, not entered: the 1590 act is in the Borthwick Institute, York (Exchequer/Prerogative act books, Retford deanery). A reading of the original act and of any surviving inventory would add detail; the subsidy roll is E 179 (TNA). ¶5
