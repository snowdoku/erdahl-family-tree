About the family

Father of Raymond Hayter. Born in Prince Edward Island according to both his son's 1939 marriage registration and his son's 1947 death registration. Wife Lydia Adelaide Hicken, also of Prince Edward Island. No BC death registration for a Benjamin Hayter appears in the public BC index (to about 2006), so he probably died before the family moved west, or outside British Columbia. ¶1

Children proved by their own BC records naming Benjamin Hayter and Lydia Hicken as parents: Raymond, Cecil Elwood, Alice Vera May, Bertha Olive, Winnifred Eunice, Edith Rita and Rena Pearl. ¶4

Parents proved: the 1891 census of Lot 64, Kings County, lists Benjamin, aged 17, as son in the household of Thomas Hayter (born England about 1832) and his wife Grace A.; the 1881 census of Lot 64 lists him, born 1874, in the same household. The Island Register's Hicken lineage names his parents as Thomas Hayter and Grace Moore, and Thomas Hayter married Grace Amelia Moore on 4 December 1862 (PEI marriage register index). He was one of at least ten children. ¶6

Children (ten): Glen Gordon (1902), Rosebud (1904), Rex (1905), Raymond (1907), Cecil Elwood (1909), Alice Vera May (1912), Bertha Olive (1914), Winnifred Eunice (1916), Edith Rita (1919), Rena Pearl (1922) - all born at Gaspereaux except, by one compiled source, Raymond at Montague. Three daughters were baptised as teenagers in PEI in December 1933 and July 1934. ¶11

Life story

His widow Lydia is described as a widow at her death in December 1969, so he died before then. No BC death registration exists for him in the public index (to about 2006), and he is not named as a witness or informant in any of his children's BC records from 1937 to 1949. He probably died in Prince Edward Island, or never came west. Inference, not proved. ¶3

1901 census: enumerated as 'Benjamin Hater', head, married, farmer working on his own account, English origin, Baptist, at Murray River South, Lot 64, Kings County, with his wife Della (Lydia), born March 1881/82. The schedule-2 extract shows him owning 50 acres, with a house of 7 rooms and 3 other buildings. His parents lived nearby at High Bank. ¶8

Marriage: to Lydia Adelaide ('Della') Hicken of Gaspereaux, 27 February 1901 according to the Island Register (not yet verified against the PARO marriage licence). His children's BC records call her Della Hicken, Lydia Adelaida, Lydia Adeline and Lydia Adelaide. ¶9

Occupation: farmer in 1901 and 1911; lobster fisherman in 1931. ¶15

Marriage notice: The Baptist weekly Messenger and Visitor (Saint John, NB), 6 March 1901, p. 13, printed: 'HAYTE-HICKER.—At the home of the bride, Feb. 27, by Rev. H. Carter, Benjamin Hayte[r] of High Bank, Lot 64, P.E.I., to Lydia Adilda [Adelaide] Hicker [Hicken] of Gaspereaux, Lot 61, P.E.I.' This contemporary notice confirms the date (27 February 1901), the place (the bride's home at Gaspereaux) and a Baptist officiant, which until now rested on compiled genealogies; the PEI marriage register itself has still not been read. ¶23

1911 census: farmer on his own account at Gaspereaux, District 139 Kings, ED 37, with Lydia and children Glen (b. Apr 1902), Rosebud (Jan 1904), Rex (Oct 1905), Raymond (Aug 1907) and Cecil (Dec 1909); all Baptist, English origin. His widowed mother-in-law Bexie Johnston (formerly Hicken) and her son George Hicken lived next door. ¶10

1921 census: Benjamin Hayter, 47, head, fisherman, owner of a 6-room wooden house on Lot 61 at Gaspereaux (Sturgeon sub-district), father born England, Baptist; with Della, 39, and Rex 15, Raymond 13, Cecil 11, May 9, Olive 7, Winnifred 5 and Rita 2. His mother-in-law Bexie, now married to James Cleary, and her son George A. Hicken lived next door. ¶12

The move west: his children left PEI from 1925 - Rex and Rosebud crossed from Canada into the US at Seattle and Sweet Grass in late 1925, Glen at Seattle in April 1929; Rex died in a logging camp at Harrison Mills, BC, in April 1929, and Raymond was a grocery salesman at Harrison Mills by 1931. ¶13

1931 census: Lydia was first entered as head of the family's own house at Gaspereaux (5 rooms, valued at $400), with six children at home. Ben Hayter, aged 57, was added afterwards on a later page in another hand as head of the same household (dwelling 114, family 117), occupation fisherman, lobster fishing, on his own account; his father's birthplace is given as England. He was presumably away fishing when the house was first visited. Raymond, by then, was a grocery salesman at Harrison Mills, British Columbia. ¶14

Death: the Island Register (and, per the coordinator, a Find a Grave note) gives his death as 30 May 1936 at Charlottetown, PEI. NOT verified: no primary record retrieved. Consistent evidence: he was alive in PEI in June 1931; Lydia and the younger children moved to Penticton, BC about 1934; Lydia was a widow at her death in 1969; and no BC death registration exists for him. The record that would prove it is his PEI death registration (PARO, death registrations 1906-1968). If he died in Charlottetown while his wife was in BC, the place of death (possibly a hospital) is worth checking. ¶16

Death confirmed by a contemporary diary: Lauretta (Machon) Brooks of Murray Harbour wrote, in her summary for May 1936, 'May 30th Ben HAYTER died in Ch.town aged 62. Buried from Baptist Ch. M. River June 1st.' Her alphabetical death list gives the same entry: Hayter, Ben, 62, 30 May 1936, Charlottetown. So he died in Charlottetown on 30 May 1936 and was buried from the Baptist church at Murray River on 1 June 1936. This is a private diary, not the civil registration, which remains to be seen. ¶17

Death: Benjamin Hayter died at Charlottetown on 30 May 1936, aged 62, and was buried from the Baptist church at Murray River on 1 June 1936. This is recorded at the time by his neighbour Lauretta (Machon) Brooks of Murray Harbour in her diary (May 1936 summary: "May 30th Ben HAYTER died in Ch.town aged 62. Buried from Baptist Ch. M. River June 1st."), and again in her own list of local deaths ("Hayter, Ben | 62 | 30-May-36 | Charlottetown"). The age of 62 agrees with his birth on 12 April 1874. The diary is a contemporary private record, not a civil registration. The 1936 PEI death registration, held by the Public Archives and Records Office (PARO), would give the cause of death, the informant and the cemetery, but it could not be reached online. ¶18

Burial: His funeral was held from the Baptist church at Murray River on 1 June 1936, so he was very probably buried in the Murray River Baptist burial ground (now Murray River Cemetery), where his brother Thomas G. Hayter (d. 1930) lies. No gravestone for Benjamin has been found on Find a Grave. ¶19

Number of children: the 2015 obituary of his youngest child, Rena Pearl Bourne, calls her 'the last of her nine siblings to pass away', which makes ten children of Benjamin and Lydia - the ten now proved from records. ¶21

Dead by December 1951: the Summerland Review's report of his daughter Rita's wedding on 29 December 1951 calls her the 'daughter of Mrs. D. Hayter, Penticton, and the late Mr. Hayter'. This supports the unsourced Find a Grave statement that he died in Charlottetown in 1936. ¶22

Records that disagree 3

Birthplace: his son Cecil's 1938 marriage registration gives Benjamin Hayter's birthplace as High Bank, Prince Edward Island; his daughter Rita's 1939 marriage gives 'Mary River', almost certainly Murray River. Both are in the Murray Harbour district of southern Kings County. His wife and all their children were born at Gaspereaux, Kings County. ¶2

Birth: 12 April 1874. The 1901 census gives his birth as April 12, 1874 (age 26); the 1911 census gives April 1874 (age 37); the Island Register gives 12 April 1874 at Murray River. His children's BC marriage registrations give his birthplace as High Bank, PEI (Cecil, 1938) and Murray River, PEI (Edith Rita, 1939) - neighbouring places in Lot 64, Kings County, where his father farmed. ¶7

Conflicting evidence on the date of death: The 1935 Penticton, British Columbia, city directory already lists his wife as "Hayter, Mrs Benj, widow". Lydia also stated on a 1966 form that she arrived in Penticton on 27 July 1934, coming directly from Gaspereaux. Benjamin was alive and in PEI in June 1931. Against a death in 1931–34 stands Lauretta Brooks's diary. It places his death in its May 1936 summary, between entries dated 19 May and 29 May 1936, and gives his age as 62. He was born on 12 April 1874, so he was 62 only between April 1936 and April 1937. Had he died in 1931–34, he would have been 57 to 60. The diary also gives a specific funeral date, 1 June 1936, from the Murray River Baptist church. The diary is therefore preferred, and the date of death is kept as 30 May 1936. The 1935 directory's "widow" may reflect a separation, with Lydia moving west with the younger children in 1934 while Benjamin stayed in PEI, or simply the directory canvasser's assumption. The 1936 PEI death registration at PARO would settle the date, and the Charlottetown Guardian of about 1–3 June 1936 would probably carry a notice. ¶20
