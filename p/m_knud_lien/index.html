Life story

Context: the 1701 census (manntall) for Nordmøre and Romsdal lists two men at a farm called Lien in this district, Knudt Ørgiens (born about 1641, a tenant farmer) and his son Knud Eriksen (born about 1690). Neither can be shown to be Knud Gundersen's kin, and a 62-year-old Knud Eriksen is a poor fit for a bachelor marrying in 1751, so these are recorded only as context. ¶4

Engagement (index): Norddal (Sunnmøre) register 1736-1760 (1524P), p. 80, serial 184: Knud Gundersen of Aarset was engaged on 18 Sep 1751 to Eldri Sørensdtr. of Grøningsæter (trulovar Ole of Berdalen). This is very probably the Sunnmøre betrothal by Hr. Astrup referred to in his 28 Oct 1751 Grytten marriage (inference from names and dates; the index does not name the priest). If so, he was living at Aarset in September 1751, before settling at Lien in Grytten; his own origin may be Aarset rather than Lien. Not checked on the scan. ¶6

He stood godfather at a Grytten baptism on 23 Oct 1751, five days before his own wedding, and a second child of 'Knud, Lien' was baptized 30 Dec 1753. ¶3

Marriage scan read, Grytten p. 187: 'Dagen efter [27 Oct 1751] Copuleret i Hejns Kirke... Trolovede Folk: Ungk. Knud Gundersøn Lien og Elri Sørens D. ibid. Som forhen var Trolovede af Hr. Astrup paa Sundmeur.' The entry gives no ages, sureties or fathers. The earlier betrothal by a priest in Sunnmøre suggests one of the couple came from, or was living in, Sunnmøre in 1751 (an inference only). ¶5

Of Lien 'af Wolds Sogn' (Voll annex, Grytten). Father of Olava, baptized 11 Jun 1752. His patronymic is not given. ¶1

Identified (Probable, upgraded from a bare patronymic). The Grytten parish register records the marriage of 'Knud Gundersøn' of Lien, described as 'U.K.' (ungkar, a bachelor), to Elri Sørensdatter of Lien on 28 Oct 1751. Olava Knudsdatter was baptized 11 Jun 1752 as the daughter of 'Knud of Lien af Wolds Sogn' (Lien in the Voll annex of Grytten parish), about seven and a half months after that wedding, which fits a first child. Only one Knud of Lien appears in the Grytten register in these years, and no other Knud of Lien married in the period. The patronymic Gundersøn means his own father was named Gunder. ¶2

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.
