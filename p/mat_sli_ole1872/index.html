Life story

Oline Slind's eldest brother, the first child of Gunder Olsen and Kari Olsdatter, born about 1872 and already in the household at the 1875 census as 'Deres s' aged three. In the 1891 census he is 'sonnesson' in the house, unmarried. He is gone from Slind by the 1900 census. ¶1

His life after Slind, from the Selbu gaardshistorie, page 100 of Selbu i fortid og naatid vol. 7: 'Ole d.e., f. 1872, reiste til Amerika... 1894,... og g. 1902 m. Sigrid... Slind, f. 1880, og reiste til Amerika... 1907. Barn: [one] 27.6.1903, Kristine 7.1.1905, Gunder 7.5.1906, [one] 1907, [one] 1910, [one] 1912.' So the eldest son emigrated about 1894, CAME HOME, married a Sigrid Slind born 1880 in 1902, had three children in Selbu, and emigrated for good about 1907 with his family, having three more children afterwards. His brother Ole the younger did exactly the same thing a decade later. 'd.e.' is den eldre, the elder of the two brothers both called Ole. ¶11

WHERE HE WENT: WALKS CAMP, LINCOLN COUNTY, COLORADO - and three graves in one Norwegian Lutheran churchyard on the high plains prove it. 'Ole G Slind, 1872 - 1952'; 'Sigrid T Slind, 1880 - 1946'; and 'Gunder Slind, 7 MAY 1906 - 8 MAY 1988', all in Walks Camp Lutheran Cemetery, Lincoln County, Colorado. The Selbu gaardshistorie's line for him reads 'Ole d.e., f. 1872... g. 1902 m. Sigrid... Slind, f. 1880... Barn: [one] 27.6.1903, Kristine 7.1.1905, GUNDER 7.5.1906...' - and the Colorado stone of the son gives 7 MAY 1906 TO THE DAY. A husband born 1872, a wife born 1880 and a son born 7 May 1906, together in one cemetery, is not a coincidence that needs arguing for. Rating raised to Confirmed. ¶14

A possible American trace, not proved: the Trondheim emigrant protocol no. 9b, 1893-1895, carries an 'Ole G. Slind, b. 1872, of Selbo', expedited 28 February 1894, and Find a Grave has an 'Ole G Slind 1872-1952' at Walks Camp Lutheran Cemetery, Lincoln County, Colorado. The initial G is right for a son of Gunder and the year is right, but neither record has been tied to this household, so it is recorded as a lead rather than as a fact. ¶3

HE EMIGRATED TO WHITMAN COUNTY, WASHINGTON, AND HE WENT FIRST. Oline's eldest brother, the elder of Gunder Olsen Slind's two sons both named Ole, was never traced beyond the 1891 Selbu census, where he is a nineteen-year-old "hjv. son" (son helping at home); he had left the farm by 1900. The American record now places him: ¶4

THE TWO OLES BOTH ENDED IN THE SAME COUNTY. The elder Ole was at Lacrosse, Whitman County from about 1908; the younger Ole (b. 24 Jan 1884) was at Colfax, in the same county, by 1930, and is buried in Selbu Cemetery outside Lacrosse. That is why Oline's 1955 obituary could say "Ole Slind of Colfax, Washington" of one brother while the other had been farming thirty miles away for forty-seven years: the elder was presumably dead by then. His own death is not in the Washington death index 1907-2024 under any Slind spelling, which is an unexplained negative and the main gap left on him. ¶8

1910 federal census, Whitman County, Washington, LACROSSE precinct, enumeration district 276, page 77B-78A, lines 99-100 and 1-4 (image file 0756.tif/0757.tif): "Ole Slind, 38, born Norway; Sigrid Slind, 30, born Norway; Charoline Slind, 7, born Norway; Christina Slind, 5, born Norway; Gunder Slind, 3, born Norway; Alma Slind, 2, BORN WASHINGTON." Age 38 in 1910 gives a birth about 1872; the youngest child born in Washington puts the family's arrival in 1907 or 1908. ¶5

It is confirmed by a federal record. His daughter CORA SIGNORA SLIND, born 20 June 1912 at LACROSSE, WASHINGTON, filed a Social Security application (NUMIDENT SS-5, SSN XXX-XX-6598, as Cora Signora Campbell) naming FATHER: OLE G SLIND, MOTHER: SIGRID T SLIND. So he too used the middle initial G for Gundersen, exactly as his sister Oline and his younger brother Ole did. ¶7

So he died in 1952, three years before his sister Oline, which is exactly why her October 1955 obituary names only ONE surviving brother Ole - 'Ole Slind of Colfax, Washington' - although she had two. The elder was already dead. ¶15

Context, flagged as context: Walks Camp was a Norwegian Lutheran homesteading settlement in the dry-land wheat country of eastern Colorado, settled from the Dakota and Minnesota colonies in the first decade of the twentieth century. His arrival there about 1907, with a wife and three small children, is the second generation of the migration moving on from the places the first generation had filled up. ¶16

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

The identification rests on the Norwegian naming custom doing exactly what it is supposed to do. HIS ELDEST DAUGHTER IS CHAROLINE (Karoline), his mother's name KARI; HIS ELDEST SON IS GUNDER, his father's name. A Slind from Selbu, born 1872, who names his first daughter for Kari Olsdatter and his first son for Gunder Olsen, is Gunder and Kari's son. ¶6

A candidate grave, rated Speculative and not entered as fact: Find a Grave has an 'Ole G Slind, 1872 - 1952, Walks Camp Lutheran Cemetery, Lincoln County, Colorado' - Walks Camp being a Norwegian Lutheran settlement on the Colorado high plains. The initial G is right for Gundersen and the birth year is right. He died before his sister Oline, which is consistent with her 1955 obituary naming only ONE surviving brother Ole, of Colfax, Washington. Confirming it needs the memorial's own detail or a Colorado death record. ¶12

Corrections 1

Correction to the tree's framing: the Slind migration to Washington was not a 1950s event and did not begin with the 1884 Ole. It began with this man, about 1908, and the colony he joined was older still. ¶10

Notes on method and coordination

Note for anyone searching American records: his parents had TWO sons called Ole, this one born 1872 and the younger born 1884, and both appear together in the 1891 census household. An 'Ole Slind' in a United States record from this farm may be either brother, and the two should not be merged without a date. ¶2

Note for the next agent: with Ole the elder in Colorado and Ole the younger in Washington, NEITHER of Oline's brothers called Ole was the 'Ole Slind' farming beside her in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, in 1950. That man remains unidentified. ¶13
