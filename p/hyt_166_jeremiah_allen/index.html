About the family

Children (Boston births, vol. 24, read in OCR): Sarah (1723), Elisabeth (22 April 1725), Jeremiah (10 February 1727), John (1729) and Mary (1732), all 'of Jer[emiah] and Rebekah Allen'. A son named John fits his own father John Allen. ¶3

His father's will (Suffolk Probate 55:294, 1756) names 'my two Grand Children named John & Elizabeth Children of my said son Jeremiah'. This shows that Jeremiah had a daughter Elizabeth living in 1756, which strengthens the identification with Elizabeth (Allen) Prince, but the will gives her no married surname, and the 1765 and 1768 divisions set Jeremiah's share off only to his 'Children or legal Representatives' without naming them. His grade (Probable, on his link to Elizabeth Allen) is therefore unchanged. New supporting evidence: his sister was Elizabeth Blanchard, 'wife of Mr Hezekiah Blanchard of Boston' (Suffolk Probate 67:343), which explains why Job and Elizabeth Prince named their first son Hezekiah Blanchard Prince (1749), and a John Prince, felt maker, was surety for Jeremiah's widow in 1760. Still inferred, not named. ¶7

Life story

Birth: 'Jeremiah of John & Eliza. Allen born June 11', in the 1699 births of the Boston register (Record Commissioners vol. 9; OCR of the printed page, checked against the year heading). ¶1

Marriage: 'Jeramia Allen & Rebecca Kilby' were married at the Brattle Square Church, Boston, on 18 October 1722 (Boston Marriages, vol. 28, 1722 section; TAG 27 (1951): 203, which says the bride was Christopher Kilby's daughter Rebecca, b. 27 Dec. 1701). Waldo Lincoln's Waldo Genealogy (1902) i:119 identifies the man who married Rebecca Kilby in 1722 as the Jeremiah Allen born 11 June 1698/9 (the son of John and Elizabeth), as distinct from Jeremiah Jr. (b. 1710), son of Jeremiah Allen Esq., who married Abigail Waldo and then (1729) Elizabeth Oulton. ¶2

Occupation and death: 'Jeremiah Allen late of Boston Felt Maker deceased Intestate'; administration granted to 'Rebecka Allen of Boston Widow' about 11 April 1760, with sureties Samuel Hill, shopkeeper, and John Prince, felt maker, both of Boston (Suffolk Probate 56:365, docket 12319; image read). The docket has only the certificate of administration; bond and inventory were never recorded and there is no division, so his own estate does not name his children. He was living on 8 Dec. 1756, when his father's will named him. ¶6

Deed evidence for his daughter Elizabeth (signed-in lane; grade unchanged, Probable, graded on his link to Elizabeth Allen): on 5 March 1793 'John Allen, Taylor, and Sarah Allen Singlewoman, both of Boston' sold to Samuel Parkman and Charles Bulfinch one undivided moiety of a parcel of marsh land on Cambridge Street at the West part of Boston, bounded 'Northerly on land of Capt. Job Prince', 'Saving and Reserving out of the same One fifth part of one third part thereof claimed by Capt. Job Prince' (Suffolk Deeds 175:141-142; FamilySearch DGS 7442693 image 466; image read). The other moiety was sold the same month by Ebenezer Wells, Thomas Wells, Hezekiah Welch, William and Hannah Bird and Mary Newell, the heirs of Susanna Welch, John Allen the goldsmith's daughter (175:142-143). A one-fifth share of one third fits Job Prince holding the share of one of five heirs of Jeremiah, one of John Allen's three children (Probate 64:465, 1765). The deed does not name Elizabeth or say why the Princes claimed the share, so it is circumstantial; the claimant may be Job Prince jr., since Job senior's grantor-index entries end with a deed by his estate in Feb 1789 (163:250). Not found in: Job Prince to Nathaniel Patten, 12 Mar 1784 (Deeds 142:66-67; Elizabeth signs only to release dower); Job Prince to Mungo Mackay and others, trustees for his creditors, Dec 1788 (Deeds 164:31-33; lists his West End lots with no title recital). The 1789 deed 167:31 indexed under Jeremiah Allen 'set off to Jeremiah Allen dec'd in division of his Father's Est.' is by James Allen, son of the other Jeremiah (lots from the 1749 partition, Deeds 77:79), not this family. ¶8

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Upgrade: graded on his link to Elizabeth Allen. A Suffolk deed of 22 Aug. 1814 (register copy, DGS 007442718 image 570, p. 248; image read) by David Higgins of Boston, mariner, and John and Sarah Tucker quitclaims to Samuel Dunn a cow pasture on South Allen Street, West Boston, 'formerly being part of the Real estate of which Jeremiah Allen dec[ease]d seized & possessed, & by an unwritten agreement, as we fully believe, among the heirs of said Jeremiah Allen set off by parole to Elizabeth Prince whose heirs we are in part ourselves, and James Prince Esquire, Samuel Prince Esquire and Thomas I. Prince of Boston, and [Thomas] Williams Esq[uire]... being other heirs at law of said Elizabeth Prince'; the warranty covers persons 'claiming under Elizabeth Creed late of Prince Edwards Islands, deceased', and excepts the sixth part 'in the heirs of the late Cap[tai]n Job Prince'. So Elizabeth Prince, wife of Capt. Job Prince and mother of James, Samuel and Thomas Prince (Boston births 1754-1766), took a share of Jeremiah Allen's real estate as one of his heirs. His father's will (Suffolk Probate 55:294, 8 Dec. 1756) names 'Elizabeth', child 'of my said son Jeremiah', so the heir is his daughter. Continuity: the pasture lies on Allen Street within John Allen's twenty-acre farm, whose division (Probate 64:465, 1765) gave one third to 'the Children or legal Representatives of Jeremiah Allen eldest son of the deceased'; the rival Jeremiah Allen (merchant, d. by 1755) left children Jeremiah, Martha and William and no Elizabeth, and held no part of John Allen's farm. The earlier 'Identity (Probable)' notes are superseded. ¶9

The case for this identification 1

Identity (Probable, graded on his link to Elizabeth Allen): no record yet names Elizabeth (Allen) Prince as his daughter. The link rests on (1) her death notice, 'in 61st yr' on c.6 Dec 1785, which fits only the Elisabeth born 22 April 1725, the only Elizabeth Allen born in Boston 1724-1726; (2) Capt. Job Prince's mansion house stood 'between McLean Street and Allen Street, West End, his estate reaching to Charles River' (Hezekiah Prince's diary, 1793), which is exactly the twenty-acre farm that Rev. James Allen settled on his son John Allen, this Jeremiah's father, in 1706-1710 (Gleaner XI, which names McLean, Allen and Poplar streets as lying within John Allen's farm); (3) in 1781 the proprietors of the twenty-acre tract at West Boston were 'Capt. Job Prince, Mr. Hezekiah Blanchard, the Heirs of James Allen' and others (Boston Town Records, vol. 26, p. 263), i.e. Job Prince held beside the heirs of James Allen (Jeremiah's elder brother, b. 1697) and Hezekiah Blanchard, who married an Elizabeth Allen in 1723; (4) Job's first son was named Hezekiah Blanchard Prince (1749). The relationship is inferred, so Probable. ¶4

Corrections 1

Correction (to an earlier lead, not to the tree): earlier passes took the Jeremiah Allen, merchant, who died intestate by April 1755 leaving widow Elizabeth and minor children Jeremiah, Martha and William (Suffolk dockets 10999-11001, 11022), to be Rebecca Kilby's husband. Rebecca was still living when her father made his will in Sept. 1738 (TAG 27:203), and the Waldo Genealogy assigns the Oulton marriage (1729) and the children Jeremy (1742), William (1744), Mary (1746) and posthumous Jeremiah (1750) to the other Jeremiah, son of Jeremiah Allen Esq. The 1755 estate is therefore probably not this man's, and its lack of a distribution says nothing about Elizabeth Prince. His own death is not found; Suffolk docket 12319 (Jeremiah Allen, administration 1760, Reg. certif. 56/365) is a candidate. ¶5
