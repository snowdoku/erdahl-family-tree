About the family

Father: Jonathan is named as the eldest son of William Brewster of Plymouth, gent (died 10 April 1644), in the Plymouth Colony deed recording the settlement of William's estate: 'left onely two sonnes surviveing vizt Jonathan the Eldest and Love the yeonger' (Plymouth Colony Deeds I:198, The Mayflower Descendant 3:27, read on the page image). He and his brother Love were granted administration on 5 June 1644. The arbitrators gave him his father's arms as the first-born and about 68 acres at Duxbury beside the house he had built on his father's land. This supersedes the earlier 'lead only' note: William is now entered (see his page). His mother is not entered, because no record read names her. As with everything above Mary Brewster, this line reaches Raymond Hayter only if Mary was the mother of Ruth (Turner) Prince, which is still Speculative. ¶5

Family at New London (compiled history): Caulkins lists one son and four daughters at New London: Benjamin (married Anna Dart 1659, settled at Brewster's Neck on his father's farm), Elizabeth (married first Peter Bradley, second Christopher Christophers), Ruth (married John Picket about 1652), Grace (married Daniel Wetherell 4 August 1659) and Hannah (married Samuel Starr 25 December 1664). She also names Mary 'who married John Turner of Situate' among the children left in the old colony, and says that Ezekiel Turner, 'a grandson of Mr. Brewster, from Situate', settled in New London about 1675 (History of New London, 1852, pp. 277-278). Her claim that his wife Lucretia came on the Mayflower in 1620 with a child William conflicts with his own family record (the Brewster Book), which puts their marriage in 1624 and William's birth in 1625; the family record is preferred. ¶9

Mother (Probable): Mary, wife of William Brewster (see her page). The Leiden affidavit of 25 June 1609 names 'William Brewster, Englishman, aged about forty-two years; Mary Brewster, his wife, aged about forty years; and Jonathan Brewster, his son, aged about sixteen years' in one house (Dexter, England and Holland of the Pilgrims, p. 505). It does not say Mary was Jonathan's mother; ages and the absence of any other known wife make it Probable. His age 'about sixteen' in June 1609 agrees with birth on 12 Aug 1593. ¶12

Life story

Birth: 'Johnnathan Brewster was borne at Scroby in Nottinghamshyre. the 12th of August 1593' (his own family record, the 'Brewster Book', The Mayflower Descendant 1:7). ¶1

Marriage and children: 'Johnnathan Brewster marryed Lucretia Oldam. of Darby. the 10th Aprill. 1624. who had children: William Mary Johnnathan Ruth Beniamin Elizabeth Grace Hanna' (MD 1:8). The same record gives the births of William (Plymouth, 9 March 1625), Mary (Plymouth, 16 April 1627), Jonathan (Plymouth, 17 July 1629), Ruth (Jones River, 3 October 1631), Benjamin (Duxbury, 17 November 1633), Elizabeth (Duxbury, 1 May 1637), Grace (Duxbury, 1 November 1639) and Hanna (Duxbury, 3 November 1641), and the marriages of Mary to 'John Turner of Situate the Elder' (10 November 1645), Ruth to 'Joh(worn) Pickett' (14 March 1651, as written; possibly 1651/2 Old Style) and William to Mary Peame of London (15 October 1651). The editor says Jonathan made these entries himself, the earlier ones at one sitting in one ink. ¶2

Death (compiled history): Frances Manwaring Caulkins's History of New London (1852), a compiled history, heads her notice of him 'Jonathan Brewster, died in 1661' and adds in a footnote that he was living in March 1660/1, citing the Connecticut Colonial Records, vol. 1, p. 362 (the print is indistinct and could be read 862). She found no probate papers. The colonial record she cites has not been read, so the year rests on her alone. ¶6

Estate (compiled history): Caulkins says bills of sale recorded in 1658 conveyed all his property in the New London town plot and his house and land at Poquetannuck, with his oxen, cows, yearlings and swine, to his son Benjamin Brewster and his son-in-law John Picket, as trustees; they also conveyed land to his daughters Grace and Hannah (History of New London, 1852, p. 276; the deeds themselves have not been read). ¶7

Career (compiled history): Caulkins says he settled first at Duxbury and was several times its representative, then traded along the coast from Plymouth to Virginia as master and probably owner of a small vessel, which brought him to Pequot Harbor. She quotes a letter of Roger Williams to John Winthrop telling of his losses to creditors in the spring of 1649 (citing Massachusetts Historical Collections, 2nd series, vol. 9, p. 281), and says he was 'Clarke of the Towne of Pequitt' (town clerk of the plantation that became New London) in September 1649 (History of New London, 1852, p. 277). Her statements about his trading house and about his being chosen an assistant in 1657 were not checked on the page images and are not recorded here. ¶8

Death window: Caulkins gives 'Jonathan Brewster, died in 1661' (History of New London, p. 276), with a footnote that he was living in March 1660/1 (citing Connecticut Colonial Records vol. 1 p. 862, not yet read); the trustee's release of 14 February 1661/2 calls Lucretia 'the late wife of my father Mr. Jonathan Brewster', so he was dead by then. The History of Norwich (p. 212) says the precise date has not been ascertained. No burial record or gravestone is known; the Manwaring probate digest vol. I has no estate entry for him. ¶10

Last record alive: the Connecticut General Court's order at its March 1661 session concerning wampum due to 'Mr. Brewster' (The Public Records of the Colony of Connecticut, vol. 1, ed. Trumbull, 1850, p. 362, with the editor's footnote on p. 577 dating it to the session after the Commissioners' September 1660 meeting; page images read). The entry names only 'Mr. Brewster'; the volume's index assigns it to Jonathan. Caulkins's citation 'Col. Rec. vol. 1 p. 862' is evidently a misprint for p. 362, since the volume has no p. 862. A 'Mr. Brewster' also appears among the 1660 deputies (p. 329, OCR only). ¶11

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence: Confirmed as the father of Mary Brewster, wife of John Turner the Elder (see her page), because his own family record (written by him, but not at the time of the births: the editor says the entries through the 1645 marriage were made at one sitting), in a faithful printed transcription read on the page images, names Mary as 'the daughter of Johnnathan' and records her marriage to John Turner of Scituate the Elder. This does not settle whether Mary was the mother of Ruth (Turner) Prince, which is still Speculative (see Mary Brewster). His descent to Raymond Hayter depends on the Speculative link Mary Brewster -> Ruth (Turner) Prince: if Ruth's mother was another wife of John Turner, Jonathan Brewster is not an ancestor. ¶3

Open questions and the next record to read 1

Lead only, not added: the same record lists the deaths of 'Mary Brewster the wyfe of william Brewster' (Plymouth, 17 April 1627) and 'William Brewster' (Plymouth, 10 April 1644), and Jonathan is reputed to be a son of Elder William Brewster of the Mayflower. The Brewster Book does not call Jonathan William's son, so no parents are entered; that generation is for the orchestrator to decide. ¶4
