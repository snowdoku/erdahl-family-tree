About the family

Wife of Peder Amundsen of Brækken in Grytten parish, Romsdal. She is named as 'Eli Pedersd.' of Brækken, mother, in the baptism of her daughter Anne on 29 Oct 1791 at Grytten church, and she stood godmother at a Brækken baptism on 3 Jan 1793. ¶1

Her patronymic means her father was named Peder, which is almost certainly why the couple's eldest surviving grandson's line carries the name Peder: her daughter Anne's own eldest son was Peder Johnsen Sætnes, born 1817. ¶2

Life story

Her baptism, age, marriage date and death have not been found. The indexed Grytten registers contain no Eli Pedersdatter born at Brækken, so she came into the farm from elsewhere, probably by marriage before 1787. ¶3

How sure are we?

Speculative. A plausible candidate only. Treat this link as a question, not a fact.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence lowered from Probable to Speculative (audit, 13 Oct 2026). The records about this person stand, but her identification as the mother of Anne Pedersdatter (wife of John Johnsen Sætnes) is now Speculative: see the note of the same date on Anne Pedersdatter, which names the records (Grytten confirmation 19 Oct 1834, p. 495 no. 5, 'Anne Pedersd. fra Lesjøe'; Lesja marriage 13 Mar 1816, pp. 834-835). ¶5

Corrections 1

Correction: no longer linked as mother of Anne Pedersdatter (13 Oct 2026), because she was a Lesja woman - 'fra Lesjøe' in her daughter's 1834 confirmation, married in Lesja in 1816 as a maiden of Kloppen - and she fits the 1801 Lesja household of Peder Ingebretsen and Anne Pedersdatter at Eeneboe, whose sons Peder and Ingebret match her 1816 co-godparents. The Grytten Anne Pedersdatter born 18 Oct 1791 at Brækken remains her daughter; she stays in the tree. ¶6

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Record checked and set aside: an Eli Pedersdatter 'of Hagen' married Søren Jonsen of Setnes at Kors in 1814 - the elder brother of Anne Pedersdatter's husband. It is tempting to make the two Pedersdatters sisters, which would make two Setnes-Ødegård brothers marry two sisters, but the 1814 marriage entry names neither father, the bride is of Hagen rather than Brækken, and no daughter Eli of Peder Amundsen is indexed. The idea is recorded here only so that nobody repeats the guess as a fact. ¶4
