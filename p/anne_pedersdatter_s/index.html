About the family

Parentage found (Probable). She was born 18 Oct 1791 at the farm Brækken in Grytten parish and baptized at Grytten church on 29 Oct 1791, the daughter of Peder Amundsen and his wife Eli Pedersdatter of Brækken. The godparents were the minister's own wife (entered only as 'Min kone', my wife), Marit Ellingsdatter and Jørgen Ingebretsen of Østigaard, Marit Pedersdatter of Brevig, Just Endresen of Bakke, and Lars Styving. ¶3

Parentage lowered to Speculative (audit, 13 Oct 2026): the Brækken identification (Peder Amundsen and Eli Pedersdatter) rested only on her being the one Anne Pedersdatter baptised in Grytten 1785-1796, a search that never included Lesja. Kari Johnsdatter's Grytten confirmation of 19 Oct 1834 (1539P 1818-1853, p. 495, no. 5, read on the scan: mother 'Anne Pedersd. fra Lesjøe') and the Lesja marriage of 13 Mar 1816 (0512P 1777-1819, pp. 834-835, index: groom Joen Joensen of Klevstuen, bride Anne Pedersdatter, maiden of Kloppen) both point positively to a Lesja-born woman, which contradicts the Grytten-birth argument. Her birth date 18 Oct 1791 and place Brækken come from that identification and are equally Speculative; her burial age (60 in Jan 1850) gives only about 1790. ¶7

Life story

Age check: census age 10 (Feb 1801) gives birth about 1790-91; burial age 60 (Jan 1850) about 1789-90. These agree within normal error; no conflict. ¶11

The only remaining doubt is that no marriage record has been found for her and John Johnsen about 1816, and her son Peder's October 1817 baptism is not in the online index, so there is no document that names her father and her husband together. ¶5

Origin in doubt (finding, not yet resolved): her daughter Kari's Grytten confirmation of 19 Oct 1834 (p. 495, no. 5, read on the scan) calls her 'Anne Pedersd. fra Lesjøe', Anne Pedersdatter from Lesja, and she married as a maiden of Kloppen in Lesja on 13 Mar 1816. Both point to a Lesja-born woman and conflict with this record's identification of her as the Anne Pedersdatter baptised 1791 at Brækken in Grytten. That identification rested on her being the only Anne Pedersdatter baptised in Grytten 1785-1796; it did not test Lesja. The Brækken parentage (Peder Amundsen and Eli Pedersdatter) should be treated as unproved until the Lesja marriage entry and Lesja baptisms about 1788-1795 are read. ¶6

Lesja marriage entry read on the scan (13 Oct 2026): she married on 13 Mar 1816 as 'Pige Anne Pedersdatter Klaappen' (maiden, of Kloppen in Lesja). No age, birthplace or father is given. ¶8a

Her own baptism has not been found. The Lesja index (0512P 1777-1819) has no Anne with father Peder and mother Anne Pedersdatter 1789-1793, and a reading of the scans for pp. 292-315 (Feb 1790 to Feb 1791) found none; those pages were read at reduced resolution, so the negative is soft. Pages for Mar 1791 to Dec 1792 were not read. The earlier birth date 18 Oct 1791 and place Brækken (Grytten) belonged to the Grytten identification and are replaced by 'about 1791, Lesja' (inferred from the 1801 census age). ¶10

Death: Probable. Anne Pedersdatter Sætnæsødegaard, cottar's wife (husmandskone), died 29 Jan 1850, age 60, and was buried 4 Feb 1850 at Grytten. Her parents and the couple's marriage (about 1816) have not been found. ¶1

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Why this identification: she is the only Anne Pedersdatter baptized anywhere in Grytten parish between 1785 and 1796 in the indexed registers, and her burial record calls her Anne Pedersdatter Sætnæsødegaard, aged 60, in January 1850, which points to a birth about 1790. A birth in October 1791 makes her 58 at death - well inside the normal error for a stated age. Brækken (also written Sogge-Brækken) lies beside Sogge, the farm next to Setnes on the shore of Romsdalsfjorden, so she married a near neighbour. Her eldest known son was named Peder, after her own father, which the patronymic Pedersdatter independently supports. The one other Anne Pedersdatter of this age in Grytten, Anne Pedersdatter Myrabøe (born about 1791), can be ruled out: she came to Grytten as a servant from Fron in 1831 and moved away again in 1833. ¶4

Upgrade: Confirmed as the mother of Peder Johnsen Sætnes (see his page), under the grading rule that a person's grade covers the link to the child in the tree. Peder's Grytten confirmation of 15 Sep 1833 (1539P 1818-1853, p. 492 no. 4, index) names his parents as 'John Johnsen', lodger, and 'Anne Pedersd', 'Hustru' (wife), of Setnæs, and his 1817 Lesja baptism names Jon Jonsen and Ane Pedersdatter of Kloppen; her daughter Kari's 1834 confirmation calls her 'Anne Pedersd. fra Lesjøe'. This grade covers only her link to Peder: her own parentage (Peder Ingebretsen and Anne Pedersdatter of Eneboe) keeps its earlier grade of Probable. ¶12

Corrections 1

Correction: her parents are now given as Peder Ingebretsen (b. about 1753) and Anne Pedersdatter (b. about 1757), cottagers at Eeneboe (Søreneboe) in Lesja (Peder Ingebretsen and Anne Pedersdatter), replacing Peder Amundsen and Eli Pedersdatter of Brækken in Grytten (13 Oct 2026). Grounds: (1) in the 1801 census of Lesja (Verket annex, Eeneboe, household 4; scan read) the household of Peder Ingebretsen, 48, husmann med jord (cottager with land), and his wife Anne Pedersdatter, 44, holds exactly three children: Peder Pedersen 15, Ingebret Pedersen 2 and Anne Pedersdatter 10. (2) When she was betrothed in Lesja in 1816 as the maiden Anne Pedersdatter of Kloppen (Klaappen), her husband-to-be stood godfather in Jan 1816 beside Peder Pedersen Klaappen and Ingebret Pedersen Klaappen - the same names as the two Eeneboe brothers - to a child of Guri Pedersdatter; the couple also had a daughter Gurri, baptised 1 Jan 1784 at Sør Eneboe (index: father Peder Engebrigts., mother Ane Pedersd.). (3) Her daughter Kari's 1834 Grytten confirmation calls her 'Anne Pedersd. fra Lesjøe'. (4) Her burial age (60 in Jan 1850) gives about 1790, fitting census age 10 in Feb 1801. (5) In the 1801 census of Lesja the only young Ingebret Pedersen is the one at Eeneboe (the other, at Rindal, was born about 1746), so the 1816 pair Peder and Ingebret Pedersen points to this household (audit, index search of the 1801 census; spelling variants such as Engebret may be missed). Other girls named Anne Pedersdatter of about the right age lived in Lesja in 1801 (at Husum, b. about 1789; Taarud, about 1787; Andgaard, about 1786), so the identification rests on the brothers, not on her name and age. Gap: the family is at Eeneboe in 1801, and she and the two brothers are 'of Kloppen' in 1816; no record yet shows the household moving to Kloppen. The match rests on this household and sibling set; no record names her parents and her husband together, so it is Probable, not Confirmed. ¶9

Searches that found nothing 3

Her marriage to John Johnsen (about 1816) is not found among indexed marriages in Grytten, Veøy, Hen, Voll, Eid or Nesset for 1813-1817, and the Oct 1817 baptism of son Peder is not indexed. Her parents remain unknown. ¶2

Search note: her own confirmation (about 1805-1808) was looked for in Lesja ministerialbok 1777-1819 (0512P, Digitalarkivet source 9237). The register is one chronological list and confirmations are not indexed (an lt=kf search for Lesja 1800-1812 returns 0). Canvases 338 (pp. 672-673, Jul-Aug 1806) and 340 (pp. 676-677, Oct-Nov 1806) hold baptisms, churchings, burials and marriages only, with no confirmation list. The confirmations may be entered elsewhere in the volume or in a separate book; not found. ¶13

Baptism search, negative: Lesja ministerialbok 1777-1819 (source 9237) canvases 158-168 (pp. 316-337, 2 Mar - 28 Dec 1791) were read in full at 1400 px, every baptism checked. No child of Peder Ingebretsen and Anne Pedersdatter appears. Baptisms of other Annes in these pages (26 Jun 1791 d. of Ole Erlandsen Hole; 14 Aug d. of Gunder Trondsen Kolstad; 11 Sep d. of Ole Hansen Lindsøe; 30 Oct d. of Joen Pedersen; 6 Nov d. of Mentz Olsen Norderhuus; 20 Nov d. of Hans Larsen Dombaas; 14 Dec d. of Trond Jørgensen Flittig; 18 Dec d. of Samuel Gudmundsen) are not hers. A 'Peder Ingebretsen, Norderhuus-Ølen' stood godfather on 25 Dec 1791 (p. 337), so a Peder Ingebretsen lived at the Verket end of Lesja then. With Feb 1790 - Feb 1791 already read (soft negative), the pages left are Jan - Dec 1792 (from canvas 169, p. 338). ¶14

Open questions and the next record to read 1

Lead: two men of Kloppen with her patronymic, Peder Pedersen Klaappen and Ingebret Pedersen Klaappen, stood godfathers with her husband-to-be in Jan 1816 to a child whose mother was Guri Pedersdatter (wife of Poul Joensen Ovram). They may be her brothers, and Guri her sister; if so, her father was a Peder of Kloppen in Lesja. Unproved; the Lesja baptisms about 1785-1795 (0512P) under father Peder at Kloppen should be read next. Her Brækken (Grytten) parentage stays Speculative. ¶8b
