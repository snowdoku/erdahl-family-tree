How sure are we?

Speculative. A plausible candidate only. Treat this link as a question, not a fact.

1 on the grade · 1 correction · 2 open questions

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Graded on his link to his daughter Elizabeth Cornish. Hawley's Visitation of Essex of 1552, Everard pedigree (Harleian Society 13:8), shows 'Raffe Josselyn = Elizabeth da. & coh. of Will. Cornishe', with their daughter 'Agnes, da. of Raphe of Canfield Josselyn' wife of John Wiseman, and another daughter and coheir of William Cornish, Joan, married to Thomas Everard; the pedigree makes William a younger son of Thomas Cornish (wife Eden) and brother of John Cornish, who married the heiress of Humphrey Waltham. The 1612 Everard and Mildmay pedigrees speak of coheiresses of 'Cornish of (Much) Waltham'. The 1552 visitation was made while Agnes (Josselyn) Wiseman was living, so it is close to the family, but it is still a heraldic pedigree, and no will, fine or inquisition read names William as Elizabeth's father. Speculative. ¶1a

Corrections 1

Correction: a deed-based account contradicts the visitation on who fathered the Cornish coheiresses; grade stays Speculative (lowest) and the link is now in more doubt. Morant, History of Essex vol. 2 (1768), Great Waltham, manor of Mascalls alias Langleys, working from the Tufnell deeds ('Ex cartis Sam. Tufnell Arm.', now ERO D/DTu): 'John Cornish, son and heir of John Cornish, son and heir of Thomas Cornish... and Idonea his wife, confirmed their part of this maner to Thomas Everard, of Great Waltham, who had married their daughter', and Thomas Everard 'by Joane, or Mary, daughter and co-heir of John Cornish of Langleys, had six sons'. So Morant makes the Everard coheiress (Joan) a daughter of a JOHN Cornish, where Hawley's 1552 visitation makes her and Elizabeth (Josselyn) daughters and coheirs of William, younger son of Thomas Cornish and 'Eden' (= Idonea). The original that survives (ERO D/DTu 85A, exemplification of a common recovery, 12 Feb. 1515/16, read as the catalogue abstract) has Thomas Everard of Great Waltham recovering the Cornish moiety of the manor (endorsed 'the one half of the manor of mascalles in Waltham magna pars Cornyshe'), with 'John Cornyssh (s. of John Cornyssh)' as vouchee; it names no William and no daughter. Other Great Waltham deeds name John Cornysshe senior (1476, 1492), John Cornysshe junior (1501, 1521) and Thomas Everard and John Cornysshe junior together (1501, D/DTu 82); none of the ERO or TNA catalogue entries found names a William Cornish of Great Waltham. If Elizabeth Josselyn was Joan Everard's sister and coheir, her father may have been a John Cornish, not William. ¶2a

Open questions and the next record to read 2
