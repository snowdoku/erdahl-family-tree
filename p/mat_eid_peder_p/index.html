About the family

Eldest brother of John P. Eidem and the man who opened the chain: he emigrated alone in 1880, homesteaded in Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota in 1882, and thereafter brought the rest of the family and the Slind cousins out on prepaid tickets. ¶10

He is named for his maternal grandfather Peder Bersvendsen of Eidem. ¶29

Life story

Birth: born at Selbu, Norway in 1858 — the gravestone gives 4 November 1858 — the eldest of the five children of Paul Eidem. ¶1

Childhood: 'He grew up in his native town, with such school privileges as the times afforded. He had to go to work herding cattle when he was eleven years.' ¶2

Baptism: born 24 NOVEMBER 1858 and baptised 27 December 1858 at Selbu church. Selbu parish register 1842-1858, baptisms serial no. 2 (the entry falls at the very end of the volume and the page is not numbered); the 1843-1859 volume carries the parallel entry. Father 'Paul Ingebrightsen', mother 'Kari Pederd', residence entered as 'Eceben' - the transcriber's mangling of Eidem. ¶27

Printed source: his given name and his birth year 1858 occur on page 206 of Selbu i fortid og naatid, bind 4 (1992), chapter BRENNHAUGEN, together with his parents, his three younger siblings' birth years, and the words Amerika and Minnesota. ¶35

Context, what a homestead required: under the Homestead Act of 1862 a settler entered up to 160 acres of surveyed public land for a small fee, then had to live on it and cultivate it for five years. To "prove up" he published the notice above for six consecutive weeks so that the claim could be contested, then appeared before the court with neighbours who swore on oath to his continuous residence and cultivation; only then did the patent issue. Five years from an 1882 entry falls due in 1887, which is exactly when he published. ¶17

Godfather: he stood as one of the six faddere at the baptism of his youngest brother John on 29 October 1876, entered as 'Peder Paulsen Eidem' in the Selbu register 1860-1877, folio 26 entry 122. He was seventeen; three and a half years later he sailed for America. ¶34

Emigration, Confirmed, and the spelling that hid it: Trondheim police office, First accession, emigrant protocol no. 4 (1878-1880), page 73, line 21, group of travellers 00227, sequence number 1121 - 'Peder P. EIDEN' (with an N; this is why a search on the surname Eidem misses him entirely), male, year of birth 1859 (calculated), age 21, occupation Arbeidsmand (labouring man), residence Selbo, destination 'Sacred Heat Minn' = SACRED HEART, Renville County, Minnesota, payment 'Bill. bet. i Amerika' (ticket prepaid in America), permissions column 'Meldt' (he reported himself to the police). Contract and expedition date both 27 April 1880, steamer Tasso ('Dampskibet Tasso'), Allan Line. ¶11

Emigration: he left TRONDHEIM on 27 APRIL 1880, aged 21, recorded as of 'Selbo' and bound for Sacred Heart, Minnesota — the entry is in the Trondheim police office emigrant protocol no. 4 (1878–1880), indexed on Digitalarkivet. He crossed the Atlantic to QUEBEC and reached Renville County, Minnesota on 24 May 1880, a journey of four weeks door to door. ¶3

Further detail from the original print, in its own words: he 'had to go to work at herding cattle when only eleven years old'; 'In 1880 he crossed the ocean, landing at Quebec, and arriving in Renville county, Minnesota, May 24, 1880'; 'in 1882 sought a homestead farm in Lac-qui-parle county. Here he built a claim shanty 10 by 12 feet, and a straw shed for the oxen with which he began his farming.' The shanty's dimensions - ten feet by twelve - are the measure of what the family started from, and were not previously recorded. 'He is a Republican, and has served on the town board two years. He owns a good farm of two hundred and twelve acres well provided with farm buildings, a fine wind mill, and a small grove, the planting of his own hands.' ¶38

Homestead: after three years of hired farm work he took homestead land in Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota in 1882, 'built a claim shanty and a straw shed for oxen and began his farming'. By 1904 he held 212 acres with good buildings, a windmill and a grove of trees he had planted himself. Context: a claim shanty was the minimum habitable structure the Homestead Act required a claimant to put up and live in while proving up the claim. ¶4

Public office: 'He was politically motivated and served on town boards often.' ¶5

Homestead, 1882-1888: the printed 1904 biography's claim that he "came to homestead land in Lac Qui Parle County" in 1882 is confirmed by the contemporary legal notice. The Lac qui Parle County Press (Madison, Minn.) of 11, 18, 25 November and 2, 9 December 1887, page 4, under the standing head "Final Proof Notices", printed: "Land Office at Benson, Minn., Oct. 28, 1887. Notice is hereby given that the following-named settler has filed notice of his intention to make final proof in support of his claim, and that said proof will be made before the Judge, or in his absence the Clerk of the District Court of Lac qui Parle county, at Lac qui Parle, Minn., on Friday, January 6, 1888, viz: PETER P. EIDEM, homestead application No. 11,909, for lots 1, 2, 3 and 4, of section 8, township 117, N. of range 46, W. 5th P. M. He names the following witnesses to prove his continuous residence upon, and cultivation of, said land, viz: David Woolf[e]n, George Hicks, J. M. Wilson and N. P. Bates, all of Marietta P. O., Minn. C. F. Wilkins, Register." ¶15

The farm: lots 1, 2, 3 and 4 of section 8, Township 117 North, Range 46 West of the 5th Principal Meridian - that is, the fractional north tier of section 8 in MEHURIN TOWNSHIP, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota, in the north-west part of the township. The 1916 county history says William B. Moore was among the first to settle on section 8, in 1878. ¶16

Minnesota state censuses: 'Peter P. Eidem', 26, born Norway, in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County in 1885, three years after taking the homestead; 'Peter P. Eidem', 36, with 'Carrie Eidem', 30, and Gunder Eidem, 6, in Augusta Township in the same county in 1895; and 'Peter P. Eidem', 45, with 'Carrie Eidem', 41, still in Augusta in 1905. ¶14

1885 Minnesota state census: Mehurin township, Lac qui Parle County, page 0566 line 34 - 'Peter P Eidem, M, 26, born Norway'. He is one of only two Eidems in the entire county in 1885 (the other is Andrew Eidem, 32, in Augusta township), and he is ALONE: no wife, no mother, no brothers. Peder was the bridgehead of the family in Minnesota, in place a year before his mother Kari and his brothers Mikal and John reached the state in May 1886. ¶23

Tax: he is in the Lac qui Parle County delinquent personal-property tax lists printed in the Press as "Eidem, Peter" (1886, $7.32; 1887, $7.31), "Eidem, P P" (1889, $3.42; 1890, $1.80; 1891, $6.28) and "Eidem, Peter" again in 1893 - an unbroken presence in the county from 1886 to 1893. ¶18

Marriage: he married Kari ('Carrie') Slind in 1888; she was born in Norway in 1863. Slind, like Eidem, is a farm name in Selbu — the two families intermarried again in the next generation when his brother John married Oline G. Slind. ¶6

Church office, 1888 - the earliest American record of this family yet found: "P. P. Eidem" is named as the officer (b., i.e. secretary) of Zoar congregation (Zoar menighet, also spelled Soar) at Revillo, Grant County, South Dakota, in 1888. This congregation was organised in 1884 under the Konferentsen (the Norwegian-Danish Conference), had 36 souls in 1885 and 40 in 1888, was served by the Rev. S. E. Sorensen 1884-88 and the Rev. C. T. Moen in 1889, had a women's society and a burial ground, and was dissolved in 1889 when it united with Immanuels congregation to form Trefoldigheds (Trinity) congregation at Revillo. It is the parent of the name later taken by the Hauge Synod Zoar congregation of 1896 in whose churchyard he is buried. ¶8

Homestead patent, 1888 - the notice closed out: the patent issued on 17 OCTOBER 1888, General Land Office document number 8087, accession STA-MN0250__.137, for LOTS 1, 2, 3 AND 4 OF SECTION 8, TOWNSHIP 117 NORTH, RANGE 46 WEST OF THE 5TH PRINCIPAL MERIDIAN, Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota. This is the same land as the final-proof notice of October 1887, and it completes the chain: entry 1882, publication of the notice November-December 1887, proof before the District Court 6 January 1888, patent 17 October 1888. The nine-month interval between proof and patent is the ordinary time for the Benson land office to forward the papers to Washington. ¶25

Marriage: married 13 October 1888 Karen (Kari) Eliasdatter Slind, b. 7 September 1863 in Selbu, daughter of Elias Larsen Slind (b. 1826) and Marit Estensdatter (b. 16 April 1832 at Guldseth). She emigrated in April 1885 and came to Lac qui Parle County the same year. THEY HAD NO CHILDREN. She died 30 November 1915. He was a member of the town board about nine years. ¶31

Marriage, exact date, from the original print: 'Mr. Eidem was married October 13, 1888, to Miss Carrie Slind, who was born in Norway October 7, 1863.' This tree had only 'in 1888'. The marriage date is therefore 13 OCTOBER 1888, in Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota. ¶37

Return visit to Norway and the crossing back, Confirmed: Trondheim emigrant protocol no. 10 (1895-1902), page 191, group 01392, lines 4 and 5, expedited 25 July 1900, ship Salmo, White Star - 'Peder Paulsen Eidem', 41, married, residence AMERICA, destination 'Marietta, Minn', and on the next line his wife 'Kari Paulsen Eidem', 36, married, residence America, same destination. On the line immediately above them is the nineteen-year-old Oline Gundersen Slind of Selbo, also for Marietta, on a ticket prepaid in America: the couple were taking her back with them, and she married Peder's younger brother John within four years. ¶13

Land in South Dakota as well: on 1 JUNE 1898 he received a second patent, document number 2082, accession STA-SD2170__.362, for LOTS 1 AND 2 OF SECTION 22, TOWNSHIP 117 NORTH, RANGE 47 WEST OF THE 5TH PRINCIPAL MERIDIAN, DEUEL COUNTY, SOUTH DAKOTA. Range 46 West is the last range in Minnesota on that line and range 47 West the first in South Dakota, so this land lies only a few miles west of his Mehurin homestead, across the state border. He therefore farmed on both sides of the state line, which helps account for the "two hundred and twelve acres" credited to him in the 1904 printed biography, and it is the physical link between the Lac qui Parle County Eidems and the Deuel County / Zoar Lutheran Eidems. ¶26

The 1899-1900 visit home: 'In 1899 they both travelled back to their old fatherland to see family and friends. It was a pleasant trip for them, and they did not return until the following year.' That journey is the Trondheim emigrant protocol of 25 July 1900, group 01392, page 191, in which 'Peder Paulsen Eidem, b. 1859, married, residence AMERICA' and 'Kari Paulsen Eidem, b. 1864, married, residence AMERICA' are expedited for Marietta, Minn. on prepaid tickets along with Oline Gundersdatter Slind and Ole Johnsen Slind. ¶32

Context: his 1904 biography is the single printed source that names this family's father and parish, and it is the reason the whole American line can be put back into Selbu. ¶7

Route: the printed 1904 biography of him says he crossed to Quebec and reached Renville County on 24 May 1880, twenty-seven days after leaving Trondheim. That is consistent with the protocol's Allan Line, which ran the cheap Glasgow and Liverpool service to the St Lawrence; 27 April is just as the St Lawrence opens for the season. He travelled alone: no Eidem relative appears anywhere on page 73 of the protocol, and the prepaid ticket means a Selbu man already in Renville County paid his passage. ¶12

Source upgrade: his 1904 biography has until now been cited in this tree from a RootsWeb transcription. The ORIGINAL printed volume is free and full-text searchable on archive.org, and the citation should be to that: Compendium of History and Biography of Central and Northern Minnesota (Chicago: Geo. A. Ogle & Co., 1904), page 469, 'PETER P. EIDEM'. The page number is confirmed independently of the transcription - the OCR of the scan carries the printed folio 469 immediately before the article and 470 immediately after it - and Selbu occurs exactly once in the volume's 3.5 million characters, in this article. ¶36

1905 Minnesota state census: Augusta township, page 0691 line 29 - 'Peter P Eidem, M, 45, W, born Norway, father Norway, mother Norway', with his wife Carrie (41), the adopted or foster son Gunder (16), and his mother Carrie Eidem (70) in the house. ¶24

Emigration and America, from Selbygbogen (Minneapolis, 1921) p. 149, written from his own answers: 'Peder P. Eidem, Marietta, Minn., born 4 Nov. 1858 at Brendhaugen in Selbu, son of Paul Ingebrigtsen Eidem (b. 1826 at the farm Marstad) and Kari Pedersdatter (b. 20 Oct. 1834 at Brendhaugen under Oestre Eidem). Emigrated 26 Apr. 1880, came by way of QUEBEC to SACRED HEART, MINN., on 24 May 1880, was there two years, and then came to Lac qui Parle County and BOUGHT LAND IN 1882 ONE MILE SOUTH-WEST OF WHERE MARIETTA NOW IS.' He was the first of the family out, and every later emigration in it was paid for from the money he and his brother made on that land. ¶30

Burial plot: Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, lot 2 — the lot that also holds Kari P. Eidem (d. 1925), Lilla Eidem (d. 1915), Mrs. Marit E. Slind (d. 1917) and May Christine Gieseke (d. 1987). ¶21

Clean negative on his obituary, recorded so nobody repeats the search. He died 23 October 1935. The Minnesota Digital Newspaper Hub's run of MINNEAPOLIS TIDENDE, the Norwegian-language paper that printed his mother's obituary in 1925 and his brother Mikal's death in 1923, ENDS IN 1935, and a search of the whole hub for 'Eidem' between 1 September 1935 and 28 February 1936 returns nine issues, NONE of them Minneapolis Tidende and none of them relevant (Rochester Post-Bulletin, Duluth Herald, St. Cloud Daily Times, and six issues of the Swedish-language Svenska Amerikanska Posten). No western-Minnesota paper of 1935 is digitised anywhere free. His obituary exists on microfilm of the Madison or Dawson paper and nowhere online. ¶39

Land, 1940: he had died in 1935, but the surname was still on the Lac qui Parle County tax roll in two townships - see the notes on his brother John P. Eidem. ¶19

Gravestone, photographed and read: a grey granite die with a carved fleur-de-lis scroll in the tympanum and an inset cross, inscribed 'PEDER P. EIDEM / NOV. 4, 1858 / OCT. 23, 1935 / KAREN P. EIDEM / SEPT. 7, 1863 / NOV. 30, 1915' over 'EIDEM' in raised letters. A separate flat footstone beside it reads simply 'PEDER P. EIDEM'. Husband and wife share one monument. ¶20

The case for this identification 1

Identification: rated Probable. The entry prints only the initials "P. P. Eidem", which match Peder Paulsen exactly; he was 29 going on 30 in 1888; the surname is rare in that county; and he is buried in the churchyard of the congregation that re-used this one's name. No record read so far spells out the patronymic in this context. If the identification holds it places the family in Grant County, South Dakota, as settled householders by 1888 - twelve years earlier than the 1900 founding of the Mehurin congregation and sixty-two years earlier than the 1950 census on which the line had been resting. ¶9

Corrections 3

Correction: the South Dakota state burial register records him as 'PEDER P EIDEM 10-23-1900' and then enters his real death date against a phantom 'KAREN P LORRAINE EIDEM 10-23-1935'. The photographed stone shows one monument to two people and settles it: Peder P. died 23 October 1935 and Karen P. died 30 November 1915. The register's 1900 is that database's placeholder year, used when it holds a month and day but no year. ¶22

Correction: his birth date is 24 November 1858, not 4 November 1858. The 4 November in the American records is a dropped digit; the Selbu parish register, written six weeks after the event, gives 24 November. He was the first-born of the five. ¶28

Correction: the 'Kari Paulsen Eidem' of that 1900 protocol is NOT a sister of Peder's and NOT one of Paul and Kari's five children. She is Peder's own wife, Kari Eliasdatter Slind, entered under her husband's American surname as married women routinely were. An earlier researcher read her as a sixth sibling. The five children are Peder, Anne, Ingebrigt, Mikel and John, and no more. ¶33
