Life story

Samuel Bennett senior, gent., of Coss (Cosh, Coshin), Co. Limerick; living in 1709. He and his wife Susanna (als Cooke) were the first party to the marriage settlement, 13 Feb 1708/9, of 'Samuel Bennett junior, son and heir apparent of' Samuel senior, with Anne, daughter of William Collis of Liseaige, Co. Kerry (Registry of Deeds book 2 p. 215, memorial 420, registered 2 May 1709). The deed also names his second son William, third son James, fourth son Charles and daughter Prudence as beneficiaries, and Francis Bennett of Ballyhendon, Co. Cork, as a party. ¶1

Memorial 420 read in full on the image (FamilySearch signed in, 2 Nov 2026; Registry of Deeds book 2 p. 215, registered 2 May 1709). Lease and release of 13 and 14 February 1708[/9]. The release is made between 'Samuell Bennett Sen[io]r of Cos[s] in the County of Limerick Gent and Susanna his Wife of the First part', 'the said William Collis' [of Liseaige, Co. Kerry, gent.] 'and Francis Bennet' [of Ballykendon, Co. Cork, gent.] 'of the second part', and 'Samuel Bennett Jun[io]r Son and Heir Apparent of the said Samuell Bennett Sen[io]r and Anne Collis Eldest Daughter of the said William Collis of the Third part'. It recites that 'a Marriage is soon to be had and solemnized' between Samuel junior and Anne, and that William Collis paid a portion of £300. The lands are Castlereagh (121 acres), 50 acres in Curroughrosty (parish of Galbally), 96 acres in Cloghasty and 7 acres in Mitchelstown, 'in the Parish of Ballingarry Barrony of Cosler [as transcribed; i.e. Connello] and County of Limerick'. Castlereagh and Curroughrosty, 'whereof the said Samuell Bennett Sen[io]r was hitherto seized in right of his said Wife', go to Samuel junior for life, then to his sons in order of seniority, 'and for want of such Issue then to the Heires Males of the Body of the said Susanna', and then to Susanna's right heirs. Cloghasty and Mitchelstown go to Samuel senior and Susanna for life and then to Samuel junior and his sons. In default of his sons they go to 'William Bennett Second Son of the said Samuell Bennett Sen[io]r', then to James Bennett, third son. After the deaths of Samuel senior and Susanna, those lands are charged with £300 payable to William, James and Charles Bennett and to Prudence Bennett, daughter of Samuel senior. This is the full memorial behind the Index Project abstract: it names Samuel junior as 'Son and Heir Apparent' of Samuel senior in so many words. His grade (Confirmed, on his link to Samuel Bennett) now rests on the image, not only on the abstract. ¶3

Living 29 Jun 1713: memorial 12165 (registered 1718) is a lease of Tankerdstown, parish of Uregare, Co. Limerick, to 'Samuel Bennett of Coshin, Co Limerick, gent.', for the lives of James, William and Charles Bennett, each 'son of' Samuel. They are the same third, second and fourth sons named in the 1709 settlement (memorial 420), so this is Samuel senior. The same day, his son Samuel the younger took Ballincallow (memorial 10967). His own parents were not found: the Registry of Deeds starts in 1708, and no Bennett will of the Limerick diocese before 1709 was found in the OCR text of Phillimore's Indexes to Irish Wills, vol. 3 (in Cork and Ross there are Richard Bennett of Nichollstown, 1636, and Francis Bennett of Cork, 1720, the latter probably the Francis of Ballyhendon who was a party in 1709). ¶4a

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confirmed, graded on his link to his son Samuel Bennett: the 1709 settlement, read in the Registry of Deeds Index Project's labelled index of the memorial, names the groom as his son and heir apparent. Continuity: the groom married Anne Collis. Four years later, in 1713, 'Samuel Bennett the younger' of Ballincallow, with 'Ann now wife of' him, leased Ballincallow (memorial 10967). In 1734/5 'Samuel Bennett junior of Ballincollow, deceased' is the father of George Bennett (memorial 55996). The name, the 'junior' and the wife Ann are the same in each, and no other Samuel Bennett junior appears in Co. Limerick in the index. Added. ¶2

Open questions and the next record to read 1

Test record: Limerick and Cork Bennett wills in Vicars' Index to the Prerogative Wills of Ireland, before 1700; the Limerick hearth money roll (1660s) and the Books of Survey and Distribution, Co. Limerick, for a Bennett at Coss/Cosh (barony of Coshlea). ¶4b
