About the family

Father of Jonathan: a Plymouth Colony deed recording the arbitration of his estate begins 'Whereas William Brewster late of Plym gent deceased left onely two sonnes surviveing vizt Jonathan the Eldest and Love the yeonger', says he died intestate, and calls them 'Jonathan and Love his sonnes' returning 'from the buriall of their father' (Plymouth Colony Deeds I:198, printed in The Mayflower Descendant 3:27, read on the page image). The arbitrators (Edward Winslow, William Bradford, Thomas Prence and Miles Standish, whom Jonathan called 'my fathers deere and auncient frends') gave Jonathan 'his fathers Armes' 'because hee was the first borne of his father'. The award, finished at Plymouth on 20 August 1645, closes by calling Jonathan and Love 'his onely children remayneing' (MD 3:29-30, read in the archive.org OCR text, not on the image). ¶2

Father: William Brewster of Scrooby, postmaster and archbishop's bailiff, died 1590 (William Brewster). The York administration act of 24 July 1590 grants his estate to 'Willmo Brewster filio eiusdem', and Stanhope's 1590 letter to Davison about young Brewster's claim to the Scrooby post 'in his father's tyme' identifies that son as Davison's former servant, whom Bradford's memoir makes the Elder (Dexter, England and Holland of the Pilgrims, pp. 317-324; Bradford pp. 488-490). This replaces the earlier 'Lead only' note about the Scrooby father. His mother is still not entered (see the father's notes on Prudence). ¶10

Mother (Probable): Mary (Smythe) Simkinson, widow of John Simkinson of Doncaster and daughter of William Smythe of Stainforth, per J. G. Hunt, NEHGR 124 (1970): 250-254, followed by Bunker (2010) and the GSMD Silver Book (Merrick 2000). The key evidence reported is the 1592 will of John Smythe of Hull calling William Brewster his nephew, alongside Thomas Simkinson; the Elder used Thomas Simkinson of Hull as his attorney in Leiden in 1609. Compiled sources only; the wills were not read. ¶12

Life story

Age: in the Leiden affidavit of 25 June 1609 he called himself 'aged about forty-two years' (Dexter p. 505), which points to birth about 1566-67, against ABT 1564 from Bradford's 'about 80' at death. The birth date is left as ABT 1564 here; both are approximations. ¶11

Death and estate: Jonathan Brewster's family record gives 'William Brewster dyed at Plymouth... 10th of Aprill 1644' (The Mayflower Descendant 1:7). On 5 June 1644 the General Court granted administration of 'mr Willm Brewster deceased' to Jonathan Brewster and Love Brewster, who exhibited an inventory on oath (Court Orders II:101; Plymouth Colony Wills 1:53). The goods were appraised by Myles Standish and Thomas Prence at £107 0s 8d, and the Latin books by Bradford, Prence and Reynor on 18 May 1644; goods and books together came to £150 0s 7d. ¶4

Estate division: the arbitrators awarded the Duxbury dwelling house, where Love had lived since his marriage, to 'Love and Sarah his wyfe', with half the estate. William Vassall surveyed the Duxbury upland: about 68 acres went to Jonathan, next to his own house, and about 43 acres of better land to Love. Jonathan was also to pay Love £4, partly for the board of 'Isaack Allerton a grandchild of the said Willm' (Plymouth Colony Deeds I:198-199, MD 3:28-29; p. 29 read in the OCR text only). ¶5

Bradford's account: William Bradford's history (written about 1650) lists 'Mr. William Brewster; Mary, his wife; with 2. sons, whose names were Love & Wrasling' among the 1620 Mayflower passengers, adding that 'the rest of his children were left behind, & came over afterwards'. He says Brewster was 'about 80. years' old at death, that his wife had died long before, that Wrestling died young and unmarried, that Love died in 1650, and that 'his eldst sone is still liveing, and hath 9. or 10 children'. Bradford does not name the eldest son. The birth year ABT 1564 is inferred from 'about 80' at death in 1644. ¶6

The case for this identification 2

Descent caveat: William Brewster is an ancestor of Raymond Hayter only if Mary Brewster (see her page), wife of John Turner the Elder, was the mother of Ruth (Turner) Prince. That Mary -> Ruth link is still Speculative; no record names Ruth's mother. Everything above Mary depends on it. ¶1

Identity: the Jonathan of the 1644 deed lived at Duxbury in a house he had built on his father's land and had a 'greate charge of children'. This matches Jonathan Brewster (see his page), born at Scrooby, Nottinghamshire, in 1593, whose own family record lists eight children, the younger ones born at Duxbury 1633-1641. Confirmed as Jonathan's father on that deed. ¶3

Corrections 1

Correction to the descent caveat above: the Mary Brewster to Ruth Turner link is now Probable, not Speculative (see Mary Brewster). William's own rating stays Confirmed as Jonathan's father (Plymouth Colony Deeds I:198). His place in Raymond Hayter's ancestry depends on four Probable links below Ruth Turner: Mary to Ruth, John Turner to Ruth, Job Prince (1695) to Job Prince (1723), and Job Prince (1723) to Elizabeth (Prince) Higgins Creed. FINAL.md for line 5 lists the record that would prove each one. ¶9

Alternatives considered and set aside 2

Wife not entered: Bradford names his wife as Mary, and Jonathan's family record gives the death of 'Mary Brewster the wyfe of william Brewster' at Plymouth on 17 April 1627. No record read names her as Jonathan's mother (Jonathan was born in 1593 and the marriage date is unknown), so she is not linked as his mother. ¶7

Lead only, not entered: compiled works make him the son of William Brewster of Scrooby, postmaster and bailiff of the Archbishop of York's manor there, and his wife Prudence. No record of that has been read. The next records are the Scrooby father's will and probate (Exchequer Court of York, Borthwick Institute) and the Nottinghamshire records naming the son. ¶8
