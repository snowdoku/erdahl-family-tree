About the family

Children (Scituate town register): 'Thomas, s. Thomas, July 10, 1686', 'Benjamin, s. Thomas, Feb. 28, 1693-4' and 'Job, s. Thomas, Apr. 14, 1695' are entered under Prince in the Scituate births (Vital Records of Scituate, vol. 1, p. 300; town-record entries, page image read). The mother is not named. ¶3

Children in Boston: the Boston birth registers give 'James of Thomas & Ruth Prince born Nov. 23' (1687 list, p. 175) and 'Benjamin of Thomas & Ruth Prince born Feb. 28' (1693 list, p. 208), both read on the page images; by OCR also Ruth, born 6 March [1690] (p. 189), who died on 12 September (p. 193) (Boston Record Commissioners vol. 9). Benjamin is the same child as 'Benjamin, s. Thomas, Feb. 28, 1693-4' in the Scituate town record. The family was entered in both towns, which fits the 1902 genealogy's statement that Thomas was a shipmaster sailing from Boston. This record names his wife Ruth, and strengthens the identification of his widow with the Ruth Prince who married Israel Silvester in 1701. ¶8

Father-in-law's will: John Turner Senior of Scituate named a grandson Thomas Prince and a daughter Ruth in his will of 4 March 1695[/6] (The Mayflower Descendant, vol. 5, pp. 41-45). ¶9

Brother: on 24 June 1704 Samuel Prince of Sandwich was appointed guardian of 'Thomas Benjamin & Job Prince sons unto Thomas Prince late of Boston', as their uncle; two of the boys were under fourteen (p. 47). On 7 July 1704 Samuel signed an acquittal, as guardian to 'the Children of my Brother Thomas Prince Late of Boston', for £185 14s received from Israel Silvester of Duxbury, shipcarpenter (p. 57). Elder John Prince's will of 1676 names both a son Samuel and his youngest son Thomas. ¶14

Life story

Record: the Kingston gravestone record names him 'Thomas, s. Elder John and Alice Honor of Hull,' born 1658, died 1696 in Barbadoes, and names Job (b. 1695) as son of Thomas and Ruth Turner (Vital Records of Kingston, pp. 116-117 and 371, G.R.1). ¶1

Named in his father's will: John Prince senior of Hull, in his will of 9 May 1676, calls Thomas 'my youngest Son' and says he was then under age, to live with his mother until the end of 1678 (Suffolk Probate Records vol. 6, original p. 128, docket 832). ¶10

Of age at Hull, 1680: with his sisters Martha Wheaton of Hull and Elizabeth Loring of Hingham he sold his share under 'the last will and Testam'. of our sd. ffather' Elder John Prince (d. 1676) to 'our beloved Brother Samuel Prince of the towne of Hull'; he was then 'of the town of Hull' and signed his own name; acknowledged 31 January 1680[/1] (Suffolk Deeds Lib. XII, 58-59). The deed calls the widow Ann 'our loveing mother', the usual usage for a stepmother. It proves Elder John's son Thomas had a brother Samuel; the 1704 Plymouth guardianship shows Ruth's husband Thomas had a brother Samuel Prince of Sandwich. Samuel Prince's letter from Sandwich of 27 Sept 1689 names his brother Isaac, so Samuel of Sandwich had the brothers Isaac and Thomas that Elder John's will gives his son Samuel; his descent from Elder John Prince is now Confirmed (see the Upgrade note on John Prince). ¶17

Widow's remarriage: the Scituate town register records the marriage of 'Israell [Silvester] and Ruth Prince, Oct. 3, 1701' (Vital Records of Scituate, vol. 2, p. 258, page image read). Israel and Ruth Sylvester then had Ruth (26 June 1701), Israel (5 May 1705) and Grace (Nov. 1706) at Duxbury (Vital Records of Duxbury, births pp. 150-151, page images read), the three Sylvester children the 1902 Prince Genealogy gives to Thomas's widow. A daughter of Thomas (married about 1685) could be no older than 15 in 1701, and the only other Ruth Prince found nearby, Ruth d. of Isaac and Mary, was born at Hull on 10 June 1698 (Vital Records of Hull, births p. 32, OCR) and married John Soper at Hull in 1718; so this Ruth Prince is taken to be his widow. This is an inference: the record does not call her a widow or name her husband. So Thomas was dead before 3 October 1701. ¶4

Estate (Plymouth County Probate Book 2): on 24 November 1703 Judge Nathaniel Thomas granted administration of the estate of 'Thomas Prince late of Boston in the County of Suffolk, Mariner', who had 'lately died intestate', to Israel Silvester and Ruth his wife, 'late relict widow' of Thomas Prince (p. 25). The inventory, taken on 13 December 1703, begins with £315 in cash (p. 31); the total was £426 18s. In her account of July 1704, 'Ruth Silvester late Relict widdow of Thomas Prince … now wife of Israel Silvester' charged for bringing up 'her former Husbands three sons he had by her' for eight years and six months: Thomas, Benjamin and Job. The eldest son Thomas had a double share and Benjamin and Job a single share each, with the widow's third (p. 47). ¶12

Death: the widow's charge for raising the boys for eight and a half years up to July 1704 puts his death in about January 1695/6, when Job was about nine months old. The place (Barbados) rests only on the Kingston gravestone record. ¶13

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 3

Confidence (Speculative): the only source read is the Kingston gravestone record, apparently a family memorial cut a century after his death; no contemporary record of him, of his marriage or of Job's birth has been seen. ¶2a

Confidence raised from Speculative to Probable by the Scituate births (vol. 1, p. 300): the town register names Thomas as father of Job, born 14 April 1695, who is the Kingston Job Prince (born 1695, son of Thomas and Ruth Turner, per the gravestone record). Not Confirmed because no contemporary record names Job's mother or calls Ruth Silvester the widow of Thomas Prince. Records that would confirm: Plymouth County probate (administration or guardianship) for Thomas Prince's estate c.1696-1701, or a deed naming Ruth Silvester as former wife of Thomas Prince (queued for the FamilySearch lane as S0119). ¶6

Confidence raised to Confirmed: the probate account is a primary record naming Job as one of the three sons Thomas Prince had by his wife Ruth. ¶15

Corrections 3

Correction: the 1902 Prince Genealogy (p. 32) says Thomas died at Barbados in 1716 and his widow then moved to Duxbury and married Israel Sylvester. The town records show Ruth Prince marrying Israel Silvester at Scituate on 3 Oct 1701, with Sylvester children born from 1701, so a 1716 death is impossible. The Kingston gravestone record's death in 1696 at Barbados fits, but no contemporary record of his death has been found; the death year is kept as 1696 on that authority. ¶5

Correction: the 'mother' in his father's will of 1676, with whom he was to live until the end of 1678, was his stepmother Anna, widow of William Barstow, whom Elder John married about 1670. His own mother, Alice Honor, had died about 1668 (Lincoln, History of Hingham, vol. 3 p. 120; compiled). ¶11

Correction: the earlier notes calling his confidence 'Speculative' (Kingston gravestone only) and then 'Probable' (Scituate births) are superseded. His rating is Confirmed, as the father of Job Prince (b. 1695), on a primary record: his widow's probate account of July 1704 names 'her former Husbands three sons he had by her … Thomas Benjamin & Job' (Plymouth County Probate Book 2 p. 47). The Confirmed rating covers only the Thomas-Ruth-Job link. His own descent from Elder John Prince of Hull is a separate link, rated Probable on Elder John's record. ¶16

Searches that found nothing 1

Searched, not found: no marriage of Thomas Prince and Ruth Turner and no death of Thomas Prince in the printed Vital Records of Scituate; no birth, marriage or death of a Thomas Prince in the printed Vital Records of Hull (OCR search). ¶7

What would settle it: Hull town records or the Plymouth/Suffolk probate of Thomas Prince (d. 1696), and the Scituate/Hull records of his marriage to Ruth Turner. Elder John Prince of Hull and Alice Honor are recorded here as a lead only; do not add them as a further Speculative generation. ¶2b
