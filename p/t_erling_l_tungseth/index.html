About the family

Family: his first wife was Lydia Larson, and their son Erling Martin Tungseth junior was born at Hawley, Minnesota on 6 Oct 1917 and became a Lutheran minister for 62 years, serving in the Twin Cities, Rochester, Milwaukee and Cambridge; he died in 2007. The other children linked on his Find a Grave memorial are Evelyn Marian (Haug), 1920-1980; Esther Doraine (Hinschberger), 1925-2014; and Arleen M. (Monan), 1933-1987. In 1966, aged 72, he married Elizabeth 'Liz' Syred Jewell Bostwick, 1897-1985. The 1930 census put the household at Thief River Falls. ¶6

Life story

Olga's youngest brother, and the one public figure in this family. He was born 9 Sep 1893 in Kristiansund, Norway - five weeks before his father sailed for Manitoba, and seven months before his mother carried him and his seven brothers and sisters out of Kristiansund on the Dominion Line on 2 May 1894. He is the last name on that emigrant list, entered simply as 'Erling Tungset'. Both of his Norwegian grandparents on the Tungset side died in the months around his birth and the family's departure: Tørri Jakobsdatter on 20 Jun 1893 and Paul Ellingsen on 14 Jan 1894. He never knew them, and he was an American from the age of seven months. ¶1

His own account of his schooling, given to the Minnesota Legislative Manual: Clay County rural schools, then Wahpeton High School in North Dakota, then Augsburg Seminary and the University of North Dakota. He was raised on the family farm in Clay County. ¶2

He was an ordained Lutheran pastor, and the denomination was the Lutheran Free Church, not his sister's Church of the Lutheran Brethren - Augsburg Seminary in Minneapolis was the Lutheran Free Church's own seminary, and the Farmers Independent of Bagley, Clearwater County, Minnesota, carried a notice on 7 Jul 1932 that 'Rev E L Tungseth of Thief River Falls will speak' at a Lutheran Free Church gathering. He is also recorded as the pastor officiating at First Lutheran Church in Goodridge, Pennington County, in 1930. Olga married into the Lutheran Brethren; her brother stayed in the Free Church. The two bodies were close neighbours in this part of Minnesota but distinct. ¶3

1895 Minnesota census: Erling Tungseth, 1 (faint; possibly 2), born in Norway, in his father Peter's household in Skree Township, Clay County (p. 6, read from the image; the census index reads 'Arling'). ¶10

1900 census: Erling Tungseth, son, born September 1893 in Norway, 6, single, immigrated 1894 (hard to read), at school, in his mother Caroline's household in Skree Township (ED 316, sheet 185A, line 7, read from the image). ¶11

Birth month: the 1900 census's September 1893 agrees with his birth on 9 Sep 1893 in Kristiansund; the 1910 census index's birth year 1894 is only a calculation from his age, 16, which is itself correct for April 1910, so there is no real conflict. ¶14

1905 Minnesota census: 'Earling' (Erling) Tungseth, 11, born in Norway, occupation struck through, at home in his father Peder's household in Skree Township, 11 years in Minnesota (Enumeration District 10, schedule sheet III, line 36, read from the image). ¶12

1910 census: Erling L. Tungseth, son, 16, single, born in Norway, immigrated 1894, speaks English, no occupation, in his father Peter P O Tungseth's household in Skree Township (ED 59, sheet 187A, line 40, read from the image). ¶13

Public office: elected mayor of Thief River Falls, Minnesota, serving 1931 to 1934; then Minnesota state senator for District 65, covering Clearwater, Pennington and Red Lake counties, for two terms, 1935 to 1942. He was elected on 6 Nov 1934 on the Nonpartisan ballot and is described in the legislature's records as an Independent and a Liberal Progressive. His majority over Paul A. Harris in 1934 was just 101 votes, which forced a recount. He was re-elected on 8 Nov 1938 and his second term ran to 3 Jan 1943. His total service was 2,919 days. His committees included Education, Public Welfare, Municipal Affairs and University. The Legislative Manual of 1941 records his religion simply as Lutheran, and his occupation at election as 'Ordained Lutheran Pastor'. ¶4

He died at Bellevue, King County, Washington on 29 Jun 1968, aged 74, and is buried at Sunset Hills Memorial Park there - a photograph caption on his Find a Grave memorial places the grave in the Lincoln section, lot 274, space 1. His brother Carl died in Chelan County and the Valders settled at Ferndale, so three of the eight ended in Washington state. ¶5

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade · 1 record that disagrees · 1 alternative set aside

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence is Probable rather than Confirmed because the birth date rests on his own statement to the Legislative Manual and on a gravestone, both of which agree, rather than on a baptism register. His Kristiansund baptism should be in the parish register for late 1893; it is not in the online index. ¶8

Records that disagree 1

Name spelling: Find a Grave gives 'Erling Ludwig'; a user-built Geni tree gives the Norwegian form 'Erling Ludvig'; the Minnesota Legislative Reference Library knows him only as 'E. L. Tungseth'. The Norwegian emigrant list of 1894 gives only 'Erling'. No Norwegian baptism has been found, so the middle name is recorded here in the Norwegian form with the American spelling noted. ¶7

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Not yet found: his 1968 obituary - the Internet Archive holds scans of the Thief River Falls Times for January to June 1968 and his death on 29 Jun falls just past them, so the July 1968 issues or the Bellevue and Seattle papers are the place to look; his ordination record and full parish list, which the Lutheran Free Church and successor Evangelical Lutheran Church in America archives would hold; his draft registrations for 1917-18 and 1942; and local coverage of the 1934 recount, for which the Farmers Independent of Bagley for 1 and 8 Nov 1934 is indexed in Chronicling America. ¶9
