About the family

Her parents: the Blennerhassett Pedigree, a manuscript written about 1733-36 by Captain John Blennerhassett of Kerry and printed verbatim by Hickson (Old Kerry Records, 1872, pp. 55-56), says: Anne Collis, first daughter of William Collis and Mary Cross, 'by her husband Samuel Bennett of Ballincollon, County Lymerick, deceased left issue three sons viz: George, William and Joseph, and four daughters viz: Mary, Isabella, Martha, and Prudence Bennett... Mary Bennett, eldest daughter of Samuel and Anne, married William Creed of Ballindall in the County Lymerick by whom she has issue, anno 1733, three daughters, viz: Anne, Jane, and Sarah Creed.' This is the passage that Noel Murphy cited as 'the Blennerhassett Pedigrees', and it is the source of her maiden name. The author was the husband of Elizabeth Cross, sister of Mary's grandmother Mary Cross, and he witnessed her parents' marriage settlement in 1709 (Registry of Deeds memorial 420). ¶4

Life story

Mary (Bennett) Creed, wife of William Creed of Ballyneal, Co. Limerick (Blennerhassett Pedigrees, as abstracted by Noel Murphy). Faulkner's Dublin Journal of 15 May 1762 reported that 'Mrs Mary Creed relict of William Creed late of Ballyneal' had died in Limerick 'after a tedious illness'; she was buried as a widow in St John's churchyard, Limerick, on 8 May 1762. As William and Mary Creed she baptised Ann (26 July 1733) and Jane (23 September 1735) at Ballingarry. ¶1

The Bennett family structure in the pedigree is borne out by the deeds: the marriage settlement of Samuel Bennett junior and Anne Collis, 13 Feb 1708/9 (memorial 420); a 1713 lease of Ballincallow to Samuel Bennett the younger for the lives of himself, Ann his wife and George his son (memorial 10967); and the 1734/5 marriage articles of George Bennett, eldest son of Samuel Bennett junior of Ballincollow, deceased, with Sarah Hilliard (memorial 55996), whose trustee Joseph Gubbins was the husband of Anne Collis's sister Martha, as the pedigree says. ¶5

Memorial 160893 read (FamilySearch signed in, 2 Nov 2026; book 241 p. 551, image 284; opening read on the image, the rest from FamilySearch's machine transcript). 'Bennett & wife to Creed', registered 3 June 1766. Deed of 4 November 1765 by which 'Jos[eph] Bennett of Windsor in the province of Nova Scotia, Cl[er]k, and Eliz[abe]th Bennett his wife', in consideration of £300, granted to 'W[illia]m Creed then late of Lim[eric]k' the following: their houses outside the South Gate of Cork, held by tenants including Bartholomew Brady; a house in Cork called the Trap House; and 'their proport[io]n of the farm of Ballincollow' in Co. Limerick, 'then under tenure to Mr W[illia]m Bennett'. The deed is subject to a proviso of redemption, so it is a mortgage. The witnesses were William Haskill of Halifax, Nova Scotia, and Patrick Dickson, then of Halifax and now of 'Bally bringe' [as transcribed], Co. Limerick, gent.; Dickson swore the memorial at Cork on 20 June 1766. The memorial states NO kinship between Bennett and Creed, so it cannot raise any grade. It does confirm that the grantor was the Nova Scotia missionary and that he held a share of the Bennett farm of Ballincollow. Graded on her link to William Creed: stays Probable. ¶10

Bennett context: the 1765 deed by the Rev. Joseph Bennett of Windsor, Nova Scotia, to William Creed, late of Limerick city (Registry of Deeds memorial 160893), shows that the Bennett with whom the captain is linked by tradition held a share of Ballincollow, Co. Limerick, the seat of a gentry Bennett family (Samuel Bennett the younger and his wife Ann, 1713; George Bennett of Castlecreagh, eldest son of Samuel junior, deceased, married Sarah Hilliard of Kerry in 1734-35; William Bennett, gent., of Ballincollow, 1749-1777). No record found links Mary Bennett, wife of William Creed of Ballyneal, to the Ballincollow family, and no record names the Rev. Joseph Bennett as her brother. ¶3a

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 3

Speculative as the mother of Captain William Creed (d. Charlottetown 1809): his family's tradition of a Bennett uncle who brought him across the Atlantic, and his son Joseph Bennett Creed, point to a Bennett mother; she is the only Creed wife named Bennett found in Co. Limerick in that generation. No record names him as her son. Added. ¶2

Upgrade (Speculative to Probable), graded on her link to her son William Creed: the pedigree names her brothers as George, William and Joseph Bennett. The captain's family tradition says he crossed the Atlantic with an uncle named Bennett, and he named his son Joseph Bennett Creed (see his page). The 1765 deed (memorial 160893) shows a Joseph Bennett, then of Windsor, Nova Scotia, holding a share of Ballincollow 'then in the possession of William Bennett', so he was of her family: her brother William Bennett, gent., held Ballincollow 1749-1777 and named a son William Collis Bennett (memorials 178400, 206318). That Joseph Bennett, the SPG missionary born about 1727-28 (Eaton), fits a younger son of Samuel Bennett junior, who married in 1709 and was dead by February 1735. In the same deed he mortgaged property to 'William Creed, then late of Limerick city', and Mary herself died a widow in Limerick city in May 1762. So her brother Joseph is documented with a William Creed recently of Limerick city, in Nova Scotia, two years before the captain is said to have reached St John's Island. This is inferred kinship (naming pattern, the uncle tradition, place and date), not a record naming the captain as her son, so Probable and not Confirmed. This supersedes the 'Speculative' and 'Grade unchanged (Speculative)' statements in the earlier notes. ¶6

Audit 11 (2 Nov 2026): Probable kept, graded on her link to William Creed, at the low end of Probable. The positive case is the family tradition of a Bennett uncle, the son's name Joseph Bennett Creed, and the 1765 mortgage by the Rev. Joseph Bennett (of the Ballincollow family, so very probably her brother Joseph of the pedigree) to 'William Creed then late of Limerick', made in Nova Scotia. That is a real convergence, not a bare name match. But each step is an inference: the captain's identity with the 1765 mortgagee, the Rev. Joseph's identity with the pedigree's Joseph, and the 'uncle' itself, which comes only from a descendant's typescript. The parentage also requires the 1809 Limerick notice's 'aged 80' to be wrong. Other Creed households in Co. Limerick with Williams (Uregare, Maidstown) are not excluded. If a baptism or will names another father for the captain, this link falls to Speculative. The test record is unchanged. ¶12

Records that disagree 1

Contradiction resolved for this grade: the 1809 Limerick Chronicle notice gave the captain's age as 80 (born about 1729). The pedigree lists only daughters for Mary 'anno 1733', so a son born about 1729 would contradict this parentage. The age of 80 is not reliable. It reached Limerick second-hand from Prince Edward Island, and the same notice gives the wrong day of death (18 April for 11 April). The family tradition gives a birth of 1739-1743. His uncle Joseph was born about 1728. His first child was born about 1767 and his last in 1790. All of these fit a birth after 1735. On that reading he was born after the three daughters, and the notice's age does not block the link. ¶7a

Corrections 1

Correction to the note on the 1771-73 Cork memorial (image 326, ark 3Q9M-CSJW-H9S1-L): the grantor is 'James Hubatt Creed' (the transcript also has 'Hullatt Creed'), not 'Breed'. He assigned to Edward Creed the leases of land at Killnap and of commons in the North Liberties of Cork, first granted by George and John Rye to a James Hubatt (Hullatt) in 1734, 1741 and 1742 and 'now Legally vested in the said James Hubatt Creed'. This was security for a bond of 13 May 1771 for £550, repayable on 18 February next. The deed was witnessed by John Terry of the City of Cork, gent., and Joseph Bennett, and the memorial by Joseph Bennett and a Clark, woolcomber; Edward Creed signed it. The memorial uses no kinship words for Bennett or between the two Creeds. Not graded. ¶11

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Rival: the only other William Creed of the right generation in Co. Limerick, William son of Francis Creed of Ballygrennan, died at Ballygrennan in 1797. ¶7b
