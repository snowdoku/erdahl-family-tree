About the family

Wife of Humphrey Turner and mother of John Turner: 'Lydia' is named as the mother of John, son of Humfry Turner, baptised at All Saints, Terling, Essex, on 24 March 1621/22 (FreeREG transcription of the Terling register, file line 651; also Ancestry 9841 index, recordId 27664165). No surname is given in either index. ¶1

Children (inference): besides John, a Lydia, daughter of Humphry Turner, was baptised at Little Baddow, Essex, on 17 Feb 1629/30 (Ancestry 9841 index; mother not shown). Humphrey Turner's will of 1669/70 names a daughter Lydia Doughtey. Not entered as a person. ¶3

Father's will read on the original: ERO D/ACW 6/45 (free image) has 'Also I give and bequeath unto Lidia Gaymer my daughter', as printed in NEHGR 151. She was still unmarried and under 21 in Oct 1613, so the will does not mention a Turner. Her grade is unchanged (Probable): it rests on the step from the Terling/Sandon Lydia to Lydia, wife of Humphrey Turner of Scituate, and the will does not touch that step. ¶12

Life story

Primary record: as 'Lydia his wife' she released her right in her husband Humphrey Turner's gift of land to 'her abovenamed sonnes' Joseph and Nathaniel Turner, when he acknowledged the deed on 3 July 1669 (Plymouth Colony Deeds vol. 3 pp. 175-176). She was therefore living in July 1669 and was the mother of at least Joseph and Nathaniel. The deed does not name her maiden name or her son John, so her place as John Turner's mother still rests on the Terling baptism of 'John son of Humfry & Lydia' (S0125, not yet read). ¶6

Maiden name Gaymer and parents (Probable): Vernon Dow Turner's article in NEHG Register 151 (1997) prints, from photocopies of the original registers, the Sandon (Essex) marriage of 'Humphry Turner and Lidda Gamar October 24' 1618 and the Terling baptism 'Lydia Gaymer the daughter of Richarde Gaymer was baptised the xviiith day of May 1602', and quotes Richard Gaymer's will of 8 Oct 1613 leaving part of the Angell tenement 'unto Liddia Gaymer my daughter' (ERO D/ACW 6/45). This pass checked the Terling side in FreeREG: the baptism of Lydia daughter of Richarde Gaymer, 18 May 1602; the marriage of Richarde Gaymer and Margaret Mason at Terling, 23 Jul 1590; the burial of 'Margaret, wife of Richarde Gaymer', 2 Jul 1602; and the Fairstead marriage of Richard Gaymer and Johan Robinson, 28 Feb 1602/3. Only one Lydia Gaymer occurs in FreeREG Essex 1540-1640, and the only Humfry Turner in the Terling baptisms names his wife Lydia. The surname is therefore entered, and Richard Gaymer and Margaret Mason are added as her parents, at Probable. The Sandon marriage, the hinge of the identification, is still unread (not in FreeREG; Essex Record Office, Sandon register, FHL film 0853324 as cited); reading it would make the Gaymer link Confirmed. ¶7

Birth: baptised at Terling All Saints on 18 May 1602 (FreeREG, file line 163). She was about 16 at her marriage in October 1618 and was named in her father's will in 1613, when she was under 21. ¶8

Surname tradition: Jacob Turner's Genealogy of the Descendants of Humphrey Turner (Boston, 1852) already heads the family 'Humphrey Turner, Lydia Gamer', with no source given, and the Turner memorial stone in the First Parish Cemetery, Norwell, erected in 1856, also calls her Lydia Gamer (inscription 'HIS WIFE LYDIA GAMER' as printed in the Briggs family history, 1937; the 1856 date per the compiled Turner sketch in archive.org genealogical-research-files-06-04-through-11, citing Briggs). The surname thus came down in the Scituate family 145 years before V. D. Turner published the Sandon marriage of 'Humphry Turner and Lidda Gamar' (24 Oct 1618) and the Terling baptism of Lydia, daughter of Richard Gaymer (18 May 1602). The agreement between an American family tradition and the English registers strengthens the identification. It is still not a record that names the Scituate Humphrey as the Sandon bridegroom (Somerby's English searches of the 1840s-50s may have fed the 1852 book; Somerby Papers 20:288 already had the Little Baddow baptism of Lydia, daughter of Humphrey Turner, 17 Feb 1629/30, per C. A. Torrey, TAG 19 (1943)), so confidence stays Probable. ¶9

Sandon marriage read on the image: under 1618, 'Humphry Turner and Lidda Gamar October 24' (FamilySearch DGS 004298638, item 24, image 70 of 93, image ark 3:1:S3HY-6WSQ-RVK; indexed in England, Essex, Parish Registers, 1538-1997 as 'Humpfry Turner' x 'Ladda Gemar', 1:1:XKZW-K5C). The film was marked center-only in the catalog (S0145), but the image opens from home through the index record. This verifies NEHGR 151's transcript. Her grade is unchanged (Probable): the step from the Terling/Sandon couple to Humphrey and Lydia Turner of Scituate is still by name, age and the NEHGR argument. ¶11

Terling baptism of her son, read in the index: FreeREG has 'John', son of Humfry TURNER and Lydia, baptised at Terling on 24 Mar 1621/22, the entry NEHGR 151 quoted. It shows a Humphrey and Lydia Turner at Terling, Lydia Gaymer's home parish, three years after the Sandon marriage of 1618. Her grade stays Probable: the step from the Terling couple to Humphrey and Lydia Turner of Scituate rests on names and family pattern, not on a record that links the two places. FreeREG has no transcription of the Sandon marriage (no Gaymer marriages in Essex 1615-1620 except Andrew Gaymer x Judeth Day at Colchester St Nicholas, 21 Aug 1620, and Robert Gaymer x Elizab Bush at Coggeshall, 30 Nov 1620). ¶10

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the identification · 1 alternative set aside · 1 search with no result · 2 open questions

The case for this identification 1

Identity (Probable): The identification of this Terling John with John Turner Sr. of Scituate rests on: (1) the parents' names, Humfry and Lydia, which match Humphrey Turner of Scituate and the wife 'Lydia' that Vernon Dow Turner (NEHG Register 151, 1997) reports from a Scituate land record (Plymouth Colony Deeds vol. 3, not read here); (2) Humphrey's will of 1669/70, which names 'my eldest son John' and a daughter Lydia (Doughty), and a Lydia daughter of Humphry Turner baptised at Little Baddow, about 4 miles from Terling, on 17 Feb 1629/30 (Ancestry 9841 index); (3) age: baptised March 1622, John Sr. married Mary Brewster in November 1645 at about 23; (4) Humfry Turner is the only Turner father named Humfry in the transcribed Terling baptisms of 1617-1640 (all nine FreeREG Terling Turner baptisms of those years were opened; the last, William, 12 May 1632, was son of William and Joane), and the two other Terling John Turners of 1623 and 1625 had fathers Thomas and John. It is Probable, not Confirmed, because no record read ties the Terling family to Scituate. ¶2a

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Maiden name (Speculative, not entered): Vernon Dow Turner, NEHG Register 151 (1997): 286-290, identifies her as Lydia Gaymer, daughter of Richard Gaymer, miller, of Terling, and cites a marriage 'Humphry Turner and Lidda Gamar' at Sandon, Essex, on 24 October 1618. A Lydia, daughter of 'Richarde Gaymer', was baptised at Terling All Saints on 18 May 1602 (FreeREG, file line 163), so a Lydia Gaymer of the right age existed in the same parish. The marriage itself was not found: FreeREG Essex marriages 1612-1625 and Ancestry 9852 (Turner x Gaymer, Gamer, Gamar; Lydia/Lidia/Liddia/Lidda Gaymer, Gamer, Gamar, Gaymor) have no entry. So the surname is left blank. ¶4a

Searches that found nothing 1

Death: not found. Humphrey Turner's will of 1669/70 names no wife, so she probably died before February 1670 (inference only). ¶5

Open questions and the next record to read 2

What would confirm it: Plymouth Colony Deeds vol. 3 naming Humphrey's wife Lydia, and an English record (the Sandon 1618 marriage or a Terling or Little Baddow entry) linking this Humfry to the later emigrant. ¶2b

What would settle it: the Sandon parish register, marriages 1618 (Essex Record Office; FHL film 0853324 as reported), and Richard Gaymer's will of 1613, which NEHGR 151 quotes as leaving a tenement to 'Liddia Gaymer my daughter' (will not read). ¶4b
