About the family

David Erdahl's great-grandfather, the father of Harry. A dairyman of Penticton, British Columbia. ¶1

Family witness: Raymond Hayter of Penticton witnessed his sister Alice's wedding to Kristian Verner Bak Thomsen at Penticton United Church on 24 February 1937, and, as 'Ray Hayter, Penticton', his brother Cecil's wedding at Kaleden on 11 October 1938. The witnesses at his own wedding in 1939, Mrs. V. Thomsen and V. Thomsen of Naramata, are now identified as his sister Alice Vera May and her husband Verner Thomsen. Proved: the same couple lived at Naramata in 1937, and Verner was his middle and usual name. ¶6

His siblings, proved from their BC registrations and gravestones: Rex (1905-1929), Cecil Elwood (1909-1963), Alice Vera May Thomsen (1912-1982), Bertha Olive Stevenson (1914-2000), Winnifred Eunice Oland (about 1916-2011), Edith Rita Borgstrom Hermiston (1919-1997) and Rena Pearl Bourne (1922-2015). All were born at Gaspereaux, Prince Edward Island. ¶7

Life story

Grave: Lakeview Cemetery, Penticton, Section L, plot 046-2, according to Find a Grave. His flat marker reads 'Raymond Hayter / 1907 - 1947'; the photograph has been read. ¶8

1911 census: Raymond, son, born August 1907, aged 3, Baptist, in his father Benjamin Hayter's household at Gaspereaux, Kings County, PEI. ¶12

1921 census: Raymond, son, 13, at school, in his father's household at Gaspereaux (Lot 61, Sturgeon sub-district); his father was a fisherman. ¶13

1931 census of Canada (read): Fraser Valley district 229, sub-district 2, Kent District Municipality, Harrison Mills, page 16, line 7. Raymond Hayter, aged 23, single, born PEI of PEI parents, English origin, Baptist, was a lodger in the household of Acton Kilby, a grocery merchant. Raymond worked as a salesman in the grocery store and had earned $800 in the past year. Kilby's general store at Harrison Mills is now the Kilby Historic Site. Raymond had come west by 1929, the year his elder brother Rex was killed in a logging camp near Harrison Mills. The rest of the family was still in Prince Edward Island at the 1931 census, and Raymond moved on to Penticton about 1934. ¶9

1931 census: Raymond Hayter, single, 23, born PEI of PEI-born parents, Baptist, a salesman in a grocery store earning $800 a year, living as an employee (first written 'lodger') in the household of Aston Kilby, merchant, at Harrison Mills, Kent District Municipality, British Columbia - near where his brother Rex had died in a logging camp in 1929. ¶14

Context: when Raymond lodged with Acton Kilby at Harrison Mills in 1931, Kilby's business was the general store, gas station and post office of a CPR-station settlement of about 300 people, many of them men of the Chehalis Logging Company, whose camp had killed his brother Rex in 1929 (Wrigley's British Columbia Directory, 1932). Raymond is not listed at Harrison Mills in the 1932 directory. ¶21

Federal voters' lists: Raymond Hayter is on the list for the Yale riding (which included Penticton) for the general election of 1935, so he was already living in the Okanagan by October 1935. In 1940 'Raymond Hayter' and 'Mrs Raymond Hayter' are on the same Penticton page of the Yale list as his mother ('Mrs Benjamin Hayter') and his sister Winnifred; in 1945 'Ray Hayter' and 'Mrs Ray Hayter' are again listed at Penticton. These come from the anonymous index to Canada, Voters Lists 1935-1980; the page images, which would give the street address and occupation, have not yet been read. ¶16

Arrival of the family in Penticton: his mother's own Pioneer Medallion application of 1966 says she first came to British Columbia on 27 July 1934, straight from Gaspereaux, PEI, to Penticton. Raymond's death registration gives him 13 years in Penticton by 1947, so he joined his mother and younger sisters at Penticton in 1934, after about five years in the Fraser Valley. ¶17

City directories, Penticton (British Columbia and Yukon Directory, read page by page): 1935 and 1937 'Hayter Raymond lab' (labourer), listed with his mother ('Mrs Benj wid') and his siblings Cecil, Olive and Vera; 1939 'Hayter Ray drvr Gartrell's Dairy', a delivery driver for Gartrell's Dairy Ltd (W. F. Gartrell, manager); 1940 'Hayter Ray drvr Valley Dairy', driving for John Mitchell's Valley Dairies; 1941 'Hayter Ray prop Hayter's Dairy', with a separate business entry 'Hayter's Dairy (R Hayter)'; 1942, 1943, 1944 and 1946 'Hayter Ray prop Ray's Dairy'. He is absent from the 1947 edition, which was canvassed after he fell ill in the summer of 1946. ¶19

Marriage registration, BC 1939-09-473800 (Penticton district no. 847, filed 18 Jul 1939): Raymond Hayter, dairyman, bachelor, aged 31, United Church, residing at Penticton, born Prince Edward Island, Canada; father Benjamin Hayter, born P.E.I.; mother's maiden name Hicken, born P.E.I. Married 15 July 1939 at the United Church manse, Penticton, by licence 38072 B, to Erna Knodel. Witnesses Mrs. V. Thomsen and V. Thomsen, both of Naramata - very probably his sister Alice Vera May Hayter, who had married Kristian V. B. Thomsen at Penticton on 24 Feb 1937 (BC 1937-09-443446). That identification is an inference, not yet proved. ¶2

The dairy: his records place him only on 'Green Avenue, Penticton', with no house number. He was already a dairyman at his marriage in July 1939, and his death registration gives seven years in the occupation, ending in June 1946, so he ran the dairy from about 1939 to 1946. Context: Green Avenue crosses south Penticton about two kilometres north of Skaha Lake. In the 1940s that was the rural edge of the District Municipality of Penticton, which agrees with the family memory that Harry was born on a farm. The exact lot has not been identified; a 1939-1948 BC directory, the 1940 National Registration return or the city assessment rolls would settle it. ¶10

The dairy business, as the records now show it: Raymond started in the Penticton milk trade as a driver for other men's dairies (Gartrell's in 1939, the Valley Dairy in 1940), set up on his own by 1941 as Hayter's Dairy, later listed as Ray's Dairy, and ran it until he stopped work in June 1946. His death registration's 'seven years' as a dairyman counts from about 1939, his first dairy job. The farm was on Green Avenue, where his widow was still living in 1948; the directories of these years print no street address for him. ¶20

Death registration, BC 1947-09-003117 (Penticton district no. 14/47): Raymond Hayter died at Penticton Hospital on 19 March 1947, aged 39 years 6 months 19 days, born 28 August 1907 in Prince Edward Island; permanent residence Green Avenue, Penticton; 13 years in Penticton, 18 years in British Columbia (so in BC from about 1929 and Penticton from about 1934); Canadian citizen; married, wife Erna Knodel Hayter; dairyman, last worked June 1946, seven years in that occupation; father Benjamin Hayter, mother Lydia Adelaide Hicken, both born Prince Edward Island. Informant his brother C. E. Hayter of Penticton. Buried 24 March 1947 at Lakeview Cemetery, Penticton; undertaker Penticton Funeral Chapel. He had been under a doctor's care since 11 July 1946. ¶3

He died when his son Harry was a small child - at most about seven, and possibly younger. ¶5

Will and probate: Raymond made a will. The BC Archives hold three files on his estate: letters probate granted by the Supreme Court at Penticton in 1947, Probate No. 832 (GR-3228, vol. 5, item 59); the full Penticton probate/estate file 832/1947 (GR-1428, file 1390, microfilm reel B09197); and the registered copy of the will in the British Columbia wills series, no. 47308 (file AAAF0428, box B08666). Letters probate, rather than letters of administration, are granted only to an executor named in a will. None of the three is digitised. The estate file normally includes the will, an inventory and valuation of the estate, and an affidavit naming the widow and each child with their ages; it has not yet been read. ¶18

3 corrections

Corrections 3

Correction to family memory: the family remembers Raymond as an immigrant to Canada from England. Both of his own civil records give his birthplace, and both his parents', as Prince Edward Island. The English origin is probably real but a generation or more further back, in the Prince Edward Island Hayter family; that is the next thing to test. ¶4

Correction: the compiled Island Register Hicken genealogy gives Raymond as born 15 August 1907 at Montague, P.E.I., and died 15 April 1947 at Penticton. His own BC death registration gives born 28 August 1907 and died 19 March 1947; the BC dates are preferred. Montague as his birthplace is unconfirmed - his siblings are all said to have been born at Gaspereaux, a few miles away. ¶11

Correction: the Island Register's Hicken lineage gives Raymond's birth as 15 August 1907 at Montague and his death as 15 April 1947. His own BC civil registrations give 28 August 1907 and 19 March 1947; the 1911 census agrees on August 1907. The civil records are preferred. ¶15
