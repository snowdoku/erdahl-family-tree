Life story

Jane Boyle, 'youngest dau. of Michael Boyle', wife of Thomas Caldwell, esq., and mother of Mildred, wife of Dean John Eveleigh (Brady, vol. 2, p. 428). The Blennerhassett Pedigree (c.1733) independently says Mildred's mother was a Boyle and 'cousin german to Primate Boyle'. That fits a daughter of Michael Boyle, Bishop of Waterford and Lismore (died 1635), whose brother Richard, Archbishop of Tuam, was the Primate's father. Her father is not added, because which Michael Boyle is meant is not stated in either source. Added. ¶1

Lodge's Peerage of Ireland (1789, vol. 1, p. 144; same text in the 1754 first edition) lists the children of Michael Boyle, merchant of London, and his wife Jane Peacock as eleven sons and two daughters, the last named being 'Jane, to Thomas Caldwell of Nowbry in Berkshire'. This is the same couple that Brady gives as Mildred Eveleigh's parents ('Thomas Caldwell, esq., by Jane, youngest dau. of Michael Boyle'). Lodge names her last of the two daughters, matching Brady's 'youngest'. Added. ¶3

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Probable, graded on her link to her daughter Mildred Coldwell: two independent sources (Brady, and the contemporary family pedigree) make Mildred's mother a Boyle, but neither is a primary record. ¶2a

Grade unchanged (Probable, on her link to her daughter Mildred Coldwell). Lodge adds her parents and her husband's place, Newbury, but says nothing of Mildred. ¶5a

Corrections 1

Correction: suggested that her father was Michael Boyle, Bishop of Waterford (died 1635). Lodge shows instead that she was the Bishop's SISTER: both were children of Michael Boyle, merchant of London, and Jane Peacock. This also reads the Blennerhassett Pedigree correctly. Its words 'Mildred Coldwell (daughter of * * * * Boyle who was cousin german to Primate Boyle)' fit Mildred herself as first cousin of Primate Michael Boyle, whose father, Archbishop Richard Boyle of Tuam, was Jane's brother. The dates fit too: Jane's brothers were born in the 1570s-80s, while a daughter of the Bishop could hardly have had a daughter old enough to marry the Dean, who was born about 1598. (The coordinator's working guess that the Bishop was a son of Roger Boyle of Preston and Joan Naylor is also wrong: Roger and Joan were the parents of the 1st Earl of Cork, and the Bishop was the Earl's first cousin, as Cotton's Fasti says.) Added. ¶4
