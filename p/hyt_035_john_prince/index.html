About the family

Son Samuel (compiled sources): the editors of the Hinckley Papers (Collections of the Massachusetts Historical Society, 4th series, vol. 5, 1861, footnote near p. 192) and the Prince Genealogy (c.1902) both make Samuel Prince of Sandwich, who was guardian in 1704 of 'the Children of my Brother Thomas Prince', a son of Elder John. They say he was born or baptised in 1649, married Mercy Hinckley in 1686, and lived at Hull, Sandwich, Rochester and Middleborough, dying in 1728. The printed Sandwich vital records show Samuel and Mercy's children there but name no father for Samuel. The record that could settle it is Samuel's own will (Plymouth County Probate Book 5 p. 748, 1728/30), or a Hull deed of the Elder's land. ¶10

Parents (Probable): his son Samuel's obituary of 1728, written by his grandson the Rev. Thomas Prince, calls him 'the eldest son of the Rev. Mr. John Prince Rector of East Shefford in Barkshire' and Elizabeth, 'a Daughter of Dr. Tolderbury D.D. of Oxford', and says he studied at Oxford two or three years before Laud's persecution drove him to Massachusetts (New England Weekly Journal, 16 July 1728; NEHG Register 5 (1851): 378-379). A John Prince, B.A. Magdalen 1601, was 'perhaps' rector of East Shefford in the 1620s (Foster, Alumni Oxonienses), and a John and Elizabeth Prince baptised daughters at Great Shefford in 1614 and 1615/16. Birth: 'died August 16, 1676 in the 66th year of his Age' places his birth between August 1610 and August 1611; no birthplace is recorded (East Shefford, his father's living, is likely but unproved). ¶14

Life story

Death: Hull's printed vital records give, from a gravestone, 'John, eldest s. of Rev. John, Aug. 16, 1676, in his 66th y. Rector of Little Shefford, Berks, Eng. Came to New England in 1633. One of original planters of Hull. First Ruling Elder of the Church' (Vital Records of Hull, deaths p. 72, Prince, g.r.; page image). The g.r. wording, with his English rectorship and arrival, suggests a later memorial stone rather than a contemporary one. His birth about 1610 is inferred from the age. ¶1

Savage (compiled, OCR only): Savage's Genealogical Dictionary (1860, vol. 3, within the Prince entries) lists John Prince of Hull's children as John (1638), Elizabeth (1640), Joseph (1642), Martha (1645), Job (1647), Mary (1649), Samuel, Sarah (1651), Benjamin (1652), Isaac (1655), Deborah (25 August 1657) 'and Thomas', and says his will of 9 May 1676 'names only eight ch.' without saying which. Savage calls the first wife Margaret, not Alice Honor, and gives the death as 6 August 1676 against 16 August in the Hull record. These are compiled statements and leads only; the Suffolk probate file of the 1676 will (queued for the signed-in lane) would show whether Thomas is named. ¶4

Will: John Prince senior of Hull made his will on 9 May 1676, 'weake in body'. It names his wife Anne and eight children: John, Joseph, Elizabeth (wife of Josiah Loring), Martha (wife of Christopher Wheaton), Job, Samuel, Isaac, and Thomas, 'my youngest Son', who was 'yet under age' and was to live with his mother until the end of 1678. Executors were his sons John and Joseph; the witnesses were Zechariah Whitman and Nathaniel Bosworth. The witnesses swore to it on 18 October 1676 before Governor John Leverett and Edward Tyng, Assistant (Suffolk Probate Records vol. 6, original p. 128, Suffolk docket 832; inventory recorded vol. 12 p. 96, not read). ¶5

Savage's 'names only eight ch.' is borne out: Mary, Sarah, Benjamin and Deborah, listed by Savage, are not named in the will. ¶7

Link to Thomas strengthened: in 1704 Samuel Prince of Sandwich acted as guardian for 'the Children of my Brother Thomas Prince Late of Boston' (Plymouth Probate Book 2, p. 57), and Elder John's 1676 will names both a son Samuel and a youngest son Thomas. He stays Probable only because no record states that Samuel of Sandwich is Elder John's son Samuel. ¶9

Deed by his children, 1680 (new primary record): 'wee Thomas Prince Christopher Wheaton & Martha his wife of the town of Hull and Josiah Loaring and Elizabeth his wife of Hingham' sold to 'our beloved Brother Samuel Prince of the towne of Hull' for £54 'all our rights title and interest of and in the Estate of our dearely beloved ffather Elder John Prince deced. in Hull aforesd. in the yeare One thousand Six hundred Seventy & Six according to the true tenure of the last will and Testam'. of our sd. ffather', Samuel to honour 'the dowry of our loveing mother Ann Prince the Relique of Elder John Prince'. Signed 1680 by Thomas Prince, Christopher Wheaton and Josiah Loring (Martha and Elizabeth with marks or seals); witnesses John Prince, Hannah Liscom, John Newman, Seth Shove; acknowledged by Thomas Prince, Christopher Wheaton and Josiah Loring before John Hull, Assistant, 31 January 1680[/1]; entered 21 May 1681 (Suffolk Deeds Lib. XII, 58-59, printed transcription). This is a primary record that Elder John's son Thomas, the 'youngest Son' of his 1676 will, was of age by January 1680/1 and had a brother Samuel Prince, then of Hull. ¶12

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Confidence raised to Probable: the will is the record the earlier note asked for. It shows Elder John had a youngest son Thomas still under 21 in 1676, so born after about 1655, which fits the baptism of 8 August 1658 given by the Prince Genealogy and the Kingston gravestone's 's. Elder John' for Thomas Prince (b. 1658). It is not Confirmed because no record yet states in so many words that Thomas of Scituate, husband of Ruth Turner, is this Thomas of Hull. ¶6

Upgrade: Probable to Confirmed, as father of Thomas Prince (husband of Ruth Turner). Three primary records now tie the family together. (1) His will of 9 May 1676 names sons John, Joseph, Job, Samuel, Isaac and Thomas, 'my youngest Son' (Suffolk Probate 6:128). (2) The 1680 deed above: his son Thomas sells to 'our beloved Brother Samuel Prince of the towne of Hull' his share of 'our dearely beloved ffather Elder John Prince' (Suffolk Deeds XII:58-59). (3) Samuel Prince's own letter to his father-in-law Governor Thomas Hinckley, dated 'Sandwich, Sept 27, '89', reports news 'from my brother Isaac', who had it from letters brought 'for some of Scituate' (Collections of the Massachusetts Historical Society, 4th ser. vol. 5, The Hinckley Papers, pp. 216-217, printed from the original among Hinckley's papers). So Samuel Prince of Sandwich had brothers Isaac and Thomas: Isaac by his own letter, Thomas by the 1704 Plymouth guardianship, where 'Samuel Prince of Sandwich' acts for 'the Children of my Brother Thomas Prince Late of Boston', the sons Thomas, Benjamin and Job of Ruth (Turner) (Plymouth Probate Book 2 pp. 47, 57). Elder John's sons Samuel, Isaac and Thomas are the same set of brothers; Isaac Prince of Hull married Mary Turner, Ruth's sister, and baptised a daughter at Scituate in 1681. That Samuel of Hull (1680) and Samuel of Sandwich (1689, 1704) are one man is thus shown by records, no longer only by the Hinckley Papers editors and the 1902 Prince Genealogy. Samuel Prence of Sandwich was admitted a freeman of Plymouth Colony on 3 June 1690 (Plymouth Colony Records vol. 6 p. 239). ¶13

The case for this identification 1

Identity (Speculative): the Kingston gravestone record names Thomas Prince (b. 1658) as 's. Elder John and Alice Honor of Hull' (Vital Records of Kingston, p. 371, G.R.1), and the 1902 Prince Genealogy (p. 32) makes Thomas the youngest of Elder John's nine children, baptised 8 Aug 1658. No contemporary record of Thomas's birth or baptism has been found (not in the Hull vital records). What would raise it: Elder John Prince's will or estate settlement (Plymouth Colony/Suffolk probate, 1676) naming a son Thomas. ¶2

Corrections 1

Correction: the first wife 'Margaret' that Savage attaches to him (noted in patches 026 and 035) belongs to a different man, John Prince of Hingham, who died in February 1690 leaving a widow Margaret (Prince Genealogy memoir, introductory section; that John's probate is Suffolk vol. 8 p. 25). Elder John of Hull married first, at Watertown in May 1637, Alice Honor, who died about 1668. About 1670 he married secondly Anna, widow of William Barstow of Scituate (d. 1668). Anna is the 'beloved wife' of his 1676 will (Lincoln, History of Hingham, 1893, vol. 3 p. 120, and vol. 2 p. 34; the Prince Genealogy memoir; both compiled, no marriage record cited). ¶8

Searches that found nothing 2

Origin search: the England baptism index (Ancestry 9841, Select Births and Christenings) has no John Prince baptised at East (Little) or Great Shefford, Berkshire, about 1605-1615, with a father John. It does have Joane Prince, daughter of John and Elizabeth, baptised at Great Shefford on 9 October 1614, which is consistent with a John and Elizabeth Prince family in the Sheffords, the reputed parents, but does not name the father as rector. A John Prince, son of John, baptised at St Mary's, Reading, on 3 August 1609 belongs to a Reading family with children to 1619 and would have been in his 67th year in August 1676, not his 66th; he is not taken as Elder John. No probate of Elder John Prince was found in the archive.org text of the Suffolk probate index (the OCR separates names from entries). His confidence stays Speculative: no record read names Thomas (born 1658) as his son. ¶3

Samuel Prince's will (negative for this link): Samuel Prince, 'late of Rochester now of Middleboro'', made his will on 10 October 1726; it was proved on 4 August 1730 (Plymouth County Probate Book 5 pp. 748-749, 751; FamilySearch DGS 7051008). It names his wife Mercy and his children Samuel (deceased), Joseph, Thomas, Mercy, Martha (wife of Ezra Bourne) and Mary (wife of Peter Thacher). It does not name his father, any Hull land, his brother Thomas or Thomas's sons, so it does not settle whether he was Elder John's son. The Thomas Prince named in it is Samuel's own son. The codicil and witnesses (p. 750) were not read. ¶11
