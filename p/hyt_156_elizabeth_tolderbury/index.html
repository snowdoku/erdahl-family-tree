About the family

Wife of the Rev. John Prince, rector of East Shefford, and mother of Elder John Prince of Hull (Probable): Samuel Prince's obituary (New England Weekly Journal, 16 July 1728; NEHG Register 5:378) says the rector 'had Married Elizabeth a Daughter of Dr. Tolderbury D.D. of Oxford, by whom he had 4 Sons & 7 daughters that all grew up', and that Elder John was 'their eldest son'. A John and Elizabeth Prince had daughters Joane (9 Oct 1614) and Dorothye (2 Mar 1615[/16]) baptised at Great Shefford, Berkshire (Ancestry 9841 index). ¶1

Parents (Speculative): Noyes-Gilman Ancestry (1907), p. 306, calls her 'Elizabeth Toldervey, daughter of Walter and Ann Toldervey and granddaughter of Rev. John Toldervey, "Bedell of Arts, University of Oxford," and his wife Alyce', married about 1609, probably at Oxford. The same parentage is repeated in the compiled chart book Descendants of William Richardson (1620-1657) (archive.org descendantsofwil00klue: 'Elizabeth Tolderbury, dau of Walter and Ann Toldervey'). Neither book cites a record. It conflicts in part with the earliest statement, the 1728 obituary by the Rev. Thomas Prince ('a Daughter of Dr. Tolderbury D.D. of Oxford'), but it explains the 'granddaughter' reading in the 1902 Prince Genealogy and it fits what the Oxford records show: John Toldervey was elected esquire bedell of Arts in 1540 and was dead by 10 May 1567, and a Walter Toldervey was a citizen of Oxford by 1613 (Register of the University of Oxford II.1, citizens' lists, index p. 308). The Toldervey family were Oxford citizens (Henry Toldervey was mayor; James, John and William Toldervey also appear). Walter and Ann are entered as Speculative parents: a plausible, specific compiled claim with a real Walter Toldervey in Oxford at the right date, but no baptism, will or marriage seen. FreeREG has no Toldervey, Toldervy, Tolderbury or Tolderby entries in Oxfordshire or Berkshire 1550-1650 (searched 1 Nov 2026). Reading John Toldervey's will (Oxford Chancellor's Court, proved 1567/68) or Walter Toldervey's probate would settle it. ¶3

Sisters: Katherine Toldervey (m. Edward Garrard of Inkpen, East Shefford 1626; called 'da: to Walter Toldervey of the Citty of Oxford' in the 1665-6 Visitation of Berkshire) and Ursula Toldervey (m. John Milton, East Shefford 1621/2) appear to be her sisters: Edward Garrard's 1633 will reportedly calls both John Prince and John Milton his brothers-in-law. Not added to the tree. ¶8

Life story

Ursula Toldervey married John Milton at East Shefford (original place given as East Shefford; the index normalises it to Great Shefford) on 17 Feb 1621[/2] (England, Berkshire, Parish Registers index, 1:1:6D9R-BRWY), in the Rev. John Prince's own parish, eight months before Edward son of John and Elizabeth Prince was baptised there. She is the only Toldervey in the Berkshire indexes 1570-1660, and very probably a sister or kinswoman of Elizabeth (Toldervey) Prince. Circumstantial only: the entry names no parent. ¶6

Marriage (reported, not read): WikiTree (Tolderby-3) cites the Findmypast Berkshire Marriages index, from the Great Shefford bishop's transcript, for the marriage of Elizabeth Toldervey and 'John Prynce' at Great Shefford on 26 Sep 1606. That corrects Noyes-Gilman's 'about 1609, probably at Oxford'. It also gives a baptism of John, son of John Prince, at Great Shefford on 3 Sep 1612 (Findmypast Berkshire Baptisms index, bishop's transcript; mother not stated in the report). Note the conflict: Elder John Prince died 16 Aug 1676 'in his 66th year', which points to a birth in Aug 1610-Aug 1611, not 1612. Either the age at death is out by a year or two (common) or the 1612 John is a younger brother of the same name, or his first son died. Not a grade change: her link to Elder John stays Probable. ¶7a

Note: Edward Garrard of Inkpen's will (PROB 11/163/610, dated 23 Jan 1630/1, image read) names 'Mr John Prince my brother in lawe' as an overseer, and a memorandum is made 'before my sonne in lawe John Williams and my Nephew John Prince in his owne howse'. The nephew John Prince is probably her son (born by about 1610 to be a witness in 1630). Grade unchanged; see Walter Toldervey. ¶9

Maiden name: the surname is entered as the obituary gives it. The 1902 Prince Genealogy misquotes the same obituary as 'granddaughter of Dr. Tolderbury'; the printed text says 'Daughter'. Foster's Alumni Oxonienses has no Tolderbury who was a Doctor of Divinity: it lists John Toldervey, B.A. 1537, esquire bedell of the university 1541, will proved at Oxford 23 June 1567; Christopher Tolderbey, M.A. 1574, canon of Lincoln 1583; and William Toldervey, an Oxford bookseller privileged in 1617, aged 27. Which of these, if any, was her father is unknown; no father is entered. ¶2

Surname: the index to the Register of the University of Oxford, vol. II, treats Tolderbery, Tolderberg, Tolderbey and Tollerbie as spellings of the Oxford family Toldervey, so the 1728 obituary's 'Tolderbury' and Noyes-Gilman's 'Toldervey' name the same family. A Walter Toldervey (Oxford citizen 1613) witnessed the will of Thomas Harvey of West (Great) Shefford on 1 Sep 1620, in the parish where her daughters were baptised 1614-1620 (PCC, Soame fol. 114). This raises Walter (see his page) to Probable as her father. Her own grade, on her link to Elder John Prince, is unchanged. ¶5

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the identification · 1 open question

The case for this identification 1

Toldervey/Tolderbury question weighed: (1) the 1728 obituary by her grandson Rev. Thomas Prince (New England Weekly Journal, 16 Jul 1728; NEHGR 5: 378) says 'a Daughter of Dr. Tolderbury D.D. of Oxford'. This is a family statement written about 120 years after the marriage. (2) The Oxford degree register for 1571-1622 (Clark, Register II pt 3) has only two Tolderveys: Christopher Toldervey (variants Tolerve, Tollerby), student of Christ Church 1569, M.A. 1574, and John Toldervey of Balliol, B.A. 1621. No Toldervey took the B.D. or D.D. in that period, and John Toldervey the esquire bedell (d. 1567) was not a doctor either. So no 'Dr. Tolderbury D.D.' existed at Oxford in the right years; the obituary's title is probably an inflation of a university connection (the bedell, or Christopher of Christ Church). (3) Noyes-Gilman (1907) gives Walter and Ann Toldervey and the bedell John as grandfather; it cites nothing, but Walter Toldervey is a real Oxford citizen of 1613. Christopher Toldervey later lived at Chartham and Ash-next-Sandwich, Kent (Canterbury marriage licences; Ash monument to Christopher and Jane), which fits the Berkshire curate less well. Verdict: the surname Toldervey and an Oxford origin are well supported; the father's name is not proved either way. Walter and Ann stay Speculative; no Christopher link entered. ¶4
