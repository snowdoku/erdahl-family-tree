Life story

Identified in Norway. Baptism: 'Erik Johns Eidem', BORN 28 OCTOBER 1817, baptised 9 NOVEMBER 1817 - Selbu parish register, Selbu local parish 1816-1824, births and baptisms, pages 44-45, serial no. 26. Father JOHN ERIKSEN; mother BRYNHILD JENSDATTER, residence Eidem. ¶4

Correction, and the answer to the question whether he and Sivert Johnson Eidem were brothers: THEY WERE NOT. Erik's father was JOHN ERIKSEN, a husmann (cottager) on the Eidem farm - household 016 of the 1825 census of Eidem, aged 38 - while Sivert Johnsen's father was JOHN SIVERTSEN, the gaardmand of household 001 on the same farm. Two unrelated Johnsen lines living on one farm, which is exactly the trap that the farm-name surname sets. The 'Erick Johnsen / John Erickson' alternation that looks so suggestive runs inside the John Eriksen family, not into ours. ¶5

But they became IN-LAWS. His daughter BRYNILD ERIKSDATTER, b. 1843, married JOHN SIVERTSON EIDEM, b. 1842, in Selbu church on 3 April 1866 - the entry names the bride's father as 'E. Johnsen' and the groom's as 'S. Johnsen'. That marriage is why three Eidem land entries cluster on one section of Union County: Severt S. Eidem, his brother John Sivertson Eidem, and John's widow - whose father and brother took out patents in the same county. ¶6

1865 census: 'Erik Jonsen', aged 47, Husfader, married, HUSMAND M. JORD (cottager with land), at rural residence 0054 PLADSEN in the Eidem cluster, census district 005 Femte, Selbu - four doors from Sivert Jonsen's freehold. The holding sowed a quarter-barrel of barley, an eighth of mixed grain, half of oats and one and a quarter of potatoes, and carried 4 cows, 6 sheep and 5 goats, and no horse. Five residents: himself, ANNE LARSDATTER 56 (his wife), Lars Eriksen 20, Ingeborg Eriksdatter 17 and Johana Eriksdatter 12. His daughter Brynild, 22 and about to be married, was out in service at Eidem Negaard. ¶7

EMIGRATION, 12 MAY 1868: Trondheim police office, First accession, Emigrant protocols no. 1 (1867-1870), group 00028, page 51, line 13, person no. 0249, sequence no. 725. 'Erik Johnsen Eidem', age 50, residence SELBO, agent T. M. Moe, contract 11 May and expedition 12 May 1868, ticket 86 kroner 60 oere, DESTINATION 'QVEBEK' - Quebec, the Canadian route by which most Trondelag emigrants of the 1860s reached the upper Midwest. His wife ANNE LARSDATTER (59) and his daughter INGEBORG ERIKSDATTER (19) are in the same party. ¶8

Land patent, 1874: patent of 10 DECEMBER 1874, document number 666, accession STA-SD1060__.102, for the SOUTH HALF OF THE SOUTH-EAST QUARTER OF SECTION 22 and the WEST HALF OF THE NORTH-EAST QUARTER OF SECTION 27, TOWNSHIP 92 NORTH, RANGE 50 WEST OF THE 5TH PRINCIPAL MERIDIAN, Union County, Dakota Territory. ¶1

The patent immediately before his, document 665 of the same day, belongs to JOHN ERICKSON EIDEM - that is, to a son of an Erick. Consecutive numbers on the same day for a father and son is the same pattern as Brynhild Jonsdatter and John Sivertson Eidem eighteen months earlier. ¶3

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade · 1 on the identification

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Rated Probable rather than Confirmed on the American end: the baptism, the 1865 household and the 1868 emigration are one man beyond doubt, and he is the only Erik Johnsen Eidem of Selbu in the emigrant index, but no document yet read ties that man to the Union County patent in so many words. The land-entry case file would. ¶9

The case for this identification 1

Why he may matter: his patronymic is JOHNSEN, the same as Sivert Johnson Eidem (1815-1872), the head of the Elk Point Eidem family. Two men surnamed Eidem, both sons of a John, both in Union County in the first years of settlement, are more likely to be brothers than strangers - but Eidem is a FARM name, not a family name, and men from the same farm in Selbu need not be related at all. Rated Speculative and no link asserted. ¶2
