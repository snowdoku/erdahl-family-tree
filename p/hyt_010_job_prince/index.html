About the family

Children: the Boston birth register records eight children of Job Prince and Elizabeth his wife: Hezekiah Blanchard (15 August 1749), Job (28 September 1751), Elizabeth (28 May 1753), Thomas (27 September 1754), James (20 February 1758), Samuel (2 April 1761), Samuel (9 March 1766) and Abigail (14 March 1767) (Record Commissioners vol. 24, pp. 271-315, each read from the page image). A daughter Sarah (c.1755) appears in the OCR text but was not checked against the image. ¶2

Siblings (Kingston VR): Thomas (1720, G.R.1), Ruth Leach (1721-1794, G.R.1), Kimball (born 28 April 1726, a town-register entry 's. Job and Abigail', also G.R.1; died 10 April 1814 aged 88), James (1729-1759, died in North Carolina, G.R.1) and Christopher (born 8 March 1729/30, town-register entry 'Cristifer (Princ), s. Job and Abigail'; died 1799 in Nova Scotia, G.R.1). Thomas is probably the Capt. Thomas Prince who died 5 January 1768 aged 47 years 3 months 2 days, whose widow Lydia died in 1809 (inferred from the age; the entry does not name his parents). ¶9

Children's deaths (newspaper index): 'Abigail, daughter of the late Capt. Job Prince', died in Boston aged 23 (Columbian Centinel, 11 January 1792; index page image read). Two James Prince entries could be his son James (b. 1758): a Capt. James Prince who died at Providence in 1819 aged 61, and James Prince, 'late Collector of Newburyport', who died in 1830 aged 70; neither names his father (leads only). ¶21

Life story

Birth: the printed Kingston vital records list 'Job, ch. Job and Abigail (Kimball), ----, 1723', taken from the gravestones of the Old Burying Ground (G.R.1); no day or month is given (Vital Records of Kingston, p. 116, read from the page image). ¶6

Origin (Probable): he is identified with the Kingston-born Job of 1723 because (1) that Job is recorded as dying in Boston in 1790, and the Suffolk probate index has an administration on a Job Prince in exactly that year (docket 19540); (2) he is not in the Boston birth register; (3) he would have been about 25 at his Boston marriage in December 1748; (4) his Boston children were named Job, Thomas, James and Abigail, the names of the Kingston Job's father, two of his brothers (Thomas b. 1720, James b. 1729) and his mother; (5) no other Job Prince of the right age was found in the printed vital records of Hull, Salem, Marblehead, Beverly, Duxbury, Plymouth or Kingston. Nothing contradicts it. ¶8a

Marriage: Job Prince and Elizabeth Allen were married in Boston on 26 December 1748 by the Rev. Jonathan Mayhew (Boston Marriages, Record Commissioners vol. 28, p. 259, read from the page image). ¶1

Boston office, 1769: at a meeting of the Boston selectmen on 13 December 1769, Benjamin Henderson, collector of taxes for 1769, presented 'Capt. Job Prince and Mr. Edward Carnes' as his bondsmen (sureties), and they were accepted (Selectmen's Minutes, Record Commissioners vol. 23, p. 51, read from the page image). The printed minutes also name Job Prince as Henderson's bondsman for the taxes of 1770, 1771, 1772 and 1773, but those entries were seen only in the OCR text, not on the page images. ¶12

Fire-ward, 1774: at the annual Boston town meeting of 1774 the town chose sixteen fire-wards, among them 'Capt. Job Prince' (Boston Town Records 1774, Record Commissioners vol. 18, p. 151, read from the page image). Fire-wards directed the fighting of fires and the saving of goods. The OCR text shows him chosen fire-ward in other years of 1770-1777 as well, but only the 1774 entry was checked on the image. ¶13

PEI land, 1775 (enrichment): he bought from 'David Higgins of the Island of Saint John Esqr' and Elizabeth his wife (his daughter Elizabeth, married to Higgins in 1773) one undivided third of a Kings County township for £1,000 sterling; acknowledged at Boston 18 Feb 1775, registered Charlottetown 13 Sept 1784 (PEI Land Registers 1795-1797, images 325-326, ark 3:1:3Q9M-CSRV-G96J; transcript read). No relationship is stated in the deed. This is the origin of the Prince interest in Island land behind the later Creed-Prince dealings. ¶32

Parker deposition, second page: image 263 of 'Suffolk. Deeds 1819-1825' continues Samuel D. Parker's 1823 statement and keeps two groups of heirs apart: the 'Heirs at law of Capt Job Prince Junr the husband of said Elizabeth' (the widow Elizabeth Prince, some of them minors) and the 'heirs of old Captain Job Prince who executed the release or conveyance to Elizabeth Prince'. It confirms that 'old Captain Job Prince the father of James Prince' on image 262 is this man (b. 1723), not his son Job Jr. (d. 1798). The page adds no relationship word for Elizabeth Creed. The other depositions in images 255-264 and 288-290 were scanned in the transcript for 'daughter', 'Creed' and 'Higgins' with no further hit; image 290 (deposition about Benjamin Clarke and the Massachusetts General Hospital sale) concerns the estate of Job Prince Jr. ¶33

Occupation: R. P. Murray's study of Captain William Creed describes 'Capt. Job Prince of Boston' as Creed's partner in privateering ventures in 1779-80 (a compiled study, used here as a lead only). ¶3

War years, 1779: at the adjourned Boston town meeting of Wednesday 4 August (1779, from the volume's sequence and the weekday), 'Capt. Job Prince Senr.' was named for Ward No. 7 on the committee to raise subscriptions for fortifying the harbour (Boston Town Records, Record Commissioners vol. 26, p. 74, read from the page image). At a later meeting in the same volume (p. 114, read from the page image; the year is not on that page, probably 1780) 'Capt. Job Prince' was first-named on the committee chosen to decide what land the town should improve for raising vegetables. ¶14

Vegetable land committee, 1781: on Monday 26 March 1781 the Boston town meeting voted that 'Capt. Job Prince Senr.', Isaac Pierce, Mr. Rouse and Jeremiah Belknap be a committee to improve the land at West Boston for raising vegetables (Boston Town Records 1781, Record Commissioners vol. 26, p. 181, read from the page image). The sweeper's OCR reading that he was one of the proprietors of a twenty-acre tract at West Boston was not checked on the image and is not relied on here. ¶15

Merchant, 1785: at a meeting of the Boston selectmen on 11 August 1785, collector Thompson proposed 'Isaiah Doan Merch't. & Capt. Job Prince Senr. Merch't.' as his bondsmen, who were approved (Selectmen's Minutes, Record Commissioners vol. 25, p. 276, read from the page image). ¶16

Estate administered by James Prince: an additional inventory of 'Job Prince' of Boston, deceased, lists a piece of land at West Boston valued at £35, sworn by James Prince, administrator, on 6 March 1792 (Suffolk County probate records, vol. 91, pp. 162-163, FamilySearch DGS 7703713 image 108; image read). The entry does not state James's relationship, and no heirs are named. A son James was born to Job and Elizabeth Prince in 1758, and in 1812 a James Prince paid $1,048.88 into the estate of Elizabeth Creed (Suffolk probate vol. 110 p. 389), which fits James as Elizabeth's brother handling family money. The 1790 letters of administration (docket 19540) and any distribution naming heirs have not yet been found in vols. 89-90. ¶18

Probate: the printed Suffolk County probate index lists two estates for a Job Prince in this period: an administration in 1790 (docket 19540) and a will in 1798 (docket 20807). Which one is this Job Prince, rather than his son Job (born 1751), has not been settled; neither file has been read (Index to the probate records of the County of Suffolk, vol. 3, p. 59). ¶4

Death: the same Kingston gravestone record lists 'Job, ch. Job and Abigail (Kimball), ----, 1790, in Boston' (Vital Records of Kingston, p. 371). The G.R.1 entries for this family use one formula and record deaths far from Kingston up to 1799 (the father in Jamaica 1731, James in N.C. 1759, Christopher in Nova Scotia 1799), so they probably come from a family memorial cut after 1799 (an inference; the printed volume does not describe the stone). ¶7

Probate: the 1790 administration (Suffolk docket 19540) now fits this Job Prince, who died in Boston that year; the 1798 will (docket 20807) is then probably his son Job (born 1751). Neither file has been read. ¶10

Death notice (index): 'Prince, Job, sen., Capt., d. in Boston, aged 66', in the Massachusetts Centinel of 27 February 1790 (AAS obituary index, vol. 4 p. 3678; page image read; the notice itself not yet read). Aged 66 fits his Kingston birth in 1723, and 'sen.' distinguishes him from his son Job. The same index has 'Abigail, daughter of late Capt. Job Prince, d. Boston, aged 23' in the Columbian Centinel of 11 January 1792 (OCR only), matching his daughter Abigail born 1767, and 'Prince, Job Capt., d. in Boston, aged 47' in the Columbian Centinel of 6 January 1798, matching the son Job born 28 September 1751, whose will was proved that year (docket 20807). ¶20

Probate docket: the Suffolk docket book entry for docket 19540 reads 'Job Prince 1790: Regd Certif of Adm 89:480; Bond no record; Inv 89:604; Inv (Addl) 91:163; Inv (Addl) Lincoln Co. 91:281; List of Debts 89:630; Petn & Decree 90:623; Comrs Return 91:444; Oath Sale R.E. 91:355; Evidence Perpetd & Sale R.E. (C.C.P.) no record; Acct of Sales no record; Acct 91:39; Acct 91:283; Acct 91:448; Distribn (insolv.) 91:462' (Suffolk probate docket book nos. 18055-23599; FamilySearch DGS 7703036 images 146-147; images read). It confirms that docket 19540 was an insolvent estate, with commissioners on claims and a distribution to creditors. It has no division, guardianship or distribution to heirs, so his probate cannot name daughter Elizabeth. His grade is unchanged. New lead: an additional inventory of land in Lincoln County (Maine), vol. 91 p. 281, and the Lincoln County registry may hold deeds by his heirs. The registered certificate of administration was not found at vol. 89 p. 480 (DGS 7703712 image 259 holds pp. 480-481, entries of July 1790 for other estates), so the cited page may be wrong. ¶28

Probate (sign-in S0234): the printed Suffolk probate index (DGS 7703032 image 500, p. 59) lists Prince, Job: 1693 adm. 2096; 1708 adm. 3080; 1790 administration 19540; 1798 will 20807. Docket 19540 is his estate: in the record book (FamilySearch full text, 'Suffolk Probate Records 1792, 1793', ark 3:1:3Q9M-C9YR-BB3J; image read via transcript only) James Prince, administrator on the estate of 'Capt. Job Prince late of Boston, deceased', presented on 6 March 1792 an additional inventory: 'A piece of Land situate at West Boston' £235 (appraisers Samuel Clap, Nehemiah? James, Rich[ar]d? W. Cooper); an account current of the estate follows. No mention of his late wife Elizabeth or of an Allen inheritance in that entry. This fits the 1793 deed reservation of one fifth of one third of the Cambridge St. marsh 'claimed by Capt. Job Prince' as part of his estate. ¶29a

1814 deeds: James Prince, Esq., of Boston, released his interest in the South Allen Street pasture to David Higgins, mariner, 20 Aug. 1814, and Thomas Williams of Roxbury? (Norfolk Co.) and Frances his wife did the same 19 Aug. 1814 (DGS 007442718 image 569); David Higgins and John and Sarah Tucker then quitclaimed five sixths to Samuel Dunn, 22 Aug. 1814, as heirs of 'Elizabeth Prince', the remaining sixth being 'in the heirs of the late Cap[tai]n Job Prince' (image 570). The deed does not say whether that Job is this man or his son Job (b. 1751). Grade unchanged. ¶30

Insolvent estate: on 11 July 1792 Judge George R. Minot made an order of distribution on the estate of 'Job Prince, late of Boston, merchant', reported insolvent by James Prince, administrator; the balance of about £916 was divided among creditors whose claims came to a little over £2,000 (Suffolk probate records vol. 91 pp. 462-463, FamilySearch DGS 7703713 image 261; image read, foot of p. 462 cut off by the film frame). Because the estate was insolvent there was no distribution to heirs, so his probate is unlikely ever to name his children; the parentage of Elizabeth (Prince) Higgins Creed has to rest on other records. ¶22

Origin proved: the diary of Hezekiah Prince of Kingston and Thomaston (son of Kimball Prince of Kingston, aged 21 in April 1792, d. 1840), entry for Monday 18 Nov 1793 on arriving in Boston, printed in the New England Magazine n.s. 9 (Feb. 1894): 727-728 from the original journal (editor's notes in brackets, kept apart): 'I stopped with my cousin, James Prince, who married Miss Gordon. He is U.S. marshal, the son of my uncle Job Prince. My father, Kimball Prince of Kingston, Mass., together with four brothers and one sister, were all born at Rocky Nook, Kingston. Their father was Capt. Job Prince, who married Abigail Kimball of Bridgewater, 1722. His father was Capt. Thomas Prince of Boston, who married Ruth Turner of Scituate... My grandfather, Capt. Job Prince of Rocky Nook, Kingston, died at Jamaica, W.I., of the smallpox in 1734. Three of his sons were sea captains residing in Boston. Capt. Job married Elisebeth Allen, died 1790. His mansion house was between McLean Street and Allen Street, West End... I have often been at my uncle Job's mansion, once with my father in 1785. He had eight children, Hezekiah, for whom I was named, Capt. Job, Jr., master of the big ship Massachusetts, Thomas, James, and Samuel; also three daughters, Sarah, Elisebeth, and Abigal.' So his parents, Job Prince of Kingston and Abigail Kimball, are now Confirmed as his parents (see their notes). The diary also proves that he had a daughter Elizabeth living in 1785-1793. It does not mention her marriage, so his own grade, which carries his link to Elizabeth (Prince) Higgins Creed of Prince Edward Island, stays Probable: the step from his daughter Elizabeth (b. Boston 28 May 1753) to Mrs Higgins/Creed still rests on the James Prince estate share, the name Job Prince Creed and the Prince's Pasture deed (see the identity note above). ¶27a

Probate of the younger Job (not this man): the 1798 will (docket 20807) is that of 'Job Prince of Boston, Mariner', with executors Benjamin Clark and Samuel Prince; it names his wife Elizabeth and 'Sally Dunn Prince our youngest', and in May 1800 guardians were appointed for his children Benjamin Clarke, Andrew, Mary and Sarah Dunn Prince, his widow Elizabeth being guardian of Sarah (Suffolk probate vol. 96 pp. 246-248, DGS 7703715 images 502-503; vol. 98 p. 354, DGS 7703716 image 612; images read). With minor children in 1800 he is of the next generation, very probably the son Job born 1751, with his brother Samuel as executor (inference; not stated in the record). So docket 19540 (1790) is the estate of this Job, and docket 20807 is not. ¶19

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 3

Upgrade: graded on his link to his daughter Elizabeth Prince. (1) Power of attorney dated at Three Rivers, PEI, 1 Oct 1806 (recorded Suffolk 28 July 1809; image 529 bottom right, ark 3:1:3QSQ-G9Z3-QXNT, image read): 'Know all men by these presents that we William Creed of Three Rivers in Prince Edward Island Esquire and Elizabeth Creed otherwise Prince his wife have made ordained constituted and appointed... James Prince of Boston... Esquire and David Higgins of Boston... Mariner' their attorneys to sell lands and take out administration on any estate; witness Job Creed, who swore on 1 Oct 1806 that he saw 'William Creed and Elizabeth Creed otherwise Prince his wife' execute it. (2) Quitclaim of 26 July 1809 (images 530-531, transcript read): 'We James Prince Esquire and Thomas I. Prince Merchant both of Boston... and Elizabeth [Creed] formerly of said Boston but now of... Three Rivers in Prince Edward Island... Widow acting by her attornies James Prince Esquire and David Higgins... by virtue of her power... October the first Eighteen hundred and six' release to Samuel Parkman and Harrison Gray Otis land at West Boston on Cambridge Street and Charles River (Prince's Pasture land); signed 'Elizabeth Creed by her attornies James Prince, David Higgins'. (3) Release of 26 May 1817 (images 72-73, transcript read): 'we Job P. Creed, Joseph Bennett Creed, and Samuel Creed... all of Prince Edwards Island... Gentlemen' to 'Mrs Elizabeth Prince of Boston... Widow' of their rights in 'Princes Pasture... the part which it is contemplated to be sold to the Trustees of the General Hospital by the heirs of Job Prince', signed 'by our half brother David Higgins, who is our lawful Attorney'; acknowledged before Saml D. Parker JP 30 May 1817. (4) Deposition of Samuel D. Parker, attorney, sworn 7 Nov 1823 and recorded in the Suffolk deeds (image 262, ark 3:1:3QS7-99Z3-9THJ, image read; image 263): 'it having been discovered that the Title to part of the land called Princes Pasture lying in said Boston was in James Prince, Samuel Prince, Mrs Tucker the wife of John Tucker Esquire, David Higgins and others heirs at Law of old Captain Job Prince the father of James Prince, the said heirs made & executed a deed of release or conveyance to the late Mrs Elizabeth Prince' (the widow of Job Prince Jr.); Parker drew those deeds, which is the 1817 Creed release that he took the acknowledgment of. (5) Elizabeth Creed's administration, docket 23931 (file papers images 2029-2034, read): bond 23 Mar 1812 of David Higgins with John Tucker and Henry D. Sedgwick; inventory '$1048.88 received of James Prince'. The deposition (4) names David Higgins, son of Elizabeth (Prince) Higgins Creed, among the 'heirs at Law of old Captain Job Prince the father of James Prince' together with Job's surviving children James, Samuel and Sarah (Mrs John Tucker); the Creed brothers released as heirs of Job Prince in the same transaction (3); and in 1809 (2) 'Elizabeth Creed... widow', born Prince (1), joined Job's sons James and Thomas I. Prince as a co-owner of his West Boston land. A Prince-born woman who shares Job Prince's estate with his sons, and whose sons by two husbands are his heirs at law, is his daughter: she married in 1773, so she cannot be a grandchild (his eldest child was born 1749). Matches the Boston birth of Elizabeth, daughter of Job and Elizabeth Prince, 28 May 1753, and Hezekiah Prince's 1793 diary naming a daughter Elisebeth. The earlier notes stating that his link 'still rests on the reasoning above' are superseded for the link to Elizabeth; his own link up to Job Prince of Kingston (b. 1695) is not changed here. ¶31

Audit 11 (2 Nov 2026): Confirmed kept, graded on his link to his daughter Elizabeth Prince. The 1823 Parker deposition (crop checked) names 'David Higgins and others heirs at Law of old Captain Job Prince the father of James Prince'. David Higgins can be an heir at law only as a grandson. A grandson surnamed Higgins descends through a daughter who married a Higgins, and that is the Elizabeth Prince who married David Higgins in 1773. In 1806 she is 'Elizabeth Creed otherwise Prince', and in 1809 she is a co-owner of Job's West Boston land with his sons. She cannot be a granddaughter (married 1773; Job's eldest child born 1749). She cannot be a son's widow either, because then her Higgins son would not be Job's heir. The Boston birth of 28 May 1753 and the 1793 diary's daughter 'Elisebeth' agree. This reasoning works through relationship words in primary records (heirs at law; otherwise Prince; half brother) and a closed set of named heirs. It is not a residence or name-only elimination, so Confirmed stands. ¶34

Audit 12 (2 Nov 2026): his mother's link (Abigail Kimball) is now Probable; his father's (Job Prince) stays Confirmed. See the audit 12 notes on them. ¶35

The case for this identification 2

Identity (Probable): he is placed as the father of Elizabeth (Prince) Higgins Creed because (1) a daughter Elizabeth was born to Job and Elizabeth Prince in Boston on 28 May 1753, the only Elizabeth Prince in the Boston birth register between about 1745 and 1758; (2) she had a brother James (born 1758), and Elizabeth Creed's 1812 estate was her share of her father's estate received from her brother James Prince (Murray, citing Suffolk probate docket 23931); (3) her Creed son was named Job Prince Creed; (4) her sons sold an interest in 'Prince's Pasture', Boston, in 1817; and (5) William Creed was a business partner of Capt. Job Prince of Boston. ¶5a

Which Job Prince: two men of the name were adult in Boston from the 1770s, this Job (born 1723) and his son Job (born 28 September 1751). The entries of 1769-1774 call him simply 'Capt. Job Prince'; they are assigned to the father because the son was then 18 to 23 and the title and the surety bonds fit an established ship-master and merchant (an inference). From 1779 the town clerk writes 'Capt. Job Prince Senr.', which shows a second, younger Job Prince was then active in Boston; every 'Senr.' entry (1779, 1781, 1785) is this man. On 30 July 1787 the selectmen accepted 'Capt. Job Prince Mech't.' (mechanic, that is craftsman) and Enoch James, blacksmith, as bondsmen for collector Henderson (vol. 27, p. 27, read from the page image). That entry has no 'Senr.' and calls him a mechanic rather than a merchant, so it may be the son; it is not assigned to either man. ¶17

Corrections 2

Correction: the citation 'vol. 91, pp. 162-163' given above for the additional inventory of 6 March 1792 is uncertain: the volume's own index places the additional inventory at folio 223 (about p. 446). The image itself (DGS 7703713 image 108, ark 3:1:3Q9M-C9YR-BB3J) is the reliable reference. ¶23

Correction: the James Prince who was Collector of Newburyport (d. 11 May 1830) was not this man's son: Currier's Ould Newbury (1896, compiled) calls him 'a son of Rev. Joseph Prince', the blind Boston preacher, and gives his age at death as in his seventy-fifth year, which does not fit a James born in 1758. The Capt. James Prince who died at Providence in 1819 aged 61 remains a possible match for the son James (b. 1758), on age alone. ¶24

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Not the same person as Job Prince: that is his father, Job Prince (born 1695, died April 1731 in Jamaica). Do not merge them. ¶11

Searches that found nothing 2

Probate search (negative): his father's Plymouth estate (1731-1743) names no children, and the Plymouth probate index has no division of the Kingston real estate or guardianship naming him (signin-results S0204, S0205). His link to Job Prince of Kingston therefore still rests on the reasoning above. The next record to try is the Plymouth County deeds, about 1744-1760, for a sale of his share of the Kingston land by 'Job Prince of Boston'. ¶25

Plymouth deeds (negative): the Plymouth County grantor index for 1685-1801 has no deed by any Job Prince (FamilySearch DGS 7451109, Prince entries at images 73-75). He therefore did not sell or release a share of his father's Kingston land by a recorded Plymouth deed. His brother Kimball Prince of Kingston, housewright, conveyed his own house and 36 acres in Kingston to Ezra Fuller in 1765 (Plymouth Deeds vol. 52, about p. 87). In 1767 Kimball also acted as attorney for his half-brother Silvanus Everson, mariner, in dividing the homestead of their stepfather Ephraim Everson (vol. 53 fol. 201v). Neither deed mentions Job. Next to try: the grantee index (DGS 7451089), and Suffolk County deeds for a Kingston conveyance by Job Prince of Boston. ¶26
