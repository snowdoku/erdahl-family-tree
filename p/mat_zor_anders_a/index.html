About the family

Relationship to our own line is not established. He is an Eidem of Selbu with a different patronymic — Andersson, not Paulsen — so he is not a brother of Peder P., Mikal and John P., and the project's wave-2 guess that he is 'a cousin' remains a guess. What he does prove is that more than one Eidem household came out of Selbu to this one township. ¶19

Life story

An Eidem of the Marietta / Mehurin colony whose patronymic is Andersson, not Paulsen, so he is a cousin rather than one of Paul Eidem of Selbu's five children. Dates from the gravestone at Zoar Lutheran Cemetery (Find a Grave memorial 136223223). ¶1

Gravestone, photographed and read: a flat rose-granite marker inscribed 'FATHER / ANDERS A. / 1853 - 1932'. Note that the stone says 1853 while the Find a Grave index says 19 October 1852; the conflict is recorded, and the indexed day-and-month is kept here because it is the more specific of the two. ¶17

Burial plot: Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, lot 15, with his daughter Benda and with Beret Kulsethmo (1841-1929) — South Dakota state burial register, 'ANDERS A EIDEM 1932, lot 15'. ¶18

Emigration: 'Anders' trunk said Rochester, MN when he emigrated in 1874, following his two sisters, Kari and Brynhild; and ahead of his brother Bardo Eidem in arriving in Minnesota.' So the Eidem emigration from Selbu ran in a chain across at least four siblings and began by 1874, six years before Peder P. Eidem of our own line left in 1880. ¶13

Early years in America: 'Anders spent some time in Minnesota, farming with his sisters' husbands and others. Anders is shown as baptismal sponsor for his sister, Kari's daughter at South Zumbro Lutheran Church close to Rochester and Kasson, MN in 1878.' ¶14

Tax: he appears in the Lac qui Parle County delinquent personal-property tax lists printed in the Press as "Eidem A A" (1887, $0.41; 1890, $0.87), "Eidem, A" (1889, $0.58; 1891, $1.58) and "Eidem, Andrew" (1892, 1893). ¶3

Witness to a homestead proof, 1888: The Lac qui Parle County Press of 3, 10, 17, 24, 31 August and 7 September 1888 printed the final-proof notice of Erland Klockstad, homestead entry No. 11,482, for the NE1/4 of section 20, township 118 north, range 46 west, 5th P. M. (Augusta Township), and named his witnesses as "John Rue, John Paulson, Albert Johnson, ANDREW EIDEM, all of Marietta, Minn." To swear a homesteader's proof a man had to have known the claimant's land personally for the whole five years, so Andrew Eidem was settled near Marietta well before 1888. ¶2

Land: 'About 1890 he moved to Marietta with hopes of getting his own farm. He did, purchasing a good farm from the Winona & St. Peter Railroad in 1899 for a bargain... His farm was on the MN/SD border, on the MN side, not far from Estelline, SD.' Context, flagged as context: the Winona & St. Peter was the Chicago & North Western's land-grant line across southern Minnesota, and railroad land sales rather than homesteading are how a good deal of this township changed hands in the 1890s. ¶15

Land patent, 1892: indexed as "ANDRES A EIDEM", he received a United States land patent on 11 NOVEMBER 1892, document number 6972, accession STA-MN1420__.481, for LOT 4 OF SECTION 20, TOWNSHIP 118 NORTH, RANGE 46 WEST OF THE 5TH PRINCIPAL MERIDIAN - Augusta Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota. ¶20

Marriage: Lac qui Parle County certificate C01496, 'EIDEM, ANDERS A.' and 'KLEGSETH, BRYNHILD', 6 May 1901. Klegseth is the clerk's rendering of Kulsetmo, a Selbu farm name. ¶7

Marriage: 6 May 1901, at Zoar Lutheran Church, to the widow Brynhild (Kulsetmo) Klegseth of Selbu, who had come out a widow with three small daughters. Their children were Anton Bernhard 'Tony' (1902-1962), Nikolai 'Nick' (1904-1981) and Benda Elvina (1908-1999); 'Anders' children and grandchildren speak very highly of Anders as a kind and patient and loving man.' ¶16

1905 state census: Augusta Township, Lac qui Parle County, schedule page 691, line 22 - 'Andrew Eidem, M, 52, W, born Norway', with the children Gerza (Gurine/Gina) 14, Lena 12, Inga 10, Antoine 3 and Nickoli 8 months on lines 24 to 28. His three eldest daughters were born about 1891, 1893 and 1895, which is ten years before the 1901 wedding; either he and Brynhild married first in Norway and remarried civilly in Minnesota, or the 1901 record is a second marriage. The Minnesota death index gives Gina Eidem's mother's maiden name as KULSETHMOE, so the eldest daughter is Brynhild's. ¶9

Death: 18 November 1932, Lac qui Parle County - Minnesota death certificate index 1932-MN-006876, 'Anders Eidem'. ¶10

A FOUNDER OF ZOAR LUTHERAN CHURCH. His Find a Grave biography, written by a descendant of his brother Bardo: 'Anders was a founder of Zoar Lutheran Church at Marietta, close to his farm. He, his wife and Tony and Benda are all buried at Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, right outside the beautiful little Norwegian Lutheran Church where many Selbu-ites settled along with Anders.' This is the first retrieved statement of who founded the congregation. ¶11

Birth and family in Norway: 'Anders was the last of 7 children born to Anders Andersson and Brynhild Bardosdatter Mogarn at the Eidem farm, just outside the village of Selbu. Anders' father died before he was born and his mother never remarried.' His father Anders Erikson Eidem is given as 1801-1852 and his mother Brynhild Bardosdatter 'Mogard' Mosletta as 1809-1888. ¶12

Named as father on the delayed birth registration of his son Anton Bernhard, filed 16 July 1935 at Marietta on his widow's affidavit (Lac qui Parle certificate 1901-10783): 'FATHER: Anders A. Eidem, residence Augusta Twp, Lac qui Parle, White, age at last birthday 48, birthplace Norway, occupation Farmer. MOTHER: full maiden name Brynhild Kulsethmo, residence Lac qui Parle, White, birthplace Norway, occupation own home. Number of children of this mother, including this child: born alive and now living 4, born alive but now dead 0, stillborn 0.' Aged 48 in December 1901 he was born about 1853, which matches the Anders Andersson Eidem, 19 October 1852 - 18 November 1932, buried at Zoar Lutheran. ¶8

Land, 1940: eight years after his death the Augusta Township list in the 1940 county atlas still carries "A. A. Eidem. Lot 4 less R. R." and "A. A. Eidem. Lot 1" - the parcels were still assessed in his name or his estate's. ¶4

An Eidem of the same Lac qui Parle cluster but a different patronymic - Andersson, not Paulsen - and therefore a cousin of the Paulsen brothers rather than a brother. He farmed in Augusta Township. ¶6

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

2 on the identification

The case for this identification 2

The identification of the newspaper's "Andrew Eidem" / "A. A. Eidem" with the Anders Andersson Eidem of the Zoar gravestone is Probable, not Confirmed: it rests on the initials, the place and the dates, not on a document naming both forms. ¶5

This resolves two loose ends at once. The 1940 county atlas entry "A. A. Eidem. Lot 4 less R. R." in Augusta Township, whose section number could not be read because the printed column runs past the right-hand trim of the Internet Archive scan, is this same parcel - SECTION 20 - still carried in his name eight years after his death, the "less R. R." being the strip the railway took out of it. And the 1888 final-proof notice of Erland Klockstad for the north-east quarter of section 20, township 118 north, range 46 west, on which "Andrew Eidem of Marietta" swore as a witness, concerns the ADJOINING QUARTER OF THE SAME SECTION. A homestead witness had to know the claim at first hand for the whole five years, and he did: he lived on the next quarter. ¶21
