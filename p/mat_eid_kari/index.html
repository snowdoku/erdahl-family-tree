About the family

Her father's family was at Eidem a generation before Paul arrived: Peder Bersvendsen is already entered as 'residence Eidem' at his first marriage in June 1819, fifteen years before Kari was born. The Eidem connection in this family comes down Kari's side, not Paul's. ¶22

Children: six confinements - Peder (24 Nov 1858), Anne (17 Nov 1860), Ingebrigt (30 Sep 1863), a stillborn daughter (6 Nov 1866), Mikel (29 Jan 1869) and John (17 Sep 1876). She was 24 at the first and nearly 42 at the last. ¶26

Her children in the Norwegian registers, all baptised with the mother's residence given as Eidum or Eidem: Peder 27 Dec 1858, Anne 27 Dec 1860, Ingebrigt 8 Nov 1863, Michael 29 Mar 1869 and John 29 Oct 1876. Five children, matching the 1904 printed biography exactly. Two of them - Anne born 1860 and Ingebrigt born 1863 - are not yet in this tree. ¶34

Life story

Gravestone: 'Kari P. Eidem, 20 Oct 1834 – 4 Oct 1925', Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, Antelope Valley Township, Deuel County, South Dakota — the Norwegian Lutheran burial ground of the Selbu colony that settled Marietta and Mehurin Township in Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota, three miles across the state line. ¶1

FOR THE NORWAY AGENT: look for a Kari Eidem, b. 1834, travelling with minor children in the Trondheim police emigrant protocols nos. 5 (1880–1882) through 8 (1888–1892). ¶4

Gravestone, photographed and read: a flat granite marker, 'MRS. KARI P. EIDEM / OCT. 20, 1834 / OCT. 4, 1925'. The 'Mrs.' is cut into the stone — she is commemorated as a widow, which fits the project's reading of her as Paul Eidem's widow, who followed her sons to America after his death in Selbu in 1881. ¶15

Burial plot: Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, lot 2, with her son Peder P. Eidem and his wife Karen P. Eidem. The state burial register writes her 'KARI KAREN EIDEM' and gives the date 11-04-1925, transposing the stone's 4 October 1925; the photographed stone is the better evidence. ¶16

Baptism: born 20 OCTOBER 1834 and baptised 29 October 1834 at Selbu church, entered as 'Karen Pedersd' in the Selbu parish register 1824-1842, page 105 serial no. 86. Father Peder Bersvendsen, mother Mali Evensdatter. ¶19

The 20 October 1834 on her American gravestone is exact. A Selbu parish register of 1834 and a South Dakota headstone cut in 1925 give the same day, with ninety-one years and an ocean between them; her birth date is therefore hardened from 'about 20 October 1834' to 20 October 1834. ¶20

Printed source: her birth year 1834 and her given name both occur on page 206 of Selbu i fortid og naatid, bind 4 (1992), in the chapter BRENNHAUGEN (pp. 205-207), the farm-history account of the cottage under Eidem that she inherited the use of from her mother Mali Evensdatter and that her husband worked. The same page carries Amerika and Minnesota, which is where she went as a widow and where she died in 1925. ¶31

Her Norwegian family is found, 12 Oct 2026 (slind-deep agent). The Selbu marriage register, page 341 serial 8, marries 'Karen Pedersdatt:', born 1835, OF EIDUM, to Paul Ingebrigtsen of Marstad on 31 MAY 1858, and names her father as PEDER RICHARDSEN. The 1865 census of Selbu then finds the couple at rural residence 0065 Braendhaugen, a cottar's plot on the Eidem farm, in the household of 'Mali Evensdatter, 70, Husmoder, e, Pladsbrugerske' - a widow holding the plot - who is described as Paul's MOTHER-IN-LAW ('hendes svigerson'). MALI EVENSDATTER, born about 1795, is therefore Kari Pedersdatter's mother, and Kari was born on the Eidem farm, where her mother still held the plot in 1865. ¶32

This answers the question of why the family is called Eidem. Kari, not her husband, is the Eidem: she was born there and her mother held the holding. Her husband came in from Marstad in 1858. ¶33

Birth date raised from ABT 20 OCT 1834 to 20 OCT 1834. Her gravestone at Zoar and her 1925 obituary in Minneapolis Tidende print the same date independently. The obituary's stated age of 90 years 11 months 11 days is three days short of the arithmetic from 20 October 1834 to 4 October 1925 (90 years 11 months 14 days) - a trivial slip, and it does not disturb the date both documents give. ¶38

FOR THE SELBU AGENTS: the woman to find in the Selbu registers is Kari Pedersdatter, born 20 October 1834, married in the SPRING OF 1858 to Paul Eidem of the farm Eidem, widowed in 1881, emigrated 1886. The marriage entry of spring 1858 will name both fathers and the witnesses; it is the single most valuable record left for this branch. ¶44

Confirmation: confirmed at Selbu church on 6 May 1849 as 'Karen Pedersdatt.', girls no. 6 (the number is written over a 5), born '20/10 34', birthplace Eidum, parents 'H. Peder Bersvendsen, Mali Evensdatt.' - H. standing for husmand, cottager. Selbu ministerialbok 1843-1859, B. Confirmerede 1849, pp. 246-247. The register gives her full birth date, 20 October 1834, in the age column: an independent church witness to the date on her gravestone and to her parents as named at her baptism. ¶46

Confirmation marks: 'god' (good) for knowledge and 'god' for conduct and diligence, written as ditto marks against the fuller wording of the first girl's entry. Smallpox vaccination certificate dated 4 September 1837; the vaccinator, by a further ditto, is probably B. Andersen. ¶47

Marriage: married at Selbu church 31 May 1858 Paul Ingebrigtsen of Marstad. She was 23, he 33, and her residence is entered as Eidum. The couple then lived on her widowed mother's cottage holding Braendhaugen under Eidem. ¶23

Birth year: the 1858 marriage entry and the 1865 census (age 31) both give about 1834-1835, which agrees with the 20 October 1834 cut on her gravestone at Zoar Lutheran in Deuel County, South Dakota. ¶35

Marriage, from the obituary: she married Paul Eidem in the SPRING OF 1858, in Selbu. Her eldest son Peder Paulsen Eidem was born 4 November 1858, which places him as the first child of that marriage. Her husband died in 1881 - the second independent statement of that year, the first being the 1904 printed biography of Peder. ¶40

Arrival: the obituary says she came to Marietta on 1 MAY 188[6?]. The hub's OCR renders the year as '188,' and the digit is not recoverable from the word data. The project already holds her emigration as Trondheim police emigrant protocol no. 7 (1885-1888), year of departure 1886, so '1 May 1886' is the reading that reconciles the two records and '1882' is not. The obituary also confirms what the 1895 and 1905 Minnesota state censuses show - that from her arrival to her death she lived in her sons' households and never kept her own. ¶41

1865 census: Braendhaugen, Selbu, entered as Kari Pedersdatter, 31, 'hans Kone' (his wife), born Selbu parish, in the household of her mother Mali Evensdatter, 70, Pladsbrugerske and Husmoder, with her husband Paul as 'hendes Svigersoen Husfader' and their three eldest children. ¶24

1875 census: Braendhaugen (plads), loepenr/bnr 63, Selbu, entered as Kari Pedersdatter, b. 1834 Selbu, 'Kone', in a household of five. ¶25

The emigrant widow. Wife of Paul Eidem of Selbu, who died in Norway about 1881 and never emigrated; mother of Peder (b. 1858), Mikal (b. 1869) and John P. Eidem (b. 1876). Her American gravestone at Zoar Lutheran reads 'Kari P. Eidem, 20 Oct 1834 - 4 Oct 1925'; the Trondheim emigrant protocol spells the P. out as PEDERSDATTER. ¶6

Widowhood and emigration: widowed 20 December 1881 when Paul died, aged 47, with John then 5 years old. She later followed her sons to America and died 4 October 1925, aged 90, in Deuel County, South Dakota, buried at Zoar Lutheran cemetery - more than forty years a widow and some four decades of them on the other side of the Atlantic. ¶27

Emigration, Confirmed: Trondheim police office, First accession, emigrant protocol no. 7 (1885-1888), page 22, group of travellers 00148, sequence 246, line 38 - 'Karin Pedersdatter', female, year of birth 1835 (calculated), age 51, household position 'Kone' (wife), residence Selbo, destination 'Chicago Ill', ticket 'Bill. bet. i Amerika' (paid in America). Contracted 7 April, expedited 8 April 1886, steamer Hero, Allan Line. On the two lines immediately after her, with the household position 'Soen' (son), stand Mikkel P. Eidem, 17, and John Eidem, 9. The clerk's household bracket is the primary proof of her motherhood of both boys. ¶7

Destination note: the stated destination on the 1886 ticket is Chicago, not Minnesota, although her eldest son Peder had been homesteading in Lac qui Parle County since 1882. In 1886 the through ticket from Trondheim was commonly sold only as far as Chicago, where the emigrant trains terminated and the onward prairie ticket was bought. Nothing in the family's American record suggests they settled there. ¶10

EMIGRATION, 8 APRIL 1886: she left Trondheim with her two youngest sons on three consecutive lines of one ledger page. Trondheim police office, Emigrant protocols no. 7, header year 1886, group 00148, page 22. Line 38, person 0018: 'Karin Pedersdatter', age 51, residence Selbo, household position Kone. Line 39, person 0019: 'Mikkel P. Eidem', age 17, household position SOEN (son). Line 40, person 0020: 'John Eidem', age 9, household position SOEN. Contract 7 April 1886, expedited 8 April 1886, all three with payment 'Bill. bet. i Amerika' - tickets bought for them in America by her two eldest sons. Her stated age of 51 agrees exactly with a birth on 20 October 1834. ¶28

Two points of record criticism on that entry. The clerk gives her marital status as 'g' (gift, married) and her position as 'Kone' (wife) although she had been a widow since December 1881: Kone was the protocol's standing term for an adult woman travelling with children and is not evidence of a husband. And the stated destination is 'Chicago Ill', not Minnesota - Chicago was the rail transfer point for everyone bound for western Minnesota. A researcher filtering Selbu emigrants on a Minnesota destination loses this entire party. ¶29

1895 Minnesota state census: 'Carrie P. Eidem', 61, born Norway, in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota, sheet mnv290_73 page 184 line 11, living in the household of her son Mike Eidem (26) and his wife Betsy (23), with their infant son Paul M. Eidem (2 years 1 month, named for his dead grandfather) and her unmarried son John Eidem (19). An age of 61 in 1895 fits a birth of October 1834 exactly, and the household is the 1886 travelling party nine years on. ¶9

1895 state census: she is in Minnesota, in the family, and her age is right. Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, page 184, line 11 - 'Eidem Carrie P., 61, F, W, born Norway'. The four people enumerated with her on lines 8 to 12 are her son Mike (Mikal) Eidem, 26, born Norway; his wife Betsy, 23, born Norway; their son Paul M., 2 1/2, born Minnesota; and her youngest son John, 19, born Norway. Aged 61 in June 1895 she was born about 1834, which agrees with the 20 October 1834 on the Zoar Lutheran stone. ¶11

1895 Minnesota state census: Mehurin township, Lac qui Parle County, page 0184 line 11 - 'Eidem Carrie P., 61, F, W, born Norway'. She was living in her son Mikal's house with her youngest son John, 19. An age of 61 in June 1895 points to a birth about 1833-34, consistent with the October 1834/1835 already in the tree. ¶17

1905 state census: still with her sons' households, now in Augusta Township, Lac qui Parle County, schedule page 691, line 32 - 'Carrie Eidem, F, 70, born Norway' - in or beside the household of her eldest son Peter P. Eidem, 45, and his wife Carrie, 41. Aged 70 in 1905 is the same woman ten years on. ¶12

1905 Minnesota state census: Augusta township, Lac qui Parle County, page 0691 line 32 - 'Carrie Eidem, F, 70, W, born Norway, father born Norway, mother born Norway'. By 1905 she had moved from Mikal's house in Mehurin to Peter P. Eidem's house in Augusta. She appears again as 'Carrie Eidem, mother, b. 1835 Norway' in John P. Eidem's Mehurin household in 1910, and in Mikal's household in Deuel County, South Dakota in 1920. She moved between her sons for at least twenty-five years. ¶18

Selbygbogen (Minneapolis, 1921), p. 149, printing what her eldest son told the compiler, gives her as 'Kari Pedersd. (born 20 Oct. 1834 at Brendhaugen under OESTRE EIDEM)'. Her birth date is therefore attested three times over from three unconnected directions: the Selbu parish register of 1834, a Norwegian-American bygdelag book of 1921, and a South Dakota gravestone of 1925. ¶30

Her death at 90, in 1925, means she lived more than forty years in America. ¶3

Congregation and burial: she died 4 October 1925 and is buried at Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, Antelope Valley Township, Deuel County, South Dakota, the burial ground of the Hauge Synod Zoar congregation organised in 1896, of which her sons Peder Paulsen and Mikal Paulsen Eidem both held office. As the immigrant widow of the generation that came out of Norway, her burial entry in Zoar's ministerial register of 1895-1957 - Minnesota Historical Society microfilm M678, reel 44 [ALC 129] - is the single entry in this record set most likely to state her home parish in Norway outright, because a pastor recording the death of an aged immigrant habitually wrote down where she was born. ¶5

Death: 4 October 1925, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota - Minnesota death certificate index 1925-MN-006863, 'Karie Eidem'. ¶13

Obituary, Confirmed, and it states the parish: Minneapolis Tidende (Minneapolis, Minn.), Thursday 5 November 1925, page 7 - 'Mrs. Kari P. Eidem doede Soendag den 4de Oktober i sin Soens, John Eidems Hjem i Marietta, Minn., i en Alder af 90 Aar, 11 Maaneder og 11 Dage. Hun var foedt i SELBU, TRONDHJEM, NORGE, den 20de Oktober 1834. Gift med Paul I. Eidem Vaaren 1858. Hendes Mand doede i 1881. Hun efterlader sig tre Soenner, Peder og John Eidem i Marietta og James Eidem i Greenbush, Minn., ogsaa 26 Boerneboern og 69 Boerneboerns Boern. Hun kom til Marietta den 1ste Mai 188[6?], og har havt sit Hjem hos sine Soenner. [Hun blev] begraven Thorsdag den 8de Oktober paa Zoar Kirkes Gravplads. Pastor OE. E. Hanson holdt Begravelsespraediken.' Translation: 'Mrs. Kari P. Eidem died on Sunday 4 October at the home of her son John Eidem in Marietta, Minn., at the age of 90 years, 11 months and 11 days. She was born in Selbu, Trondhjem, Norway, on 20 October 1834. Married to Paul I. Eidem in the spring of 1858. Her husband died in 1881. She leaves three sons, Peder and John Eidem of Marietta and James Eidem of Greenbush, Minn., also 26 grandchildren and 69 great-grandchildren. She came to Marietta on 1 May 188[6?] and has had her home with her sons. She was buried on Thursday 8 October in Zoar church's burial ground. Pastor OE. E. Hanson preached the funeral sermon.' Minneapolis Tidende was the Norwegian-language paper of record for Minnesota, so this is her own immigrant community's statement of where she came from. ¶37

Her descendants in October 1925: 26 grandchildren and 69 great-grandchildren living. A measure of how large the Lac qui Parle Eidem connection already was, and a caution that this tree holds only a fraction of it. ¶42

Burial, from the obituary: buried Thursday 8 October 1925 at Zoar church's burial ground, the funeral sermon preached by Pastor OE. [Oesten/Oesten] E. Hanson of the Marietta Norwegian Lutheran congregation. ¶43

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence raised from Probable to Confirmed. The 1895 census puts a sixty-one-year-old Norwegian-born Kari P. Eidem in one household with the two brothers Mikal and John Eidem, and the 1905 census puts a seventy-year-old Carrie Eidem with the third brother Peter P.; a widow living in turn with her sons, under her husband's patronymic P. for Paulsen, is exactly what the Zoar Lutheran stone and the patronymics had predicted. ¶14

The case for this identification 1

Identification, inferred: she is recorded here as the widow of Paul Eidem of Selbu (d. Norway 1881) and the mother of Peder P., Mikal Paulsen and John P. Eidem, on the strength of her burial among them, her age, and the pattern of a Norwegian widow following her grown sons to America. No record retrieved states the relationship, so this is Probable, not Confirmed. ¶2

Records that disagree 1

Contradiction, recorded honestly: her marital status in the 1886 protocol is written 'g' (gift, married) rather than 'e' (enke, widow), although the 1904 printed biography of her eldest son has her husband Paul dying in Norway in 1881. The same line gives her household position as 'Kone' (wife), so the likeliest reading is that the clerk entered her standing role in the household rather than her civil state; the alternative is that Paul died later than 1881. The record that would settle it is the Selbu burial register for 1881. ¶8

Corrections 3

Correction: her father was PEDER BERSVENDSEN, not 'Peder Richardsen'. The 1858 marriage index gives the bride's father as 'Peder Richardsen', and an earlier researcher took that literally and called Richardsen the most distinctive string in the family. It is an AMF transcription error. The 1834 baptism - a direct contemporaneous entry naming both parents - gives Peder Bersvendsen and Mali Evensdatter, and Mali Evensdatter is independently confirmed as Kari's mother by the 1865 census, which has her living in the household as Paul's mother-in-law. Rikard/Richard is not a Troendelag name of the 1780s; Bersvend is a common Selbu and Tydal name. Anyone searching Norwegian records for a Peder Richardsen of Selbu is chasing a ghost. ¶21

Correction: she died in MINNESOTA, not South Dakota. The death place in this file read 'Deuel County, South Dakota', which is where she is buried. Her obituary says she died at her son John Eidem's home in Marietta, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota, and the Minnesota death certificate index entry already cited here (1925-MN-006863, Lac qui Parle County) agrees. Zoar Lutheran Cemetery is three miles across the state line in Antelope Valley Township, Deuel County, South Dakota, and the Marietta Norwegians buried there because Zoar was their congregation. ¶39

Correction: her father is PEDER BERSVENDSEN (Peder Bersvendsen). 'Peder Richardsen', in the note dated 12 Oct 2026 and in two source titles for the 1858 marriage, is the indexer's misreading of that entry (p. 341 no. 8, re-read 13 Oct 2026; the index still says Richardsen). Her baptism of 1834 names Peder Bersvendsen and Mali Evensdatter. The 1825 census ties the same Peder to Eidem and to his mother Kirsten Ingebrigtsdatter. Do not search for a Peder Richardsen. ¶45
