About the family

ONE OF THE TWO 'MISSING' CHILDREN - the second of the five whom nobody in the American family had heard of. ¶1

He is named for his paternal grandfather Ingebrigt Johnsen, the second son taking the father's father's name. ¶3

Life story

Baptism: born 30 September 1863, baptised 8 November 1863 at Selbu church. Selbu parish register 1860-1877, page 48 serial no. 133. Father Poul Ingebrigtsen, mother Kari Pedersdtr, residence Eidem. The parallel entry is in the 1859-1868 volume. ¶2

Baptism: baptised 8 November 1863 in Selbu, the third of the five children of Paul (Pal) Ingebrigtsen and Kari (Karen) Pedersdatter of Brennhaugen under Eidem. The register gives the father as 'Poul Ingebrigtsen' and the mother as 'Kari Pedersdtr', residence 'Eidem'. Church book from Selbu parish 1860-1877 (1664P), births and baptisms, page 48, serial no. 133. He carries his paternal grandfather's name, Ingebrigt Johnsen - the Selbu naming custom working exactly as it should, and corroboration of the father's parentage. ¶10

Printed source: his given name and the year 1863 both occur on page 206 of Selbu i fortid og natid, bind 4 (1992), in the chapter BRENNHAUGEN, pp. 205-207, alongside his parents and his brothers Peder (1858) and Mikal (1869). ¶14

Two agents found this person independently on the same day, one from the Selbu birth register and one from the baptism entry, and gave them separate ids; the records are merged here under the register's id, which carries the exact birth date. ¶15

1865 census: Braendhaugen, Selbu, person 006, 'Ingebrigt Paulsen', 3, their son, born Selbu parish. ¶4

1865 census: aged 3 at Brendhaugen, rural residence 0065, census district 005, in his widowed grandmother Mali Evensdatter's household. ¶11

1875 census: Braendhaugen (plads), Selbu, person 004, 'Ingebrigt Paulsen', b. 1863 Selbu, son, still at home aged 11-12. His later life is a brick wall; see the findings dossier. ¶5

1875 census: still at home at Brendhaugen (plads), lopenr/bnr 63, aged 12 and entered as born 1863 - the household then being his father Paul Ingebrigtsen, husmand med jord (cottager with land), his mother Kari, his elder brother Peder who 'hjelper Faderen' (helps his father), himself, and the infant Mikael. ¶12

Emigration: left Trondheim 8 August 1883, contract 7 August, aged 19, unmarried, occupation Arbeidsmand (labourer), residence Selbo. Trondheim police office, Emigrant protocols no. 6, header year 1883, group 00524, page 194, line 14, person no. 0002, sequence 3363. TICKET 191 kroner 20 oere, paid by himself - alone among the family, who all travelled on tickets bought for them in America. Permission note 'Meldt' (his military-service clearance was filed). DESTINATION: WATSON, MINNESOTA - in Chippewa County, the county next east of Lac qui Parle, and the staging point of the Selbu colony. He was the second of the family to go, three years after his eldest brother Peder and three years before his mother and the two youngest boys. ¶7

Context: he is the second of the 'two unidentified' children of the five that the 1904 Minnesota biography of his brother Peter counted, and he had not previously been given a place in this tree. No emigration record has been found for him; he is not in the 25 July 1900 Trondheim party. ¶13

AMERICAN IDENTITY FOUND: he is JAMES P. EIDEM of Greenbush, Roseau County, Minnesota. Selbygbogen (Minneapolis, 1921), p. 278, prints: 'James P. Eidem, Greenbush, Minn., born 30 Sept. 1863 at Brendhaugen in Selbu (baptismal name INGEBRIGT). He is a brother of Peder P. Eidem at Marietta, Minn.' The birth date is the exact day the Selbu baptismal register gives for Ingebrigt, son of Poul Ingebrigtsen and Kari Pedersdatter of Eidem, and the book states the baptismal name and the brotherhood outright. ¶6

America: Selbygbogen says he was in Lac qui Parle County ten years and then moved to Roseau County, on the Canadian border, where he was still farming in 1921. He married on 13 September 1895, in Lac qui Parle County, Ida Pederson, born 14 November 1872 near Stavanger, Norway, daughter of Jonas and Malene Pederson. They had ten children, all born in Roseau County: Clara (10 Aug 1896), Manda (1 Apr 1898), Annie (7 Oct 1900), Palmer (24 Sep 1902), Julia (22 Sep 1904), Johnny (11 Jan 1907), Emil (12 Dec 1908), Inga (14 Feb 1911), an unnamed child born 1 March 1913 who died, and Lloyd (19 Dec 1914). He belonged to the Lutheran Free Church. ¶8

A fourth son of Paul and Kari Eidem of Selbu, unknown to this project until his mother's obituary was read. Minneapolis Tidende, 5 November 1925, p. 7, lists the survivors of Mrs. Kari P. Eidem as 'tre Soenner, Peder og John Eidem i Marietta og JAMES EIDEM I GREENBUSH, MINN.' - three sons, Peder and John Eidem of Marietta and James Eidem of Greenbush, Minnesota. ¶16

Corroborated independently in the settlement's own newspaper: the Lac qui Parle County Press (Madison, Minn.) delinquent real-estate tax list of 29 December 1893, p. 4, prints 'Eidem, James' in the same list as 'Eidem, Mike' - so a James Eidem held taxable land in Lac qui Parle County in 1893, in the same years and the same county as his brothers Peder and Mikal. ¶17

By 1925 he had left the colony and was living at GREENBUSH, ROSEAU COUNTY, MINNESOTA, on the Canadian border some 400 miles north of Marietta - a second-generation move onto the last cheap farmland in the state, which is the common pattern for younger sons of the 1880s Norwegian settlements. ¶18

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence Probable, not Confirmed: the relationship is stated by a newspaper obituary rather than by a vital record, and no birth, marriage, census or death record for him has yet been retrieved. 'James' is the American form; in Norwegian he would have been a Jens, and the Selbu register will hold him as a Jens Paulsen of Eidem. ¶19

The case for this identification 1

Why he was lost: he left the Marietta colony about 1893 for Roseau County, some 350 miles north, and the Lac qui Parle branch of the family lost touch. He is the reason the 1904 biography of his brother Peter could say there were five children when the family in later generations knew of only three. ¶9
