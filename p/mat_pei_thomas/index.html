About the family

Father of Benjamin Hayter and grandfather of Raymond Hayter. Born in England in October 1831 (1901 census: 'Oc[t] 27 [or 17], 1831', aged 69; the 1881 and 1891 censuses give born about 1832, England). He is the 'English origin' of family memory: Raymond's father was born in PEI, but his grandfather Thomas was born in England. ¶1

Life story

Baptism (image read): baptised 'Thomas George', aged nine months, at St Mary's Roman Catholic church, Halifax, Nova Scotia, on 4 August 1832, 'lawful son of Thomas Hayter and Martha Elizabeth Gibbs' (register entry no. 264; FamilySearch DGS 005190390, image 124; indexed as 'Heyton'). This fits his birth in October 1831 (1901 census) and his immigration in 1832: the family evidently stopped at Halifax on the way to PEI. Birth place left as England, which every census gives. His parents' grades now rest on this record (see Thomas Hayter). ¶18

1841 census: he is probably one of the two boys under 16 in his father's household (Thomas Hayter, weaver, Lot 64; 1841 census of PEI, Kings County District 15, image 524), aged about 9. The census names only heads of family. The household included at least one English-born child (image 526) and had paid its own passage to the Island, which fits his birth in England in October 1831 and his immigration in 1832. This strengthens his parentage but does not prove it, because he is not named (see Thomas Hayter senior). ¶16

Marriage: the register entry itself has now been read (PEI Marriage Registers, DGS 004594919, image 82). Thomas Hayter, bachelor, and Grace Amelia Moore, spinster, were married by licence on 4 December 1862 by Daniel Gordon, J.P. The witnesses were Martin Martin and Neil Mathewson. The entry was recorded at the Provincial Secretary's office on 2 February 1863. The printed form has no columns for residence, age or parents. No Hayter or Moore signed as a witness, so the entry says nothing about either party's parents. The Islander's notice places the wedding at Georgetown. ¶17

Marriage: to Grace Amelia Moore, 4 December 1862, Prince Edward Island (PEI marriage registers 1832-1888, index; register image not yet read). ¶3

Marriage notice: The Islander of 19 December 1862 reported: "At Georgetown, on the 4th instant, by D. Gordon, Esq., Mr. Thomas Hayter, to Miss Grace Amelia Moore, both of Murray Harbour." This matches the PEI marriage register entry of 4 December 1862. The notice adds two details. The couple were married at Georgetown by a justice of the peace, so it was a civil ceremony and not a church wedding. Both bride and groom were described as "of Murray Harbour", the district name then used for the Lot 64 settlements. ¶12

Land, 1863: The 1863 Lake topographical map of Prince Edward Island marks two holdings in Lot 64 with the name T. Hayter, which the engraver spelt "T. Haylor" and "T. Haytor". One is inland on the road west of the Toronto settlement, next to R. Moore, J. Hooper and T. Giddings Jr. This is very probably the young couple's farm beside Grace Moore's family. The other is a south-shore farm in the White Sands run, very probably that of his father, Thomas Hayter senior. ¶13

Community: In 1863 Thomas Hayter witnessed the will of James Richards senior of Murray Harbour, and he swore to it at probate on 8 September 1863. In 1879 he witnessed the will of John Hooper of Murray Harbour, a neighbour on the 1863 map, and swore to it at probate on 1 March 1880. Both wills are transcribed on the Island Register. ¶14

Middle name (context only): his son Thomas, born about 1867-69, appears as 'Thomas G. Hayter' in the 1891 census of Lot 64, on his gravestone (Murray River Community Cemetery, 1867-1930) and in his 1930 Surrogate Court estate file ('Thomas G. Hayter late of Murray River'). No record found expands the G. If it stands for George, it echoes the Halifax baptismal name 'Thomas George' (1832). This is a naming pattern, so it supports his identification with the Halifax infant only as circumstantial evidence (Probable). ¶21a

Land, 1880: Meacham's Illustrated Historical Atlas of Prince Edward Island (1880), plan of Lots 63 and 64, marks "Thomas Hayter 100" acres on a shore farm at High Bank, Lot 64, on the Northumberland Strait. It lies between Murdoch McSwain and Donald McLean, with John McKenzie, Hector Gillis and John Giddings further along. Directly inland are the farms of Humphry Moore (93½ acres), William, Charles, Benjamin and John Moore, and William and John Hooper. He is the only Hayter landholder on Lots 63–64 in 1880. In 1863 the Lake map showed this same run of shore farms, beside McLean, McKenzie and Giddings, held by a "T. Haytor", very probably his father. Thomas junior had evidently taken over his father's farm by 1880. This is where Benjamin was born in 1874. ¶15

1881 census, Lot 64, Kings County: Thomas Hayter, born about 1832 in England, with wife Grace (born about 1838, PEI) and children Sarah (1864), William (1865), Elizabeth (1867), Thomas (1869), Humphrey (1871), Mary (1872), Benjamin (1874) and James (1879). His is the only Hayter household in PEI in 1881. ¶4

In the 1881 census (HGIS Canada person-level transcription, Township 64, household 83) he is Thomas Hayter, 49, Baptist, of English origin, born in England, a farmer, with Grace (43) and children Sarah, William, Elizabeth, Thomas, Humphrey, Mary, Benjamin and James, all Baptist. ¶11

Directory, 1889-90: 'Hayther Thos, farmer', at High Bank, Lot 64, King's County, 40 miles from Charlottetown and 22 from Georgetown (Frederick's PEI Directory). ¶6

1891 census, Lot 64: Thomas Hayter, head, born about 1832 England; Grace A., wife; sons William H., Thomas G., Humphrey, Benjamin, James, Joseph W. and Charles, and daughter Mary Jane. ¶5

Land Purchase deed (FamilySearch signed in; image read): Prince Edward Island Land Records 1892-1897, image 392 of 899 (ark 3:1:3Q9M-CS1M-ZSZX-F). The Commissioner of Public Lands (Angus McMillan) conveys to 'Thomas Hayter of Lot or Township No. Sixty four', for $146, 100 acres on the shore of the Gulf of St Lawrence at High Bank, Lot 64, beside land leased to Donald McLean the younger, 'being the land described in a lease to the said Thomas Hayter dated 6th day of March A.D. 1856'. Marginal plan: 'lease 100 acres Thomas Hayter 6 March 1856, High Bank'. The FamilySearch transcript dates the deed to about 1895. This is the record behind the Ron Hayter family history's '1856 Cunard lease to Thomas Hayter, the Younger': the 1856 lessee is the same man who bought the High Bank farm about 1895, i.e. Thomas junior of the 1880 atlas. The deed names no parents and does not use 'the Younger'. Crop:. ¶20

Immigration: the 1901 census gives his year of immigration to Canada as 1832 - so he came to Prince Edward Island as an infant, with his parents. 1832 was a heavy year for English emigration to PEI (ships that season came from Yarmouth, Bideford, Plymouth, Bristol and London). The parish and county in England are not yet known. ¶2

1901 census, Lot 64 polling division 2, dwelling 80 (High Bank): Thomas Hayter, head, married, aged 69, born England, immigrated 1832, English origin, Baptist, farmer on his own account; wife Grace, born 13 January 1838; sons James (b. 1878), Joseph (b. Feb 1883) and Charles (b. 21 March 1884), all farm sons. The schedule-2 extract records no owned land against his name, while his sons William (80 acres) and Benjamin (50 acres) at Murray River South each owned farms - suggesting the land had passed to the sons. (The Island Register's 1901 heads-of-household extract also lists a 'Murdoch Hayter' at High Bank in the next dwelling; the image shows that man was a Scots-born Presbyterian of another surname, so there was no Murdoch Hayter.) ¶7

Religion: Baptist (1901); his son's family were still Baptist in 1911 and 1931. ¶9

Death: not in the 1911 census, where his sons head their own households; so he died between 1901 and 1911, probably in Lot 64. Not yet found: his death or burial record. He is not among the ten Hayter memorials at Murray River Cemetery on Find a Grave (which includes his son Thomas G., 1867-1930). ¶8

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Audit 11 (2 Nov 2026): his parents' link to him (Thomas Hayter, Martha Elizabeth Gibbs) is graded Probable, not Confirmed. The Halifax baptism of 'Thomas George' (1832) is very probably his, but the identification across places rests on birth month, immigration year and the absence of another Hayter family. See the audit 11 notes on his father. ¶19

Searches that found nothing 1

Baptism in England - not found (wave 6). No baptism of a Thomas Hayter (or variant) in October 1831 to mid-1832 to a father Thomas was found in the England-wide christening indexes (Ancestry 1351 and 9841), FreeREG, or the Somerset, Bristol (Anglican and nonconformist), Dorset, Wiltshire (Anglican and nonconformist), Hampshire, Devon, Gloucestershire-nonconformist, Westminster or London baptism collections. The only Thomas Hayter christened in that window, at Warminster on 10 April 1832, was the son of John and Sarah. If he was baptised at all before sailing, the likeliest home of the record is an unindexed nonconformist register (TNA RG 4), such as a Bible Christian, Wesleyan, Baptist or Independent chapel. ¶10
