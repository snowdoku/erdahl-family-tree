About the family

Brother of Raymond Hayter. He is the 'C. E. Hayter of Penticton' who reported Raymond's death in March 1947. ¶1

Children: Cecil and Evelyn had eight children, four sons and four daughters, according to Evelyn's 2014 obituary. All are presumably living and are not recorded here. ¶6

Life story

Marriage, BC 38-09-465867: Cecil Hayter, truck driver, bachelor, aged 27, United Church, of Penticton, born Gaspereaux, Prince Edward Island; father Benjamin Hayter, born High Bank, PEI; mother Della Hicken, born Gaspereaux, PEI. He married Evelyn Ruby Preston, 19, of Kaleden, on 11 October 1938 at Kaleden, by licence, Rev. Allen F. G. Kenney (Pentecostal) officiating. The witnesses were Ray Hayter of Penticton (his brother Raymond) and Dorothy Preston of Kaleden. ¶2

PEI records: in his father's household at Gaspereaux in 1911 (born December 1909). In the 1931 census, aged 21, a labourer on his mother's farm at Gaspereaux - so he moved to BC after June 1931. ¶8

In December 1951 Cecil, then of Kaleden, gave his sister Rita away at her second wedding, in West Summerland; he and his wife were among the guests (Summerland Review, 3 January 1952). ¶9

Orchard: The History of Kaleden (about 1958, p. 57) lists among the original orchard plantings ten acres planted in 1910 by Mr. and Mrs. Harry Elsey of Pilot Mound, Manitoba. 'Since then there have been several owners. Now the property of Cecil Hayter.' This was Cecil's orchard at Kaleden, where he was an orchardist from about 1947 until his death. ¶7

Death, BC 63-09-006308: Cecil Elwood Hayter, orchardist, of Box 18, Kaleden, died at Penticton General Hospital on 13 May 1963, aged 53, born 25 December 1909 at Gaspereaux, PEI. He had been an orchardist for 16 years, so from about 1947. Parents Benjamin Hayter and Lydia Hicken. The informant was his wife Evelyn. He was buried at Lakeview Cemetery, Penticton, on 17 May 1963 by Roselawn Funeral Home. ¶3

Military service: his gravestone at Lakeview Cemetery, Penticton (Section I-17-5, photographed on Find a Grave and read), is a military marker: 'K84 CPL. C. E. HAYTER / 1909 - 1963 / 9th Armoured Regiment - B.C.D. / At Rest'. He served as a corporal in the 9th Armoured Regiment (British Columbia Dragoons) in the Second World War. His death registration says he had lived in Penticton for 24 years 'except while overseas'. The 'K84' on the stone is probably the start of a K-series service number; read it from the service file. ¶4

Lead: a classified advertisement in the Summerland Review of late April and May 1947 offered 'all your shingling requirements capably handled by Hayter & Spaurel. Write Box 719, Penticton.' This was probably Cecil, between his work as a truck driver and his orchard at Kaleden; not proved. ¶10
