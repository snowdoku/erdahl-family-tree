About the family

Eldest child of Benjamin Hayter and Lydia Hicken; Raymond's eldest brother. In his father's household at Gaspereaux in the 1911 census as Glen, son, born April 1902, Baptist. ¶1

Probable children (Find a Grave index): Raymond G. Hayter, born 19 February 1930, died 10 March 1930, buried Aberdeen, Bingham County, Idaho; and Rex G. Hayter, 15 March 1931 - 5 April 1975, buried American Falls - named, it seems, for Glen's brothers Raymond and Rex. Not yet linked by a primary record. ¶3

Wife: Mary Marguerite Hoffer, born 27 February 1911 in North Dakota, died 11 January 1976 at American Falls, daughter of Ludwig Hoffer (1888-1924) and Marie Anna 'Mary' Langheinrich (1884-1972), according to her Find a Grave memorial. The Hoffers belonged to the German-Russian community around Aberdeen, Idaho, into which Glenn's sister Rosebud also married. ¶8

Children (Find a Grave family links and gravestones): Raymond G. Hayter, born 19 February 1930 at American Falls, died 10 March 1930 at Aberdeen aged 19 days, buried in Aberdeen Cemetery under the inscription 'Our Darling Son Raymond G. Hayter'; Rex G. Hayter, 15 March 1931 - 5 April 1975, buried beside his parents in Falls View Cemetery (plot E-302-4); and Glenna Hayter, born 21 February 1934 at American Falls, who married John Harold Caywood in 1951 and later a Swigart, and died 13 February 2001 (buried Hillcrest Memorial Gardens, Caldwell, Idaho). The first two sons carry the names of Glenn's brothers Raymond and Rex - Rex had been killed in a British Columbia logging camp in April 1929, a month before Glenn's wedding. ¶9

His daughter Glenna's Social Security claims index entry names her parents as Glenn Hayter and Mary Hoffer, which ties the American Falls family together by a federal record. ¶10

Life story

Emigrated to the United States: the Find a Grave index has Glenn Gordon Hayter, born 27 April 1902, died 10 January 1994, buried at American Falls, Power County, Idaho - the same day as the Island Register's birth date for Glen G. Hayter of Gaspereaux, who it says died at American Falls. The Island Register gives his wife as Mary M., born 27 February 1911 in North Dakota, died January 1976 at American Falls. ¶2

Emigration: as Glenn Gordon Hayter, born about 1903 at Gaspereaux, he entered the United States at Seattle on 20 April 1929 (US border-crossing index), presumably from British Columbia, where his brothers Rex and Raymond were working. Not in his parents' 1921 household. ¶4

Marriage and grave: Glenn Gordon Hayter shares an upright double headstone in Falls View Cemetery, American Falls, Idaho (plot E-302-2) with his wife Mary Marguerite. The stone, read from the Find a Grave photograph, gives 'GLENN GORDON / APR. 27, 1902 / JAN. 10, 1994' and 'MARY MARGUERITE / FEB. 27, 1911 / JAN. 11, 1976', with the words 'MARRIED MAY 18, 1929' beneath a heart and interlocked rings. A cluster of potatoes is carved on the shoulder of the stone, the emblem of a potato farmer in the American Falls - Aberdeen district. ¶6

Arrival in Idaho: he entered the United States at Seattle on 20 April 1929 and married four weeks later, on 18 May 1929. His sister Rosebud had been in the same district since about 1926, which probably explains why he went to southeastern Idaho rather than staying in British Columbia with his brothers. ¶7

Earlier censuses (index only, images not yet read): in 1930 Glen G. Hayter, born Canada about 1903, was head of a household with his wife Mary M. at Buhl, Twin Falls County, Idaho; in 1940 he was at Aberdeen, Bingham County, with his wife Mary M. and daughter Glenna M. ¶12

Marriage record: the Western States Marriage Index, compiled from the Power County, Idaho, register, records the marriage of Glenn Gordon Hayter (keyed as 'Hagter') and Mary Marguerite Hoffer (keyed as 'May Maiguainte') on 18 May 1929 in Power County - the date carved on their headstone. ¶13

1950 census: Glenn G. Hayter, 47, born Canada and a naturalized U.S. citizen, was farming on his own account on Route 1 outside Aberdeen, Bingham County, Idaho (enumeration district 6-4, sheet 15, enumerated 10 April 1950; 'East on Boat Dock Road from Experiment Station'). The household was his wife Mary M., 39, born North Dakota, and five children, all born in Idaho, including Rex G. (19) and Glenna M. (16). The census gives both his parents as born in Canada. The other three children are not named here because they may be living; their names are in the hayter-diaspora dossier. ¶11
