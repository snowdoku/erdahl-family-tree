About the family

David Erdahl's maternal great-great-great-grandfather, the father of Anna Olene (Satter) Eidem. Reached 11 Oct 2026. ¶1

Children: five are now known — Gustav Parelius 'Gust' (1864), Fred (1868), Lizzie Carrie (1870), Anna Olene (1872) and Lewis. Lewis appears on no Find a Grave listing and was recovered from two independent printed sources, Lizzie's 1934 obituary and the gift list at Gust's wedding. ¶25

Named as her father in his daughter's obituary: Leader-Courier (Elk Point, South Dakota), 18 March 1937 - 'Anna [Olene] Satter, daughter of GUNDER and GURI ANNA SATTER, was born March 21, 1872 in Union county, South Dakota, on the farm just west of Spink.' The parentage no longer rests on a Find a Grave family listing alone. ¶33

Parentage, and how it was proved: his parents are attached on the strength of his own marriage entry naming his father as 'Gunder Sivertsen' and his birthplace as 'Frosten', cross-checked against a complete enumeration of the Frosta register. In Church book from Frosta parish 1827-1839 only ONE Gunder Sivertsen appears as a father anywhere in the book — an inderst at Snytan in 1831 and 1833 and at Moxnes by 1835, with the same wife throughout. Of the six boys christened Gunder in Frosta between 1834 and 1837, only two had a father named Gunder and so could carry the patronymic Gundersen: this one, and the son of Gunder Monssen, gaardmand of Hovdahl, born 23 June 1837 — and that man is independently accounted for, marrying at Frosta on 11 July 1864 as 'Gunder Gunderssen, b. 1837 Frosten, ungkarl, of Hofdal'. This is therefore not a name-and-year fit but an identification from a primary record with the competition eliminated. ¶39

Siblings: full sisters Anne Cicilie (b. 1831), Mette (b. 1833, married at Frosta in 1860), Hanna (b. 1838 at Søndre Kjølen, Skogn, married at Frosta in 1860) and Oline Margrethe (b. 1842, probably died 1843). Half-sister Gunelie Margrethe was born in 1853 from his father's second marriage to Martha Erichsdatter Skaaraas. ¶50

Life story

The surname is rendered Satter, Sather and Satler in American records; it is the Norwegian farm name Saeter. His daughter Anna Olene was born at Spink, Union County, Dakota Territory, on 21 March 1872, so the family was already on the Missouri bluffs by then. ¶2

Mayme (Eidem) Eidem's maternal grandfather, named as the father of Anna Olene Satter on her Find a Grave memorial. The surname is also recorded as SATHER and as SATLER; Satter is itself an Americanisation of a Norwegian farm name, most likely Saeter or Seter ('the summer pasture'), which is among the commonest farm names in Norway and so is not by itself a clue to the parish. ¶4

Gravestone: his stone at Brule Creek Cemetery, Spink Township, Union County, South Dakota is photographed and reads GUNDER SATTER / BORN June 13, 1835 / DIED Sept. 12, 1909, with SATTER cut into the base. Read from the photograph, which is why his dates are rated Confirmed rather than Probable. ¶17

Birthplace: Frosta, Frosta kommune, Nord-Trøndelag, Norway, as given on his Find a Grave memorial. Both of this couple's bynames are Frosta farm names — Sæter/Sætra and Valstad — which is consistent, but the parish register itself has not yet been read, so the Norwegian origin is stated here at Probable even though the dates are Confirmed. ¶18

Name: the surname is Americanised inconsistently. His own stone reads SATTER; his wife's stone beside it reads SATHER; the South Dakota records also throw up Satler. In Norway he would have been Gunder Gundersen, a patronymic, with Sæter as the farm byname — so his father was also a Gunder. ¶19

Gravestone, photographed and read: a tall white marble obelisk, 'GUNDER SATTER / BORN / June 13, 1835 / DIED / Sept. 12, 1909'. Find a Grave memorial 63592002, photograph by contributor 'Rod'. The file previously carried only the years 1835 and 1909; both dates are now exact, and confidence rises from Probable to Confirmed. ¶32

The surname in his own generation is SATHER as often as Satter: his wife's stone names him 'G. G. SATHER' and the Elk Point papers print him as both 'G. G. Sather' and 'Gunder Satter' in the same decade. He farmed at SPINK, Sioux Valley Township, Union County. ¶34

Baptism: born 12 JUNE 1835 and baptised at Frosta 28 JUNE 1835, Church book from Frosta parish 1827-1839 (1717P), page 53. Father GUNDER SIVERTSEN, 'Indst' (inderst, a lodger with no land of his own), of MOXNES; mother KAREN HELGESDATTER. The pastor's remark is 'Lægd Moxnæss' — the household was on the lægd, the parish poor-relief rotation under which a destitute family was quartered on each farm of a district in turn. He was born into a pauper household and died a landowner in Dakota. ¶37

Move to Skogn, 1838: he is the 'Gunder Gundersen, 3 Aar' in the Skogn in-migration list of 1838 (no. 2), which brought his parents and sisters from Frosta to the cottar holding of Søndre Kjølen in Ekne sokn, Skogn. He grew up there. His mother died there on 10 November 1842, when he was seven, and his father married again in 1847. ¶48

Confirmation: he was confirmed at Alstadhaug church, Skogn, on 26 September 1852, aged 17. He is entered as 'Gunder Sæthe' (or Sathe), born 12 June 1835 at Frosta, son of 'Hmd. Gunder Sivertsen, K. Karen Helgesdatter' (Ministerialbok for Skogn, Alstadhaug sokn 1850-1862, Confirmerede 1852, p. 198, boys no. 11). His knowledge was graded 'godt' (good). The byname Sæthe is the farm Sæther (Sæthe) in Alstadhaug, presumably where he lived or served at seventeen. This is the earliest record of him under the name that later appears in America as Sather, Satter and Setter. ¶49

Context: Union County is the oldest-settled corner of Dakota Territory, organised in 1862. At the 1870 census the whole Territory held under 12,000 people, so a Norwegian family homesteading at Spink by 1870 is among the earliest settlers there. ¶22

Church: the family's congregation was the Norwegian Evangelical Lutheran Congregation by Brule Creek, organised by fifteen settlers on 12 August 1863 and later renamed St. Paul's Evangelical Lutheran Congregation in Dakota. Its log church was built in 1867, its cemetery opened in 1869, and its first settled pastor was J. E. Nesse (1869-1881). Gunder and Guri lie in its Spink Township burial ground; their daughter Anna lies in the Elk Point one. ¶30

Marriage: married at VÆRNES CHURCH, Nedre Stjørdal (Stjørdal Værnes), on 19 JUNE 1863 — not in Trondheim, as had been assumed, but in the bride's own parish. Church book from Stjørdal/Nedre Stjørdal (Værnes) parish 1859-1870 (1714Q), Viede, folio 243, entry no. 16. He is entered as 'Ungk[arl]: Gunder Gundersen Næs, af Trondhjem — f[ødt] i Frosten', aged 27; she as 'Pige Guriana Petersdatter Valstad', aged 23. The groom's father is named as GUNDER SIVERTSEN and the bride's as PETER OLSEN. Witnesses (forlovere): Torsten Valstad and Peder Ols[en] ibid[em] — the second being the bride's own father, of Valstad. Banns read on Trinity Sunday and the first and second Sundays after Trinity 1863 (31 May, 7 and 14 June). His vaccination is recorded 'v. 19/2 36' — vaccinated 19 February 1836, at eight months old. ¶36

Residence 1863: at his marriage his residence is given as 'Næs af Trondhjem' — he had already left Frosta for the town before he married. By September 1864 the couple were at LADEMOEN, the working-class district across the river from Bakklandet, where he is described as 'arbeidsmand' (day-labourer) at their son Gustav Parelius's baptism. ¶40

Crossing bracketed: his eldest son Gustav Parelius Satter was born in JULY 1864 in NORWAY (so stated in both the 1900 and the 1910 federal censuses), and his daughter Anna Olene was born at Spink, Union County, on 21 March 1872. With the 1870 census showing the family not yet in Union County, the family crossed the Atlantic between 1 June 1870 and the summer of 1871. Whether Lizzie, born 1870, was born in Norway or in Dakota would halve that window again. ¶7

Trondheim, 1864: at the baptism of his eldest son at Bakklandet in Trondheim on 4 September 1864 he is recorded as 'Gunnar Gundersen, Arbmd.' — arbeidsmand, a day-labourer — with the household living at Lademoen. The family had therefore already left the land for the town before they left Norway, which is the commonest route out of Norway in the 1860s and explains why a farm byname attaches to a man who was briefly an urban labourer. ¶26

Emigration: the family left Norway in 1865 carrying their infant son Gust, born 1864. By May 1868 they were at Sioux City, Woodbury County, Iowa, where Fred was born, and by May 1870 they were on their own homestead in Dakota Territory, where Lizzie was born. (Immigration year from the research summary on Gust's memorial; the birthplaces from the children's own records.) ¶20

The homestead: his daughter Lizzie's 1934 obituary places her birth in May 1870 'on the Satter homestead, one mile north of the Spink store' in Union County. The family were therefore on the land within five years of landing. ¶21

1870 census: 'Setter, Gee', aged 36, FARMER, real estate $600, personal estate $200, born Norway, head of dwelling and family 122, Sioux Valley, Union County, Dakota Territory; post office Elk Point; enumerated 21 August 1870 by E. H. Webb, Ass't Marshal; page 13 (stamped sheet 137), lines 16-20; NARA M593 roll 118. The household is 'Annie' 35 (Norway), 'Gusta' 6 (Norway), 'Godfrey' 3, and 'Mary' 1/12 born in Dakota with 'born within the year — May'. This is the family's earliest American record and it had never been found: THE SURNAME AS ENUMERATED IS 'SETTER', which is why every index search under Satter, Sather and Gunderson failed. The reel carries no OCR, so the entry exists in no online index at all and was found by reading the microfilm page by page. ¶41

Context: a pre-emptor had to pay cash for his claim — $1.25 an acre, so $200 for 160 acres. In August 1870, five years off the boat, he was already returning $600 of real estate and $200 of personal estate to the marshal, four years before he patented the land. ¶42

Context, how he got it: he was in Dakota Territory by early 1872. A homestead entry required five years' residence and so could not have patented before 1877. A patent in September 1874 therefore means a PRE-EMPTION, or a homestead commuted to cash - under the Pre-emption Act of 1841 a settler who occupied and improved up to 160 acres of surveyed public land could buy it outright after as little as six months at the government minimum price of $1.25 an acre, so 160 acres for about $200. Pre-emption was the quicker but more expensive route and tells us he had, or could borrow, the purchase money. ¶13

Land patent, 1874 - found under the spelling SATHER: on 1 SEPTEMBER 1874 the United States issued him a land patent, General Land Office document number 515, accession STA-SD1050__.487, indexed as "GUNDER SATHER", for the NORTH HALF OF THE SOUTH-EAST QUARTER and the NORTH HALF OF THE SOUTH-WEST QUARTER OF SECTION 21, TOWNSHIP 93 NORTH, RANGE 50 WEST OF THE 5TH PRINCIPAL MERIDIAN, Union County, Dakota Territory - 160 acres in two eighty-acre strips across the middle of the section. This is the first land record ever found for him, and it was missed until now because the land office wrote the name Sather while the census and the church book write Satter; both are the Norwegian farm name Saeter. ¶12

Land, the neighbourhood: Ole Walstad patented the north-east quarter of section 17 in the same township, T93N R50W, on the SAME DAY, 1 September 1874 - and Gunder's wife was Guri Anna Pettersdatter Walstad. Haldo Thompsen Eidem's Union County patent, document 503, is also dated 1 September 1874, twelve document numbers earlier in the same volume. John Sivertson Eidem had patented the SE1/4SE1/4 of section 35 of this same township the year before, and the rest of the Eidems were in section 1 of T92N R50W immediately to the south. The marriage of his daughter Anna Olene Satter to Severt S. Eidem about 1899 was a marriage between families who had taken up land side by side a quarter of a century earlier. ¶14

Where the land is, in plain terms: Township 93 North, Range 50 West of the 5th Principal Meridian lies in Union County in the far south-eastern corner of South Dakota, on the bluffs above the Missouri. His daughter Anna Olene was born at SPINK in Union County in March 1872, two and a half years before the patent issued, and Spink is one of the county's twelve civil townships; I have not been able to confirm from a free source which civil township covers T93N R50W, so the identification of his farm with Spink specifically is left open rather than asserted. ¶15

1880 federal census: NOT SEARCHED, and the reason is worth recording. Ancestry's 1880 collection (dbId 6742) is broken for anonymous clients - every query, including a control search for 'John Smith', returns a page reading only 'Unknown error'. Every other census collection works from the same client. The 1880 census of Spink township is the single most valuable unread record for this family. ¶8

1880 census: head of household as 'SATTER, Gunder' (indexed 'Gunder Satler'), married, farmer, born Norway of Norwegian parents, at dwelling 78, family 84 in Spink Township, Union County, Dakota Territory, enumerated 15-16 June 1880 (ED 5, sheet 407A, lines 3-11). The household, as written: Gunder 48 or 49 (the ink is ambiguous; indexed 49), head, farmer, born Norway; 'Anna' 49, wife, keeping house, Norway; Gustav 16, son, farmer, Norway (indexed 'Burrton'); Godfried 12, son, farmer, Iowa (indexed 'Badfred'); Eliza R. 10, daughter, at school, Dakota Territory; Anna C. 8, daughter, at school, D.T.; Sophia 6, daughter, at school, D.T.; Mattilda 4, daughter, D.T.; Lewis 2, son, D.T. All parents born Norway. ¶51

1880 census, the children identified: Gustav = Gust (see his page), Godfried born Iowa = Fred (see his page, born Sioux City 1868), Eliza R. = Lizzie (see her page), Anna C. = Anna Olene (see her page), Lewis = Lewis (see his page). Sophia, 6, and Mattilda, 4, are two daughters not previously known (Sophia Satter, Mattilda Satter). ¶52

1880 census, ages: his stated age of 48 or 49 is three to four years too old for his baptismal birth date of 12 June 1835 (he was 45 on the census day), and his wife's 49 is likewise about five years over her April 1836 birth. The baptisms are the better evidence; the census ages are recorded as written. ¶53

1880 census, neighbour: the next dwelling (79, family 85) is 'Walstead, Ole', 37, alone, farmer, born Norway - his wife's brother Ole Pettersen Walstad (see his page). This confirms from the image the Find a Grave statement that the Satters lived next door to Ole Walstad. This census supersedes the earlier note that the 1880 census was not searched. ¶54

1885 Dakota Territorial census and 1895 South Dakota state census: these do not survive for Union County. I established the surviving coverage empirically by running four common Norwegian surnames through each index: 1885 returns only Moody, Lake, Turner, Spink County, Hyde, Sanborn, Faulk, Beadle, Charles Mix and Hand; 1895 returns only Brule, Charles Mix and Beadle. Union County is in neither. Guri Anna died in 1895 and the 1895 schedule would have been the last record of her alive; those pages are lost, not merely un-digitised. ¶9

Second marriage: Guri died 6 July 1895 and in 1900 he married Aagot Christine Aagesdatter Wangberg (1867-1915), thirty-two years his junior, who is buried as Aagot Christine Aagesdatter Wangberg Sather. ¶23

1900 federal census: Spink township, Union County, South Dakota, Ancestry image 4120605_00246 - 'Gunder Sasher [Satter], BOARDER, born JUNE 1835, Norway'. At sixty-four, five years after Guri Anna's death, he had given up his own household and was boarding in another man's house in the township where his children had been born. The stated month and year match the Brule Creek Cemetery stone (13 June 1835 - 12 September 1909) exactly. ¶10

Source of the '1900' second-marriage date: it comes only from the Find a Grave memorial of Aagot Christine Aagesdatter Wangberg Sather (w4 satter dossier); no marriage record has been read. The 1900 federal census lists him as a BOARDER, which fits a marriage after June 1900 - or the date is wrong. The April 1897 Courier phrase 'her parents, Mr. and Mrs. Gunder Satter' would fit an earlier marriage; the date stays unproved. ¶44

Remarried by mid-1901 (read on the page images). The Union County Leader of 1 August 1901 reports 'Mr. and Mrs. G. Sether and Miss Wangberg worshiped in the Ahlsborg church Sunday', and an issue of about October 1901 'Mr. and Mrs. Gunder Satter returned Tuesday from a few days visit with relatives in Sioux City'. Since he was a wifeless boarder in the June 1900 census, the second marriage falls between mid-1900 and 1 August 1901, consistent with the Find a Grave '1900'. Neither item names the wife; the Miss Wangberg in his company points to Aagot Wangberg's family. No marriage notice was found on Fulton. ¶46

1905 South Dakota state census: 'Gunder SATHER, b. 1835, Norway'. The state clerk wrote the name Sather, which is why a search on Satter misses him. He died on 12 September 1909, so this is his last census. ¶11

1905 South Dakota state census: 'Sather, Gunder, 70', post office Spink, Spink Township, Union County, Section 21, Township 93 North, Range 50 West, card 217 — and the very next card, 218, is 'Sather, Mrs. Gunder, 35', of the same section. Consecutive cards in this index mean one household, so this is independent documentary confirmation of the second marriage, which until now rested only on a Find a Grave spouse listing. His stated age of 70 fits a birth in June 1835 exactly. ¶28

His land and the 1905 census agree exactly. The patent found by the land agent — issued 1 September 1874 under the spelling SATHER, document 515, the N half of the SE quarter and the N half of the SW quarter of section 21, Township 93 North, Range 50 West, 160 acres, a pre-emption — is the same section 21, T93N R50W on which the 1905 state census still finds him thirty-one years later. He bought that land for cash under the pre-emption law rather than proving up a homestead, which means he was already living on it and could find the purchase money; at the statutory $1.25 an acre that was $200 in the early 1870s, a serious sum for a man who had been a day-labourer in Trondheim in 1864. ¶31

Death: he died at Sioux City, Iowa on 12 September 1909, twenty miles from Elk Point, and was brought back to Brule Creek for burial. His memorial notes he 'died at the age of 74 yrs and 1 month', which is arithmetically exact for 13 Jun 1835 to 12 Sep 1909 and so checks the stone independently. ¶24

He left a will. The Union County Courier of 23 September 1909 prints a 'Notice of Time Appointed For Proving Will' in 'the matter of the estate of Gunder Sather, deceased': by order of 22 September 1909 the County Court at Elk Point set Saturday 9 October 1909, 10 a.m., 'for proving the will of said Gunder Sather, deceased, and for hearing the application of Aagot Sather for the issuance to Gust Sather of letters of Administration with the will annexed' (Jerome Mangan, judge). This is the first primary record found naming his second wife Aagot, alive in September 1909; until now the second marriage rested on Find a Grave and on 'Mrs. Gunder Sather, 35' in the 1905 state census index. The will and the probate file (Union County Court, Oct 1909) should name every heir. ¶43

No obituary found in the Union County Courier of 16 September 1909 (Local Happenings read on the image); the 23 September locals page (Fulton file 1602) has not yet been read. ¶45

No obituary found in the Union County Courier. The Local Happenings and correspondence of 16, 23 and 30 September 1909 were read on the images (the 30 September 'Deaths' list names others only); the only Courier items for his death are the two probate notices of 23 September 1909. The Elk Point Leader and the Sioux City papers for September 1909 remain unread. ¶47

Cemetery register: the DT58 register of Union County lists GUNDER SATTER, died 12 September 1909, North Brule Creek cemetery, lot 9, block 2 (city column Elk Point). The date agrees with his gravestone; Guri Anna Sather is entered in the same lot and block. ¶55

The reason is that his gravestone at Brule Creek Cemetery has now been read from a photograph — GUNDER SATTER / BORN June 13, 1835 / DIED Sept. 12, 1909 — and his existence and place are independently in the 1905 South Dakota state census and in a federal land patent of 1 September 1874 on the same section. Corrections 1

Correction: his birth date should be read as 12 JUNE 1835 from the baptismal register, not the 13 June 1835 cut on his Brule Creek gravestone. A one-day error on a stone cut in America seventy-four years after the event is the likelier of the two to be wrong. ¶38

Searches that found nothing 2

1870 federal census: ABSENT. I searched the 1870 census of Union County, Dakota Territory under Satter, Sather and the given name Gunder, and the surname Gunderson in Spink and Brule townships. There is no Gunder Satter, no Guri and no Satter household of any kind in Union County in June 1870. The only Satters in the whole of Dakota Territory that year are Thomas and Engber Satter of Clay County, the next county west, with children Hannah, Olive and Rachael - probably kin, but not this family. ¶6

1870 agricultural census, a clean negative: the published index of about 1,400 Dakota Territory farms holds no Satter, Sather, Sater, Walstad or Valstad in Union County — the only Satter in the Territory is a Thomas Satter of Clay County. Since Lizzie was born on the Satter homestead in May 1870 and the agricultural schedule was taken that June, the likeliest reading is that the claim was a month old and had nothing to report. He was not yet a producing farmer. ¶29

Open questions and the next record to read 2

Open question on his own parents: Find a Grave puts his birth at Frosta on 13 June 1835, and the Frosta register holds one near-fit — a Gunder born 12 June 1835 and baptised 28 June, son of Gunder Sivertsen, an inderst (landless lodger) at Moxnes on the parish poor-relief roll, and Karen Helgesdatter; that couple out-migrated from Frosta to Skogn on 26 February 1838. The patronymic, parish and year all fit and the day is one out, but no further record ties the two men together, so this is recorded as a lead only and no parents are attached to him here. ¶27
