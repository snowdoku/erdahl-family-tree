About the family

David Erdahl's maternal great-great-grandfather, and the immigrant generation on this line. 1950 census: 'Eidem, John P., head, W M 73, married, born Norway', household 8 on the same sheet as Palmer G. Eidem's household 5, in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota, enumeration district 37-25. His wife is 'Oline S., 69, born Norway' and his son 'Peter C., 40, born Minnesota'. ¶1

Parentage: Probable, not Confirmed. He is placed as a son of Paul Eidem of Selbu on his burial among that family at Zoar Lutheran, his Norwegian birth in the right decade, and the patronymic initial 'P.' No baptism or passenger record has yet been retrieved. ¶10

Children, from his wife's 1955 obituary, which names all eight: Clara (Mrs. Julius Jornlin), Olga (Mrs. Peder Lerseth), Cora Belinde (Mrs. Elmer Peterson), Barbara (Mrs. Cle Kyllo), May (Mrs. Herbert Gieseke), Palmer, Peder Conrad and John Jr. Five of the ten members of this household are buried at Zoar: John P. (lot 1A), Oline (lot 1), Clara Jornlin (lot 12), May Gieseke (lot 2) and John Jr. (lot 3A), with Peder Conrad also there. ¶43

He is named for his great-grandfather John, the father of Ingebrigt Johnsen. He was the youngest of the five and was five years old when his father died in December 1881. ¶57

His father Paul 'Eidem' is PAUL INGEBRIGTSEN, a husmann on the Eidem farm, buried 26 December 1881 - so John was five when his father died, not four, and the 1904 printed biography's '1881' is right to the year. See the notes on Paul I. Eidem for the whole chain. ¶66

Life story

Birth: 17 September 1876 in Norway (Minnesota death index). The 1950 U.S. census, which I read from the original sheet, gives his age as 73 and his birthplace as NORWAY, and the sample-line questions confirm that both of his own parents were born in Norway. ¶5

Baptism: born 17 SEPTEMBER 1876 and baptised 29 October 1876 at Selbu church. Selbu parish register 1860-1877, page 26 serial no. 122. Father 'Poul Ingebrigtsen, b. 1826 (calculated)', mother 'Karen Pedersdr, b. 1834 (calculated)', residence Eidum. ¶55

His birth date of 17 September 1876, carried in the American records, is confirmed to the day by the Selbu parish register - an independent Norwegian primary source agreeing exactly with what the family had. His birthplace can now be given as Eidem in Selbu rather than simply 'Norway'. ¶56

Printed source: his given name John and his birth year 1876 occur on page 207 of Selbu i fortid og naatid, bind 4 (1992), chapter BRENNHAUGEN - the page that also carries Oline, Slind, 1881, 1901, 1904, Palmer Gilbert, Clara, Olga, Amerika and Minnesota. The Norwegian farm history followed this family across the Atlantic and printed his marriage year and the names of his American children. ¶62

Emigration: born in Norway about 1877, and in Minnesota by about 1910, since his son Peter C. was born there. Eidem and the neighbouring Slind are both farm names in Selbu, Sor-Trondelag, which is where his origin should be sought - in Digitalarkivet's censuses of 1865, 1875, 1891 and 1900, in the Selbu parish registers, and in the Trondheim emigrant lists. That work is the open frontier of this line. ¶3

Emigration: he emigrated as a child. His father died in Norway in 1881, when John was four, so he came out with his widowed mother some time between 1881 and the mid-1890s — the Trondheim police emigrant protocols nos. 5 to 8 (1880–1892) are the place to find him. The crossing itself has not yet been found. ¶6

Gravestone, photographed and read: a rose-granite die on a base, scroll cartouche, 'EIDEM' across the top and below it 'OLINE G. 1881 - 1955' and 'JOHN P. 1876 - 1963'. Years only — the stone carries no month or day for either of them. ¶39

Emigration: John P. Eidem left Norway as a nine-year-old child. The Trondheim police office emigrant protocol no. 7 (1885-1888), page 22, group of travellers 00148, records three people on consecutive lines 38, 39 and 40 of that page, contracted 7 April and expedited 8 April 1886, on the steamer Hero of the Allan Line, all on tickets 'Bill. bet. i Amerika' (bought and paid for in America): Karin Pedersdatter, aged 51, household position 'Kone' (wife); Mikkel P. Eidem, aged 17, 'Soen' (son); and John Eidem, aged 9, 'Soen' (son) - all of Selbo, all for the stated destination 'Chicago Ill'. The clerk's household-position column is what proves the family: these two boys are written down as the sons of this woman. ¶17

Emigration, why this is him: an age of 9 on 8 April 1886 gives a birth window of 9 April 1876 to 8 April 1877, and John P. Eidem was born 17 September 1876. His brother on the line above, 'Mikkel P. Eidem' aged 17, gives a window of 9 April 1868 to 8 April 1869 and Mikal Paulsen Eidem was born 28 January 1869; their mother 'Karin Pedersdatter' aged 51 gives 9 April 1834 to 8 April 1835 and Kari P. Eidem was born 20 October 1834. All three are buried at Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, Antelope Valley Township, Deuel County, South Dakota. The patronymics spelled out in the protocol - Mikkel PAULSEN and Karin PEDERSDATTER - are exactly the 'P.' initials the three American gravestones carry. ¶18

Route of the 1886 crossing: the Hero was not an Atlantic liner but a small Wilson Line North Sea steamer running Trondheim to Hull; the 'Line' column of the protocol, here Allan, names the company that carried the party onward from Liverpool or Glasgow. In 1886 the Allan Line's business was the St Lawrence route to Quebec in the open season and Portland, Maine or Halifax in winter, so the family most likely landed in Canada or at Portland and not at New York. No arrival manifest has been found for them: Ellis Island did not open until 1892, Castle Garden's index is offline, and the Library and Archives Canada passenger lists would not answer to this network. ¶20

Emigration: HE CAME TO AMERICA AS A CHILD OF NINE. Trondheim police office, Emigrant protocols no. 7, header year 1886, group 00148, page 22, line 40, person 0020: 'John Eidem', age 9, household position Soen (son), residence Selbo, destination Chicago Ill, payment 'Bill. bet. i Amerika'. He is on the line below his mother Karin Pedersdatter, 51, and his brother Mikkel, 17. Contract 7 April 1886, expedited 8 April 1886. He was not an adult emigrant in his own right, as the 1904 biography of his brother might lead an American researcher to assume; he was a fatherless boy taken out by his widowed mother on a ticket his eldest brothers had paid for. ¶58

1895 Minnesota state census: the 1886 travelling party is together again in one household in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota, on sheet mnv290_73 page 184 - line 8 Mike Eidem 26 b. Norway, line 9 Betsy Eidem 23 b. Norway, line 10 Paul M. Eidem aged 2 years 1 month b. Minnesota, line 11 Carrie P. Eidem 61 b. Norway, and line 12 JOHN EIDEM, 19, b. Norway. The infant of the house is named Paul for the grandfather who died in Selbu. This is the earliest American record of John P. Eidem found, and the only one that catches him before his marriage. ¶21

1895 state census: Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, page 184, line 12 - 'Eidem John, 19, M, W, born Norway'. He was living in the household of his brother, line 8 'Eidem Mike, 26, M, W, Norway', with Mike's wife 'Eidem Betsy, 23, F, W, Norway', their son 'Eidem Paul M., 2 1/2, M, W, Minnesota', and on line 11 'Eidem Carrie P., 61, F, W, Norway' - his mother Kari, then sixty-one. At nineteen he was a younger brother living in the elder brother's house, which is the standard shape of a Norwegian chain migration. ¶36

The 'P.' of John P. Eidem is the patronymic Paulsen, frozen into an American middle initial: he was the son of Paul Eidem of Selbu. The 1895 census household, in which the sixty-one-year-old Kari P. Eidem lives with him and his brother Mikal, is the Minnesota record of that. ¶38

1895 Minnesota state census: Mehurin township, Lac qui Parle County, page 0184, line 12 - 'Eidem John, 19, M, W, Norway', father and mother both of foreign birth. He was living in the household of his elder brother 'Mike' (Mikal) Eidem, 26, with Mikal's wife Betsy, 23, their son Paul M., 2 1/2, and their widowed mother Carrie P. Eidem, 61. The whole page holds twelve inhabitants and two legal voters; the enumerator was H. W. Bates of Marietta, who took it on 6 June 1895. Mikal is credited with 9 years 1 month in Minnesota and 5 years 2 months in this enumeration district. ¶45

Immigration bracket: the 1895 schedule gives Mikal 9 years 1 month in Minnesota at 6 June 1895, and the 1905 schedule gives John himself 19 years 1 month in Minnesota at 1 June 1905. Both count back to MAY 1886. Two enumerators ten years apart agree to the month: the family reached Minnesota in May 1886, when John was nine. ¶46

Marriage: Lac qui Parle County marriage certificate C01528, 'EIDEM, JOHN' and 'SLIND, OLIVE', 25 September 1901. Their first child was born 7 July 1902, nine and a half months later. This is a county civil marriage record indexed in the Minnesota Official Marriage System; I have read the index entry, not the licence itself. ¶34

Marriage: 25 September 1901, to Oline G. Slind, from her 1955 obituary. Find a Grave's family panel independently records 'm. 1901'. The couple then 'settled on a farm in Mehurin township, Lac qui Parle county'. ¶42

Birth registrations of his children: he is named as the father on seven Minnesota birth registrations in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, between 1902 and 1914, every one of which I read as a record image. He appears as 'John Eidem' (1902, 1904, 1911, 1914) and 'John P. Eidem' (1907, 1909), always a farmer, always born in Norway, and he signed the 1909 certificate of his son Peder Conrad himself, as the informant, there having been no attending physician. The ages he gave - 25 in July 1902, 28 in August 1904, 29 in May 1906, 30 in September 1907, 33 in October 1909, 34 in June 1911 and 37 in May 1914 - are internally consistent and place his birth between September 1876 and August 1877, which agrees with the 17 September 1876 on his Minnesota death record. ¶33

1905 Minnesota state census, taken 8 June 1905, Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, sheet mnsc_131 page 632, lines 4 to 7: John P. Eidem, 28, born Norway, both parents born Norway; Lena Eidem, 25, born Norway, both parents born Norway; Clara Eidem, 3, born Minnesota; Palmer Eidem, 1, born Minnesota. 'Lena' is Oline - the same shortening that appears on Palmer's 1904 Minnesota birth card, where the mother is entered as 'Lena Lind'. ¶22

Immigration: the 1905 Minnesota state census records him as having been a resident of the state for 19 years and 1 month, i.e. since about May 1886, and a resident of the Lac qui Parle enumeration district for 11 years, i.e. since about 1894. His elder brother Mikal, enumerated with him ten years earlier, gives 9 years 1 month in the state in June 1895 - the same arrival, May 1886. The two brothers came over together, aged about 17 and 10, and their widowed mother Kari came with or soon after them. ¶35

1905 Minnesota state census: Mehurin township, Lac qui Parle County, Enumeration District No. 1, schedule sheet 4 (page stamp 243), line 4 - 'Eidem John P., M, 28, W, born Norway, father born Norway, mother born Norway, 19 years 1 month a resident of this State, 11 years a resident of this enumeration district, occupation FARMER'. Enumerated between Tuesday 6 June and Thursday 8 June 1905 by Wm. F. Bates. The household on lines 4-7 is John P. (28), his wife Lena (25), Clara (3) and Palmer (1) - four people; Olga was not yet born. ¶47

1910 federal census: Mehurin township, head of household, b. 1877 Norway, with wife Olive, children Clara P., Palmer G., Olga J., Cora B. and the newborn Peter C., and his widowed MOTHER Carrie Eidem, b. 1835 Norway - eight people (Ancestry image 31111_4330233-00489). ¶48

Township and school office, Mehurin Township, 1916: "The present officers of Mehurin township are: Robert A. Willis, clerk; R. A. Hertzberg, treasurer; C. B. Ufford, assessor; G. L. Shackelford, R. H. Lee and John Eidem, supervisors." The same county history lists the officers of School District No. 108, Mehurin township, as "Peter Wahl, John Eidem, R. E. Telkamp." He was therefore an elected supervisor of his township and a trustee of its school district by 1916, aged thirty-nine. His fellow supervisor R. H. Lee is Roald R. H. Lee, a charter member of the Mehurin Norwegian Lutheran congregation in 1899 and its secretary in 1900 - the township board and the congregation were largely the same men. ¶14a

Township office, 1916: the History of Chippewa and Lac qui Parle Counties (1916) names the officers of Mehurin township as "Robert A. Willis, clerk; R. A. Hertzberg, treasurer; C. B. Ufford, assessor; G. L. Shackelford, R. H. Lee and JOHN EIDEM, supervisors." He was then 40. The same volume lists the board of school district No. 108, Mehurin township, as "Peter Wahl, JOHN EIDEM, R. E. Telkamp." Robert A. Willis and C. B. Ufford are still on the 1940 tax list, which ties the two sources to the same handful of families. His eldest brother Peder was said in 1904 to have "served on town boards often"; these are the offices of settled, propertied farmers rather than of hired men. ¶31

Public office, 1916 - new: he was a SUPERVISOR OF MEHURIN TOWNSHIP, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota. The county history of 1916 prints: 'The present officers of Mehurin township are: Robert A. Willis, clerk; R. A. Hertzberg, treasurer; C. B. Ufford, assessor; G. L. Shackelford, R. H. Lee and John Eidem, supervisors.' He also sat on the board of common school district No. 108, Mehurin township: 'No. 108. Mehurin township - Peter Wahl, John Eidem, R. E. Telkamp.' A township board of three supervisors ran the roads, the poor relief and the local assessment; it is the lowest tier of American rural government, and a Norwegian-born tenant's son sitting on it sixteen years after landing is a measure of how fast this family established itself. Context: his elder brother Peder had 'served on the town board two years' by 1904, so both brothers held the office. ¶63

His baptism is found, 12 Oct 2026 (slind-deep agent), and it proves the parentage that this record has carried as Probable. Church book from Selbu parish 1860-1877, births and baptisms page 26 serial 122, year 1876: child JOHN, baptised 29 OCTOBER 1876; father 'Poul Ingebrigtsen', AGE 50; mother 'Karen Pedersdr', born 1834 (calculated), residence EIDUM. A John baptised at Eidem in Selbu in October 1876 to a father aged 50 and a mother born 1834 is the John Paulsen Eidem whose Minnesota death index gives a birth of 17 September 1876 in Norway and whose mother was Kari P. Eidem, born 20 October 1834. The baptism is six weeks after the stated birth, which is normal in Selbu. ¶65

His four Norwegian-born siblings, from the Selbu registers: Peder baptised 27 Dec 1858, Anne 27 Dec 1860, Ingebrigt 8 Nov 1863 and Michael 29 Mar 1869. Five children in all, which is exactly the number the 1904 Compendium biography of Peter P. Eidem states. ¶67

1920 federal census: Mehurin township, head, b. abt 1877 Norway, with Olena and seven children - Clara, Palmer, Olga, Cora, Peder, May and John. Nine people, the fullest single view of the family; the household runs off the foot of one sheet onto the next (Ancestry images 4312190-00666 and -00667). ¶49

Selbygbogen (Minneapolis, 1921), p. 150: 'John P. Eidem, Marietta, Minn., born 17 Sept. 1876 at Brendhaugen in Selbu, brother of the foregoing. Emigrated in 1886; on 25 Sept. 1901 married Oline Gundersdatter Slind, born 10 Feb. 1881 at Slind (Nordpaagarda) in Selbu, daughter of Gunder O. With Barbara Ann, born 1926 after the book went to press, that makes the eight children named in Oline's 1955 obituary. ¶59

The 1921 book adds: 'Mr. Eidem and his brother Mikal live about four miles south of Marietta, on the west side of the state line, in Deuel Co., S. D.... John E. has been a member of the town and school boards. Farmer. Hauge's Synod.' ¶60

Godparents (faddere), read off the scan of the register page because the AMF transcription of the Selbu books does not carry them: 'Torsten Johnsen & Peder Paulsen Eidem. Paul Pedersen Uglem. Agnis Pedersdtr., Ane Paulsdtr. & Brynhild Pedersdtr. Eyen.' The last column records 'Kirkeg. s. d.' - kirkegang samme dag, the mother's churching on the same day. The given name of the third sponsor is the one word of the six I am not fully certain of; the farm name Uglem is clear. Two of the six are the child's own siblings: PEDER PAULSEN EIDEM, his eldest brother, then seventeen and four years off emigrating, and ANE PAULSDTR., his sister Anne, then fifteen - which is independent proof that Anne was alive and in Selbu in October 1876, nine years before she died. A UGLEM man stands third, and Uglem is the farm his grandmother Mali Evensdatter came off, so the sponsors reach back across a marriage made in the 1820s. The two Pedersdoetre, Agnis and Brynhild of Eyen, are on the naming logic Kari Pedersdatter's own sisters or nieces and are the best surviving lead to her siblings. ¶61

Congregation, 1926 - from the federal religious census: the Marietta pastoral call to which this household belonged is documented in the United States Census of Religious Bodies, Schedule 1926, whose signed schedules survive and are online. The call was five congregations under one pastor, the Rev. Simon E. Hanson of Box 185, Marietta, Minnesota, a graduate of the Hauge Synod's Red Wing Seminary: Augusta and Mehurin in Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota, and three in South Dakota, of which Zoar was one. Augusta had 157 members and owned the $3,500 parsonage; Zoar had 83 members, 44 male and 39 female, with 23 children under 13, a church worth $3,000 free of debt and a summer vacation Bible school of 13 scholars; Mehurin had shrunk to 27 members, owned no parsonage but paid a share of Augusta's, and sent its children to Sunday school at Augusta. Zoar's schedule gives its place as Antelope Valley, Deuel County, South Dakota, in the pastor's own hand, and its synod as the Norwegian Lutheran Church of America, South Minnesota District. Context: the practical meaning is that one man kept the books of all five congregations in one house at Marietta, which is why Zoar's register and Augusta's are today on the same reel of microfilm. ¶15

Congregation: he lived in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota, which lay between two Norwegian Lutheran congregations about four and five miles away. Zoar Evangelical Lutheran congregation (Hauge Synod, organised 1896) stood roughly 3.6 miles north-west across the state line in Antelope Valley Township, Deuel County, South Dakota, and is where he and his wife and most of his kin are buried. The Mehurin Norwegian Lutheran congregation (United Norwegian Lutheran Church, organised 12 November 1899 / 6 January 1900), of which his brother Mikal was a founding deacon, had its church about five miles south in section 4 of Manfred Township. Context: that a Minnesota farmer's church and churchyard lay in South Dakota is not an anomaly - this was one Norwegian settlement straddling the state line, and the burials are the proof of the membership. ¶16

1930 federal census: Mehurin township, head, b. abt 1877 Norway, with Olive G. and five children still at home - Palmer G. (25), Cora B., Peter C., May E., John A. - and the four-year-old Barbara (Ancestry image 4610057_01066). ¶50

1950 census, read from the page image: Minnesota, Lac qui Parle County, Mehurin Township, ED 37-25, sheet 2, dwelling and household 8, line 20 - Eidem, John P., head, W M 73, married, born Norway, worked 35 hours, occupation Farmer; line 21 Oline S., wife, W F 69, married, born Norway; line 22 Peter C., son, W M 40, never married, born Minnesota, 35 hours, farm helper. Land, a clean negative: he never took public land. The complete national return of the Bureau of Land Management General Land Office for the surname EIDEM is 43 records, every one of which has been read, and none is his; nor is there any record for Mikal, Ludwig or Palmer Eidem. He was born in September 1876 and came to America as a young man, by which time the public domain in Lac qui Parle County was gone - the county's run of final-proof notices dies out in the late 1880s. The eighty acres and two government lots in section 20 of Mehurin Township that he held in 1940 were therefore BOUGHT, and the record that documents them is a deed in the Register of Deeds grantee index at the Lac qui Parle County courthouse in Madison, Minnesota, not a federal patent. ¶54
