About the family

Children in America: all eight children who sailed in 1894 are on the 1895 Skree census. In 1900 his wife reported eight children born and seven living. By 1910 Paul and Anna had left home (probably Paul to a farm in Divide County, North Dakota, and Anna, married, to Arizona; index only); Marie (Mary) is not seen after 1895 and is probably the child who had died by 1900 (inferred, see her own record). ¶18

Life story

Baptized 5 May 1853, Kvernes. At home on Tungset nedre in 1865. ¶1

His baptism entry: born 10 Mar, baptized 5 May 1853, son of Paul Ellingsen and Tørri Jacobsdatter, the mother's residence given as 'Tungert' (Tungset). It is no. 40 on page 172 of the Kvernes parish book. No godparents are recorded in the index. ¶4

The 1877 marriage index garbles almost every name in the entry. He is 'Peder Olaus Paulsen', born 1853 'I Kornes' (Kornstad), living in Christiansund; his father is written 'Paul Ellingsen Tamgeth'; the bride is 'Caroline Henrikke Gurine Solmør' and her father 'Peder Johnsen Solnøs'. Read against the family's other records these are Tungseth, Sætnes and Sætnes. The entry is no. 74 and the date 21 Sep. ¶5

Merchant (handelsmand) at Toldbodbakken, Kristiansund in 1885. ¶2

In the 1885 census he is at Toldbodbakken in Kristiansund, entered as 'Handelsmand' - a shopkeeper or trader. In the 1891 census the family is at 47 Toldbodsmujet, property no. 45, in census district 006 Hammersmaget, and he is head of household no. 1. His birthplace is given there as 'Kvernæs Herred Kornstd', which independently confirms that he was born in the Kornstad part of Kvernes. ¶6

The building the family lived in was a crowded Kristiansund tenement: the 1891 census lists forty people at that one address in seven households, the other heads being Carl Severin Jensen, Gurine Wangberg, Emilie Marie Eriksen, O. Harholt junior, Albert Marten Maur and Gusine Vahlum. The Tungsæt household of nine kept one servant, Bridt T. Baarseth, born 1865 in Halse. ¶7

Emigrated 11 Oct 1893 (destination Manitoba); family followed in 1894 and settled near Hawley, MN. ¶3

He left first and alone. The Nordmøre police emigrant protocol records 'Peder Tungseth', aged 40, 'Kjøbmand' (merchant), of Kristiansund, expedited 11 Oct 1893 with the Dominion Line, destination Manitoba, ticket bought in Kristiansund. No ship is named. Listed with him as person 2 on the same entry is an Anna Tungseth, born about 1874 by the clerk's calculation, unmarried, same residence and destination. She cannot be his daughter Anna, who was eleven, and she is not accounted for; she may be a sister, a niece, or simply an age misread for the daughter. This is flagged as unexplained. ¶8

A correction to add to the published record: Find a Grave's memorial for him lists only four children - Anna, Carl, Gustav and Anton - and omits Paul, Marie Lovise, Olga and Erling. The Norwegian 1891 census and the 1894 emigrant list show eight children, and all eight sailed for Minnesota. ¶9

1895 Minnesota census: Peter Tungseth, 42, white, born in Norway, farmer, heads a household in Skree Township, Clay County, with his wife Caroline, 44, and eight children - Paul 14, Anna 13, Carl 11, Gustav 10, Anton 8, Mary 6, Olga 4 and Erling 1, all born in Norway. Read from the census image, p. 6. His age agrees with his birth in March 1853. ¶10

1900 census: on 14 Jun 1900 Peder is not in his family's household in Skree Township. His wife Caroline is entered as head and farmer on an owned farm, free of mortgage, with six of the children (ED 316, sheet 185A, read from the image). He has not been found anywhere in the 1900 United States census index under Tungseth, Tungset, Tungsett, Tungsaet, Tungsat, Tungsath, Tungsette, Tungseat, Tongseth, Tungsted, Tunseth or Tunset, nor as Peder or Peter Tungseth, nor in the 1901 Canadian census index (Ancestry indexes 7602 and 8826, searched 13 Oct 2026). The Tunseth family of Grand Forks County, North Dakota (Paul, born 1854, wife Olive) is a different family. Where he was in June 1900 is unknown; he may be indexed under a garbled name or as Peder Paulsen. ¶11

Years married: the 1900 census gives Caroline as married 22 years and the 1910 census gives Peder and Karolina as married 27 years; the 1910 figure is clearly written '27' on the image. The Kristiansund marriage of 21 Sep 1877 fits the 1900 figure exactly (22 years and nine months on 14 Jun 1900) and would make 32 years in April 1910, so the 1910 entry is an enumerator's or informant's error and the 1877 date stands. ¶15

Death certificate: FamilySearch's index of his Minnesota death certificate ('Minnesota, Death Records and Certificates, 1900-1955', page 100, certificate 2539) gives 'Peder Olans' [Olaus] Tungseth, male, aged 61, born 10 Mar 1853 in Norway, married, a farmer, died 2 Jan 1915, and names his father as Paul E. Tungseth and his mother as Torri Jakobsdatter, both born in Norway. The MNHS death index used earlier leaves the parents blank; this abstract of the certificate supplies them, and they agree with his 1853 Kvernes baptism (son of Paul Ellingsen and Tørri Jacobsdatter) - 'Paul E.' is Paul Ellingsen with the farm name used as a surname. His parent links were already Confirmed from the baptism, so confidence is unchanged. The certificate image was not viewed; the informant, cause of death and place within Clay County are still unread. ¶19

1905 Minnesota census: Peder Tungseth, 53, farmer, born in Norway of Norwegian parents, with 11 years in Minnesota and in the United States, heads the household in Skree Township, Clay County, with Caroline 55, Carl 21, Gustar (Gustav) 19, Anton 18, Olga 16 and Earling (Erling) 11 (Enumeration District 10, schedule sheet III, lines 30-36, read from the image). Eleven years' residence points to arrival in 1894. ¶12

1905 Minnesota census ages: Peder's 53 and Caroline's 55 are each one year more than their birth dates give in June 1905 (52 and 54), and Olga's 16 is one more than her birth on 23 Jan 1890 gives (15). Carl, Anton and Erling are entered at their correct ages and Gustav one year young, so this is not a consistent shift; it is the usual inaccuracy of census ages. The Norwegian baptism and census dates are the better evidence, and the birth dates in the tree stand. ¶13

1910 census: Peter P O Tungseth, head, 57, married once, for 27 years, born in Norway of Norwegian parents, immigrated in 1894 and naturalized (column read 'Na'), speaks English, a farmer on a general farm (class of worker read as 'employee'), owns his farm with a mortgage. With him in Skree Township are his wife Karolina P., 59, and the children Carl P. 26, Gustav A. 25, Anton J. 23, Olga K. 20 and Erling L. 16 (ED 59, sheet 187A, family 7, lines 34-40, read from the image). 'P O' is Peder Olaus, with the Norwegian patronymic P[aulsen]. ¶14

Immigration year: the 1910 census gives 1894, the year his wife and children came, but the Nordmøre emigrant protocol shows him leaving Kristiansund on 11 Oct 1893 for Manitoba. If both are right he spent the winter of 1893-94 in Canada and entered the United States in 1894; this is an inference, not proved. His naturalization record has not been looked for; the Clay County district court naturalization files are the source. ¶16

1910 census, second reading: the signed-in lane re-read the same 1910 entry from the FamilySearch image (DGS 004972527, image 380) and read the class-of-worker column for Peter O. Tungseth as 'employer'. The 1910 codes are W (wage earner), Emp (employer) and OA (own account), so the earlier rendering 'employee' is probably a mis-expansion of 'Emp': he was a farmer who employed others. The image should be checked once more before this is relied on. ¶21

Death: the Minnesota death index records Peder Olaus Tungseth, died 2 Jan 1915 in Clay County, certificate 1915-MN-002539; the parents' fields are blank. This is index-only: no certificate image is online, so the place within the county, his recorded age and his cause of death are not known. ¶17
