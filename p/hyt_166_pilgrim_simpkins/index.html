About the family

Parents entered: his mother Isabel is named in the arbitration deeds of 21-24 Jan. 1669/70 (Suffolk Deeds IX:224-226): 'it pleased god to take out of this life m[rs]. Izable Simkins of Boston... sometime in September last past who left Issue viz[t] Pillgram Simkins her sonne Debora the now wife of George Burrell and the sd Rebecca Terill her two daughters'; the sisters released to 'the sd Pillgram Simkins my brother' the dwelling house held under 'the Originall deed from Lenard Buttles to Nicolas Simkins deceased' of 22 Oct. 1652. His father is Capt. Nicholas Simpkins (Probable, see Nicholas Simpkins). Age: TAG 28:87 gives his age at death as about 96 (born about 1624), but his mother deposed in the 1650s that she was about 44, and his first child was born in 1660, so he was probably born in the 1630s; no birth date is entered. ¶4

Life story

Capt. Pilgrim Simpkins of Boston. The Boston register records his children by (1) Miriam, who died 10 Nov. 1660 (Nicholas, born 22 Oct. 1660), and by (2) Catharine: William (3 Oct. 1662), Rebecca (14 Mar. 1665), Sarah (21 Sept. 1668), Thomas (30 May 1671) and John (22 Mar. 1677) (Record Commissioners vol. 9, OCR). TAG 27 (1951): 202 calls Christopher Kilby's wife Sarah 'daughter of Capt. Pilgrim and Katharine (Richardson) Simpkins'. Graded Probable on his link to Sarah Simpkins: the 1668 birth names him as her father, but her identity with the bride of 1691 is inferred (see her notes). ¶1

Died at Boston 3 Dec. 1720, aged about 96, buried in the Granary; married (1) Miriam, who died 10 Nov. 1660 ('Miriam wife of Pilgrim Simpkins deceased the 10th of November', vol. 9); (2) 27 Nov. 1661, 'Pilgrim Sympkins & Katherine Richardson were married 27th November' by Gov. Bellingham (vol. 9). Mariner; with Katharine his wife conveyed land to Thomas Tyler, mariner, 13 July 1688 (Suffolk Deeds 21:425) (TAG 28:87-88). ¶3

Verified from the original (sign-in S0231), resolving the flag in the earlier upgrade note: the record-book copy of his will (Suffolk Probate Records vol. 22 pp. 60-61, docket 4383, DGS 007703078 image 41) reads '... it shall be equally Divided amongst my Children, namely, John Sinkins, Mariam Tyler, Rebecca Kilby and Sarah Kilby, and whereas my son Thomas Sinkins is dead, his Children shall have their Fathers equal part'; wife 'Kathrine Sinkins' sole executrix; son Nicholas 5s. 'if he comes for it'; made 19 Nov. 1714, his mark; witnesses Joshua Windsor?, Joseph Wise, John Cole; probate granted to the widow 2 Jan. 1720 [/1] (Samuel Sewall, judge; John Boydell, reg.). The file papers (Box 25, case 4383, image group 103021798 image 965) hold the widow's own petition: 'my late Husband, Pilgrim Simpkins, late of Boston dec[ease]d... I... do hereby appoint my beloved Son in law Christopher Kilby' to present the will, signed 2 Jan. 1720/1 with her mark. Both agree with the TAG 28:88 abstract; grade (Confirmed) unchanged, now on the original. ¶5

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Upgrade: graded on his link to Sarah Simpkins. His will, made 19 Nov. 1714 and proved 2 Jan. 1720/1 (Suffolk Probate docket 4383), as abstracted with quotations in TAG 28:88, leaves all to 'loving wife Katharin Simkins' for life, 5s. to son Nicholas, and the rest 'to be divided between the children: John Simpkins, "Mariam Tyler," "Rebecca Kilbe," and "Sarah Kilbe"', with the children of his deceased son Thomas to take their father's share. On 2 Jan. 1720/1 the widow, aged and infirm, declined the executorship and asked that 'her beloved son-in-law, Christopher Kilby' be appointed; Christopher Kilby, victualler, was appointed administrator de bonis non with the will annexed on 13 July 1722, and sold the real estate in 1724 (Suffolk Deeds 37:254-5). The will therefore names Sarah Kilbe as his daughter, and the estate papers name her husband Christopher Kilby as son-in-law. Continuity: Sarah of Pilgrim & Catharine born 21 Sept. 1668 (Boston register), baptised Old South 1676, married Christopher Kilby 20 March 1691; the other married daughter 'Rebecca Kilbe' was the wife of his brother Deacon John Kilby (m. about 1690). No rival Sarah Kilby: John Kilby Sr.'s daughter Sarah had been Mrs. Richard Draper since 1689. ¶2a
