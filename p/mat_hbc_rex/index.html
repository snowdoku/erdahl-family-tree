Life story

Gravestone, Hatzic Cemetery (photographed on Find a Grave and read): 'In Loving Memory / REX HAYTER / Born Oct. 9, 1905 / Died April 1, 1929 / Gone but not forgotten', with HAYTER on the base. ¶2

PEI records: in his father Benjamin Hayter's household at Gaspereaux in 1911 (born October 1905) and 1921 (aged 15). He was the first of the family to go west: the US border-crossing index has Rex Hayter, born about 1905 at Gaspereaux, PEI, entering at Seattle on 27 November 1925 and again on 6 September 1927. ¶5

Death: the BC death index has Rex Hayter dying at Harrison Mills on 1 April 1929, aged 23 (registration 1929-09-424450). The index offers no image of this registration. A Find a Grave contributor's transcription of it gives: single; a faller in a logging camp; died at Chehalis Logging Camp No. 2, Harrison Mills, in a logging accident; parents Benjamin Hayter, born Highbanks, PEI, and Lydia A. Hecken (Hicken), born Gaspereaux, PEI. Those parents and birthplaces match his siblings' records exactly. The parentage rests on that transcription until the registration is read; the gravestone, the index and the transcription all agree. ¶3

Context: he was killed in the Fraser Valley logging camps in 1929, the year his brother Raymond's own records place Raymond's arrival in British Columbia. The two eldest sons evidently came west to work in the woods before the rest of the family followed. ¶4
