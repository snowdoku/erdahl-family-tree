About the family

PEI evidence: no George or Phebe Hicken of the immigrant generation appears in any PEI index, whether the baptism index (1788-1943), the baptisms/marriages/burials index (1780-1982), the marriage registers (1832-88) or the death cards (1810-1913). Every one of the five English-born Hicken siblings who settled around Three Rivers and Gaspereaux by 1831 gave the names George and Phebe to early children: Eliza Wise's son George Hicken Wise (1832); William's Phebe (1835) and George Samuel (1839); Thomas's George Benjamin (1834); George's Phebe (c.1839); and Mary Ann Moore's George (1840) and Phebe (c.1841). This naming pattern supports the compiler's statement that their parents were George and Phebe. The surviving 1841 census return for Lot 61 (PARO) is the record that could show the elder George as a head of household in P.E.I. ¶6

Family testimony: Lydia Adelaide (Hicken) Hayter, his great-granddaughter, wrote on her 1966 British Columbia Pioneer Medallion application that her grandfather "came from Worcestershire, England in 1812 to settle at St. Mary's Bay, now known as Gaspereaux" and built the Methodist church at Sturgeon. Her grandfather was George Hicken (c.1812-1899), so the "1812" is probably his birth year rather than a voyage (his sister Mary Ann was baptised in Worcestershire in 1814). The memory of a Worcestershire origin, recorded independently of any index, agrees with the Doddenham baptism and supports the identification of this George and Phebe as the Island family's parents. ¶14

Family in 1841: three other Hicken heads in Lot 61 match his known sons. George Hicken Junr, next door, had a household of five that fits George, Frances and three daughters. William Hicken, carpenter, had a household of seven and a lease from about 1830 (image 513). Thomas Hicken, carpenter, had a household of six and a lease from about 1821 (images 515-516). The census does not state relationships. 'Senr' and 'Junr' normally marked father and son, but were also used for unrelated men of one name in one place. ¶18

Life story

The immigrant ancestor of the Prince Edward Island Hickens, on the account of the compiled Island Register genealogy 'The Descendants of George and Phoebe Hicken' (submitted by Vimy Gregory): born in England before 1781, married Phebe Allbut about 1795 at St Margaret Pattens (a parish in the City of London), and died on the Island before 1861. Children, all born in England: Elizabeth 'Eliza' (29 Jan 1802; m. John Wise of Plymouth), William (24 Aug 1806), Thomas (Mar 1809; m. Hannah Reynolds 1831 at Gaspereaux, d. 1858 Newburyport, Mass.), George (c.1812) and Mary Ann (c.1814; m. William Sabine Moore 1839). ¶1

Royal Artillery: a George and Phebe Hicken appear in Kent parish registers transcribed in FreeREG: their son James was baptised at All Saints, Canterbury, on 7 September 1800 - father's occupation 'Private in the Royal Artillery' - and buried there on 28 September 1800 aged five weeks; a child Jane, of George and Phebe 'Hickin', was baptised at St Leonard, Deal, on 27 December 1801; and Eliza, daughter of George and Phebe Hicken, was baptised at St Leonard, Deal, on 19 August 1804. These are the only baptisms to a George and Phebe Hicken under any spelling in FreeREG for 1796-1818. The surname is otherwise unknown in Kent at that date, and an Eliza fits the eldest daughter of the PEI family, so this is very probably the immigrant couple - which would make George a regular soldier, a gunner in the Royal Artillery, moving between the Kent garrisons, and would explain a wedding in the City of London. The identification is Probable, not proved: no record yet links this soldier to the Hickens of Gaspereaux. ¶4

Family in England, assembled from parish baptisms (England Births and Christenings index, FamilySearch extractions of the registers, and FreeREG): George and Phebe Hicken had Mary baptised at St Mildred, Canterbury, 17 April 1796; George at St Leonard, Deal, 24 December 1797; James at All Saints, Canterbury, 7 September 1800 (George then "Private in the Royal Artillery"; James buried there aged five weeks on 28 September); Jane at Deal, 27 December 1801; Eliza at Deal, 19 August 1804; William at Little Cowarne, Herefordshire, 24 August 1806; and Mary Ann at Doddenham, Worcestershire, 28 August 1814. These are the only baptisms to a George and Phebe Hicken under any spelling in either index. ¶7

Army service: George was a private in the Royal Artillery at Canterbury in September 1800. Canterbury had an artillery barracks, and the coastal batteries around Deal were manned through the invasion scares of 1797-1805, which fits the family's moves between the two towns from 1796 to 1804. By August 1806 the family was in rural Herefordshire, which suggests George left the army about 1802-1805 - many artillerymen were discharged in the reductions after the Peace of Amiens (1802). Inference only; his discharge has not been found. He is not in the item-indexed Royal Artillery records of service (WO 69, including the Royal Artillery pensions register 1815-18) or in any Chelsea or Kilmainham discharge series (WO 97, WO 121, WO 119) under any spelling, so he was probably not discharged to a pension. The Royal Artillery muster books (WO 10) for 1796-1805 at The National Archives would show him quarter by quarter. ¶9

Herefordshire and Worcestershire: Little Cowarne (Herefordshire, near Bromyard) and Doddenham (Worcestershire, on the Teme) lie about fifteen miles apart. The only other Hicken family in that district at the time was John Hicken, who married Elizabeth at Clifton upon Teme on 25 November 1811 and had daughters baptised at Martley (1814) and Knightwick (1816), parishes adjoining Doddenham - probably a kinsman of George, though nothing yet proves it. Hicken was otherwise a Staffordshire, Warwickshire and Black Country name. ¶10

Emigration: the family was still in Worcestershire in August 1814. In the 1841 census of Prince Edward Island, according to the Island Register Reynolds genealogy, his son Thomas held land in Lot 61 in the twentieth year of a 999-year lease, which puts the start of the lease in about 1821-22, when Thomas was only twelve - so the lease was almost certainly taken by the father. The Hickens were therefore on Lot 61 by about 1821-22, having emigrated between late 1814 and about 1822. The Lot 61 return of the 1841 census itself has not been read. ¶11

Land petition, 1822 (abstract, secondary): the compiler's notes in the same Hicken file (image 96) abstract a land petition signed by George Hicken on 28 October 1822: a native of Great Britain who arrived in 1820, had tried with little success to support 'a large and fairly young' family as a settler, and had served 7 years 9 months in the Royal Artillery and 4 years in the [?] Volunteers; he asked for a town and pasture lot in Georgetown and Royalty (ordered 28 Oct 1822). This, if the abstract is accurate, ties the Lot 61 settler to the Royal Artillery private George Hicken, husband of Phebe, of the Canterbury and Deal baptisms (1796-1804). ¶25a

1841 census: the 1841 census of Prince Edward Island (Kings County, District 15, Lot 61, image 512) lists 'George Hicken Senr', a farmer (by ditto mark), as head of a household of two: one male upwards of 60 and one female upwards of 60. The next household is 'George Hicken Junr'. On the religion and land page (image 513) both members are entered as Church of England. He held 65 acres on a 999-year lease of which 25 years had run, at 1s 2d an acre, a rent of 3 pounds 15s 10d currency. This is the first record read on the Island of the immigrant George Hicken; he was alive in 1841. ¶15

Arrival (inference from the 1841 census): a lease with 25 years expired in 1841 began about 1816. That fits the family still being at Doddenham, Worcestershire, in August 1814 (Mary Ann's baptism). If he took the lease new, the family emigrated between late 1814 and about 1816, earlier than the about 1821-22 previously inferred from Thomas's lease. If he bought an existing lease, he could have come later. ¶16

Age: he was upwards of 60 in 1841 and so was born before about 1781. This is consistent with the Royal Artillery private of 1800, whose first known child was baptised in 1796. ¶17

1841 census, Lot 61 (household detail): the census lists heads only. The first 'G. Hicken' on Lot 61, taken to be George Senior, had 2 persons in the household, both born in England, both having paid their own passages, with 20 acres arable and 40 bushels wheat, 15 barley, 100 oats, 100 potatoes, 3 horses, 12 cattle, 12 sheep and 21 hogs. The second G. Hicken (George Jr) had 5 persons (1 born in England, 4 on the Island); William Hicken 7 (1 England, 1 Scotland, 5 Island); Thomas Hicken 6 (1 England, 4 Island, 1 British Colonies) (FamilySearch DGS 008131115 images 514, 517; images read). This places the immigrant couple, both English-born, alive on Lot 61 in 1841; 'Senr' is not written in the census, so the identification of the first George as the father is an inference from the farm and household size. ¶20

Will: George Hicken of Township Number Sixty One, King's County, PEI, made his will on 8 August 1847 (proved March 1849 per the file notes). It says the farm he held under lease on Lot 61, about 115 acres, was divided: 50 acres on the east side 'already sublet by me to my son Thomas under Lease bearing date the first day of March 1834', and the remaining 65 acres with the houses 'I hereby devise and bequeath to my son George', subject to his debts and to a third of the produce, milk, butter and wool for 'my wife Phoebe' if she survived him. To 'my son William Hicken or his heirs' a feather bed, to pass 'after the death of his mother my Wife Phoebe', and his copy of the 'Antiquities of Josephus'. He asked to be buried at St Andrew's with a plain headstone. Executor Johnson Aitken of Lot 59; witnesses George Hicken and William Moors. Source: typescript of the will in the PARO genealogy files ('Prince Edward Island. Genealogies 1799-2001', FamilySearch, image 97), read via Full-Text search. ¶23

Land Purchase deed, 1878: the Commissioner of Public Lands (Joseph Pope) conveyed 115 acres on the shore of St Mary's Bay, Lot 61, to George Hicken of Lot 61 on 1 March 1878. The title note at the foot of the registered copy reads 'Lease George Hicken 61/195... 12/8/20' and 'Ext[ract] of Will devising to Geo Hicken Junr - to be produced' (PEI Land Records 1885, FamilySearch image 265; image read). So the 115-acre leasehold of the will passed under a will to George Hicken junior, and the lease to George Hicken is dated 12 August 1820 (reading of '12/8/20'). This agrees with the land petition abstract (arrived 1820) rather than the c.1816 start inferred from the 1841 census. ¶24

Original will found: the signed original of the will of 8 August 1847 is in the PEI Probate Estate Case Files as 'H - Will no. 5, Estate of George Hicken late of Lot 61' (FamilySearch collection 'Probate Estate Case Files 1855-1905', images 1012-1014). Its text agrees with the PARO typescript: the 115 acres on Lot 61, the 50 acres sublet to 'my son Thomas' by lease of 1 March 1834, the rest of the farm to 'my son George', and a third of the produce to 'my wife Phoebe'. The original now replaces the typescript as the source for his Confirmed link to George Jr. ¶30

Death: unknown. The compiler says he died on Prince Edward Island before 1861; no Hicken of his generation appears in any PEI index. A death card for a "Hicon", born 1751, died 9 May 1841 at Little York, belongs almost certainly to the Hiccox family of Lot 34 (John Hiccox, farmer, is in the 1841 census there), not to him. ¶13

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 3

Why this is the Prince Edward Island couple (confidence raised from Speculative to Probable): two of these children match English-born Island settlers exactly - William (baptised 24 August 1806, the very date the Island genealogy gives for William Hicken, who was 75 in the 1881 census of Township 61) and Mary Ann (baptised August 1814; Mrs Mary Ann Moore of Albion died in March 1889 aged 75). The Doddenham baptism was not known to the Island compiler, so it is independent corroboration. Eliza (Deal 1804) matches the compiler's eldest daughter Eliza, wife of John Wise. And all five English-born Island siblings named early children George and Phebe. No single record yet states that the soldier of 1800 and the Island settler are one man, so this stays Probable. ¶8

Confidence (unchanged, Probable): the 1841 census shows an elderly George Hicken on Lot 61 with a woman also over 60. Heads named George junior, William and Thomas live in the same lot. This fits the Royal Artillery family: William was baptised in 1806, and the gap between him and Mary Ann (1814) holds Thomas (about 1809) and George (about 1812). It also fits the August 1814 Worcestershire residence and the lease of about 1816. The page read gives no birthplaces (the Lot 61 birthplace page, image 514, has not been read) and no relationships, so no record yet equates the soldier with the settler. It would be settled by Thomas Hicken's 1858 Massachusetts death record naming his parents, or by an English birthplace for the old couple on image 514 together with a Doddenham 1814 abode. ¶19

Upgrade: Probable to Confirmed, graded on his link to George Hicken (see his page, b. c.1812). The 1847 will names 'my son George' and devises him the Lot 61 farm. Continuity from the will's George to the tree's George: George Hicken Junr headed the household next door to George Hicken Senr on Lot 61 in 1841 (1841 census img 512); George Hicken Senr witnessed George junior's marriage to Frances Thompson on 31 Jan 1837; the 1878 Land Purchase deed of the same 115 acres to George Hicken of Lot 61 cites the will 'devising to Geo Hicken Junr'; and the 1891 census lists George, 79, widower, born England, in the same lot. No rival George Hicken of the son's generation lived on Lot 61 (George L., the next George, was born c.1852). ¶26

The case for this identification 1

Evidence so far: the 1881 census of Township 61 lists William Hicken, 75, and Mary Moore, 67, both born in England, living near George Hicken, 69 - consistent with the compiler's sibling group and with an emigration between about 1814 and 1830 (Thomas married at Gaspereaux in 1831). Nothing yet read names the parents of any of them; the Kent baptisms below make the couple real, but the link to the Island family is still the compiler's, so George and Phebe are rated Speculative as ancestors. ¶2

Corrections 2

Correction: the compiled Island Register genealogy says George married "Phebe Allbut" about 1795 at St Margaret Pattens in the City of London. The London Church of England marriages index shows a George Hicken (also indexed as Hickan) marrying JANE Allbut at St Margaret Pattens - banns or marriage on 7 December 1794 and an entry of 1795, on two separate register images. The bride there is Jane, not Phebe. No marriage of a George Hicken to any Phebe is indexed in England, and no children of a George and Jane Hicken are indexed anywhere. Either this is our George and the register's "Jane" is the woman later called Phebe, or it is another man; only the register image (London Metropolitan Archives, St Margaret Pattens registers, also on Ancestry) can settle it. The 1795 marriage at St Margaret Pattens recorded for George and Phebe in this tree should be treated as unproved. ¶12

Correction: the arrival year. The will file's petition abstract (arrived 1820) and the 1878 deed's lease date (12/8/20) point to 1820, not c.1816 as inferred from the 1841 census ('25 years' of a 999-year lease). The 1841 lease term is probably approximate. Family testimony (1966) said 1812, which is too early: the family was at Doddenham in August 1814. ¶27

Searches that found nothing 7

Negative: FreeREG (England) has no marriage of a George Hicken to a Phebe Allbut/Albutt/Allbutt anywhere 1785-1805, and no run of baptisms for these children; St Margaret Pattens is not transcribed there. Both surnames cluster in the West Midlands (Hicken in Warwickshire and Staffordshire; Allbut(t) in Staffordshire, Worcestershire and the Black Country), which makes a City of London wedding notable but not implausible. ¶3

Not found: George Hicken (or Hickin) does not appear in the item-level catalogue of Royal Artillery records of service (TNA WO 69) or of Chelsea pensioners' discharge papers (WO 97), both searched on TNA Discovery. Those catalogues are partial for WO 69 and cover only men discharged to pension for WO 97, so this is not a negative on his service. ¶5

English baptisms of the missing sons (negative, 2 Nov 2026): no baptism of Thomas Hicken (born about March 1809) or of George Hicken (born about 1812, the George Hicken Junr of Lot 61 in 1841) to George and Phebe has been found in any free index. FreeREG was searched for Thomas and for George Hicken baptisms 1807-1813 in all counties with fuzzy surname matching (98 and 52 hits; none Hicken to a George), and for any Hicken-like baptism 1805-1816 in Herefordshire, Worcestershire and Shropshire (13 hits, none relevant; FreeREG does not cover Little Cowarne, Doddenham, Knightwick or Martley). The England-wide christening index (Ancestry 9841, a free search view of the FamilySearch extractions) returns for father George and mother Phebe Hicken only the seven known baptisms, Canterbury 1796 to Doddenham 1814. The only other George Hicken baptism of the period, Walsall St Matthew, 4 Jul 1810, is a son of Thomas and Esther (a twin of Kate), so not this family. The gap between William (Aug 1806) and Mary Ann (Aug 1814) therefore stays unfilled. The record still needed is the Doddenham, Knightwick, Martley or Little Cowarne register (or bishop's transcripts) for 1807-1813, which is in the signed-in FamilySearch lane. ¶21

Marriage (negative): FreeREG has no marriage of a George Hicken (fuzzy) to a Phebe or any other bride in Kent, or anywhere else, in 1793-1800, apart from George Hicken x Hannah Hamphlet at Nottingham St Mary, 10 Aug 1798, a different man whose son Thomas was baptised there to George and Hannah in 1803. No Hicken burial is indexed in Kent 1794-1800 except the infant James (Canterbury All Saints, 28 Sep 1800). No baptism of a Phebe Allbut c.1762-1782 is in the England-wide christening index. The question of whether the 'Jane' Allbut of St Margaret Pattens (1794/95) is the same woman as Phebe remains open. ¶22

Knightwick and Doddenham registers 1807-1812 read (negative, 2 Nov 2026): the printed transcript 'The Parish Registers of Knightwick and Doddenham, County of Worcester, from A.D. 1538 to A.D. 1812', ed. J. B. Wilson (London, 1891; FamilySearch Books 516335, catalog koha:250788) was read page by page for January 1807 to November 1812 (printed pages 80-84, viewer images 84-88). From about 1780 the Knightwick register (Vol. II, 1695-1812) also records the baptisms at Doddenham Chapel, so these pages cover both parishes. No Hicken (or Hickin, Hicking) baptism or burial appears; the book's index of persons (printed p. 189) has no Hicken entry at all, only Hichman and Hickman. So neither Thomas (about March 1809) nor George junior (about 1812) was baptised at Knightwick or Doddenham before December 1812, and the family was probably not yet living there (John Hicken's daughters were baptised at Martley in 1814 and Knightwick in 1816; George's Mary Ann at Doddenham in August 1814). ¶28

Little Cowarne and Martley (negative): FamilySearch's indexed Herefordshire Bishop's Transcripts give, for any Hicken at Little Cowarne, only William's baptism of 24 Aug 1806 (two index entries, parents George and Phebe). The Little Cowarne parish register film for baptisms 1666-1812 (DGS 7908015, film 1040016 items 4-7) is restricted (locked images) and could not be read. FamilySearch's England-wide index of Hicken births and christenings 1805-1816 has, in this district, only Jane (Martley, 8 Apr 1814) and Elizabeth (Knightwick with Doddenham, 5 Apr 1816), both daughters of John and Elizabeth Hicken. The gap between William (1806) and Mary Ann (1814) therefore stays unfilled; Thomas (1809) and George junior (about 1812) were probably born while the family lived somewhere not yet identified, perhaps elsewhere in Herefordshire or still with the army. The remaining record is the Little Cowarne register 1807-1812 (restricted film 1040016; Herefordshire Archive and Records Centre). ¶29

Land petition of 28 October 1822 (negative on FamilySearch): the FamilySearch catalogue has no PEI land petitions or Executive Council petitions, and full-text searches for Hicken with Artillery or petition find only the PARO genealogy-file abstract already used. The original petition is at PARO only (Executive Council / Land Petitions series). ¶31
