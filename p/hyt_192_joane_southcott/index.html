Life story

Joane, daughter of John Southcott of Bovey Tracey, Devon, gent., second wife of John Eveleigh of Holcombe and Clyst St Lawrence, and his administratrix in 1586 (Heverly family history, citing Vivian and Worthy). Added. ¶1

Two wills read. Grade unchanged (Probable, on her link to John Eveleigh). (1) Her son Nicholas Evelegh's will (PROB 11/131/535) calls the Principal 'my brother', and Nicholas held the park and grist mills of Bovey Tracey, her family's parish. That fits Vivian's chart, which puts both men among her sons, but the will names neither parent. (2) Her brother Thomas Southcote's will (PROB 11/96/208, dated 1593, proved 1600) does not name her or any Eveleigh. Its overseers are 'my Nephewe George Carie Esquier' (son of his sister Mary and Thomas Cary of Cockington), 'my brother in lawe Edward Drewe Sargeant at lawe' and 'my sonne in lawe Thomas Rudgwaye'. So the will names one sister's son but is silent on the rest of his sisters, and this neither supports nor refutes the link. ¶5

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 3

Speculative, graded on her link to John Eveleigh: only the Heverly family history (from Vivian) says the Principal of Hart Hall was her son, and Foster's identification of his father is itself 'probably'. ¶2a

Grade kept at Speculative, graded on her link to John Eveleigh. Her husband's link to the Principal is now Probable, but which of John Eveleigh's two wives bore the Principal is stated only in the Heverly history's version of Vivian, with 'order of birth uncertain'. The argument for Joane runs as follows. The Middle Temple register calls George the '2s.' of John of Holcombe, after Robert, the first wife's son and heir. Heverly puts George, born about 1556, in the second family. If that is right, Joane had married John by about 1556, and the Principal, born about 1559, would be hers. That rests entirely on Heverly's reading. ¶3a

Upgrade (Speculative to Probable), graded on her link to her son John Eveleigh. Vivian's p. 336 has now been read from the page image (a facsimile in the Heverly history, leaf n59). The descent line from the marriage of John Eveleigh of Holcombe with 'Joane da. of John Southcott of Bovytracye in Devon, 2 wife' runs down the right-hand side of the chart to every son: George (1 son and heir), 'John Eveleigh, 2 son' who married 'Prudence, da. of Dr. Barnes, mar. in Oxford', Josias (3), Richard (4), Nicholas (5), Thomas (6), Sir James (7) and Charles (8). The first wife Alice Collyns's line carries only Robert, Margaret and Elizabeth. Independently in date, Westcote's View of Devonshire (written about 1630, while several of her children were alive; pp. 563-564) says John Evelegh married 'secondly Joan, daughter of John Southcot, of Bovey-Tracey, esq., and had issue George..., Richard, John, Nicholas, Thomas, Sir James, Josias, Peter...' and 'John Evelegh married, in Oxford, Prudence, daughter of Doctor Barnes, and had issue'. Vivian's footnote on Prudence cites Westcote, so the two are not wholly independent, but Westcote is near-contemporary. Worthy's abstract of the 1586 administration (read: 'Granted to Joan his relict') shows her as John's widow. This is still a compiled pedigree, so Probable, not Confirmed. The earlier notes' 'which wife bore the Principal rests on Heverly alone' is superseded. ¶4a
