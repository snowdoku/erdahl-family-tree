Life story

Two sets of Oxford leases follow her as her father's successor in his tenancies. (1) Magdalen College (St John's Hospital) house on Canditch: Robert Barnes 1588, her husband the Principal 1604, 'Prudence Eveleighe, widow' 1615 (renewed 1632, 1638), then 'John Eveleigh, of St Giles, Oxford, clerk' 1654, two years after her burial (Salter, Cartulary of St John's Hospital, vol. 2, pp. 314-315; rentals vol. 3, pp. 319, 324). (2) Christ Church (ex-Oseney) lease of 22 Dec 1612 in St Giles, bounded by 'the farme place once of Ruley late in the tenure of Robert Barnes, doctor of physick, now of Prudence Eveleigh' (Salter, Cartulary of Oseney Abbey, vol. 2, p. 199). This confirms that she lived in St Giles, where she was buried in 1652 and where the Dean later held land, and that she succeeded to her father's holdings. Her will has been located in the catalogue: TNA PROB 11/224/477, 'Will of Prudence Eveleigh, Widow of Saint Giles Oxford', proved 23 Aug 1652 (not read; download needs a TNA account). Grade unchanged (Probable, on her link to John Eveleigh). ¶3a

Prudence Barnes, youngest daughter of Robert Barnes, M.D., of Oxford, and wife of John Eveleigh, M.A., Principal of Hart Hall (her father's will, 1 Jan 1603/4, abstracted by Anthony Wood). Widowed in 1604. The St Giles, Oxford, register as copied by Wood has 'Mris.... Eveleigh, widdow, 14 Aug. 1652' (Wood, vol. 3, p. 218); the Heverly family history identifies this burial as hers. Added. ¶1

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 3

Probable, graded on her link to her son John Eveleigh: the link rests on the Heverly family history (a compiled source) and on the Dean's Oxford ties (the Blackhall lease in St Giles parish, and his bequest to the poor of St Giles). ¶2a

Upgrade: Probable to Confirmed, graded on her link to her son John Eveleigh (Dean of Ross). Record: her nuncupative will, TNA PROB 11/224/477, read on the register image. Text: 'Memorandum that upon Thursday the fifth day of this instant August 1652 in the Evening of the same day Mrs Prudence Eveleigh of the parish of St Giles in the Suburbs of the City of Oxon widow lying sick in her bedchamber in her dwelling house within the parish aforesaid and being of good sense and memory did in the presence of Paul Hood Doctor in Divinity and Rector of Lincoln Colledge in Oxford, and of Margarett Andrewes Spinster being her maid servant and of Christopher London being also her servant... utter these words following that is to say As touching her lease for yeares of Blackhall farme with the appurtenances that her sonne John Eveleigh should after her death [interlined over 'decease', struck] have the said lease.' Witnesses subscribed 9 Aug 1652; proved 23 Aug 1652 (catalogue date; the register margin carries a clerk's note about the act). ¶4

Superseded: the earlier note that her link to the Dean 'rests on the Heverly family history' and needs a test record. The test record (PROB 11/224/477) has now been read and is cited above. ¶6

The case for this identification 1

Continuity: the will names the relationship ('her sonne John Eveleigh') and ties him to a specific property, the Blackhall lease in St Giles, Oxford. That lease is then followed: (1) the Dean's will of 30 Nov 1678 (Brady's abstract) leaves 'the lands of Blackhall and Coplands-meade, nere Oxford', held by lease, to Rev. B. Cross; (2) Benjamin Crosse's own will (PROB 11/378/255, image read) disposes of 'an estate called Black Hall lying in St Giles in the North Suburbs of the City of Oxon being a Lease held from St Johns Colledge', and in the same will calls 'John Eveleigh Deane of Rosse in the Kingdome of Ireland' the grandfather of his son John Crosse. Also the 1654 Magdalen lease to 'John Eveleigh, of St Giles, Oxford, clerk', two years after her death. No other John Eveleigh is known as her son or as holder of Blackhall, so the identification rests on the property chain, not on a name match across places. ¶5
