Life story

Holder of Grønfur bruk nr. 2 in Oppstryn: Aaland has 'Mons Andersson Grf., sk. 1720' (deed to half a laup, bought with Ivar P. Hellesylt), owning 1 laup in Nesi (Nesje), wife 'Brite Rasm.d. Erd.' (Erdal); his widow Brite married Paul Kristensen, who took the bruk in 1735, and a son Rasmus Monsen held it from before 1745. Aaland also notes that Mons redeemed the Hellesylt half laup in 1720. ¶1

The Nordfjord guardianship protocol no. 1 lists exactly four Monsen wards at Grønfur, all inheriting at the same skifte of 3 Jul 1727 and again on 16 May 1729: Rasmus Monsen (guardian Jørgen Erdal, fol. 7b), Anders Monssen (guardian Ingebrigt Olsen Jelde, p. 194), Marrite Monsdatter (guardian Rasmus Folvem, fol. 92b, then Ole Andersen Guddal, fol. 176) and Synneve Monsdatter (guardian Martinus Grov, p. 124). Two sons and two daughters, exactly the '4 Børn, 2 Sønner og 2 Døttre' of Sal. Mons Andersen Grønfur in the 1732 probate. Rasmus's later protocol entry (no. 2) has Poul Grønfur as guardian for 'sin Stefsøn Rasmus Monssen', the same stepfather named for Marrite in 1743. This makes the sibling set and the father very likely, but no record read writes 'Mons Andersen's daughter Marrite', so he stays Probable on his link to Marthe Mogensdatter. Mons was alive in 1726 (guardian for Synneve Olsdatter of Øvre Flo, protocol no. 1 p. 181) and dead by 1732. The 3 Jul 1727 skifte at Grønfur (Arvebog fol. 491-497, the unscanned 1715-27 Arvebok) may be his own probate. ¶5a

The '16 May 1729' Grønfur inheritance is probably not a separate A 6 probate. Guardianship protocol 1, fol. 176a (Joen Andersen Grønfur's account; scan saved as z179.jpg) reads that after the child died in 1728 his means fell half to Rasmus Monsen and half to Marrite Monsdatter, and 'Dernæst arvede ogsaa efter barnets Sal: broder den 16 maij 1729, som ogsaa paa ovennævnte Skiftebrev staar antegnet' ('next she also inherited from the child's late brother on 16 May 1729, which is also endorsed on the said skiftebrev', i.e. that of 3 Jul 1727, Arvebok fol. 491). So the 1729 division after a second deceased child was written onto the 1727 skiftebrev and into the Arvebok, which is not scanned. The Digitalarkivet probate index has no Grønfur entry for 1727-31 (domicile searches Grønf*, *rønf*, Grøn* 1729: 0 Nordfjord hits). Mons stays Probable. The test record is still the Arvebok fol. 491-497 (3 Jul 1727) at Statsarkivet i Bergen. ¶6

Probate after Synneve Andersdatter, Grønfur, A 7 fol. 155b-156a, 1732 (heading; day and month not securely read): she had served at Øvre Flo and died at Grønfur; her heirs are her brothers' surviving children and heirs, 'nemlig Sal: Mons Andersen Grønfurs efterlevende 4 Børn, nemlig 2 Sønner og 2 Døttre, som endnu er umyndige', with further heirs of a brother Anders [Joensen?]. So Mons Andersen Grønfur was dead by 1732 and left four minor children. Marrite Monsdatter Grønfur's third and smallest inheritance (1 rd 3 ort 4 s) is dated 11 Oct 1732 in the guardianship protocol, which fits an aunt's estate. The children are not named in the part read, so the link stays Probable. ¶3a

A 7 fol. 156a-157a read in full. The heading says Synneve Andersdatter had served at Øvre Flo and died at Grønfur; her heirs were her late brothers' children, 'nemlig Sal: Mons Andersen Grønfurs efterlevende 4 Børn, nemlig 2 Sønner og 2 Døttre, som endnu ere umyndige', and a second brother's line. The division (fol. 156b-157a) gives the lots as 'Den eene Broder Sal: Mons Grønfurs efterladte 4re Børn tilfalder...' and 'Den anden Broder...'. It does NOT name the four children, so the hoped-for direct confirmation is not there. ¶4

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Probable as father of Marthe Mogensdatter (see her page), graded on his link to her. Guardianship protocol no. 2, p. 111 (scan): 'Marrite Monsdatter Grønfur' inherited on 3 Jul 1727, 16 May 1729 and 11 Oct 1732; on 8 Oct 1743 'befandtes denne pige at være bleven gift med Truels Aaning', who received her means from 'hendes Stiffader Poul Grønfur'. Her husband Truls Aaning is Truls Larsen Aaning, whose wife Marthe Mogensdatter (Mons = Mogens) is in the tree; in 1736-44 Truls Aaning was also guardian for her as 'sin versøster' (p. 235, land in Nessie and Grønfur, which matches Mons's land in Nesi). The relationship to Mons is inferred, not stated: her patronymic, her farm, and her stepfather Paul of Grønfur, who by Aaland married Mons Andersen's widow. Mons Andersen is the only Mons that Aaland lists on Grønfur. ¶2a
