About the family

Oline Slind's maternal grandfather, named as 'O. Johnsen', the bride's father, in the Selbu marriage entry of 17 July 1871, and as the father in his daughter Kari's baptism of 25 July 1847 at Stokke. ¶1

Naming evidence: his eldest son at home is called Haldo, after his wife's father, and his grandson Ole after himself. That is the Selbu naming custom working exactly as expected and it corroborates the identification of the household. ¶4

HIS PARENTS ARE NAMED, and by two independent records. The Selbu marriage register for 1831, page 317 serial 19, records the wedding on 21 JUNE 1831 of 'Ole Johnsen Stokke', aged 22, born 1809 in Saelboe, to 'Marite Haldorsdtr Svinaas', born 1810 in Saelboe, and names the groom's father as JOHN OLSEN STOKKE and the bride's father as HALDO TORGERS SVINAAS. The 1825 census of Selbu then supplies the household: at rural residence 0147 Stokke, household 1, 'John Olsen, 67, Gmd.' (gaardmand, farmer) and 'Dordi Johnsd., 54, Hustru' with their sons 'Ole Johnsen, 16' and 'John Johnsen, 12'. ¶8

Life story

Birth about 1809, not 1808. The 1831 marriage makes him 22 and born 1809; the 1825 census makes him 16 at 27 November 1825, so born about 1809; the 1865 census age of 57 implies 1808. Two of three say 1809 and the marriage register is the earliest, so ABT 1809 replaces the ABT 1808 taken from the 1865 census age. ¶10

Death and burial: 'Ole Johnsen', born 1809 at Stokke, died 13 July and buried 19 July 1891, Selbu burial register 1878-1891, p. 406 no. 21; the same burial is in the 1889-1913 register, p. 341 no. 22, as 'Ole Jonsen', born 1809, Stokke. Identification strong: the entry itself gives the birth year 1809 and the birthplace Stokke, which match his 1831 marriage (aged 22, born 1809) and 1825 census (16, at Stokke). The only other Ole Johnsen of about that age buried in the window, born 1808 at Dyrdal, buried 7 July 1886, is a different man. Index read; scan not read. ¶13

1865 census: head of the household at residence 0004 Stokke, lopenr 150, Selbu, aged 57, 'Gaardbr. Selveier' - farmer and OWNER-OCCUPIER. Unlike the Slinden family his daughter married into, he owned his land. The census records the holding's returns: two barrels of barley sown, one of mixed grain, two and a half of oats and five of potatoes, and a stock of two horses, fifteen cows, fifteen sheep and sixteen goats - a distinctly larger farm than Slinden, feeding eleven people in one household. ¶2

His household in 1865 was his wife Marit Haldosdatter, 57; his married son John Olsen, 24, with John's wife Ane Mikkelsdatter, 29, and their children Ole, 1, and Marit, 3; and five more of his own children at home - Haldo, 21, Ole, 16, Christen, 10, Kari, 19 (Oline's mother) and Johanna, 13. ¶3

Printed source: the farm Stokke has its chapter in Selbu i fortid og naatid, bind 6 (1992), with all-caps headings on printed pages 8 and 17, and the family page is page 12. That page carries the given names Kari, Marit, Johanna and Berit, the patronymic Jonsen (the 1992 spelling of Johnsen), the word Amerika, the birth year 1808, and the years 1841, 1847, 1849, 1851, 1853 and 1855 - the birth years of the children John, Kari, Ole, Johanna and Christen as the 1865 census records them. It also prints the sequence of holder tenures 1808-1836, 1837-1840, 1841-1864 and 1865-1872, the last transition matching the 1865 census's picture of a freeholder handing the farm to his married eldest son while still being named as the farmer. ¶5

Spelling trap worth recording: the 1992 volume writes Jonsen, not Johnsen, and Haldor, not Haldo. A search of the printed volume on the census spellings returns nothing and looks like a negative - 'Haldo' has no hit at all on pages 8-18, while 'Haldor' does. The same trap defeats searches for Braendhaugen (printed Brennhaugen), Kulsetmo (printed Kolsetmoen), Kulset (printed Kolset) and Slinden (printed Slindan). ¶6

Context - how this page citation was obtained without reading the page: every volume of the Selbu bygdebok is access-restricted to Norwegian IP addresses on nb.no, so neither the page images nor the OCR text can be downloaded from the United States. The National Library's IIIF Content Search service, however, is open to anyone and returns, for any quoted phrase, the exact page and the pixel box of every occurrence, with the surrounding context blanked. A page's contents can therefore be profiled by asking it a long series of yes/no questions - one phrase search per name, per year, per place - and the printed folio number is itself OCR'd in the bottom margin, which fixes the page number exactly. Nothing below is read off a page image; every item is a retrieved search hit. Confidence is accordingly Probable, never Confirmed. ¶7

That marriage entry is the single most useful document found on this side of the family. It gives the exact date of a marriage wave 3 could only bracket as BEF 1841, it names both fathers, and it corrects the bride's farm and patronymic (see her note). ¶9

He married at twenty-two and was still farming the same land forty years later: the 1865 census finds him at rural residence 0004 Stokke, lopenr 150, aged 57, 'Gaardbr. Selveier' - farmer and FREEHOLDER - with eleven people in the household, two horses, fifteen cows, fifteen sheep and sixteen goats. His father John Olsen was already 'Gmd.' at Stokke in 1825, so the family's standing on that farm goes back at least to the eighteenth century. ¶11

The Selbu gaardshistorie locates his farm precisely: Selbu i fortid og naatid vol. 7 (1992), page 100, says that Gunnar Olsen Slind married in 1871 'Kari... fra MIDTI-STOKKAN' - from Midti-Stokkan, the MIDDLE Stokke holding. That is Ole Johnsen's bruk, and it names the sub-farm that the census only calls Stokke. ¶12
