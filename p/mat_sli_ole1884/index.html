About the family

Younger brother of Oline (Slind) Eidem, and the 'Slind, Ole G.' who in 1950 was farming three doors from her in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota. The 1891 Norwegian census of Selbu puts both of them in the household of the tenant farmer Gunder Olsen at Slind - Oline Gundersdatter b. 1881 and Ole Gundersen b. 1884 - which is the sibling link. ¶1

The family's arrival is documented through one of the children: 'Johan Slind', aged 8, born 1914, single, residence 'Selbu, Norway', nationality Norway, arrived Ellis Island 20 June 1922 on the Stavangerfjord out of Christiania, manifest line 20, NARA microfilm T715 roll 3129 frame 290. Thirteen days from Trondheim. ¶5

Brother of Oline G. Slind Eidem. Named in her October 1955 obituary: 'She is also survived by two brothers and two sisters; Ole Slind of Colfax, Washington, Gunder, Marit and Karen Slind of Selbu, Norway.' ¶27

His son is buried beside him: 'Dr Ole Slind, 30 Mar 1912 - 23 Sep 2008, Selbu Cemetery, La Crosse, Whitman County, Washington', inscribed 'MARRIED JUNE 15, 1940 / BORN IN SELBU NORWAY'. Ole Olsen Slind, born 30 March 1912, is the second son of the 1920 Kjosnes household and of the 1922 emigration, so the whole family of nine that sailed on the Stavangerfjord went to the Palouse country of eastern Washington. ¶45

Life story

Arrival, Confirmed: 'Ole Slind', male, aged 18, birth year 1884, single, nationality Norway (Norwegian), arrived ELLIS ISLAND on 19 MAY 1902 on the White Star liner CYMRIC out of LIVERPOOL, manifest line 29, NARA microfilm T715 roll 277 frame 18. Nineteen days from Trondheim to New York, on the same ship and the same line as his sister two years before. ¶3

Death and burial: Find a Grave records 'Ole Gunnarsen Slind, 24 Jan 1884 - 20 Jun 1975', buried at Selbu Cemetery, La Crosse, Whitman County, Washington - the burial ground of a second Selbu-Norwegian settlement, in eastern Washington. The birth date matches his baptismal entry to the day, and his son Ole, born 30 March 1912, lies in the same cemetery as 'Dr Ole Slind, 30 Mar 1912 - 23 Sep 2008', likewise an exact date match. Two exact dates in one cemetery identify the family. The patronymic is written 'Gunnarsen' for Gundersen. ¶25

Burial: SELBU CEMETERY, WHITMAN COUNTY, WASHINGTON. The Washington State Digital Archives cemetery collection records his stone as "Ole G Slind, b. 1884 d. 1975, s/w Ingeborg A and Ingeborg O" (reference hrp-cem-whi-selbu_0127) - a single stone shared with TWO wives, both named Ingeborg. The cemetery is called Selbu after the Norwegian parish the whole settlement came from, and 191 people are buried in it. This is the record that documents the Colfax migration the family had never been able to trace. ¶32

Marriage 2: Ingeborg Andersdatter MOGARD, b. abt 1884 in Norway, d. 1 March 1959 at St. Ignatius Hospital, Colfax. She is the "Ingeborg A" on the stone - A for Andersdatter, her father being Anders Mogard. She is also the wife who appears beside him in the 1950 census, aged 65. Mogard (Mogard/Mogaard) is another Selbu farm name. ¶38

Marriage 1, in Norway: Ingeborg Olsdatter KLEGSETH (b. 20 Jan 1889 Selbu), with whom he came out on the Stavangerfjord on 7 June 1922 with all six children. She died at Colfax, Whitman County, Washington on 9 August 1930, aged 41, and is the "Ingeborg O" on his stone - O for Olsdatter, her father being Ole Klegseth. ¶37

First emigration, Confirmed: Trondheim police office, First accession, emigrant protocol no. 10 (1895-1902), page 348, line 22, group of travellers 02057, sequence 2001 - 'Ole Gundersen Slind', male, year of birth 1884 (calculated), age 18, unmarried, occupation Arb (labourer), residence Selbo, destination MARIETTA MINN, payment 'Bill. bet. i Amerika' (ticket prepaid in America), permissions '3/5 02 Kapt Raeder Meldt selv' - military leave granted 3 May 1902 by Captain Raeder, and he reported himself to the police in person. Expedited 30 April 1902, ship Tasso, line Hvide Stjerne (White Star). Same patronymic, same parish, same stated destination and the same prepaid ticket as his sister Oline's entry of twenty-one months earlier. ¶2

No child of an Ole Slind was registered in Lac qui Parle County between 1900 and 1934, and he and Ingeborg were alone in the house in 1950, so the couple appear to have been childless or to have had their children in South Dakota. ¶19

First emigration: expedited at the Trondheim police office on 30 April 1902, aged 18, unmarried, occupation 'Arb' (labourer), residence Selbo, on the steamer Tasso of the Hvide Stjerne (White Star) line, ticket paid for in America, DESTINATION MARIETTA, MINNESOTA - the same village his sister Oline had gone to on a prepaid ticket on 25 July 1900. The protocol notes his military clearance: '3/5 02 Kapt Raeder Meldt selv', he reported in person to Captain Raeder on 3 May 1902. ¶21

He therefore crossed the Atlantic twice: out as an eighteen-year-old labourer in 1902 to the Minnesota county where his sister already was, home to Selbu to marry and buy a farm, and out for good in 1922 with a wife and six children, driven by the Norwegian post-war depression. ¶24

Printed source: his two emigrations are both recorded in the printed farm history - the years 1902 and 1922 stand with his own birth year 1884 on page 100 of Selbu i fortid og naatid, bind 7 (1992), in the SLINDAN NORDRE chapter. The farm he bought and worked between the two crossings, Kjoesnes soendre gnr 151 bnr 2, has its own chapters in the same volume with all-caps KJOESNES headings on printed pages 281, 284, 295, 301, 313 and 324; his purchase, his tenure and his six children should be printed there. ¶43

1905 state census: Augusta Township, Lac qui Parle County, schedule page 697, line 24 - 'Slinde Ole, M, 20, W, born Norway, father born Norway, mother born Norway, resident of this state 3 years, of this enumeration district 3 years, occupation Farm Labourer'. He was lodging between the Naseth and Smidt families, a single young man working for wages. ¶15

Immigration: three years in Minnesota as of 1 June 1905 puts his arrival at about 1902, two years after Oline Slind's own arrival in September 1900. A brother or cousin following her over is the obvious reading, and it is the reading the 1950 census supports. ¶16

He went home again. He is absent from the 1910 Selbu census, and by the 1920 census he is head of his own household at Kjosnes sondre, gnr 151 bnr 2, Selbu, in a census district named simply 'Slind', described as 'Driver eget gaardsbruk' - working his OWN farm, an owner where his father and grandfather had been tenants. With him are his wife Ingeborg Olsdatter, born 20 January 1889, six children and a nursemaid. ¶22

The gaardshistorie adds his children's years: Gilbert 1910, Ole 1912, Johan 1914, Kristine 1914, Johanna 1916 and one more in 1919 - the same six the 1922 emigration protocol lists. ¶48

He went home again, and then emigrated for good with his whole family: Trondheim emigrant protocol no. 15 (1916-1926), page 140, lines 26 to 33, group 00883, expedited 7 June 1922, ship STAVANGERFJORD, line Norske Amerika Linje, tickets paid 'Cash'. Line 26 - Ole Gundersen Slind, b. 1884, married, head, occupation Gaardbruker (farm owner), residence Selbu, destination 'U.S. Amerika'; line 27 his wife Ingeborg, b. 1889; lines 28 to 33 their children Gilbert 1910, Ole 1912, Kristine 1914, Johan 1914, Johanne 1916 and Bjarne 1919. The stated reason for emigrating, entered against every one of them, is 'Manglende adgang til at faa lonnende virksomhed' - lack of access to gainful employment. His own line carries the fullest permissions note in the Selbu series: 'Utv.att. Selbu lensm. 13/5-22. 8/6-22 sendt Trondel. K.K.' - an emigration certificate from the Selbu sheriff dated 13 May 1922, forwarded to the Trondelag control office on 8 June. ¶4

Second emigration, the whole family at once: expedited 7 June 1922 on the Stavangerfjord of the Norske Amerika Linje (Norwegian America Line), with his wife Ingeborg and the children Gilbert, Ole, Kristine, Johan, Johanne and Bjarne, fare paid in cash, destination 'U.S. Amerika'. He is entered as married and as a gaardbruker, farmer, of Selbu, and the reason given for emigrating is 'Manglende adgang til at faa lonnende virksomhed' - no way of getting profitable work. His emigration certificate was issued by the Selbu lensmann (sheriff) on 13 May 1922 and forwarded to the Trondelag county office on 8 June. ¶23

A striking double tie to the Walli family: his eldest son GILBERT married MAGNA WALLI on 25 January 1934, and twenty-seven years later the widowed father married the widowed ANNA WALLI. Father and son married into the same Walli household a generation apart. ¶40

Context, flagged as context: the Selbu colony in Whitman County was decades older than his arrival. John O. Kjosnes, buried in the same yard, was born in 1822 and died in 1909, and Kjosnes is the name of the very farm - Kjosnes sondre, gnr 151 bnr 2 - that Ole himself worked in Selbu in 1920. He did not move to a strange place in old age; he moved to where his own neighbours had gone two generations earlier, and where his elder brother Ole (b. 1872) had been farming at Lacrosse since about 1908. ¶41

THE "OLE G." INITIAL - RESOLVED. The selbu-slind agent left open why the 1950 census called him "Ole O. Slind" when his patronymic was Gundersen. Two independent American records now show that he used G, for Gundersen, exactly as his sister Oline did: his Whitman County stone reads "Ole G Slind", and his daughter Kristine's own Social Security application (NUMIDENT SS-5, SSN XXX-XX-0349) names her father as "OLE G SLIND". His 1961 Whitman County marriage licence writes him out in full as "Ole Gunderson Slind". The 1950 census's "O." was an enumerator's slip, as was suspected; the correct American form of his name is OLE G. SLIND. ¶36

What remains unresolved is the sequence of places. He was in Minnesota in April 1950 on that reading; his sister's obituary of October 1955 places him at COLFAX, WASHINGTON; he died in Whitman County in 1975 and is buried there with both wives and his son. The simplest reconstruction is that he farmed in Lac qui Parle County from 1922, buried his first wife there or in Washington, remarried, and moved to the Whitman County Selbu colony between 1950 and 1955 - where his son Ole had settled. The records that would settle it are his Washington death certificate of June 1975 and the obituaries of either Ingeborg. ¶50

Burial note: Selbu Cemetery at La Crosse, Whitman County, Washington, is the churchyard of a second Selbu colony, in the Palouse wheat country of eastern Washington, and it holds at least four of this family - Ole himself, both his wives, and his son Dr Ole Slind. Colfax is the Whitman County seat, nineteen miles from La Crosse. ¶51

He was the only one of her siblings who also emigrated, and by 1955 he was living at COLFAX, WHITMAN COUNTY, WASHINGTON. Oline's daughter Barbara, Mrs. Cle Kyllo, was living at LaCrosse in the same county in 1955 — so niece and uncle had settled within twenty miles of each other in the Palouse, and the movement of this family's second generation to eastern Washington is a real pattern and not a coincidence. ¶28

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 3

Rated Probable, not Confirmed, on one point only: the identity of the 1902 emigrant and the 1922 head of household with the 1950 neighbour. The patronymic, the birth year, the parish and the destination all agree, and the wife's name Ingeborg agrees; but the 1950 census makes Ingeborg 65 (so born about 1884-85) against the protocol's 1889, a four-year discrepancy in a stated age. There were also two other Selbu men of the same name and almost the same age - Ole Gundersen Slind b. 1880, who went to Colfax, Washington in 1907, and Ole Johnsen Slind b. 1880, who went to Marietta with Oline in 1900 - so the name alone would not do. ¶8

Rated Probable. The 1905 'Slinde Ole', aged 20 and newly arrived, and the 1950 'Slind, Ole O.', aged 66 and farming in the next township, are consistent in name, birthplace and approximate age, but no record yet links them explicitly, and no record yet states his relationship to Oline. ¶17

Rated Probable: the relationship is stated in a printed obituary, but no record of Ole Slind himself has yet been retrieved. ¶29b

Records that disagree 2

One discrepancy recorded rather than smoothed over: the 1950 census makes his wife Ingeborg 65, implying a birth about 1884-85, while the Norwegian records give her 20 January 1889, which would make her 61. His own stated age of 66 agrees with 24 January 1884 to the month. A four-year error in one stated age, with the husband's exactly right, is the commonest sort of 1950 census error. The American middle initial 'O.' is also unexplained, since his patronymic was Gundersen and his sister correctly used G; it may be taken from his own children, who were all Olsen or Olsdatter Slind. ¶26

A second discrepancy, recorded because I cannot resolve it from outside Norway. The Selbu gaardshistorie's line for him, as reconstructed from the National Library's word coordinates, reads 'Ole d.y., f. 1884, d. 1930, reiste til Amerika... 1902... og g. 1910 m. Ingeborg... f. 1889, bodde paa Oppigarden Kjosnes, men reiste til Amerika... 1922' - that is, it gives his death as 1930. The gravestone's 1975, with a birth date matching the baptismal register to the day, is much the better evidence. Either the book is wrong or my reconstruction of that one printed line is. The printed page 100 of Selbu i fortid og naatid vol. 7 settles it. ¶47

The capital in "Ole G." is stroke-for-stroke the same letter as the capital in "Eidem Palmer G." written two lines below by the same enumerator, and quite unlike the same enumerator's O in "Mayme O." on the line between them. NARA's volunteer index transcribes it "Slind Ole A", which is wrong; so is the reading "Ole O." that led an earlier wave to suspect the patronymic Olsen and to doubt that this was Oline's brother. It is him, and the initial has been G all along. ¶34

Correction: Oline's 1955 obituary describes him as "Ole Slind of Colfax, Washington", which earlier waves read as a late-life move from Minnesota. The Washington records show the family's Whitman County connection began no later than 1930, when his first wife died at Colfax, and that four of his six children married in eastern Washington between 1934 and 1944. He was enumerated in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota in 1950 and back at Colfax by 1955, so he moved between the two Selbu colonies more than once rather than migrating west only at the end. ¶42

Correction: the 1922 emigrant family did NOT settle in Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota. Wave 3 identified the 'Ole O./A. Slind, 66, born Norway' with his wife 'Ingeborg, 65, born Norway' enumerated two or four dwellings from John P. and Oline Eidem in Mehurin Township in the 1950 census as this man, reasoning from his 1902 emigration to Marietta. The 1902 crossing is his, but he went home again, and when he emigrated for good in June 1922 with Ingeborg and six children he went to WHITMAN COUNTY, WASHINGTON, where he died in 1975 and where his son was buried in 2008. The 1950 Minnesota household is somebody else - most likely another of the several Slind men in that colony - and identifying him is the open question. Wave 3 itself flagged the identification as 'Probable bordering on Confirmed' and named the gravestone as the record that would close it; the gravestone has now closed it the other way. ¶46
