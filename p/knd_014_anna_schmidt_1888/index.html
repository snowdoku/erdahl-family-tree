About the family

Parents: the death act names her as the daughter of Wilhelm Schmidt, then living (żyjący) at a place in Romania written 'Kurtbelli' (reading uncertain; also possible 'Kurbelli' or 'Kunbelli'; not identified), described as a master blacksmith (mistrz kowalski; the occupation word reads well at full resolution), and of his wife Anna Maria née Adam (z domu Adam). Her mother is not described as living or dead. ¶2

Sister of Karolina Knodel: the declarant is Gottlieb Knodel (see his page), the same settler who registered and signed his children's Pfeilsdorf births in 1905-1910 as husband of Karoline née Schmidt. An unmarried woman surnamed Schmidt who was his sister-in-law can only be his wife's sister (a brother's wife would bear the surname Knodel and be married). She is therefore a full or half-sister of Anna Karolina Knodel née Schmidt (see her page). Linking her to the same father as Karolina is an inference from that relationship and from Karolina's 1974 BC death registration, which names Karolina's father as 'Willem Schmidt'; the act itself names only Anna's own parents. ¶3

Life story

Death: On 1 Aug 1906 the settler (osadnik) Gottlieb Knodel of Płużnica, written 'Bogumił', the Polish form of Gottlieb, declared at the Standesamt (civil registry office) Pfeilsdorf that Anna Schmidt, 'his unmarried sister-in-law' (jego niezamężna szwagierka), aged 17 3/4, Evangelical, living at Płużnica, born on 24 Nov 1888 at a place in Romania written 'Tulcke' or similar (reading uncertain; first read 'S?li?ke'; possibly Tulcea, not proved), had died at Płużnica in his dwelling on 31 July 1906 at 6.30 p.m. The day is written out in words, 'trzydziestego pi[erwszego]' (thirty-first), read at full resolution; the ending is faint. He signed the act. Source: the Standesamt Pfeilsdorf death register, 1906 no. 23 (Polish certified copy of 1949, AP Toruń 69/1205/0/2.3/1013, scan 221). ¶1

Context: she was one of the Dobruja Germans who settled at Pfeilsdorf, a colony of the Prussian Settlement Commission that Paul Traeger (1922) calls almost purely Dobruja German. That a 17-year-old was living with her married sister there, while her father stayed in Romania, is consistent with that migration. Her burial place is not stated. ¶4

Burial (index): the Evangelical parish of Villisaß, Kreis Culm, has the burial of 'Ana Schmidt' on 4 Aug 1906, with one relative indexed, 'Wilhelm'. She is taken to be this Anna: she died at Pfeilsdorf on 31 Jul 1906, four days earlier; Villisaß was the parish of the Pfeilsdorf Evangelicals, where her sister's children were baptised; and her father was Wilhelm Schmidt. The index gives no age or parents, and the image has not been read, so the identification is probable, not proved. The burial image (0245316-00404) would give her age and parents and settle it. ¶5
