About the family

Sister of Oline G. Slind Eidem, still living in SELBU, NORWAY when Oline died in October 1955: 'Gunder, Marit and Karen Slind of Selbu, Norway.' She is distinct from Karen P. Eidem née Kari 'Carrie' Slind (1863-1915), who was a generation older and is buried at Zoar. ¶3

Her husband is named: Selbu i fortid og naatid vol. 7, p. 100, in the children's list of Gunnar Olsen d.e., 'Karen, f. 1890, g.m. Ole Gundersen Slind' - married to an Ole Gundersen Slind, which is why the 1920 census finds her still at Slind nordre doing 'Husarbeide hjemme', housework at home: she had married a man of the same farm cluster, almost certainly a cousin. She is one of the four siblings Oline's 1955 obituary names as surviving in Selbu. ¶4

Her husband is in the grave beside hers. Section K, Row 02, No. 014 holds 'Ole G. Slind, 14 MAY 1899 - 5 MAR 1978' - the Ole Gundersen Slind the Selbu gaardshistorie names as her husband, born 14 May 1899 and so nine years her junior. Adjacent plot numbers in the same row of the same section are as close to a stated marriage as a churchyard index gets. ¶7

Life story

Oline Slind's youngest sister, born 7 December 1890 and baptised in Selbu in February 1891, her father then entered as Gunder Olsen, gaardbruger, of 'Slind'. In the 1891 census, taken on 1 January 1891 when she was three weeks old, the enumerator left her given name blank and she is indexed only as '!! Gundersdtr.', born 1890. ¶1

Gravestone, Selbu Kirke Cemetery, Mebonden, Selbu, Section K, Row 02, No. 013: 'Karen G. Slind, 7 DEC 1890 - 6 AUG 1971'. The birth date agrees to the day with her baptismal entry of 15 February 1891. She outlived every one of her brothers and sisters and died at eighty. ¶6

She was at home through the 1900 and 1910 censuses and is in her widowed mother's household at Slind nordre in 1920, by then married - 'd g Husarbeide hjemme', a married daughter doing housework at home. Her husband's name has not been established; the 1920 household also holds grandchildren surnamed Kjosnes and Olsen, so one of the married daughters' husbands was a Kjosnes. ¶2
