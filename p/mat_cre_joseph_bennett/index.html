About the family

Children named in the will: James (= James Benjamin Creed, 1831-1906, of Lot 59), Mary (= Mary Creed, 1824-1908, who married Captain Richard Westaway in 1852) and William (= William Bennet Creed, 1827-1898, of Albion, Lot 59, his executor). The will is the record that proves James Creed of Lot 59 was Joseph's son: the same township, the same Baptist burial ground, and a brother William farming beside James in 1891. ¶4

Life story

According to the compiled genealogy he was a son of Captain William Creed and Elizabeth Prince, born 1788 at Sturgeon, and married Catherine Morrison about 1822. A brother, Samuel Prince Creed, born 20 February 1790, was baptised on 25 June 1802 as the son of William and Elizabeth Creed (PEI baptism index). No baptism of Joseph has been found. ¶5

Childhood: in the 1798 census of Lot 59 his father William Creed's household had four boys under 16, two men and two women - Joseph, then about ten, was presumably one of the boys. His elder brother Job Prince Creed's obituary (The Islander, 15 November 1867) says Job was 'a native of the new United States of America, then Colonies of Great Britain, and emigrated with his father to this Colony at a very early age', which matches the compiled account of Captain William Creed coming from Providence. ¶6

Name: the 1765 Registry of Deeds memorial 160893 shows that his father's associate in Nova Scotia was the Rev. Joseph Bennett of Windsor, who held a share of Ballincollow, Co. Limerick. This supports the family tradition that Joseph Bennett Creed was named after a Bennett uncle with whom his father crossed the Atlantic, though the deed states no relationship. Grade unchanged. ¶7

His name: he was evidently named after the Rev. Joseph Bennett (born about 1727-28; SPG missionary at Windsor, Nova Scotia; of Cork city by 1787). The Blennerhassett Pedigree (about 1733-36) names Joseph as a brother of Mary Bennett, wife of William Creed of Ballyneal, Co. Limerick, the probable parents of his father Captain William Creed. That would make the Rev. Joseph his great-uncle. In 1765 Joseph Bennett mortgaged property to 'William Creed, then late of Limerick city' (Registry of Deeds memorial 160893). This is a naming inference, not a record statement. ¶8

Administrator of his father's estate: he gave the administration bond on 11 September 1809 as 'Joseph Bennet Creed of Three Rivers, Farmer', with Samuel Nelson, merchant of Charlottetown, and David Higgins as sureties. He signed the inventory of 20 February 1810 'J. Bennett Creed, Administrator' (PEI Probate Estate Case Files, C intestate no. 20, DGS 008123338 item 2, images 253-260). The file does not name him as a son. This is the earliest record found of him by name, and it gives his residence (Three Rivers) and his occupation (farmer) in 1809. ¶9

Gravestone, Sturgeon Baptist Cemetery (Find a Grave 172996878, photograph read): the broken upper part of the stone reads '[... Cre]ed... Feb'y 13, 1868, aged 80 years, a native of P. E. Island. Also his wife Catherine Morrison, died Mar. 21, 1875, aged 85 years, a native of the Isle of [Mu]ll, Scotland.' A small footstone in front reads 'Father & Mother' and '[Jose]ph Bennet[t]'. Aged 80 puts his birth about 1787-88, on the Island. ¶1

Death notice, The Islander, 13 March 1868: 'On the 14th Feb., Mr. Joseph B. Creed, of Lot 59, aged 80 years.' The gravestone and the date of his will both give 13 February 1868; the newspaper's 14th is probably a slip or the date news reached the editor. ¶2

Will: Joseph Bennet Creed of Township No. 59, Kings County, made his will on 13 February 1868, the day he died. He left his 'dearly beloved son James' three pounds currency, payable within five years; his 'beloved daughter Mary' two pounds; a comfortable maintenance for life to his 'beloved wife Catherine'; and the rest of his estate, cattle, chattels and movables, to his 'beloved son William', whom he named sole executor. He signed 'Joseph B. Creed'. Witnesses: Alex Richard, Henry Young (his mark), Elizabeth Young and Jane Young. Filed March 1868, certified copy by G. Hubbard, Registrar; no probate granted (transcription on the Island Register). ¶3
