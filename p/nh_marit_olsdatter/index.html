Life story

'Margret (Olson) Dakken' in her son Ole's 1927 obituary. The obituary says she long outlived her husband and died in 1905 'at the age of eighty years'. That age conflicts with a birth in 1814/1816 (she would have been about 90). The obituary may be wrong, or Ole's mother may have been a different (second) wife. The Norwegian records show no sign of a second wife. ¶1

PROBABLE baptism: Marit, baptized 25 Sep 1814 in Nord-Aurdal, daughter of Ole Juelsen of Midstrand and Astri Evensdatter. The father's name and farm match the marriage record. She named a daughter Astrid (1843), which fits the usual pattern of naming the first daughters after the grandmothers. ¶3

Married Erik Olssen on 28 Dec 1838 in Nord-Aurdal. The marriage record gives her age as 22 (b. about 1816), her birthplace as 'Milstrand' (the second transcription says 'Strand'), and her father as Ole Juulsen. ¶2

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Upgrade: the records were re-read and the one gap closed. (1) The 1838 marriage (Nord-Aurdal church book 1834-1887, pp. 621-622, no. 36) gives the bride Marit Olsdatter, born at Milstrand, about 1816, and the bride's father 'Ole Juulss.'. (2) Ole Juelsen of Midstrand had two daughters named Marit: one baptised 2 Feb 1812, and the girl baptised at Ulnes on 25 Sep 1814 (parents Ole Juelsen, Midstrand, and Astri Evensd.). The Nord-Aurdal burial register shows 'Marit Olsd.', 'Barnet', of Midstrand, aged 6 weeks, buried in March 1812, so the 1812 child died and the 1814 Marit is the only daughter of that name who survived. (3) The 26 Feb 1853 Svennes emigration list gives Marit Olsdatter, 38 (born 1814/15), with her husband and the children Andrea 14, Astrid 10, Ingeborg 7 and Ole 5. The marriage record states her father, the 1814 baptism and 1812 burial identify her as Ole Juelsen and Astri Evensdatter's surviving Marit, and the emigration list ties her to Andrea. Confidence raised from Probable to Confirmed. The 1927 obituary's 'age of eighty' at her death in 1905 remains a conflict (she would have been about 90); the death itself is not proved. ¶4
