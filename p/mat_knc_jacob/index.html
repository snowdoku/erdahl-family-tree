About the family

Eldest child of Gottlieb Knodel and Caroline Schmidt; known as Jack. Death registration, BC 1980-09-007834: died 14 May 1980 at Oliver; an orchardist of Tuc-El-Nuit Drive, Oliver; born 16 March 1902 in Romania. His father Gottlieb Knodel is recorded as born in Russia and his mother Caroline Schmidt as born in Romania. He was married to Sarah Eberle, who signed as Sarah M. Knodel, informant. He was buried in Oliver Cemetery on 17 May 1980. ¶1

Life story

Emigration: Jakob Knodel, born about 1902, birthplace 'Rumanian', came to Canada in 1924 on the White Star-Dominion liner Megantic (Ancestry's index to Canada, Ocean Arrivals (Form 30A), 1919-1924, dbId 1588; card image not seen). He was the first of the family to emigrate. ¶7

Birthplace context: his parents were Dobruja Germans who joined the Prussian settlement colony at Pfeilsdorf by 1905 (Traeger 1922 describes Pfeilsdorf as almost entirely settled from the Dobruja), so his 'Romania' is very probably the Northern Dobruja, Constanța county. The village is unproved. ¶5

Find a Grave (memorial 219751802) gives his plot as Pine Section, Block K, Plot 8 and his wife as Sarah Marie Eberle Knodel (1916-2009). Its biography lists his siblings Sophie Knodel Hatcher (1908-2001), Frieda Knodel Genehr, Erna Knodel Hayter Andrews, Ludwig and Wilhelm. ¶3

Emigration: the index to 'Canada, Ocean Arrivals (Form 30A), 1919-1924' (Ancestry dbId 1588) has Jakob Knodel, b. abt 1902, birthplace 'Runanian' [Roumanian], arriving 1924 on the Megantic (rid 532954; card image 30927_2000915002-03566, not seen). He was the family's pioneer; the 1931 census also gives 1924. The Form 30A card normally names the last residence, the nearest relative left behind and the person joined in Canada. ¶9

Form 30A index, what it does not show: the Ancestry index entry for his 1924 Form 30A (recordId 532954) holds only his name, a birth year of about 1902, the birthplace 'Runanian' (Romanian) and the ship Megantic. Keyword tests of 136 words against that entry matched only 'Runanian', 'Megantic' and 'Jakob'; none of the Dobruja village names (such as Cogealac, Tariverde, Atmagea), the West Prussian places (Pfeilsdorf, Płużnica, Briesen, Toruń), the relatives' names (Gottlieb, Karolina) or relationship words matched. The index therefore gives the country only; it does not show that the card itself lacks a village or a relative. The card image (30927_2000915002-03566), which normally names the last residence, the nearest relative left behind and the person joined in Canada, is queued for the signed-in lane as S0139. ¶10

1926 Prairie census: Jacob Knodel, 24, single, born Rumania, immigrated 1924, a labourer in the household of the farmer Lucas Seefried in the RM of Newcombe, Kindersley district, Saskatchewan (sub-district 20, page 9, line 29). Citizenship Russian; he spoke English. His sister Annie, 19, a domestic who had arrived in 1926, was in the same household. ¶8

1931 census: Jacob Knodel, 29, single, born Rumania, immigrated 1924, alien, a labourer on a fruit farm at Oliver, BC, heading a household of himself, his sister 'Freida' (19) and brother Ludwig (21) in a rented one-room wooden house at $6 a month. He had earned $70 in the year and was out of work on census day, having been unemployed 26 weeks. He was the first of the family in Canada, four years ahead of his parents. ¶6

In December 1944 he signed, as 'Jakob Knodel' of Oliver, as a witness to his brother Wilhelm's marriage at St. John's Lutheran Church, Oliver. ¶2

Okanagan Historical Society member list, 1967 (31st Report): 'Knodel, Jack, R.R. 2, Oliver'. Buried Oliver Cemetery, Pine section, block K, plot 8 (Town of Oliver cemetery register). ¶4
