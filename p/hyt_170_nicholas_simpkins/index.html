Life story

Origin lead only, no parents entered: Banks, Topographical Dictionary of 2885 English Emigrants to New England 1620-1650 (1937), as read in archive.org full-text snippets, lists 'SIMPKINS, Nicholas' under 'Burcot', 'Dorchester', reference 'Banks Mss.', New England town 'Boston, Mass.'. That reads as the hamlet of Burcot in Dorchester-on-Thames parish, Oxfordshire, but the OCR columns are jumbled and the place index also files a Burcot on p. 126 next to Northamptonshire entries, so the county is not certain. Banks gives no evidence beyond his own manuscripts. Checks made: FreeREG has no Nicholas Simpkins baptised 1590-1612 in Oxfordshire, Berkshire or Buckinghamshire (fuzzy), no Simpkins baptisms at all in its Oxfordshire or Northamptonshire coverage 1585-1615, and no marriage of a Nicholas or an Isabel Simpkins anywhere 1618-1640 (fuzzy). Swift's History of Old Yarmouth (1884) and Savage (4:101) say only 'born in England in 1600' and give no parish. His grade (Probable, on his link to Pilgrim Simpkins) is unchanged. ¶4a

Origin search (FamilySearch signed in). (1) Dorchester-on-Thames, Oxfordshire: the FamilySearch catalog lists 'Parish registers for Dorchester, 1638-1919', so no Dorchester register survives for his birth (c.1600); no Simkins/Simpkins of Dorchester or Burcot is indexed 1580-1640. Banks's 'Burcot, Dorchester' cannot be tested from the parish register; Dorchester was a peculiar, so its churchwardens' presentments (Peyton, Oxfordshire peculiars) or bishop's transcripts are the remaining sources. (2) New lead, not proved: the St Benet Fink, London, register (FamilySearch index, England Births and Christenings) has 'Sarah Simkin', chr. 6 Oct 1629, d. 7 Oct 1629, and 'Mary Simkins', chr. 12 Jun 1631, both daughters of Nicholas Simkin(s) and Isabell (1:1:JQLG-YBF, 1:1:JW7Y-GJ9). A Nicholas and Isabel couple in London in 1629-31 fits Capt. Nicholas Simpkins (b. c.1600) and his wife Isabel (b. c.1610), who were in Boston by 1634, but the identification is a name pair only. No Nicholas Simkins x Isabel marriage is indexed 1620-34. Grade unchanged (Probable, on his link to Pilgrim Simpkins). ¶5a

London lead tested (FamilySearch signed in, 2 Nov 2026); grade unchanged (Probable, on his link to Pilgrim Simpkins). (1) The St Benet Fink baptisms of Sarah (6 Oct 1629, died 7 Oct) and Mary (12 Jun 1631), children of Nicholas Simkin(s) and Isabell (1:1:JQLG-YBF, 1:1:JW7Y-GJ9), are in an image-less index; FamilySearch shows the images as viewable only at a FamilySearch center or affiliate library, so they were not read (no occupation or note seen). (2) No other child of a Nicholas and Isabel Simkins/Simpkins is indexed anywhere in England 1620-1640, and no Nicholas Simkins x Isabel marriage is indexed 1615-1636 (England Marriages, fuzzy). (3) A RIVAL in London at the same dates: Nicholas Simkins married Elizabeth Saule at St Margaret Pattens on 9 Nov 1628 (1:1:NVDB-YZW), and 'Efam' Simkins, child of Nicholas Simkins and Elizabeth, was baptised there on 24 Aug 1632. So there were two men named Nicholas Simkins in the City of London in 1628-32 with different wives; the St Benet Fink couple (with an Isabell) is the better fit for the Boston couple, but the name alone cannot pick between Londoners, and a Nicholas Simpkin also baptised a child at Newcastle upon Tyne in 1633. (4) No baptism of a Nicholas Simpkins/Simkins is indexed in England 1596-1604 (the only Nicholas Simkins baptism near is Shenley, Bucks, 1639). Conclusion: the St Benet Fink family is a name-pair match only (Nicholas + Isabell, 1629-31), with a namesake in another City parish; it would at most support Probable for the English origin, and it does not touch his grade, which rests on his link to Pilgrim. ¶6a

Marriage evidence: his deed of 1 March 1648[/9], as 'Nicolas Simpkins' of Scituate, selling his dwelling house and 30 acres to John Williams Jr., covenants that 'Esbell Simpkins my wife' shall yield up her thirds; her release follows: 'I Esbell Simpkins the Wife of Mr Nicolas Simpkins' (Plymouth Colony Records 12, Deeds). In June 1654 'Capt. Nicholas Simpkins, aged 54 years' and 'Isabel Simpkins, aged 44 years' deposed together in the Holmes estate (NEHGR 5:4), so he was born about 1600. So Isabel, mother of Pilgrim (Suffolk Deeds IX:224), was Nicholas's wife from at least 1649 to his death. Pilgrim named his first son Nicholas (b. Boston 22 Oct. 1660). His grade stays Probable: no record read calls Pilgrim his son, and the 1669/70 deeds trace Pilgrim's house to 'Nicolas Simkins deceased' without calling him their father. ¶2

Verified on the image: Suffolk Deeds Liber IX pp. 223-224 (printed transcript, FamilySearch 'Suffolk. Deeds 1672-1678' image 539, ark 3:1:3QSQ-G9ZS-1NVN): George Burrell, cooper, and Debora his wife release to Pilgrim Simkins the house 'mentioned in the originall deed from Lenard Buttles to Nicolas Simkins deceased bearing date the two and twentyeth day of October 1652', left by 'Izable Simkins' who died 'in September last past' leaving issue 'Pillgram Simkins her sonn' and daughters Debora Burrell and Rebecca Terill. The deed calls Isabel the children's mother but does not call Nicholas their father, so his grade stays Probable. ¶3a

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Graded on his link to Pilgrim Simpkins. Capt. Nicholas Simpkins, first captain of the Castle in Boston harbour about 1634, of Yarmouth by 1638, back in Boston by 1649, died intestate in 1656 leaving a widow Isabel (TAG 28:87, compiled). The 1669/70 arbitration deeds (Suffolk Deeds IX:224-226) call the house that Pilgrim received the one bought by 'Nicolas Simkins deceased' from Leonard Buttles on 22 Oct. 1652, and name Pilgrim as son, and Rebecca Terill and Debora Burrell as daughters, of his widow 'Izable Simkins'. The Boston register calls Rebecca 'daughter of Capt. Nicholas Simpkins' at her marriage to William Therrell in 1654 (vol. 9). ¶1a

Probable, not Confirmed: no record read names Pilgrim as Nicholas's son; it is inferred from Pilgrim's surname, his being full brother (through Isabel) of Nicholas's daughter Rebecca, and his taking Nicholas's house. TAG 28:87 calls Pilgrim his only son. ¶1b

Open questions and the next record to read 4

Test record: the 1654 Boston marriage entry of 'Rebecca Simpkins daughter of Capt. Nicholas Simpkins' (vol. 9) gives a sister, not Pilgrim himself; a record naming Pilgrim as Nicholas's son (an administration on Nicholas's estate, 1656, Suffolk Probate vol. 2/3) is still wanted. ¶3b

Test record: the Dorchester-on-Thames parish register c.1595-1605 (Oxfordshire History Centre, PAR87) for his baptism, and the Great Migration 1634-1635 sketch (Anderson, vol. VI, R-S), which is not on archive.org. ¶4b

Test record: the St Benet Fink register images (London Metropolitan Archives P69/BEN3) for the father's occupation, and a London marriage licence or register entry for Nicholas Simkins and Isabel c.1625-29. ¶5b

Test records: St Benet Fink register images (LMA P69/BEN3, on Ancestry, or at a FamilySearch center) for occupation; London marriage allegations (Bishop of London / Vicar-General) c.1625-29 for Nicholas Simkins and Isabel; the known Boston children (Rebecca, Deborah, Pilgrim) include no Sarah or Mary, so there is no naming echo. ¶6b
