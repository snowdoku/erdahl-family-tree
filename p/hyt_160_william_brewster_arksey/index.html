Life story

The 1521 Arksey Brewster is dead by October 1521. The printed index of York act books (YAS Record Series 11, Appendix I, p. 212, page image read) has 'Oct. 2, 1521. [Brewster], William, Arkesay, Prob. Act, Doncaster' (the surname is carried down from 'Brewster, Thomas, Flayneburgh' on the line above). The appendix lists probate acts of 1514-1521 whose wills were NOT copied into the registers, so this is the will Banks dates 1521, and no text of it survives in the York registers. So the William Brewster of Bentley-cum-Arksey who died in 1521 cannot be the William taxed there in 1524 (this person). He could have been that man's father, but no record says so, and he is not entered. Arksey searches of the York indexes for 1389-1553 (YAS RS 6 and 11) and 1554-1602 (RS 14, 19, 22, 24) find no other Brewster of Arksey, Bentley, Scrooby or Sutton-cum-Lound. The 1524 man left no will or administration in the York registry up to 1602. Grade unchanged (Speculative, graded on his link to William Brewster). ¶4a

Banks (1929), pp. 35-39, the source Hunt cites, says the will of a William Brewster of Bentley-cum-Arksey was proved in 1521 and that 'another William Brewster was taxed in the same parish in 1524', and that a Robert Brewster was taxed at Bawtry, next to Scrooby. So there were at least two Williams at Bentley-cum-Arksey in the 1520s, and which (if either) was the father of William of Scrooby is not known. ¶2a

How sure are we?

Speculative. A plausible candidate only. Treat this link as a question, not a fact.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Speculative father of William Brewster of Scrooby (d. 1590): J. G. Hunt, NEHGR 124 (1970): 250, heads the Brewster pedigree with 'William Brewster, taxed in 1524 at Bently cum Arksey (next Doncaster), Yorkshire', who 'seems to have married long before 1558 Maude, sister of Christopher Man of Scrooby', with sons 'seemingly' William (b. about 1535) and Henry (b. about 1537, vicar of Sutton-cum-Lound, Notts, 1565-1594, wife Agnes buried there 15 Mar 1597/8). Hunt's own wording is tentative and no record ties the 1524 taxpayer to the Scrooby family. The GSMD Silver Book (Merrick 2000, pp. 1-5) gives him as William Brewster (c.1510-1558); that death date has not been seen in a record. ¶1a

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Namesake in the same deanery: Fasti Parochiales (YAS Record Series 107), under Owston, near Arksey: '1521 26 Aug. William Brewster, p[riest], pres. by the same, on death of Christopher Adam (Reg. Wolsey, f. 58d). V. in 1534-35 (Val. Eccl.)'. This William Brewster was vicar of Owston from 1521 until at least 1535, so he is neither the Bentley-cum-Arksey testator whose will Banks dates 1521 nor (being a priest) a likely father of William Brewster of Scrooby. Recorded so that later searches of the 1520s-30s Doncaster records do not confuse the two. ¶3

Searches that found nothing 1

Register search (FamilySearch indexes, 2 Nov 2026): the Arksey and Bentley registers are not in the FamilySearch indexes for 1538-1600, so no Brewster entry there could be checked. Neighbouring Doncaster St George (indexed, images with the partner only) has Brewster burials from 1578: Richard 1578; Agnes 1579; Isabel daughter of Robert 1580; William and Thomas, sons of Thomas, 1583; Michael son of Robert 1583; James son of Robert 1596; and a marriage of Alice Bruster 28 Oct 1577. A 'William Brewster' married Martha Beach at Armthorpe (next to Doncaster) on 25 Nov 1538 according to the Yorkshire bishop's transcripts index (a date earlier than most transcripts survive, so the index entry needs checking). None of these ties a William Brewster to Maud Man or to William of Scrooby; recorded as leads and possible rivals only. ¶5
