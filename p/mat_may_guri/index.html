About the family

David Erdahl's maternal great-great-great-grandmother, the mother of Anna Olene (Satter) Eidem, and the woman after whom the Olene in Anna Olene - and then the O. in Mayme O. Eidem - is most likely named. ¶1

Her brother: a man called Ole Walstad was living next door to the Satters at Spink in the 1880 census, gave $5 at her son Gust's wedding, and is described in the 1920 census summary of that son's household as 'uncle Ole Walstad'. He is therefore almost certainly Guri's brother, Ole Pettersen Walstad, and is the first member of her own family found in America. ¶10

Named as the mother in her daughter's obituary: Leader-Courier (Elk Point, South Dakota), 18 March 1937 - 'Anna [Olene] Satter, daughter of Gunder and GURI ANNA SATTER'. ¶15

Her brother is now identified. Her daughter's obituary says that before her marriage Anna Olene 'returned to keep house for her UNCLE, OLE VOL[STAD]' and nursed him until he died at her home. Walstad/Volstad is Guri Anna's maiden name, and the 1880 census of Spink Township is reported to show the Satter household living next door to an Ole Walstad. See mat_n2_ole_walstad. ¶16

Parentage proved: her own marriage entry of 19 June 1863 at Værnes names her as 'Pige Guriana Petersdatter VALSTAD', born in Stjørdal, with her father given as 'PETER OLSEN' — and names him again as one of the two witnesses, 'Peder Ols[en] ibid[em]'. That matches the 1836 baptism on the given name, the patronymic, the farm and the father's name together, and moves the identification from Probable to Confirmed. Her American farm byname 'Walstad' is here attested in Norway as her place of residence at her own wedding. ¶20

Siblings: she was the fourth of six children of Peter Olsen and Daaret Elisabet Pedersdatter at Lille Hvalstad — Mette (b. 10 Mar 1828), Petrina (b. 16 Oct 1830), Olive (b. 27 Sep 1833), Guri Anna (b. 26 Apr 1836), Christopher (b. 4 Mar 1840) and Ole (b. 7 Jun 1842). Her youngest brother Ole followed her to Union County, South Dakota and is the 'uncle Ole Walstad' of the 1920 census. ¶22

Life story

Her name as her daughter's memorial gives it is 'Guri Anna Pettersdtr. Walstad', a Norwegian patronymic (daughter of Petter) with the farm name Walstad/Valstad. ¶2a

Mayme (Eidem) Eidem's maternal grandmother, named as the mother of Anna Olene Satter on her Find a Grave memorial. Walstad (Valstad) is a farm name; her patronymic Petersdatter means 'daughter of Peter'. Rated Probable on the same grounds as her husband - a Find a Grave family listing, not yet a primary record. ¶3

Her own baptism, at Probable: the Stjørdal parish register for 1836 (p. 67, no. 126) records Guri Anna Petersdatter, born 26 April 1836 and baptised 10 July 1836, legitimate daughter of Peter Olsen Hvalstad, bonde (farm owner), and his wife Daaret Pedersdatter, of the farm Lille Hvalstad. Hvalstad is Walstad; the farm lies in Skatval, in Nedre Stjørdal parish, on the south shore of the Trondheimsfjord directly across the water from Frosta. Given name, patronymic, farm byname, year and region all agree. The one discrepancy is twelve days: her gravestone, read from a photograph, gives 14 April 1836. That is why this is Probable and not Confirmed. ¶12

Gravestone, photographed and read, and it is cut in NORWEGIAN: 'GURI ANNA / Hustru til / G. G. SATHER / FODT / 14 Apr. 1836 / DODE / 6 Juli 1895' - 'Guri Anna, wife of G. G. Sather, born 14 April 1836, died 6 July 1895.' A white marble obelisk with an urn finial. Find a Grave memorial 63579109. The file previously carried only 1836 and 1895; both dates are now exact, the marriage is stated on the stone itself, and confidence rises from Probable to Confirmed. ¶14

Her stone spells the married surname SATHER, her husband's stone SATTER, and the Elk Point newspapers use both. Walstad is also written Valstad; both are Norwegian farm names and neither narrows the parish by itself. ¶17

At the baptism of her eldest son at Bakklandet, Trondheim on 4 September 1864 she is 'Guri Anna Petersdtr., Hustru', of Lademoen. ¶13

1870 census: 'Setter, Annie', aged 35, keeping house, born Norway, wife of the head of dwelling 122, Sioux Valley, Union County, Dakota Territory, enumerated 21 August 1870. Her newborn daughter is on the next line: 'Mary', aged 1/12, born in Dakota, with 'born within the year — May' — Lizzie Carrie, born on the Satter homestead on 30 May 1870. ¶23

1880 census: entered as 'Anna', wife, white, female, 49, keeping house, born Norway of Norwegian parents, in Gunder Satter's household at Spink Township, Union County, Dakota Territory, 15-16 June 1880 (ED 5, sheet 407A, line 4), with seven children. Anna is her second given name (Guri Anna). Her real age was 44; the census overstates it. This is the census the earlier note said could still produce her: she is alive at Spink in June 1880, and her brother Ole Walstead, 37, lives alone in the next dwelling. ¶28

1880 census, her children: Gustav 16, Godfried 12 (born Iowa), Eliza R. 10, Anna C. 8, Sophia 6, Mattilda 4 and Lewis 2. Sophia and Mattilda were not previously in this file. ¶29

Censuses: she is in none that can be read. She died in 1895; the 1895 South Dakota state census does not survive for Union County, and Ancestry's 1880 collection is unusable from an anonymous client, so the 1880 census of Spink township - which would have shown her alive with the whole household - is the one record that could still produce her. ¶4

Gravestone: an obelisk with an urn finial standing beside her husband's at Brule Creek Cemetery, cut in Norwegian and photographed — GURI ANNA / hustru til / G. G. SATHER / FØDT / 14 Apr 1836 / DØDE / 6 Juli 1895. 'Hustru til' means 'wife of'. Her plot is Lot 9, Grave 1. ¶6

Her stone spells the family name SATHER and her husband's, in the same plot, spells it SATTER. Hers is in Norwegian and his, cut fourteen years later, in English — the Americanisation of the family happening between two graves. ¶7

Birthplace: Nord-Trøndelag, Norway, on her memorial; her byname Walstad is the Frosta farm name Valstad, and her husband was born at Frosta, so Frosta is the working parish. Pettersdatter is a patronymic: her father was a Petter. ¶8

In English she went by Anna. Her daughter Lizzie's 1934 obituary, written by the family, calls the parents simply 'Gunder and Anna Satter' — independent corroboration of the parentage of this whole quarter of the tree. ¶9

She died at Spink on 6 July 1895, aged 59, five years before her husband remarried. ¶11

Death year 1895 upheld against the 1897 newspaper item (read on the page images). The Union County Courier of 15 April 1897 does print 'Mrs. Abrahams and child of St Louis arrived on Wednesday to visit with her parents, Mr. and Mrs. Gunder Satter of Spink' - Mrs. Abrahams being her daughter Lizzie (Satter) Abraham. That single phrase does not outweigh the Norwegian gravestone ('DØDE 6 Juli 1895'): a country editor's stock 'her parents, Mr. and Mrs.' or a stepmother would both explain it, and no other post-1895 record names Guri alive. ¶25

The Courier of 11 and 18 July 1895 (Local Paragraphs, and the Neighborhood News columns for Beresford, Alcester, Nora, Mount Hope and Brule) does not mention her death; neither issue has a Spink column. Read on the images. ¶27

Illness: the Union County Leader's Spink Township column of about 23 May 1895 opens 'Mrs. Satter is on the sick list yet', and the Courier of 27 October 1893 reports 'Mrs G. G. Satter' sick. G. G. Satter is her husband: her stone calls her 'hustru til G. G. SATHER', meaning 'wife of G. G. Sather'. ¶31

Her estate: the Union County Courier of 23 September 1909 carries a 'Notice of Application for Letters of Administration' in 'the matter of the estate of Anna Sather, deceased', on the petition of 'Gust Sather and Mrs. Anna Eidem' - her son and daughter - asking that letters issue to Gust; hearing at Elk Point on 9 October 1909. It was filed ten days after her husband's death and in the same issue as the notice for proving his will. A dead 'Anna Sather' whose children, not her husband, open her estate in 1909 fits a wife who died before him and whose share was left unsettled while the widower lived. She was 'Annie' in the 1870 census. Identification Probable (the notice does not give her death date or say whose wife she was), but it is consistent with the 1895 stone and inconsistent with her outliving Gunder. ¶26

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Rated Probable: Find a Grave family links only. ¶2b

Confidence raised from Probable to Confirmed in this patch, on the strength of her photographed gravestone, which is cut in Norwegian and gives her name, her husband and both dates. Her own parentage is a separate question and is rated Probable below. ¶5

Records that disagree 1

Contradiction recorded: the marriage entry gives her age as 23, which would put her birth about 1840, four years out. I tested it by enumerating every girl christened Guri, Gurri, Guriana or Gurianna in the whole of Nedre Stjørdal from 1820 to 1858 across all three registers — seventeen of them — and exactly ONE carries the patronymic Petersdatter, the one born 26 April 1836. No candidate born about 1840 is a Petersdatter. The age of 23 is a clerk's error or the bride's own understatement, not evidence of a second woman. The groom's age is light by a year in the same entry. ¶21

Corrections 4

Correction: she was NOT born at Frosta. Her birthplace is LILLE HVALSTAD, later written Valstad, in SKATVAL in the parish of NEDRE STJØRDAL — on the south shore of the Trondheimsfjord, across the Åsenfjord from her husband's Frosta. The two parishes face one another; he came to hers to marry her. ¶18

Correction: her birth date is 26 APRIL 1836 from the baptismal register, not the 14 April 1836 cut on her gravestone. Church book from Stjørdal parish 1833-1844 (1714Q), page 67, serial no. 126: Guri Anna Petersdatter, born 26 April 1836, baptised 10 July 1836, legitimate; father PETER OLSEN HVALSTAD, bonde (farmer), of Lille Hvalstad; mother DAARET PEDERSDATTER, kone. ¶19

Correction: her death year is now given as 1899, not 1895. The South Dakota cemetery register DT58 (the WPA-era transcription of North Brule Creek Cemetery, lot 9, block 2) gives GURI ANNA SATHER, died 6 July 1899. The 1895 reading came only from a Find a Grave photograph of her weathered Norwegian stone ('DØDE 6 Juli 1895'), where a 9 is easily read as a 5. Two contemporary newspaper items show her alive after July 1895. In the Union County Leader of 18 July 1895 the Spink correspondent writes 'Miss Sophia Satter is home for a few weeks' visit with her parents'. That is twelve days after the supposed death, with no death mentioned in that column or in the Spink column of 11 July. The Union County Courier of 15 April 1897 has her daughter Lizzie visiting 'her parents, Mr. and Mrs. Gunder Satter of Spink'. Gunder's 1900 census entry as a boarder with no wife and his remarriage in 1900-01 fit an 1899 death. The year is rated Probable, not Confirmed. No 1899 death notice has been found: the Leader's Spink column of 13 July 1899 is silent, and the Courier for 1898-1902 is not digitised. A fresh reading of the stone, or of the Courier on SDSHS microfilm, would settle it. ¶30

Correction (supersedes the 1899 correction above): her death year is 1895, as cut on her stone. The orchestrator re-read the Find a Grave photograph of the stone itself on 13 Oct 2026, enlarged five times (and -date.jpg): the inscription reads 'DØDE / 6 Juli 1895.' and the last digit has the flat top and open lower bowl of a 5, distinct from the round-topped 9 before it. The South Dakota cemetery register DT58's '6 Jul 1899' (North Brule Creek lot 9 block 2) is therefore a transcription error in that WPA-era register. The two newspaper items that seemed to show her alive later are weak: the Spink correspondent's 'Miss Sophia Satter is home for a few weeks' visit with her parents' (Union County Leader, 18 July 1895) may have been written before her death or use 'parents' loosely, and 'her parents, Mr. and Mrs. Gunder Satter' (Union County Courier, 15 April 1897) is a stock phrase for a married daughter visiting home. No death notice has been found for either year. Rated Confirmed on the stone. ¶32

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Not evidence against her 1895 death: a 'Mrs. G. G. Satter' of Spink who received news of her mother's death at Sioux City (Union County Leader, 5 March 1903) is not Guri Anna, whose gravestone gives her death on 6 July 1895. By 1903 two living women could be so called: Sophia (Larson), wife of her son Gustav Parelius Satter - 'Gust G.', the second G presumably his patronymic Gundersen - or her husband Gunder's second wife, Aagot Wangberg, whom he married in 1900. The item does not say which, and the dead mother is unnamed. ¶24
