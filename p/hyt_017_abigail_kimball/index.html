About the family

Parents (Speculative): the 1897 Kimball Family in America (vol. 1 p. 54) makes her 'Abigail, b. Jan. 28, 1703-4; m., 1722, Job Prince', daughter of Christopher Kimball and Sarah Jolls of Boston, but cites no source. The Boston birth and marriage records it drew on are real (read here). Its link to Job Prince is not supported by any record: no record yet places Christopher Kimball's family in Plympton or Plymouth County, and the book's '1722' conflicts with the Plympton marriage record (24 December 1719). The link rests on three things. The age fits exactly. No other Abigail Kimball born about 1695-1706 has been found in the printed records of Boston (from 1700), Ipswich, Plympton, Kingston, Plymouth, Pembroke, Duxbury or Bradford. Her sons were named Kimball (1726) and Cristifer (1729/30, Kingston town register), and Christopher is a rare name among the Plymouth County Princes. What is missing, and would raise it: a Plymouth County deed or probate record (or a Plympton guardianship or church record) for Christopher or Sarah Kimball, or for a widow Sarah Kimball, about 1704-1725. ¶7

Parents raised to Probable: her presumed mother, as 'Sarah Kemball of Boston', intended marriage with Thomas Shurtleff of Plymouth in April 1713 (Boston Marriages, Record Commissioners vol. 28, p. 92) and married him at Plympton on 21 May 1713. This takes the Boston Kimball household to Plympton, where Abigail married Job Prince in December 1719. See the notes on Christopher Kimball. ¶8

Life story

Marriage: She married Job Prince at Plympton on 24 December 1719 ('Jobe and Abigail Kimball, Dec. 24, 1719', Vital Records of Plympton, marriages p. 367, page image read). Her own birth and parents have not been found in the printed records of Plympton, Duxbury, Pembroke, Bradford or Ipswich. ¶5

Origin: the Plymouth intention of about 1720 calls her 'Abigail CIMBOL of Plimptown' (Plympton, Massachusetts); her parents have not been searched. ¶1

Second marriage: 'Abigail Prince' married Ephraim Everson in Kingston on 15 March 1731/2 (town record), and the town register names their children as those of 'Ephraim and Abigail (Kimball) (formerly w. Job Prince)'. ¶3

Death: 'PRINCE, Abigail (Kimball), w. Job, Sept. 6, 1780, in 77th y.' (Kingston gravestone, Vital Records of Kingston, p. 371); the town register also enters her death as 'Abigail (Kimball), w. Ephraim, formerly w. Job Prince, Sept. 6, 1780 [wid., G.R.1]' (p. 344). Her birth about 1703/04 is inferred from the age. ¶2

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade · 1 on the identification · 1 correction · 1 alternative set aside · 1 open question

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Upgrade: to Confirmed as mother of Job Prince of Boston (see his page). The record is the diary of Hezekiah Prince of Kingston and Thomaston (son of Kimball Prince of Kingston, aged 21 in April 1792, d. 1840), entry for Monday 18 Nov 1793 on arriving in Boston, printed in the New England Magazine n.s. 9 (Feb. 1894): 727-728 from the original journal (editor's notes in brackets, kept apart): 'I stopped with my cousin, James Prince, who married Miss Gordon. He is U.S. marshal, the son of my uncle Job Prince. My father, Kimball Prince of Kingston, Mass., together with four brothers and one sister, were all born at Rocky Nook, Kingston. Their father was Capt. Job Prince, who married Abigail Kimball of Bridgewater, 1722. His father was Capt. Thomas Prince of Boston, who married Ruth Turner of Scituate... My grandfather, Capt. Job Prince of Rocky Nook, Kingston, died at Jamaica, W.I., of the smallpox in 1734. Three of his sons were sea captains residing in Boston. Capt. Job married Elisebeth Allen, died 1790. His mansion house was between McLean Street and Allen Street, West End... I have often been at my uncle Job's mansion, once with my father in 1785. He had eight children, Hezekiah, for whom I was named, Capt. Job, Jr., master of the big ship Massachusetts, Thomas, James, and Samuel; also three daughters, Sarah, Elisebeth, and Abigal.' The diarist, her grandson, names the six children born at Rocky Nook (his father Kimball, 'four brothers and one sister') as children of Capt. Job Prince 'who married Abigail Kimball of Bridgewater', and calls Job of Boston, husband of Elizabeth Allen (d. 1790), 'my uncle Job'. The Kingston town register ('Kimball, s. Job and Abigail', 1726; 'Cristifer, s. Job and Abigail', 1729/30) and the gravestone entry 'Job, ch. Job and Abigail (Kimball), 1790, in Boston' agree. Her own parentage (Christopher Kimball and Sarah Jolls) is not affected: the diary calls her only 'Abigail Kimball of Bridgewater', which agrees with the Prince Genealogy's 'Abigail Kimball of Bridgewater who was born in Boston' (her mother's brother Robert Jolls lived at Bridgewater). ¶9

The case for this identification 1

Identity (Probable): mother of Job Prince of Boston, on the same grounds as her husband Job; his Boston daughter Abigail (1767) carries her name. ¶4

Corrections 1

Correction: Audit 12 (2 Nov 2026): lowered from Confirmed to Probable, graded on her link to her son Job Prince of Boston (see his page). This rules on the audit 11 carry-over flag. The raise rests on Hezekiah Prince's diary of 18 Nov 1793 (printed 1894). On Job of Boston's MOTHER the diary is a family statement about births 70 years earlier, and it is wrong on the very details that matter: it says Job Prince married 'Abigail Kimball of Bridgewater, 1722' (the record: Plympton, 24 Dec 1719, and she was 'of Plimptown') and that he died in 1734 (the record: 1731). That is audit 11 pattern 1 (a late family statement is a compiled source). No primary record names her as Job of Boston's mother: the Kingston register names her with Kimball (1726) and Christopher (1729/30), but Job's 1723 birth comes only from the later family gravestone record (G.R.1), and the probate entries of 1731-45 do not name him. That she was his mother is inferred from the marriage of 1719 and Job Sr's having no other known wife (audit 11 pattern 3; audit 5 rule 1: no earlier marriage of Job Sr has been excluded on the records). ¶10a

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Birth (candidate, Speculative): Boston's town register has 'Abigall daughter of Christophr. Kimbal and Sarah his Wife, [born] 28 January 1703', in the list for 1703. The year is Old Style, so the date is 28 January 1703/4 (Boston Births 1700-1800, Record Commissioners vol. 24, p. 22, page image read). This fits her Kingston death on 6 September 1780 'in 77th y.', which places her birth between September 1703 and September 1704. Not adopted as her birth: no record yet places this Boston family in Plympton, where she was living when she married in 1719. ¶6

Open questions and the next record to read 1

Test records: a Plymouth County deed of about 1744-60 in which Job Prince of Boston acts as heir of Job Prince of Kingston beside his mother or brothers; Abigail (Kimball) Prince Everson's 1780 estate or any deed releasing her dower, naming her Prince children; or a Kingston/Plympton record of Job's birth or baptism, 1720-24. The upgrade note is superseded on the grade only; its transcription stands. ¶10b
