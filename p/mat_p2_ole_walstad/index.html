About the family

Almost certainly Guri Anna Pettersdatter Walstad's brother, and the first member of her own family traced to America. Three strands: the 1880 census of Spink, Union County shows the Satter household 'living next door to Ole Walstad'; 'Ole Walsted' gave $5 at Gust Satter's wedding about 1894, the same as the groom's own brothers; and the 1920 census of Sioux Valley Township shows Gust Satter's household including 'uncle Ole Walstad'. ¶5

An uncle to Gunder and Guri's children on the Walstad side can only be Guri's brother or her sister's husband, and he bears the Walstad name himself, so Guri's brother is much the likelier. The patronymic he should carry is Pettersen, since Guri was Pettersdatter. Rated Probable until a census page or a church record states the relationship directly. ¶6

Brother of Guri Anna Pettersdatter Walstad and therefore uncle of Anna Olene (Satter) Eidem. Named in Anna Olene's obituary, Leader-Courier (Elk Point, South Dakota), 18 March 1937: after working some years in Sioux City, Sioux Falls and Vermillion she 'returned to KEEP HOUSE FOR HER UNCLE, OLE VOL[STAD]', and 'she also took care of [him] in his later years and until HIS DEATH AT HER HOME'. The line break in the scan cuts the surname after 'Vol-'; Walstad/Volstad/Valstad are the same name and it is her mother's. ¶11

Life story

Baptism: Ole Petersen, born 7 June 1842 at LILLE VALSTAD, baptised at Stjørdal 14 August 1842, legitimate — Church book from Stjørdal parish 1833-1844 (1714Q), page 185, serial no. 229. Father Peter Olsen, gaardmand; mother Daareth Pedersdatter. He was the youngest of the six children of that house and Guri Anna's younger brother by six years. ¶16

Gravestone, read from the photograph on Find a Grave memorial 68574185: a pointed-arch granite obelisk at Brule Creek Cemetery, Spink Township, inscribed simply 'OLE WALSTAD / 1842-1922', with the white frame Brule Creek church behind it — the same church that stands behind Gunder's and Guri Anna's stones in their photographs. The stone gives YEARS ONLY and no month or day, so it cannot be tested against the Norwegian birth date of 7 June 1842. The test must come from elsewhere: his South Dakota death record of 1922, his Union County probate, or an obituary in an Elk Point paper, any of which would state a full birth date or a Norwegian birthplace. ¶18

He did not travel with his sister. The complete 1870 census of Sioux Valley and Brule Creek Townships, Union County, read page by page, contains no Walstad, Valstad or Hvalstad at all — so he arrived between August 1870 and his land patent of September 1874, five or more years after the Satters. ¶19

Land patent, 1874: the United States issued him a patent on 1 SEPTEMBER 1874, General Land Office document number 548, accession STA-SD1060__.013, for the NORTH-EAST QUARTER OF SECTION 17, TOWNSHIP 93 NORTH, RANGE 50 WEST OF THE 5TH PRINCIPAL MERIDIAN, Union County, Dakota Territory - 160 acres. ¶1

Context, what a land patent is: the deed by which the United States first conveyed a piece of the public domain out of federal ownership into private hands. Everything afterwards is a county deed. A patent therefore marks the moment a family's land in America begins, and the date is usually within a few years of their arrival in the county. ¶4

He is the obvious next step towards the Walstad family in Norway: an immigrant brother's own records — naturalisation, land entry, church transfer certificate, obituary — would name the same home parish. ¶7

His patent and Gunder's were issued on the same day, 1 September 1874, in the same township — Walstad on section 17, Sather on section 21, about a mile apart. Two families taking adjoining land in one township on one day are a migration party. Thirty-one years later the 1905 state census still has them both there, Ole on section 17 and Gunder on section 21. ¶10

The sequence of his recorded addresses is the argument for the kinship as much as the word 'uncle' is: next door to the Satters at Spink in 1880, a mile off in 1905, and inside Gust Satter's house by 1920, dying in Union County in 1922 and buried in the family's cemetery. ¶9

Corroborated, as a lead: a Find a Grave biography of her brother Gust Satter says the Satter household in the 1880 federal census of Spink Township, Union County, South Dakota was 'living next door to OLE WALSTAD'. Two Norwegian households of the same maiden name side by side on the Dakota prairie is a brother-in-law, not a coincidence. ¶12

1880 census, read on the image: 'Walstead, Ole', white, male, 37, alone, farmer, born Norway of Norwegian parents, dwelling 79, family 85, Spink Township, Union County, Dakota Territory, 15-16 June 1880 (ED 5, sheet 407A, line 12) - the dwelling next to his sister Guri Anna's husband Gunder Satter (dwelling 78). Age 37 fits his birth of 7 June 1842 (he was 38 within days). This turns the Find a Grave 'living next door to Ole Walstad' from a lead into a read record. ¶20

He also appears in the Elk Point press: Leader-Courier, 1895-1898 volume, prints a wedding-present list including 'Ole Walstad' alongside 'Anna Satter' and 'Mr. and Mrs. Henry Hanson' - the niece and the uncle giving presents at the same wedding. ¶13

1905 South Dakota state census: 'Walstad, Ole, 63', post office Spink, Spink Township, Union County, Section 17, Township 93 North, Range 50 West, card 340 — four sections from Gunder Satter on Section 21 of the same township. The stated age gives a birth about 1842, matching the Brule Creek gravestone of Ole Walstad, 1842-1922, exactly. He is buried in the same cemetery as Gunder and Guri. ¶8

Cemetery register: the DT58 register of Union County lists OLE WALSTAD, 1922 (year only), North Brule Creek cemetery, lot 10, block 2, the lot next to Gunder and Guri Anna Satter's lot 9, block 2. Year and cemetery agree with his gravestone (1842-1922). ¶21

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

1 on the grade · 2 on the identification · 2 open questions

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Confidence Probable: the relationship is stated plainly by a newspaper obituary written by the family, but no vital record for him has been retrieved and his death date is unknown beyond 'before 1898', when his niece left his house to marry. ¶14

The case for this identification 2

Why he is in this tree: he patented on exactly the same day, in exactly the same township, as Gunder Gundersen Satter, whose wife was Guri Anna Pettersdatter WALSTAD - Sather in section 21, Walstad in section 17, about a mile apart. A Walstad and a Sather entering adjoining land in the same township of Dakota Territory on the same day in 1874 is not coincidence. Guri Anna's patronymic is PETTERSDATTER, daughter of a Petter, so Ole Walstad - whose own patronymic the land office did not record - cannot be her father unless the patronymic is wrong; he is most likely her BROTHER. No Petter or Peter Walstad appears anywhere in the Union County land records. The patent states no relationship, only the name, the date and the ground, so this is rated Probable and no parent, child or sibling link has been asserted in the data. ¶2

Identification with the Ole Walstad of Union County, South Dakota (1842-1922), and why it is rated Probable rather than Confirmed: the patronymic Pettersen matches his sister's Pettersdatter and both descend from a Peter Olsen; the birth year 1842 matches both his Brule Creek gravestone and his stated age of 63 in the 1905 South Dakota state census; the farm byname Valstad is the one the family Americanised to Walstad; he patented NE quarter of section 17, T93N R50W on the very same day — 1 September 1874 — that Gunder Satter patented section 21 of the same township, a mile away; the 1880 census puts the Satter household at Spink 'living next door to Ole Walstad'; he gave $5 at Gust Satter's wedding about 1894, the same as the groom's own brothers; and the 1920 census of Gust Satter's household at Sioux Valley 'also includes uncle Ole Walstad'. An uncle on the Walstad side to Guri's children, bearing the Walstad name himself, can only be her brother. What is still missing is a single document linking the Norwegian man to the American one. ¶17

Open questions and the next record to read 2

The record that would settle it: the land-entry case file for this patent at the National Archives (Record Group 49, Land Entry Papers, Vermillion land office), or the Walstad family entry in the Union County Norwegian Lutheran church book. ¶3

NEXT RECORD: the 1880 and 1900 federal censuses of Spink Township, Union County, South Dakota; the Union County probate index for an Ole Walstad or Volstad; and the Saint Paul / Brule Creek burial register. ¶15
