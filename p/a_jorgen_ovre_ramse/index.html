Life story

Known only from the patronymic of his son, Thor Jørgensen of Øvre Ramse (born about 1614, estate settled 1681). No record of him has been found. He would have farmed at or near Øvre Ramse in Tovdal in the first decades of the 1600s, and his grandson was named Jørgen Thorsen after him in about 1650, which is the usual Norwegian pattern. ¶1

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

2 on the grade · 1 open question

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Upgrade: Speculative to Probable, graded on his link to his son Thor Jørgensen Øvre Ramse. The 1661 land register (Alminnelig jordebok for Råbyggelag, Lille Topdal, fol. 58a, DA 38714 view 62, read on the scan) gives 'Øffre Ramsøe iiij huder': the Crown owns 2 huder, Åmli prestebol a share, and four peasant owners 5¼ kalveskinn each, among them 'Tore' and 'Jørgen'; the Crown has the bygsel; the users include 'Jørgen bruger ½ hud' and 'Tore bruger ½ hud'. So in 1661 a Jørgen and a Tore (Thor Jørgensen, 50 in 1664) owned and worked equal shares of Øvre Ramse side by side. With the 1610 tax list's 'Jørgen ½ dr' at øffre Ramße, a Jørgen held land at Øvre Ramse at both ends of Thor's early life, and no other Jørgen of the right age is found there. In the 1664 vicar's census Øvre Ramse has Knud 36, Thoor 50 and Osmund 23 and no Jørgen (Thor's son Jørgen was then 14), so the older Jørgen had probably died or retired between 1661 and 1664. ¶3a

Probable, not Confirmed: no record names Jørgen as Thor's father; the link rests on Thor's patronymic, the shared farm and the absence of a rival. The 1610 and 1661 Jørgen may themselves be two men (father and son). ¶3b
