About the family

David Erdahl's great-grandmother, the mother of Harry. Family memory: she emigrated to Canada before the Second World War from a rural town that was in Poland and is now in Germany. ¶1

Siblings: she was the eighth of nine children of Gottlieb Knodel and Anna Karolina Schmidt. Her brothers and sisters were Jacob, William, Gottlieb, Anna (Schofer), Sophie (Hatcher), Ludwig, Frieda (Genehr, later Gaynor) and Otto. In 1938-39 her sister Sophie was also living at Penticton and married there as a police constable's bride. Her brothers Jacob and Ludwig were orchardists at Oliver, where Erna later lived and is buried. ¶10

Family arrival in Canada: her mother's 1974 death registration says 44 years in Canada, so the family arrived about 1929-30, when Erna was about 14. They settled in the Eatonia-Kindersley district of west-central Saskatchewan, where her parents farmed and are buried. Erna was a housekeeper at Penticton by 1939. The arrival record itself, a Canadian passenger list of 1929-30, has not been retrieved; see the dossier. ¶11

Siblings born at Pfeilsdorf and registered there (all read from the Standesamt Pfeilsdorf registers): Gottlieb (2 Nov 1905), Anna (24 Dec 1906), Sophie/Zofia (30 Sep 1908), Ludwig (21 May 1910), Frieda (4 Mar 1912), Erna herself (8 Oct 1915) and Otto (11 Jan 1918). Older brothers Jacob (1902) and Wilhelm (1904) were born before the family reached Pfeilsdorf - in Romania according to their Canadian records; Wilhelm is not in the Pfeilsdorf register. Nine children in all are known. ¶16

Life story

Birth: Born at 9 a.m. on 8 October 1915 at Pfeilsdorf (Polish Płużnica), Kreis Briesen, in the Prussian province of West Prussia, in her father's house, the daughter of the settler (Ansiedler) Gottlieb Knodel and his wife Caroline née Schmidt, both Evangelical. The birth was registered at the Standesamt (civil registry) Pfeilsdorf on 13 October 1915 as no. 36 of that year, not by her father but by the midwife, Mrs Antonie Kunkowski of Pfeilsdorf, who declared she had been present at the birth; the deputy registrar was Felske. Read from the German side-register (Nebenregister) image. ¶13

Context: Her parents were 'Ansiedler' - colonists placed on the land by the Royal Prussian Settlement Commission (Ansiedlungskommission), which from 1886 bought up large estates in Posen and West Prussia, divided them into family farms and settled German Protestant farmers on them, many of them ethnic Germans 're-migrating' from Russia and Romania. The Pfeilsdorf civil register shows a wave of such settlers arriving from about 1903, among them the Knodel, Eberle, Hildebrandt and Reister families, beside the older Polish Catholic population of estate labourers. The parish for Evangelical Christians at Pfeilsdorf was Villisaß (Wieldządz). ¶15

Emigration: Erna, aged 12, landed at Quebec on 22 July 1928 from the White Star-Dominion liner Megantic, which had sailed from Southampton, with her parents, her sister Frieda and her brother Otto; destination Winnipeg (Ancestry index to the LAC 1925-1935 passenger lists, dbId 1263, record 2638382). Exact-keyword tests of her index entry show last residence 'Pluznica' (Płużnica, Poland), nationality Polish, race German, single, and the relative 'uncle Jakob Knodel', who was evidently her father's brother left behind at Płużnica. The family went on to the Eatonia district of Saskatchewan. ¶21

Emigration: she came to Canada with her parents and her siblings Frieda and Otto on the Megantic, landing at Quebec on 22 July 1928 (LAC T-14745 frame 00366, vol 15 p 110, line 4). She is listed as Erna Knodel, daughter, single, a schoolgirl, born at 'Pluznica', Poland. The manifest gives her age as 13; from her birth on 8 Oct 1915 she was 12 that day. Her mother's age is also one year high (47, though born 19 Nov 1881, so 46), while Gottlieb (49), Frieda (16) and Otto (10) are exact, so this is a small slip by the informant or clerk, not a sign of another child. ¶26

Passenger-list index, birthplace (Oct 2026): the 'Pluznica' keyword that matches her Ancestry dbId 1263 record 2638382 sits in the index's hidden birthplace field, agreeing with the manifest's birthplace 'Pluznica'. ¶27

1931 census: On 1 June 1931 Erna Knodel, 15, single, was living in her parents' household on their farm (NW 27-25-24-W3) in the Rural Municipality of Newcombe No. 260, near Eatonia, Saskatchewan. She was entered as born in Germany, parents born Rumania, immigrated 1928, alien, Lutheran, mother tongue German, able to speak English; occupation 'at home', unpaid; not attending school. This places her arrival in Canada in 1928, at about 12 or 13. ¶20

Marriage registration, BC 1939-09-473800: Erna Knodel, housekeeper, spinster, aged 23 (so born about 1915-16), Lutheran, residing at Penticton, born Germany; father Gottlieb Knodel, born Germany; mother's maiden name read as 'Carolina Arndt Schmidt' (handwriting uncertain), born Germany. She signed her own name. Married Raymond Hayter at Penticton on 15 July 1939. ¶2

On the birthplace: the 1939 record says Germany. Family memory says a town then in Poland and now in Germany. Before the First World War the reverse movement is far more common - towns in the old German provinces of Posen, West Prussia, Pomerania and Silesia that became Polish after 1919 or 1945 - and a girl born about 1916 in Russian Poland under German wartime occupation, of a German-speaking Lutheran family, could also be recorded as born in 'Germany'. Which it was is an open question, and the answer is likely to be on the family's Canadian immigration record. ¶4

Birthplace: Find a Grave says Germany, as her 1939 marriage does. Family memory says a rural town 'then in Poland, now in Germany'. Her brother Ludwig, born 1910, gave Poland as his own birthplace in 1941; three siblings born in 1904, 1906 and about 1912 are recorded as born at 'Pfeilsdorf'; her parents were born in Russia (father) and Romania (mother). Where Erna herself was born in 1915 is not yet established. ¶12

Voters lists (Ancestry index, dbId 2983; places from keyword tests): 'Mrs Raymond Hayter', Yale, 1940; 'Mrs Erna Hayter', Kaleden (Yale), 1949; 'Mrs Erna Hayter', Oliver (Okanagan-Boundary), 1953; 'E. Hayter', widow, Oliver, 1962, on the same page as William Andrews, a Canadian Pacific Railway employee of Oliver; 'Mrs Erne Andrews', Oliver, 1968; 'Erna Andrews', Oliver, 1974. So after Raymond's death she moved from Penticton to Kaleden and then by 1953 to Oliver, where her brothers lived. She was still a widow named Hayter in 1962, and she married Bill Andrews between 1962 and 1968. ¶22

Emigration: Canadian passenger-list index (Ancestry 'Canada, Incoming Passenger Lists, 1865-1935', anonymous search) lists Gottlieb Knodel (b. abt 1879), Karolina (abt 1881), Frieda (abt 1912), Erna (abt 1915) and Otto (abt 1918) on one manifest page, arriving 22 July 1928, destination Winnipeg, Man. Index keywords that match this record (tested one by one) are Quebec, Southampton, Megantic, Polish, German, Farmer and Married: i.e. the White Star-Dominion liner Megantic, Southampton to Quebec, the family travelling as Polish nationals of German 'race'. The manifest image (Quebec reel, 1925-35 series) has not been seen; it should give their last residence (expected Plużnica) and the relative they were joining. ¶23

A Knodel family was settled in the South Okanagan by the late 1930s: BC records show Knodel marriages at Oliver in 1941 (Ludwig) and 1944 (Wilhelm), Knodel deaths at Oliver and Penticton from 1980, a Gottlieb Knodel who died at Chilliwack in 1985 aged 79, and an Anna Karoline Knodel who died at Surrey in 1974 aged 92. Which of these were Erna's parents and siblings is not yet established. ¶5

Gravestone (Find a Grave photo, read): a flat granite double marker in the Veterans Section of Oliver Cemetery with a poppy-on-maple-leaf emblem and a grape-vine ornament: 'IN LOVING MEMORY / ANDREWS / WILLIAM ARTHUR OCT. 12, 1925 - OCT. 28, 2000 // ERNA OCT. 8, 1915 - DEC. 31, 2007'. ¶8

Widowed on 19 March 1947, when Raymond died at Penticton; his death registration names her as Erna Knodel Hayter. No remarriage of an Erna Hayter appears in the BC marriage index, which is public to about 1951, and no death of an Erna Hayter appears in the BC death index, which is public to about 2006. Either she remarried after 1951, died outside British Columbia, or both. The date and place of her death are not yet known. ¶3

City directories after Raymond's death: in the 1948 British Columbia and Yukon Directory she is 'Hayter Mrs E r Green', still living on Green Avenue, Penticton. By the 1951 Penticton City and District Directory she had moved to Oliver ('Hayter Mrs E'), and she is listed there again in 1954; in 1951 only Cecil Hayter appears at Kaleden. With the voters' lists this gives her path as a widow: Green Avenue, Penticton (1947-48), Kaleden beside Cecil's family (1949), and Oliver from about 1950, near her brothers Jacob and Ludwig Knodel. ¶19

Widowhood, from the federal voters' lists: in 1949, two years after Raymond's death, 'Mrs Erna Hayter' was listed at Kaleden on the same page of the Yale list as her brother-in-law Cecil Elwood Hayter and his wife, so she was living beside or with Cecil's family there. From 1953 she was at Oliver, where her brothers Jacob and Ludwig farmed: she is 'Mrs Erna Hayter' on the Okanagan-Boundary list of 1953, 'E Hayter' in 1962 and 'Mrs Erna Hayter' (indexed 'Krne') in 1963. These are anonymous index entries; the page images, which give address and occupation, have not yet been read. ¶17

Salmon Arm, 1955 (lead for the Knodel and Harry research): the Summerland Review of 22 September 1955 lists 'Mrs. Ray Hayter' 'of Salmon Arm' among the out-of-town guests at the wedding of Ethel Margaret Gilbert and Victor Charles Smith at Summerland on 17 September 1955, in the same list as Mr. and Mrs. Dudley Hatcher, Mr. and Mrs. Fred Gaynor and Mr. and Mrs. Jack Knodel - her sister Sophie's, her sister Frieda's and her brother Jacob's households. Mrs. Ray Hayter is therefore Erna, still using her first husband's name, and living at Salmon Arm in 1955. The bride's parents, Mr. and Mrs. Roy Gilbert, had moved from Summerland to Salmon Arm in 1949; the Knodel turnout suggests a family tie to the Gilberts or Smiths that is not yet identified. ¶24

Remarriage date narrowed: she was still Mrs Erna Hayter on the 1963 federal list and appears as 'Mrs Erne Andrews' on the 1968 list and 'Erna Andrews' on the 1974 list, both at Oliver. She therefore married William Arthur 'Bill' Andrews between 1963 and 1968. The marriage itself is not yet public in British Columbia. In 1962 and 1963 'William Andrews' and Erna Hayter are indexed on the same page of the Oliver list, apart from her brothers' pages, which suggests they were already near neighbours before they married; this is an inference from the page numbers only. ¶18

Second marriage: William Arthur Andrews' BC death registration (2000-09-022261) names his wife as 'Erna Knodel', and Erna Andrews was the informant, of 9332 338th Avenue, Oliver. That ties the Andrews widow to the Knodel girl who married Raymond Hayter in 1939. The date and place of the Andrews marriage are not yet found. It took place after 1947 and is not in the public BC marriage index, which runs to 1950, so it was probably 1951 or later. ¶9

Corrections 2

Correction: earlier research recorded that no remarriage or death of Erna had been found and that she was 'born about 1916'. She was born on 8 October 1915; her gravestone gives that date. She remarried William Arthur 'Bill' Andrews, and as Erna Andrews she died at Fredericton, New Brunswick on 31 December 2007, aged 92. She was buried beside him at Oliver, British Columbia. Her death falls after the end of the public BC death index (about 2006) and outside BC, which is why the earlier searches missed her. ¶7

Correction: Family memory says Erna came from a rural town that was then in Poland and is now in Germany. It is the other way round. Pfeilsdorf was a village of the German Empire (Kingdom of Prussia, West Prussia) when she was born in 1915; it passed to Poland under the Treaty of Versailles in January 1920 and is today Płużnica, seat of gmina Płużnica in powiat wąbrzeski, Kuyavian-Pomeranian voivodeship, about 20 km north-east of Chełmno and 40 km north of Toruń. Her 1939 BC marriage record's 'born Germany' was therefore literally correct, and her brother Ludwig's 'born Poland' (born at the same place in 1910) reflects the later border. ¶14

Searches that found nothing 2

Baptism (not found in the index): Erna's baptism is expected in the Evangelical register of Villisaß, Kreis Culm, the parish where five of her elder brothers and sisters were baptised in 1905-1912, but the Ancestry index of German Lutheran registers (dbId 61250) has no entry for her (searches for Erna Knodel and Erna Knoedel, and the full Knodel surname lists, returned nothing; her brother Otto, born 1918, is missing too). The last Knodel row indexed at Villisaß is her sister Frida's baptism on 30 Mar 1912. Either the 1913-1918 entries were not indexed or they were made elsewhere. The register images after image 0245317-00209 (Frida's entry) are queued for the signed-in lane as S0153; until they are read, her baptism date, church and godparents are unknown. ¶25

Baptism, wider search (Oct 2026): neither her 1915 baptism nor her brother Otto's appears in the Ancestry dbId 61250 index under any spelling tried (Knodel, Knödel, Knoedel, Knodl, Knadel, Nodel, Knudel, Knobel), and the last Knodel row at Villisaß is still Frida's of 30 Mar 1912. ¶28
