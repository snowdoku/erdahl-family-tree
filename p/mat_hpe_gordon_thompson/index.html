Life story

Death: PEI death card, Gordon Thompson, born 1780, died 30 July 1815, Prince Edward Island (Ancestry 61503, record 15483). The card's birth year is probably calculated from an age at death. ¶1

He died when his daughter Frances, later the wife of George Hicken of Gaspereaux, was twenty months old. Confidence is Probable because the identification of his daughter Frances with Mrs George Hicken rests on name, age and birthplace rather than on a record that names both. ¶3

Origin unknown. Gordon as a given name and the 'Scottish' origin his daughter gave in 1881 suggest Scottish ancestry. The register images, not yet read, may name sponsors. ¶4

Deeds read in full-text snippets and transcript (grade unchanged, Probable): PEI Land Registers 1810-1814, deed no. 237, dated 8 May 1812, 'Between Gordon Thompson of Charlotte Town Prince Edward Island Mariner and Catharine his Wife of the one part and James Macintosh of the same place Gentleman' (image 127). A second deed (Land Registers 1807-1811, c.1810) conveys land from Edmund Water to 'Gordon Thompson and Catharine his wife', and a third (1810-1814) is a conveyance by them to Samuel Nelson. He was also a surety, as 'Gordon Thompson, Mariner', of Charlotte Town, on the administration bond for Richard Suddell's estate in 1810 (PEI Probate Estate Case Files). So he was a mariner of Charlottetown, holding and selling town land with Catharine 1810-12. None of these names children. Full-text searches found no later deed by Catharine Thompson as widow, or by George and Frances Hicken as Thompson heirs. Test record still open: a post-1815 deed or the Court of Probate distribution naming Frances. ¶11

Burial: 'Thompson, Gordon. Buried 30 July 1815, age 35 years. Record from Charlotte Parish, St. Pauls Church' (PEI death/burial card index, FamilySearch film 004594540 image 3646; image read). So the 30 July 1815 date previously given as his death is his burial at St Paul's, Charlottetown; he was born about 1780. ¶6

Estate file: 'T - Admin. no. 10, Estate of Gordon Thompson', PEI Probate Estate Case Files (FamilySearch, images 978-982). His widow petitioned the Surrogate General, Robert Gray, from Charlotte Town on 1 August 1815: 'As my Husband died intestate, I beg the favour you will make out the Letter of Administration in my name', naming Samuel Nelson, Thomas Robinson and Thomas Sims as sureties. She signed 'Chatharine Thompson, Her + Mark' (witness J. Garobbo?). The bond, dated 3 August 1815, binds 'Catharine Thompson Widow', Samuel Nelson, Thomas Robinson and Thomas Sims, merchants, and Joseph Robinson the elder and James Bagnall, printer, all of Charlotte Town. She was to administer 'the Goods Chattels and Credits of Gordon Thompson deceased'. The file names no children, and holds no inventory or distribution. The petition agrees with his burial on 30 July 1815. ¶9

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Grade unchanged (Probable) on his link to Frances (see her page). The file confirms that Catharine was his wife and widow, but it does not name Frances, so her identification with Mrs George Hicken still rests on name, age and birthplace. A Land Registers hit (PEI Land Registers 1810-1814) has a deed of 1812 to 'Gordon Thompson and Catharine his wife' for a Charlottetown town lot. It was seen in the full-text snippet only, not read. ¶10

Corrections 1

Correction: the children's birth months are June, not January: Susannah born 15 June 1809 and Peter born 27 June 1811, as the register itself reads (images read; the index's 'June' was right). The baptism entries name the mother as Catharine Curry. ¶7

Searches that found nothing 2

Burial search (negative): the Charlottetown Old Protestant Burial Ground (Elm Avenue, closed 1873) has no surviving stone-by-stone transcription online. An archived PEI Genealogical Society leaflet lists 'Thompson' among family names found in a 1947 inventory of its stones (surname only, no given names or dates), and none of the 33 archived photographs of its stones shows a legible Thompson or Carpenter. The People's Cemetery transcription has no Thompson. The 1947 inventory (McNutt typescript, Robertson Library, UPEI) is the record that would show whether his stone survived. ¶5

Pre-1815 lists (negative): the 1798 census of Prince Edward Island (heads of household only, Island Register transcription) has no Thompson head in Charlottetown and Royalty; the only Thompson heads on the Island are John Thompson (6 persons) and Geo. Thompson (5) in Prince Town. At about 18 he would not be named in 1798 unless he headed a household, so either could be a father, but nothing links them. He is not on the 1803 or earlier passenger lists, or in the pre-1815 land records, that the Island Register transcribes. ¶8
