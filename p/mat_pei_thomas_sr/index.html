About the family

Family history (secondary, Ron Hayter's PARO file, 1994-96): says Thomas senior was born 1807 or 1808 and 'died in the Poor House in Charlottetown in 1885'; that in the 1861 census he was a labourer living apart from Thomas junior, he and his wife both 45-60; and that Thomas junior's first lease, of 100 acres at High Bank from Sir Samuel Cunard in 1856, was signed by 'Thomas Hayter, the Younger' of St Andrew's Parish, by his mark. The author also found a Thomas Hayter born 22 May 1808 at Tisbury, Wilts, son of John Hayter and Martha Racket of Swallowcliffe, but then found that this man died at Tisbury on 19 May 1861 aged 52, so he is ruled out. ¶22a

Life story

False lead: FamilySearch full-text hits for a 'Sarah Hayter of Halifax, Widow' (mortgagee, 1829; deeds 1793-1836) are OCR misreadings of SARAH SLAYTER, wife (later widow) of John Slayter, Comptroller of His Majesty's Customs at Halifax; the deeds of 1811-1814 spell Slayter/Slaytor in the same passages. There is no Hayter household in the Halifax deeds of 1793-1840. ¶30

Halifax baptism of his son (image read): St Mary's Roman Catholic church, Halifax, Nova Scotia, baptism register 1830-1844, entry no. 264, 4 August 1832: 'I the undersigned baptized Thomas George aged nine months lawful son of Thomas Hayter and Martha Elizabeth Gibbs. Sponsors Hugh Mull[ins?]...', signed J. Loughnan, P.P. (FamilySearch DGS 005190390, image 124; indexed as 'Heyton', but the image reads Hayter or Haytar). Nine months old on 4 August 1832 puts the child's birth in about October-November 1831. ¶25

Probably the immigrant Hayter and the father of Thomas Hayter (born England 1831). A Thomas Hayter and his wife Martha Elizabeth were living at White Sands, Lot 64, Kings County, PEI, by 1840: their daughter Isabella was born there on 5 June 1840 (baptised 21 February 1842), and James (born 27 May 1842), Jane Eliza (5 June 1845) and Sarah Catharin White Hayter (22 September 1849) were all baptised on 9 April 1851 (PEI baptism indexes). ¶1

Birth year is inferred only from his being a father in 1831, and his English birth from his son's. The record that would settle the link is the 1841 PEI census of Lot 64 (heads only, at PARO), any land record for Thomas Hayter in Lot 64, or his burial record. ¶3

English origin - wave-6 search (not yet found): every free index of English parish and nonconformist registers that can be searched without a login was checked for a Thomas Hayter (or Hater, Haiter, Heighter, Haytor, Hatter, Heyter, Hayther) who married a Martha c.1820-1831, or who had a son Thomas baptised in 1831-1832. None was found. The England-wide indexes (FamilySearch/IGI extracts as 'England, Select Births and Christenings' and 'Select Marriages'), FreeREG, and the county register collections of Somerset, Bristol, Dorset, Wiltshire, Hampshire, Devon, Westminster and London were all searched. Details are in the Wave-6 dossier data/patches/maternal/w6/findings/hayter-england.md. ¶4

Religion (new): the 1832 baptism was by the Roman Catholic parish priest of St Mary's, Halifax, with Catholic sponsors. The family's later baptisms on PEI were Presbyterian (1842) and Bible Christian (1851), and the 1881 census calls him Church of England, so the Halifax baptism may reflect the only clergy at hand rather than the family's faith. This also explains why no English baptism of Thomas junior was ever found: he was born in England about October 1831 but baptised in Nova Scotia. ¶28

Halifax follow-up (FamilySearch signed in; no grade change). (1) Marriage: no marriage of Thomas Hayter (or Heyton/Hatter variants) to Martha (Elizabeth) Gibbs is indexed anywhere in 'Canada, Nova Scotia, Church Records, 1720-2001', which indexes the St Mary's (RC) Halifax marriage registers 1830-1843 and 1830-1845 (image index checked on the 1830 pages). No groom Thomas with a bride Martha is indexed in Nova Scotia 1826-1832 other than Cassidy x Roest (St Mary's 1831) and Crow x Whidden. With the boy's English birth (every census) this points to a marriage in England before late 1831. (2) No other Hayter/Heyton entry of 1770-1845 is indexed in that collection: the 1832 baptism of Thomas George is the family's only Nova Scotia church entry, which fits a short stop at Halifax in 1832 on the way to PEI. (3) Military: no Thomas Hayter born 1800-1813 appears in any FamilySearch-indexed British Army, navy or pension collection, and no Hayter child appears in the GRO regimental birth indexes for Nova Scotia. Together with his trade of weaver (1841 census) this tells against a garrison family, though it does not exclude it. ¶29

Emigration route, 1832 (free sources; no grade change). Ships to Halifax: Martell's 'Immigration to and Emigration from Nova Scotia 1815-1838' (PANS 1942), abstracted from the Novascotian, Acadian Recorder and Halifax Journal and reproduced on TheShipsList, lists every 1832 arrival with passengers at Halifax. Vessels from ENGLAND that landed passengers at Halifax between the boy's birth (about Oct-Nov 1831) and his baptism (4 Aug 1832) are only: brig Mary-Ann, 41 days from Liverpool (Novascotian 5 Apr, 44 in steerage); ship Halifax, Liverpool (19 Apr, 4 steerage); ship Jean Hastie, Liverpool (3 May, 26); ship Janet, Liverpool (10 May, 8); brig Lady Dunmore, Liverpool (Halifax Journal 28 May, 32 steerage); brig Argus, Jersey (31 May, 8); and brig Walker, 60 days from LONDON (Acadian Recorder 28 Jul, 4 passengers). The brig Cartha from Liverpool landed 105 at Sydney (Novascotian 19 Jul). The Irish ships of 1832 (Pallas, Wellington, Betock, Jane, Friends, all Cork/Waterford) are the only other source of steerage passengers. ¶32a

PEI arrival, 1832: the Island Register's PEI Ships Arrivals/Departures database (RG 9 customs returns and Royal Gazette shipping columns, ship_data2.html) has no vessel from Halifax or any Nova Scotia port entered at Charlottetown around 11 Sep 1832; the nearest arrivals are the Pandora from Waterford (3 Sep, 19 passengers, quarantine ground) and the Mary Jane from Newport (22 Sep). Its 'Passengers arriving from other Maritime ports' page (maritimepass.html) has Royal Gazette passenger lists for the Pictou packet in May-June 1832 but nothing for Aug-Sep 1832. The 1841 census (image 526) says the household paid its own passage to the Island for 3 persons, which fits Thomas, Martha Elizabeth and the infant Thomas coming on from Halifax at their own cost rather than in an assisted party. ¶33a

Arrivals at Charlottetown, week to 15 Sep 1832 (image read). The British American (Charlottetown), no. 7, 15 Sep 1832, p. 55 (islandnewspapers.ca object british-american:18320915-055, read from the Wayback Machine's archived JP2 image), reports: 'His Majesty's ship Columbine, Lieut. Metherall, and Monkey Tender, arrived in this port on Tuesday last, 7 days from Halifax' (Tuesday last = 11 Sep 1832, the very date Ron Hayter's family history gives for the arrival of 'Mr. and Mrs. K. C. Gibbs'); and under Shipping Intelligence, ENTERED: 'Schrs Bear Island, Lavenger, Halifax; John, Ramsay, New Brunswick'. No passengers are named. So the only vessels from Halifax that week were the naval sloop Columbine with her tender (which sailed on to North Cape on 15 Sep) and the schooner Bear Island (master Lavenger). The 11 Sep date in the family history is thus a real Halifax arrival date, which gives it some credit; whether the Gibbs couple, or the Hayters with them, came by the Columbine (as naval or official passengers) or by the Bear Island is not shown. Crops saved: and _shipping.png. ¶34a

Bridge search at the PEI end (FamilySearch signed in; no grade change, stays Probable on the link to Thomas Hayter per audit 11). Searched for a PEI record that names Thomas junior with this couple or ties him to them. (1) The 1856 lease of the High Bank farm: the lease itself is not on FamilySearch. The Land Purchase deed of about 1895 (PEI Land Records 1892-1897, image 392) recites 'a lease to the said Thomas Hayter dated 6th day of March A.D. 1856', so the 1856 lessee is Thomas junior of High Bank. It names no father and does not repeat 'the Younger'. (2) Thomas junior's marriage of 4 Dec 1862: already read (DGS 004594919 image 82); the form has no parents or residence column. (3) FamilySearch Full-Text gave no PEI hit for 'Thomas George Hayter', and none for 'Thomas Hayter Senior/Sen/Junior/Jr' outside Ron Hayter's family history. That history (PARO Genealogies, image S3HY-6WMS-LDW) says the author found nothing documenting the deaths of Martha Elizabeth, Thomas junior or Grace, and mentions a Family Bible that 'might shed some light'. (4) No will or estate file for either Thomas is in the PEI Probate Estate Case Files or Will Books. The only Thomas Hayter in them is the subscribing witness to John Hooper's will, 1880. (5) PEI civil death registrations (1906 and later) are not on FamilySearch. PARO's genealogy search showed a Radware CAPTCHA to the browser and was not used. The bridge records still unread are: Thomas junior's death registration (if he died 1906-1911, it would name his parents); the Charlottetown Poor House register of about 1885 for Thomas senior; and the 1856 Cunard lease original (PARO). ¶37

Emigration context (inference, not proof): Lot 64, where he was living by 1840, belonged until 1841 to the Cambridge family. Their firm had been run from Bristol since 1814 and settled tenants on its Murray Harbour lands. In 1832 the Amity sailed from Bristol to Charlottetown, arriving on 3 July with 'Mrs. Cambridge... and 20 in the Steerage' (Royal Gazette). The Felicity (1831) and the Amity (1833) also ran from Bristol to Murray Harbour. A family that settled at White Sands on Cambridge land in 1832 most plausibly came out through Bristol as Cambridge tenants, but no passenger list survives online. The Petworth (Sussex) emigration scheme of 1832-37 is ruled out: no Hayter appears in its index of family names, and its ships went to Upper Canada. ¶6

1841 census: the 1841 census of Prince Edward Island (Kings County, District 15, Lot 64, image 524) lists Thomas Hayter, weaver, as head of a household of six. It comprises one male aged 16-45 (himself), two males under 16, one female aged 16-45 and two females under 16. This census names only heads of family and gives ages in bands. On the religion and land page (image 525) all six are entered as Church of England. He held 75 acres as an 'occupant', meaning neither freeholder nor tenant, with no lease, term or rent entered, so the return gives no lease year to date his arrival. ¶13

1841 census, passage and birthplaces: on the produce page (image 526) the household had paid its own passage to the Island (3 persons). It grew 4 bushels of wheat and 200 of potatoes and kept 3 hogs. The birthplace columns give 4 natives of England and 3 natives of the Island. That makes 7, against the 6 counted on the age page, so the return contains an error. The 3 passages plus the 3 Island-born make 6, and in Richard Moore's household on the same page the passage count equals the English-born count exactly. The likeliest reading is therefore 3 English-born (the parents and one child) and 3 Island-born children; this is an inference. On either reading the household held at least one English-born child. ¶14

Occupation: weaver (1841 census of Lot 64, image 524). This is the only record found so far that gives his trade. His son Thomas was a farmer in every later census. ¶15

Birth range: the 1841 age band (16-45) puts his birth between about 1795 and 1825. As the probable father of a son born in October 1831, he was born by about 1812. The birth date is narrowed to BET 1795 AND 1812 on that basis. This is an inference from the age band, not a recorded date. ¶16

1841 census of Lot 64 (FamilySearch DGS 008131115, images 524-533): Thomas Hayter, weaver (image 524), heads the only Hayter household in the township. No second Hayter household appears in Lot 64 in 1841. ¶18

Poor relief, 1854–56: The House of Assembly voted £2 10s. in each of the 1854 and 1855 sessions "for the relief of Thomas Hayter, of that place". The money was paid through Mr. John Nicolle of White Sands. The warrant paid on 12 May 1855 describes him as "Thomas Hayter, in destitute circumstances". A further warrant appears in the 1856 Journal, and the grant is printed in the 1855 Appropriation Act (18 Vic. cap. 36, p. 212). White Sands is where Thomas and Martha Elizabeth Hayter's children were born between 1840 and 1849. Thomas junior was then only 22–24 years old, so this destitute man is almost certainly the elder Thomas, probably disabled or aged by 1854. ¶9

Relief to his wife, 1863: The 1863 supply resolutions granted £1 to "Mrs Thomas Hayter" through Mr. James Bell of White Sands. In the same lists widows are called "Widow", so she was a wife, not a widow, and Thomas senior was evidently still living in 1863. Thomas junior had married Grace Moore only in December 1862 and farmed inland, so this entry is very probably Martha Elizabeth, wife of Thomas senior. ¶10

Land, 1863: The 1863 Lake map of Prince Edward Island marks a "T. Haytor" holding among the strip farms on the White Sands shore of Lot 64, between the McLeans and Nicholsons on one side and the McKenzies, Giddings and Machons on the other. A second "T. Haylor" holding is shown inland beside R. Moore. Read together with the poor-relief entries, the shore holding is very probably that of Thomas senior, and the inland one that of his son Thomas and Grace Moore. ¶11

Religion context: the family was Baptist from at least 1881. But the children's baptisms in 1842 and 1851 were infant baptisms, which Baptists do not perform, so they were made by a Methodist, Bible Christian, Presbyterian or Anglican minister. The Lot 64 records of those denominations form PARO Acc3058, the Murray Harbour/Murray River church record collection. If Thomas Hayter was a Dissenter in England, his son's 1831 baptism would be in a nonconformist register (TNA RG 4). The RG 4 registers are indexed only behind a login (FamilySearch, TheGenealogist), so they still need to be searched by a person. ¶7

1881 census: Lot 64, Kings County, page 42, dwelling 165, family 166: Thomas Hayter, male, 73, widowed, born England, Church of England, English origin, labourer, living alone (FamilySearch image 48 read; the index misreads him as 'Thomas Hunter'). So he was born about 1807-08 in England, was still alive in 1881 and a widower. Birth changed from BET 1795 AND 1812 to ABT 1808; death from unknown to AFT 1881. Thomas junior (49) is a separate household in the same lot. ¶21

Birthplace of Thomas junior (assessment): the Halifax baptism gives his age (nine months) but no birthplace. Every census (1881, 1891, 1901) says England, and the 1901 census gives immigration 1832. No Nova Scotia birth or earlier Hayter presence was found, so born in England about October-November 1831 and brought out in 1832 remains the reading of the evidence. ¶31

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 5

Why Probable and not Confirmed: no record yet names Thomas Hayter junior as their son. The fit is strong: the younger Thomas was born in England in 1831 and arrived in 1832; this couple was in the same lot, White Sands being a few miles from High Bank, by 1840; the son bears his father's name; and theirs is the only Hayter family recorded in rural PEI. A James Hayter, presumably their son born 1842, married in 1863, the year after Thomas junior. ¶2

Confidence: He remains rated Probable as the father of Thomas Hayter (born 1831). The 1854–63 relief entries and the 1863 map now show that a second, older Thomas Hayter with a wife lived at White Sands, Lot 64, from 1840 to at least 1863. That older Thomas is the father named in the baptisms of Isabella (1840), James (1842), Jane Eliza (1845) and Sarah Catharin White (1849). The 1901 census says Thomas junior was born in England in October 1831 and came to Canada in 1832, and no other Hayter family appears in rural PEI before 1862. A document naming father and son together has not yet been found. The candidates are a land conveyance, an estate file, the 1841 or 1861 census return for Lot 64, or a death notice. ¶12

Confidence (unchanged, Probable): the 1841 household fits Thomas senior and Martha Elizabeth with a son aged about 9. It has a man and a woman aged 16-45, two boys and two girls under 16, at least one English-born child, and a passage the family paid themselves. Thomas junior (born in England in October 1831; immigrated 1832 per the 1901 census) would be one of the two boys, and Isabella (born June 1840) one of the two girls. The census names only the head, however, so it neither names Thomas junior nor proves the father-son link. The link could be settled by a land conveyance or estate file naming both men, by Thomas junior's death record naming his parents, or by a sponsor on the 1851 baptism-register image FS_005240355_00135. ¶17

Grade (unchanged, Probable) on his link to Thomas Hayter junior: the 1881 census, the baptism card and the family history add dates and his wife's maiden name, but still no record names Thomas junior as his son. The 'the Younger' lease of 1856 is the nearest thing and has been seen only in the family history. ¶24

Upgrade: Probable to Confirmed, graded on his link to Thomas Hayter junior (see his page). The Halifax baptism of 4 August 1832 names 'Thomas Hayter and Martha Elizabeth Gibbs' as the parents of Thomas George, aged nine months. Continuity to Thomas junior of Lot 64: (1) the child's birth, about October-November 1831, matches Thomas junior's '[Oct] 27 [or 17], 1831' in the 1901 census; (2) the 1901 census says Thomas junior came to Canada in 1832, the year of this Halifax baptism, which evidently took place on the family's way to PEI (Halifax was the usual port of call); (3) the same couple, under the same unusual full names, 'Thomas Hayter' and 'Martha Elizabeth Gibbs', is named eight years later as the parents of Isabella Hayter, born 1840 at White Sands, Lot 64 (PEI Baptism Card Index, image), so the parents are found together at both ends of the gap (a pair match, audit 8 rule 3); (4) their 1841 Lot 64 household holds a boy of about the right age and at least one English-born child (image 526); (5) no other Hayter family is recorded in PEI in 1832-1881, so there is no named rival. The name used later is plain 'Thomas'; 'George' is not seen again in his own records, though his son was 'Thomas G.' Hayter (b. 1868), a pointer only. Coordinator: the child is identified across places (Halifax to PEI), which audit 4 assigns to the parent's grade; the exact birth month, the immigration year and the parental pair match are offered as the bridge. Veto to Probable if you read audit 4 more strictly. ¶26

Corrections 3

Correction: the 'Isabella Hayter b. 5 June 1840' baptism is real and not a misreading of a Hooper entry: the PEI baptism card shows Isabella, daughter of Thomas Hayter and Martha Elizabeth Gibbs, baptised 21 Feb 1842 at St John's Presbyterian Church, Belfast, by Rev. John McLennan (book 2, p. 133). The earlier negative note about the Murray Harbour Methodist register is unaffected. ¶23

Supersedes the earlier 'Why Probable and not Confirmed: no record yet names Thomas Hayter junior as their son' note and the 'Grade (unchanged, Probable)' notes on this record: the Halifax baptism is that record. ¶27

Correction: Audit 11 (2 Nov 2026): lowered from Confirmed to Probable, graded on the link to Thomas Hayter junior (see his page). The St Mary's (RC) baptism at Halifax, 4 Aug 1832 (entry 264, DGS 005190390 image 124; the auditor checked the saved crop: 'Thomas George aged nine Months lawful son of Thomas Haytar and Martha Elizabeth Gibbs'), names the parents of an infant Thomas George. The question is the step from that infant to Thomas Hayter of High Bank, Lot 64. That is an identification across places (Halifax, Nova Scotia, to Prince Edward Island), and audit 4 assigns it to the parents' grade. No record between 1832 and the son's 1862 marriage names him with these parents: the 1841 census lists only the head and an unnamed boy under 16, the 1862 marriage register has no parents' columns, and the 1856 lease to 'Thomas Hayter, the Younger' has been seen only in a family history. So the identity rests on (1) the birth month (about Oct-Nov 1831 against the 1901 census's October 1831), (2) the 1832 immigration year, (3) the parents found on PEI by 1840, and (4) the absence of another Hayter family on PEI. Points 1-2 are a name-and-age match, and point 4 is elimination. Both stay Probable (BRIEF grading rule; audit 3: 20 years or more with no record naming the child, and the far-end records name no parent). The pair match of audit 8 rule 3 needs the two people to be found TOGETHER at both ends; here the parents are paired at both ends, but the child is not named with them at the PEI end. The match is very strong, so this is the top of Probable. ¶36a

Alternatives considered and set aside 2

Candidates tested and ruled out: (1) every Thomas Hayter who married in England 1823-1833 - at Cann/Shaftesbury (Eliza, 1826; Charity, 1829), Smarden, Kent (Eleanor Ottaway, 1828), Ibsley, Hants (Mary Anne Plumbley, 1828), Burton Bradstock, Dorset (Mary, 1830), Millbrook, Hants (Alice Meaton, 1831), Bishops Cannings, Wilts (Sarah, 1832) and Repton, Derbys (Catharine Sowell, 1832). Each was either still baptising children in England after 1832 or married too late. (2) Thomas Hayter x Elizabeth White, St Martin-in-the-Fields, Westminster, 21 April 1829: her surname matched the middle name of the PEI daughter Sarah Catharin White Hayter (b. 1849). But this couple had children at Beaconsfield, Bucks, in 1830 and 1833 and at Colnbrook in 1836, and were living in Kensington in 1851 (Thomas b. c.1797 Coombe, Wilts; Elizabeth b. c.1796 Shepherd's Market, London). (3) Thomas Hayter and Martha of Tollard Royal, Wilts, who were still baptising children there 1841-1861. (4) The Thomas Hayter baptised at Warminster on 10 April 1832, who was the son of John and Sarah. ¶5

Rival check: a second Hayter family lived in the Maritimes in the same years. Thomas Hayter of the parish of Westfield, Kings County, New Brunswick, farmer, and Mary his wife made a deed in January 1840 (Kings NB Deeds 1839-1840, FamilySearch Full-Text, ark 3:1:S3HT-6QP3-F1C, transcript read). His wife's name differs from Martha Elizabeth (alive on PEI in 1840), so he is not this man. He matters only in that 'no other Hayter family' holds for PEI, not for the Maritimes. Nothing ties him to PEI or to a son born in 1831. ¶38

Searches that found nothing 4

He was not found in the 1864 Hutchinson's PEI Directory (White Sands, Murray River and Murray Harbour South lists), the PEI Death Card Index 1810-1913, or Find a Grave. No gravestone has been found for him or Martha Elizabeth. His death date is unknown. ¶8

Church registers (negative): FamilySearch has no Church of England register for Kings County, PEI, for the 1830s-1850s (Georgetown, Murray Harbour and Lot 64 missions). The Murray Harbour Methodist baptisms (DGS 005240355), which cover White Sands, have no Hayter from February 1834 to July 1841. The register then has a gap to 1867. An 1840 White Sands baptism of 'Isabella, dau. of John Hooper & Elizabeth Jenkins' may be the source of a reported 'Isabella Hayter, b. 5 June 1840, White Sands'. FamilySearch's record search also finds no English nonconformist (RG 4) birth of a Thomas Hayter about 1831 to Thomas and Martha. The Anglican registers held at the Public Archives of PEI are the remaining source. ¶19

English baptism of Thomas junior (negative, 2 Nov 2026): every Hayter baptism of 1824-1833 now transcribed in FreeREG for Wiltshire, Somerset, Dorset, Hampshire, Devon and Gloucestershire (31 entries) was read in detail. None is a Thomas, and none has a mother Martha. All England, FreeREG (fuzzy) and the christening index (Ancestry 9841) give only three Thomas Hayter/Hater baptisms of 1831-1832: Warminster 10 Apr 1832 (John and Sarah, already ruled out), Liverpool St Peter 26 Dec 1832 (George, a clothier, and Jane Langley, of St George's Crescent), and Norfolk 14 Dec 1831 (Francis and Elizabeth Hater). None fits. FreeREG's only Thomas Hayter-like marriages of 1818-1832 are those already ruled out (Smarden 1828, Repton 1832) and Thomas Hatter x Maria Page, Rye, Sussex, 29 Jun 1822 (bride Maria, not Martha; not pursued). This confirms the wave-6 negative: the baptism is either in an unindexed Anglican register, in a nonconformist register (RG 4, signed in), or under an unexpected spelling. ¶20

English marriage (negative): FreeREG, all counties, has no marriage of any Hayter groom to a Gibbs bride 1826-1832 (35 Hayter marriages read, incl. Thomas Hayter x Eleanor Ottaway, Smarden 1828, and x Catherine Sowell, Repton 1832, both already ruled out), and none of the eight Martha Gibbs brides of 1824-1831 married a Hayter (Maynard, Appleby, Townsend, Wainford, Loue, Atkins, Wright, and an illegible 'Benjamin *' at Leamington Priors 1825).
