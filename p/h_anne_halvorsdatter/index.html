Life story

Baptism (Probable identification): Cantate Sunday, 22 Apr 1742, Gjøvdal church — 'Item Halvor Olsön Omlands barn Anne'; godparents Vraald Giermundsön Omland, Tergie Gundersön fra S…tterødal(?), Tergie Gundersön Omland, Tergie Omland's wife, Signe Tergiersdatter Omland (Åmli ministerialbok 1741-1775, 1742 no. 13, canvas 3; year heading 'Anno 1742' verified). Fits her 1801 age (59) and her 1775 description 'Anne Halvorsdatter Omland i Gievedal'. Mother not named in the entry. ¶3

Death (bygdebok, Probable): Åmli ætt og heim IX, Ne(r)garden (canvas 667) gives Herjus's wife Anne, from Gjøvdal, b. 1742, m. 1776, d. 1821; children Asbor 1776 (m. 1813 Gunnar), a child 1776-1809, a child 1777-1785, Ole 1779 (m. 1813 Anne of Oland, Gangsei), Anne 1782, Halvor 1785-1829 (m. 1820 Ragnhild). ¶6

Of Omland (Ohland) in Gjøvdal when betrothed to Herjus Olsen on 27 Dec 1775; they married 24 Mar 1776. Her son later married Anne Nielsdatter of the same farm (Ohland). Her parents were not found (a Halvor at Omland, c. 1740s). ¶2

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 1

Upgrade: Probable to Confirmed (upgrade sweep D), as mother of Ole Herjussen Gangsei. Two records name the links. (1) The 1801 census of Åmli (index, relationship words read) lists at Neergaarden Hergius Olsen (57, huusbonde, 'begge i 1ste ægteskab', farmer and smith) and Anne Halvorsdatter (59, 'hans kone', 'begge i 1ste ægteskab'), with Asborg (25), Ole (22), Anne (19) and Halvor (16) Hergiusen/Hergiusdatter, each entered as 'Deres børn' (their children), 'tiener forældrene' (working for the parents). As both spouses were in their first marriage, these are children of both. (2) The probate after Halvor Herjussen of Gangsei, 4 Feb 1829 - 12 Mar 1830 (Østre Råbyggelag skifteprotokoll 23a, fol. 276b, card 3489), names 'Avd's bror: Ole Herjusen Gangsei'. So the tree's Ole Herjussen of Gangsei (married 1813, alive 1830) is the brother of Halvor and thus the 1801 son Ole of Hergius and Anne; Ole also named a son Herjus (1824). The rare patronymic Herjussen and the probate's sibling statement join the census family to the Gangsei brothers, not age or farm alone. ¶4

Records that disagree 2

Contradiction (bygdebok against the baptism; no link changed): Åmli ætt og heim II (Gjøvdal), Omland (canvas 190), lists 'Halvor Aslakson Eppeland', b. 1711, d. 1789, who married in 1734 'Asborg' (b. 1721, d. 1799), with children Aslak 1737 (d. 1738), Gunstein 1739 (unmarried, d. 1813), a child of 1740 (d. 1780) and 'Anne 1741, g. 1775 Herjus Olson Gangsei'. The Gjøvdal baptism of 22 Apr 1742 calls her father 'Halvor Olsön Omland'. Note that the 1762 extra-tax roll for Omland has 'Halvor og H: Asbor' with a son 'Asl[?]d', which fits the bygdebok's Halvor Aslaksen and his wife Asborg (and a son named after Halvor's father Aslak) better than a Halvor Olsen. Either the priest wrote the wrong patronymic in 1742, or the book has merged two Halvors of Omland. Until the 1734 marriage, the 1789 burial or a probate (Halvor d. 1789, Asborg d. 1799; Nedenes sorenskriveri) settles which, her father's link should be treated as in doubt (it is Probable in the tree). ¶5

Weighing the conflict over her father (no link changed; builds on note). For the bygdebok's Halvor Aslaksen: the 1762 roll for Omland has 'Halvor og H: Asbor' with a son 'Asl[?]', and Åmli ætt og heim V (Eppeland, canvas 201) names 'Halvor Aslaksen Eppeland/Åmland' in an odel case of 1760, so a Halvor Aslaksen from Eppeland was on Omland in 1760-62. For the priest's Halvor Olsen: the baptism of 22 Apr 1742 says 'Halvor Olsön Omlands barn Anne' and is the only contemporary record; a Halvor Olsen of Øy married Anne Olsdatter on 30 Jun 1739 and is godfather as 'Halvor Olsön Öy' in Jun-Jul 1741 (DA 9921 canvas 1), so a second Halvor Olsen of the right age lived nearby. I read the 1741 baptisms of DA 9921 (canvases 1-2, about Apr-Dec 1741, nos. 27-63) at moderate resolution: no child Anne of a Halvor Aslaksen (or of any Halvor of Omland) is there, so the book's 'Anne 1741' is probably this same 22 Apr 1742 baptism with the patronymic silently changed, or a guess. The contemporary record outranks the compiled book, but the priest may have slipped. ¶7a
