About the family

Oline Slind's great-grandfather's elder brother, and the son who kept the farm. Entered as 'Ole Jorgenssen, 18' among the children of Jorgen Olssen at Slinden in the 1801 census, and as 'Ole Jorgensen, 43, Gmd.' - gaardmand, farmer - in the 1825 census of the same farm, in his father's household, with his wife Elen Olsd. 39 and their children Ingebor 18, Jorgen 14, Helge 10 and Ole 4. ¶1

His son Jorn (Jorgen) Olsen, born 1811, held the bruk 1835-1880 and died in 1884, which agrees with the Selbu burial index's 'Jorgen Olsen, b. 1811 Slinden, avdod 24 Aug 1884'. ¶7

Life story

Mother left blank for the same reason as his sister Marit's - see her note. A son born about 1782 to a wife born about 1758 is possible but the 1801 census's statement that the marriage was the husband's second and the wife's first makes it more likely that the two eldest children were by an earlier wife. ¶3

Born 1783, not about 1782, and his mother was Jorn Olsen's FIRST wife Ingeborg Kjosnes d.y., not Ane Gundersdatter. Selbu i fortid og naatid vol. 7, p. 116, lists him last among the children of that first marriage - 'Ola, f. 1783,... over bruket' - and then as the next holder: '1815-1845 [Ola], f. 1783, d. 1848. G. 1807 m. [Elen], [fodt] paa STOR-LANGLI 1787, d. 1845.' His stated age of 18 in February 1801 and 43 in November 1825 both fit 1783 as well as 1782, and the book's flat year is preferred. ¶5

His line, not Gunder's, stayed on the home bruk: his son Jorgen Olsen, born 1811, is 'Husfader g Gaardbr. Leilaending' - head, married, tenant farmer - at Slinden in the 1865 census and still there in 1875. Oline Slind's own branch left for the neighbouring farm Aune in the 1820s and only came back to a different Slind bruk in 1871. ¶4

So he held the bruk for thirty years, 1815-1845, died in 1848, and his wife - the 'Elen Olsd., 39' of the 1825 census - was born at STOR-LANGLI in 1787 and died in 1845. Langli is one of the farms this research was told to look for and this is where it turns up: the senior Slind line married into it in 1807. ¶6

He appears in the Sovlvskatten of 1816 as 'Ole Jorgensen, Slinden, Sonnen' - the son - his father's assessment of 9 spesidaler 0 ort 84 skilling being recorded with the remark 'Felles med sonnen Ole', jointly with his son Ole. Father and son were taxed as one economic unit, which is what a Norwegian farm handover looked like in practice: the son did the work and took the risk years before the title moved. ¶2

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.
