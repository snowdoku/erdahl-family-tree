About the family

Probable father of Lars Marcussen Eitungjerde: Lars Marcussen of Eitungjerde (buried 1757/58 aged 67, so born about 1690-91) is the only Lars son of a Marcus at Eitungjerde in the 1701 census and in Brite Larsdatter's probate. Agreeing: farm, patronymic, and naming (Lars named for his maternal grandfather Lars; Lars's own eldest son was named Marcus). Small conflict: the 1701 index age 8 (born ~1693) against the burial age 67 (born ~1690-91); both ages are approximate. Probable rather than Confirmed because no record names Lars Marcussen's parents directly; Lars's confirmation or his 1758 probate heading would not settle it, but a Tjugum/Leikanger record of his marriage to Unni Hermundsdatter or an Eitungjerde farm succession record would. ¶3

Life story

1666 vicar's census, Leikanger hovedsogn, views 159, 161 and 163 (p. 292-293, 296-297, 300-301): the Niels holders listed (Niels 35 at N.2, Niels [Ifversen?] 30, Niels Lauritsen 40, Niels Brekke 70 with son Anders 30, Niels Ingemarsen 44 with son Erich 13) have no son Marcus. With v160 and v162, the whole Leikanger hovedsogn section is now negative for a Marcus son of Niels about 11. ¶5

1701 census: Marchus Nielsen, Bunde (farmer), age 46 (born about 1655), at Eitunsgierde (Eitungjerde), Leganger parish, page 42, with his son Lars Marchusen, age 8 (index birth year 1693). Only two males are listed in the household (the 1701 census lists males only). ¶1

Probate: Ytre Sogn skifteprotokoll 2a (1702-1707), fol. 187b-188a, heading 'Eitun Gierde'. On 6 May the sorenskriver (district judge) and two lagrettemenn (court jurors) met at Eitungjerde in Vestrand[?] skipreide to divide the estate of the late Brite Lars datter, 'imellem hendes efterlevende [Huus]mand Marchus Niels[øn] og deris tilsammen avlede Børn Lars, Gi[e]rtrud[?], Ragnhilde, [?] og Gyri, Marchus Søn og Døttre' (between her surviving husband Marchus Nielsen and their children together: Lars, Gjertrud[?], Ragnhild, [one unread name] and Gyri, Marchus's son and daughters). The year of the session is not on the lines read; the protocol covers 1702-1707. ¶2

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.
