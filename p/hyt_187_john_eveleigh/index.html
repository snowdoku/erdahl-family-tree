About the family

His parents: see John Eveleigh. The Oxford connection is strong: his mother Prudence (Barnes) Eveleigh was buried at St Giles, Oxford, in 1652, the Dean left money to the poor of St Giles, and Blackhall lies in St Giles parish. Brady's statement that he was 'of the family of Pierrepoint, Duke of Kingston' is not supported by anything found and is set aside. Added. ¶6

Father now Confirmed: his uncle Nicholas Evelegh of the Middle Temple left his Bovey Tracey grist mills and lands, after his widow's death, 'unto John Evelegh sonne of John Evelegh my brother late of Oxford deceased' (codicil of 3 Nov 1611 to the will proved 1618, TNA PROB 11/131/535, image read). That raises John Eveleigh to Confirmed as his father; see the continuity note there. The bequest also explains a Devon estate in the Dean's hands. His own grade (on his link to Anne Eveleigh) is unchanged. Nicholas's will also mentions a debt to 'Mrs Wadham' over woods (Dorothy Wadham, founder of Wadham College in 1610, where the Dean took his B.A. in 1618-19, is the obvious candidate, but the will does not say so). ¶12

Life story

Dean John Eveleigh, 'of or near Bandon in county Cork'. By his wife Mildred Coldwell he had five daughters only: Alice (wife of Richard Power of Carrigaline), Anne (wife of Dr Benjamin Cross), Rebecca (wife first of Henry Parr), Jane and Elizabeth (Blennerhassett Pedigree, in Hickson, Old Kerry Records, 1872, p. 53). ¶1

Career (Brady, Clerical and Parochial Records, vol. 2, pp. 95, 308, 427-428): John Eveleigh, M.A., Vicar of Knockmourne and Derryvillane, Cloyne, 1620-34; Fellow of the College of Youghal until 1631; Chancellor of Ross 1629-63; Rector of Kilnagross 1630-78; Vicar of Timoleague and Rector of Templeomalus 1634-63; Precentor of Cloyne 1661-64 (resigning in favour of Benjamin Cross, 16 Jun 1664); Dean of Ross 1661-78. He died at Bandon-bridge in 1678. Probably the John Eveleigh, B.A. of Wadham College, Oxford, 1618-19 (Foster, Alumni Oxonienses), which gives the approximate birth year. Added. ¶3

His will (abstract in Brady, from the Cork wills then held by John Hurley; the original was destroyed in 1922): dated 30 Nov 1678 in the presence of John Poole, Provost of Bandon, proved 28 Feb 1678[/9]. He left to 'Rev. B. Cross' the 'lands of Blackhall and Coplands-meade, nere Oxford', held by lease from St John's College, Oxford, and gave £5 to the poor of St Giles, Oxford, and 50s. to the poor of Bandon-bridge. Phillimore's index of Cork and Ross wills lists 'John, Dean of Ross, Bandon, 1678'. (The Heverly family history gives the will as dated 3 Nov and proved 25 Nov 1678; Brady's dates are preferred because he quotes the abstract.) Added. ¶4

Possible English baptisms of his children (not yet verified): the printed Ottery St Mary, Devon, register (Tapley-Soper, 1908-29), as quoted in the Heverly family history (footnote 59), has 'Rebecca, dau. of John and Mildred Eveleigh, bapt. Dec. 23, 1635'. It also has baptisms of John (18 Feb 1633/4), Richard (22 Jun 1642), Margaret and Prudence (30 Oct 1643) and Jane (29 Jul 1645), each son or daughter of John Eveleigh, and an Elizabeth daughter of John buried or baptised in 1633. Richard, Margaret, Prudence and Jane were all buried there in 1645. The Dean's wife was Mildred, two of his surviving daughters were Rebecca and Jane, and his mother was Prudence. If these are his children, his family was at Ottery St Mary, the Eveleigh of Holcombe parish, in 1635 and again in 1642-45, probably as refugees from the 1641 rising. Brady shows him holding Irish benefices throughout, but non-residence was common. Heverly assigns the entries to another John Eveleigh, a clergyman whose wife was Frances. ¶7a

Note only (not a grade change): Magdalen College leased its Canditch house in St Mary Magdalen parish, Oxford, successively to Robert Barnes, M.D. (1588), 'John Eveleighe, M.A., principal of Hart Hall' (1604), 'Prudence Eveleighe, widow' (1615, 1632, 1638), and on 10 Aug 1654 to 'John Eveleigh, of St Giles, Oxford, clerk' (Salter, Cartulary of the Hospital of St John the Baptist, vol. 2, pp. 314-315). The 1654 lessee is probably the Dean, then out of Ireland, succeeding his mother after her burial at St Giles in 1652. Prudence's will is TNA PROB 11/224/477 (1652) and should settle the link. Added. ¶8

Two PCC wills read on the image. (1) His mother's nuncupative will, PROB 11/224/477 (1652): 'her sonne John Eveleigh' is to have her lease of Blackhall farm (St Giles, Oxford); that confirms Prudence as his mother (see Prudence Barnes). (2) Will of his son-in-law Benjamin Crosse, PROB 11/378/255 (dated 27 Aug 1684, proved 2 Dec 1684): 'whereas his Grandfather John Eveleigh Deane of Rosse in the Kingdome of Ireland by his last will and Testament bequeathed unto the said John Crosse [the testator's eldest son] the summe of one hundred pounds'; and 'whereas the said John Eveleigh Deane of Rosse aforesaid did by his last will aforesaid bequeath unto my eldest daughter Tryphenah Crosse the summe of one hundred pounds'. The will also leaves the Black Hall lease (from St John's College) to 'my deare wife Anne Crosse' and sons Robert and William, makes 'my beloved wife Anne Crosse' sole executrix, and names Richard Power of Carrigaline, gent. (husband of the Dean's daughter Alice in the Blennerhassett Pedigree) among the trustees. ¶10

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 3

Probable, graded on his link to his daughter Anne Eveleigh: this rests on the pedigree, written by the husband of his granddaughter Elizabeth Cross. ¶2a

Grade unchanged (Probable, on his link to Anne Eveleigh). Brady independently calls Anne 'daughter and co-heir of John Eveleigh, Dean of Ross', and the will abstract shows the Dean's Oxford lands passing to her husband Benjamin Cross, but the abstract as printed does not call Cross his son-in-law or name Anne. ¶5a

Grade unchanged (Probable, graded on his link to his daughter Anne Eveleigh),. The Crosse will proves the Dean was the maternal grandfather of Benjamin's children John and Tryphena, so the mother of those children was the Dean's daughter. It does not itself say that Anne, the wife living in 1684, was that daughter (an earlier wife cannot be ruled out from the will alone). Brady and Venn (compiled) say Benjamin married 'Anne, daughter and co-heir of John Eveleigh, Dean of Ross'. ¶11a

Searches that found nothing 1

FamilySearch indexes (2 Nov 2026), Ottery St Mary: the IGI ('England, Births and Christenings') has 'Rebecca, daughter of John Eveleigh and Mildred', christened 23 Dec 1635 (1:1:NB8S-M88; no image). This is the only Ottery entry that names a mother, and it matches the printed register. The other Ottery baptisms 1626-1645 give only 'John Eveleigh' as father (Jane, 29 Aug 1645, is indexed with father 'Joan', probably a misreading of Johan). There was at least one other John Eveleigh householder at Ottery then: John Eveleigh married Elizabeth Saunder there on 1 Oct 1625 (England Marriages), and children of a John were baptised in 1626 (Marie, Margaret), 1628 (Cecilie) and 1630 (Marie, Roger); a Frances wife of John was buried there in 1683. So only Rebecca can be assigned to the Dean from the index. The Devon Record Office burial index has Richard, Margaret, Prudence and Jane, each 'son/daughter of John', buried at Ottery in 1645 (year only; e.g. Prudence 1:1:6T6F-63DH, Jane 1:1:6T6F-63DN); the images are with the partner site and could not be read on FamilySearch. If those four were the Dean's, the Jane of the Blennerhassett pedigree must be another daughter, since this Jane died in 1645. ¶9a
