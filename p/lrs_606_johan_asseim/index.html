About the family

Son Ole (Probable): the first-named heir Ole is taken to be Ole Johansen of Asseim (see his page), who had a son Johannes born about 1732-33 (buried 1741 aged 8) and another Johannes baptised in 1742. Reasons: same farm; the patronymic Johansen matches a father Johan; he is the eldest-listed son, and an adult 'Ole Johanss.' was already owed servant's wages at Asheim in 1726 (3a fol. 200b); and the naming of Ole's sons Johannes fits a father Johan. The only other Ole Johansen known at Asheim, the son of Johannes Nielsen Ese (b. about 1724), was about 9 in 1733 and too young to be the father of a boy born about 1733, and his father had died in 1726, not 1733. No record yet names Ole Johansen's father outright, so the link is Probable, not Confirmed. ¶2

Wife: the widow Maria Jo[h]sdatter is not linked as Ole's mother, since the table does not say whether all the heirs were her children. ¶3

Life story

Probate: in the yearly table of Ytre Sogn probates sent to the Stiftamtmann for 1733 (Stiftamtmannen i Bergen, skiftedesignasjoner nr. 10, Digitalarkivet 24159, view 20, right page, Lavik skipreide), the second of two estates on 'Assem' dated 'dito' (the 30th, in a run of entries dated 26 Oct to 1 Dec 1733) is after 'Jo[h]n' of Assem (the forename is a J, o, a looped h and n: most probably Johan, possibly Jon). Heirs ('De efterlevende Arvingers Nafne'): the widow 'Maria Jo[h]sd.' (Maria Johansdatter or Jonsdatter), 29 Rd 2 Mk 13 ß; then the sons Ole, Johannes, Hermund and Endre, bracketed together at 6 Rd 3 Mk 4 1/3 ß each, and last 'Sygre' (Sigri), 3 Rd 1 Mk 10 1/3 ß, a daughter's half share. The four sons' and the daughter's shares together (about 29 Rd 2 Mk 12 ß) equal the widow's half, so the list of heirs is complete. The guardian column reads 'da Creditorene...' (the creditors...), not fully read. The table lists every heir: the other entries on the page include daughters and widows. Death before December 1733 is inferred from the date of the probate. ¶1

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.
