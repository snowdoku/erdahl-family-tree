About the family

David Erdahl's maternal great-great-grandmother. Her maiden name is the key that unlocked this generation: the California Death Index 1940-1997 records the mother's maiden name of her son Palmer Gilbert Eidem as SLIND. ¶1

Children: she bore at least seven children in Mehurin Township between 1902 and 1914, and the 'number of child of this mother' field on four of their certificates numbers them without a gap - Clara Pauline 1902 (1), Palmer Gilbert 1904 (2), Olga J. 1906 (3), Cora Belinde 1907 (4), Peder Conrad 1909 (5), May Chrisine 1911 (6), John Alvin 1914 (7). Every one of those certificates records all of her children as then living; she lost none in infancy. Her stated ages run 21, 23, 25, 26, 28, 30 and 32, consistent with a birth in the winter of 1880-81 and so with the 10 February 1881 on her gravestone. ¶22

Her brother Ole Gundersen Slind, born 24 January 1884, followed her to the same village two years later, expedited from Trondheim on 30 April 1902 for 'Marietta Minn' on the steamer Tasso, also on a ticket paid for in America. He is the 'Slind, Ole O., 66, born Norway' of household 4 on the 1950 census sheet, three doors from her own household 8. ¶33

Children, from the obituary, which names eight and no more: Mrs. Julius Jornlin (Clara) of Thief River Falls; Mrs. Peder Lerseth (Olga) of Hendricks, Minnesota; Mrs. Elmer Peterson (Cora) of Tracy, Minnesota; Mrs. Cle Kyllo (Barbara) of LaCrosse, Washington; Mrs. Herbert Gieseke (May); and Palmer, Peder and John Jr., all of the Marietta vicinity. She had twenty grandchildren living in 1955. ¶42

Siblings: 'She is also survived by two brothers and two sisters; Ole Slind of Colfax, Washington, Gunder, Marit and Karen Slind of Selbu, Norway.' So the Slind sibling group is Ole (emigrated, in Whitman County, Washington by 1955), Gunder, Marit and Karen (all still in Selbu in 1955), and Oline. This is the handle for finding the family in the Selbu parish registers. ¶43

Life story

Find a Grave's listing for Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, Deuel County, South Dakota records an 'Oline G. Slind Eidem 1881-1955', which matches her census age and carries her maiden name in the middle of her married name, as Norwegian-American women's stones often do. The discrepancy between the census middle initial S. and the gravestone's G. is noted and left unresolved rather than explained away. ¶3

Context: Slind, like Eidem, is a farm name in Selbu, Sor-Trondelag. Two Selbu farm families emigrating together, settling side by side in a Minnesota township and burying their dead side by side in a South Dakota churchyard, is the characteristic pattern of Norwegian chain migration, and it is why her great-granddaughters were given the names Inga, Sigrid and Brigetta. ¶4

Born in Norway 10 February 1881; the 1950 census gives her age as 69 and her birthplace as NORWAY. Slind is a farm name in Selbu, Sør-Trøndelag; her brother-in-law Peder P. Eidem had married another Slind, Kari, in 1888. ¶5

Her maiden name is preserved in the Minnesota death index, which records each of her sons as a 'child of SLIND (mother)', and on the California death record of her eldest son Palmer, which gives his mother's maiden name as SLIND. ¶6

Arrival, Confirmed: 'Olive Slind', female, aged 19, birth year 1881, single, nationality Norway (Norwegian), residence written 'Lilburn' (a clerk's mangling of Selbu), arrived ELLIS ISLAND on 13 AUGUST 1900 on the White Star liner CYMRIC out of LIVERPOOL, manifest line 15, film 143 frame 148. Nineteen days from the Trondheim police desk to the New York landing stage. The Trondheim ledger's line, 'Hvide Stjerne', is White Star, and the Cymric was White Star's Liverpool-Queenstown-New York ship: the departure record and the arrival record agree on the company as well as on the age, sex, birth year, marital status and nationality. The route was Trondheim to Hull on the little Wilson Line feeder Salmo, rail across England to Liverpool, and the Cymric to New York. ¶13

Baptism: 18 April 1881 in Selbu church, entered at page 50 serial 21 of the main register (ministerialbok) for Selbu local parish 1878-1891, with the remark 'Kirkeg s d' - kirkegang samme dag, the mother's churching took place the same day. Father given as Gunder Olsen, gaardmand (farmer), born 1844, of Slinden; mother as Kari Olsdr, born 1847, of Slinden. The parish clerk's duplicate register (1869-1888, page 89 serial 35) has the child as 'Olline', the mother as 'Katri Olsdatter' and the farm miswritten 'Stind'. The godparents are on the scanned page but were not captured by the transcription project, so they remain unread. ¶27

Birthplace, PROVED: 'Oline G. Slind was born in Selbu, Trondhjem, Norway, February 10, 1881. She was baptized and confirmed in the Selbu Lutheran church.' That is her own obituary, headed 'Antelope Valley Twp. Pioneer Buried Tuesday', scanned and uploaded to her Find a Grave memorial in 2026. It is the first retrieved document that names her home parish, and it converts the project's Selbu chain-migration hypothesis into a stated fact for the Slind half of the line. ¶34

Printed source, her married family: her given name Oline, her farm name Slind, her birth year 1881, the year of her marriage 1901, and her eldest son's name and birth year (Palmer Gilbert, twice, and 1904) all occur on page 207 of Selbu i fortid og naatid, bind 4 (1992), in the chapter BRENNHAUGEN for her husband's family's cottage under Eidem, along with Clara and Olga - the first two daughters named in her own 1955 obituary - and Amerika and Minnesota. ¶49

Printed source, her natal family: page 100 of Selbu i fortid og naatid, bind 7 (1992), in the SLINDAN NORDRE chapter (heading on p. 92), carries her entire family of origin in one place - the given names Gunder (twice), Kari, Oline, Marit (twice), Karen, Berit, the patronymic Gundersen and the farm name Slind, and the twelve years 1844, 1847, 1872, 1875, 1878, 1881, 1884, 1887, 1890, 1900, 1902 and 1922, with Eidem, Amerika and Minnesota. Those are, exactly, her father's birth year, her mother's, and the birth years of all seven of her siblings and herself, plus her own emigration year (1900) and her brother Ole's two emigrations (1902 and 1922). Printed p. 100 is image 102 in nb.no's viewer: volume 7's image index runs two ahead of the printed folio. ¶50

Godparents (faddere) at her baptism of 18 April 1881 (Selbu ministerialbok 1878-1891, p. 50, girls no. 21): 'Gmd. Ingebrigt Olsen Haarstad. Gmd. Renald Gundersen Grøtte. Ungk. Ole Johnsen Stokke. K. Johanna Olsd. Mogaard. Pige Marit Johnsd. Stokke. Pige Ingeborg Jensd. Overvig' - the farmers Ingebrigt Olsen of Haarstad and Renald (Reinald) Gundersen of Grøtte; the bachelor (ungkarl) Ole Johnsen of Stokke; the wife (kone) Johanna Olsdatter of Mogaard; and the unmarried girls (piger) Marit Johnsdatter of Stokke and Ingeborg Jensdatter of Overvig. The same entry gives her father as Gmd. (gaardmand, farm occupier) Gunder Olsen, born 1844, and her mother as K. (kone, wife) Kari Olsdatter, born 1847, of Slinden; the child legitimate (ægte). ¶52

Godparents, kinship (inference, not stated in the register): the bachelor Ole Johnsen Stokke cannot be her maternal grandfather Ole Johnsen Stokke (b. 1809), who had married in 1831 and been a widower since 1871, and would not be written ungkarl. The 1865 census of Stokke has, in that grandfather's household, his married son John Olsen (24) with two children, Ole (1) and Marit (3). Those two children, aged about 17 and 19 in 1881, fit 'Ungk. Ole Johnsen Stokke' and 'Pige Marit Johnsdatter Stokke' exactly - probably Oline's first cousins, children of her mother's brother John. 'K. Johanna Olsdatter Mogaard' is probably her mother's younger sister Johanna (13 in the 1865 household, so about 29 in 1881 and married by then). 'Renald Gundersen' is probably her father's uncle Reinald Gundersen, born 1822, a son of Gunder Jorgensen and Beret Olsdatter of Slinden; Reinald is a rare name in Selbu, but his residence at Grøtte in 1881 is not yet documented. Ingebrigt Olsen Haarstad and Ingeborg Jensdatter Overvig are not placed. To settle: Johanna's marriage to a Mogaard man and Reinald Gundersen in the 1875 census. ¶53

1891 census, Selbu: person 006 in the household of her father Gunder Olsen, census district 013 Ostre Vigvarvet, rural residence 061 (lopenr 184), family position 'd' (daughter), born 1881 in Selbo. The household held her parents, five brothers and sisters, a parish pauper and two visitors. ¶31

The farm she came from: Slinden, later written Slindan and then Slind, 'nordre' - the northern holding - gnr 141 bnr 2 in Selbu, on the Slind cluster of farms by Slindvatnet. Her father held it as a leilending, a tenant, never as an owner; in 1920 her widowed mother is still recorded as working a bygslet (leased) farm. In 1900 the holding returned grain or potatoes, livestock, poultry and fruit trees but no beehives or kitchen garden, and supported seven people. ¶32

Emigration, Confirmed: Trondheim police office, First accession, emigrant protocol no. 10 (1895-1902), page 191, group of travellers 01392, sequence number 2504, line 3 - 'Oline Gundersen Slind', female, year of birth 1881 (calculated), age 19, marital status ug (unmarried), occupation Arb (arbeider, labourer), residence Selbo. Contract date and expedition date both 25 July 1900. Ship Salmo, line 'Hvide Stjerne' (the White Star Line). Payment 'Bill. bet. i Amerika' - the ticket was bought and paid for in America. Stated destination: MARIETTA, MINN. ¶10

Emigration, the kinship the record proves: the three lines immediately below Oline's in the same protocol page are her travelling companions, all for the same destination - line 4, Peder Paulsen Eidem, 41, married, residence AMERICA; line 5, Kari Paulsen Eidem, 36, married, residence AMERICA; line 6, Ole Johnsen Slind, 20, unmarried, of Selbo. Peder Paulsen Eidem is the elder brother of John P. Eidem, the Lac qui Parle homesteader of 1882; his wife Kari is Kari 'Carrie' Slind, whom he married in 1888. So Oline crossed at nineteen in the company of the Selbu-born American farmer and his Selbu-born Slind wife, who were returning from a visit home, on a ticket somebody in Minnesota had already paid for. She married the farmer's younger brother within four years. This is chain migration documented line by line. ¶11

Emigration, why the middle initial settles it: her gravestone at Zoar Lutheran reads 'Oline G. Slind Eidem'. The emigrant protocol writes her as Oline GUNDERSEN Slind - the G. is the patronymic Gundersdatter/Gundersen, from her father Gunder Olsen, tenant farmer at Slind in Selbu. The 1950 census's 'Oline S.' uses the other byname, S. for Slind. Both initials are correct; they are initials of different things. ¶12

Emigration: expedited at the Trondheim police office on 25 July 1900, aged 19, unmarried, occupation 'Arb' (arbeider, labourer), residence Selbo, on the steamer Salmo of the Hvide Stjerne (White Star) line, group 01392, page 191, line 3, sequence number 2504. Her ticket was paid for in America ('Bill. bet. i Amerika'), that is, sent home to her by someone already there. Her stated destination was MARIETTA, MINNESOTA - a village in Lac qui Parle County, the same county in which the 1950 census found her fifty years later. She travelled alone, in a mixed Trondelag party bound for Minnesota, South Dakota and Illinois. ¶28

Emigration: 'At the age of 19 she came to America', so in or about 1900, a year before her marriage. She did not come as a child and did not come with the Eidems. ¶35

Marriage: she married John Eidem in Lac qui Parle County on 25 September 1901, as 'SLIND, OLIVE' - county marriage certificate C01528, read in the Minnesota Official Marriage System index. ¶20

Name as the records give it: she is 'Lena' on four of her children's registrations (1902, 1904, 1911) and on the 1905 state census, and 'Oline' on three (1906, 1907, 1909, 1914). Lena is the ordinary American shortening of Oline. Her maiden name is written out in full as 'Oline Slind' on the 1907 and 1914 certificates and as 'Lena Slind' on the 1911 one; the 1904 card's 'Lena Lind' is the same name with the S misread, and on the 1909 certificate her husband, filling the form in himself, wrote her married name 'Oline Eidem' in the maiden-name box. ¶19

1905 Minnesota state census, 8 June 1905, Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, sheet mnsc_131 page 632, line 5: 'Lena Eidem', 25, born Norway, both parents born Norway, wife of John P. Eidem, with Clara 3 and Palmer 1. Lena is the American shortening of Oline, the same form that appears on Palmer's 1904 Minnesota birth card ('Lena Lind'). Since Oline landed on 13 August 1900 and the eldest child Clara was born in 1902, the marriage of John P. Eidem and Oline Slind falls between late 1900 and 1902. ¶14

Immigration: the 1905 Minnesota state census records her as having been a resident of the state for 4 years and 9 months as of 1 June 1905 - that is, she arrived in Minnesota about September 1900, a year before her marriage and fourteen years after her husband. She came as a grown woman of nineteen, alone or with kin, not as a child with a family. That single figure dates her emigration from Norway to 1900 and is the best lead yet for finding her in a Norwegian emigrant list. ¶21

1905 state census: Mehurin Township, enumeration district 1, sheet 4, page 243, line 5 - 'Eidem Lena, F, 25, W, born Norway, father born Norway, mother born Norway', occupation entered as Housewife and then struck out. ¶23

1905 Minnesota state census: Mehurin township, ED 1, sheet 4, line 5 - 'Eidem Lena, F, 25, W, born Norway, father born Norway, mother born Norway, 4 years 9 months a resident of this State, 4 years 9 months a resident of this enumeration district', occupation written 'Housewife' and then struck through. Counting back from 1 June 1905, she reached Minnesota about SEPTEMBER 1900 - she went straight to Mehurin and never lived anywhere else in the state. Her own 1955 obituary says she came to America at the age of nineteen; she turned nineteen in February 1900, so the two agree, and the census is the sharper of the two. September 1900 is the date to hunt in the Trondhjem emigrant lists. ¶45

Census name forms, in order: 'Lena' (1905), 'Olive' (1910), 'Olena' (1920), 'Olive G.' (1930), 'Olena G.' (1940), 'Oline S.' (1950). The 1950 enumerator used her maiden surname Slind as a middle initial. ¶46

Her occupation is given as 'Housekeeper' in 1909 and 'Housewife' in 1911 and 1914, and her post office throughout was Marietta. ¶25

1940 Alien Registration — a useful absence. The Alien Registration Act of 1940 required every non-citizen resident over 14 to register, and NARA's Flexoline index of those registrations is searchable free. Oline's own brother Ole G. Slind's household appears in it (see mat_fed_ole_g_slind) but there is NO Oline Eidem and no Oline Slind of her birth year anywhere in it. She had therefore been naturalised by 1940 — in practice almost certainly derivatively, through her husband's naturalisation, which was how married women acquired citizenship before the Cable Act of 1922 and by petition after it. That makes her husband's naturalisation file the record to find, and it would name her too. ¶18

1950 census: 'Oline S., wife, W F 69, married, born Norway', in John P. Eidem's household, household 8, Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota, enumeration district 37-25, sheet 2. Her neighbours three doors away in household 4 are 'Slind, Ole O., 66, born Norway' and his wife Ingeborg, 65, also born in Norway - almost certainly her own kin, farming alongside her. ¶2

1950 census: keeping house for her husband and her 40-year-old son Peter C. in Mehurin Township, Lac qui Parle County, Minnesota. ¶7

1950 census, read from the page image: ED 37-25, Mehurin Township, sheet 2, line 21 - Oline S. Eidem, wife, W F 69, married, born Norway. Column 14, 'If foreign born, is he naturalized?', is left blank for her and for her husband, while the same enumerator answered 'yes' for the Slind couple on lines 9 and 10 of the same sheet. No naturalisation record has been found for her; Minnesota county naturalisations are at the Minnesota Historical Society on microfilm with no free online index. ¶15

Named as mother, with her maiden surname and middle initial, on three signed Social Security applications made by two of her children: 'MOTHER'S FIRST NAME: OLINE / MOTHER'S MIDDLE NAME: G / MOTHER'S LAST NAME: SLIND'. The three are John Alvin Eidem (SS-5, December 1954), Barbara Ann Eidem (SS-5, July 1946) and the same Barbara under her married name Kyllo (replacement card, August 1955). NARA AAD, NUMIDENT Application (SS-5) Files 1936-2007, Last Names E-G and K-L. ¶16

1950 federal census: Mehurin Twp, ED 37-25, sheet 1, line 21 - wife, white female, 69, married, born Norway, keeping house. Naturalisation column blank. ¶47

There is no Slind household anywhere in Lac qui Parle County in any Minnesota census from 1849 to 1905 - I searched the surname as a regular expression across the whole MNHS series. Oline arrived alone into a colony of her husband's kin, not of her own; the nearest names are the unrelated Slinde family of Hamlin township (1895) and a single Ole Slinde, 20, of Augusta township (1905). ¶48

Died 6 October 1955 in Lac qui Parle County, aged 74. ¶8

Congregation and burial: she died 6 October 1955 and is buried at Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, Antelope Valley Township, Deuel County, South Dakota - the two-acre burial ground of Zoar Evangelical Lutheran congregation (Zoar evangelisk lutherske menighet, also recorded as Soar frimenighet), a Hauge Synod congregation organised in 1896 about 3.6 miles west of her home in Mehurin Township, Minnesota, across the state line. Zoar's ministerial register, 1895-1957, in Norwegian, survives on microfilm and so covers her whole married life and her burial: Minnesota Historical Society, M678, reel 44 [ALC 129]. The register of a congregation of this kind records burials (begravede) with the deceased's age and sometimes the home parish in Norway, and a member who transferred in brought an attest, a certificate of removal from the parish she had left, which the pastor copied into the innflyttede or in-moved register. ¶9

Death: 6 October 1955, Lac qui Parle County; Minnesota death certificate index 1955-MN-007760. Her death falls just inside the window (1955-2001) in which that index carries the mother's maiden name, but the field reads 'NOT KNOWN' - the informant could not name her mother. That is a documented dead end rather than an unsearched one. ¶24

Death: 'Mrs. Eidem passed away shortly before midnight on October 6, 1955 at the age of 74 years, 7 months and 26 days.' That interval computes exactly from 10 February 1881, so the obituary confirms both of her dates against each other. ¶38

Burial: Zoar Lutheran Cemetery, Antelope Valley Township, Deuel County, South Dakota, lot 1 (South Dakota State Historical Society burial register, apps.sd.gov/dt58cemetery, 'OLINE G EIDEM 10-06-1955, lot 1, block 0, ANTELOPE VALLEY, DEUEL, ZOAR LUTHERAN'). Her husband John P. lies in lot 1A, not in the same numbered lot. ¶39

Church: she 'was an active member of the Zoar Lutheran church and was the last surviving charter member of the Zoar ladies aid, of which she was also a life member.' She was therefore present at the founding of the congregation's women's society, which places that founding at or soon after her arrival in about 1900. ¶40

Funeral: held Tuesday 11 October 1955, first at the Eidem home near Marietta at 1:30 p.m. and then at Zoar Lutheran church at 2 p.m., Rev. M. Mandsager officiating. Mrs. Charles Woolery was pianist; Mrs. Conrad Thompson sang 'Behold a Host Arrayed in White'; her son-in-law Rev. Peder Lerseth of Hendricks, Minnesota sang the Norwegian hymn 'Jeg ved mig en sovn i Jesu Navn' and spoke for the family. Pallbearers: Elias Slind, Ludwig Lund, Russell Peterson, Conrad Thompson, Ben J. Engebretson and Harold Mebust. Interment in the Zoar cemetery. ¶41

Clean negative on a second printing of her 1955 obituary. The Norwegian-American paper that would have carried it, DECORAH-POSTEN, is NOT digitised past the early 1920s - see the note under research methods: the Chronicling America title string 'decorah-posten og ved arnen (decorah, iowa) 1903-1972' is the PUBLICATION range, not the digitised range, and every Decorah-Posten page that answers any Eidem or Slind search is dated 1912, 1913 or 1920. A Chronicling America search for 'Oline' restricted to Iowa in 1955-1956 returns zero items, and 'Eidem' restricted to 1954-1957 returns fourteen pages nationally, none of them in Decorah-Posten. The case for this identification 2

Identified in the Norwegian records: she is OLINE GUNDERSDATTER SLIND, baptised in Selbu, Sor-Trondelag, on 18 April 1881, born 10 February 1881, legitimate daughter of the tenant farmer Gunder Olsen and Kari Olsdatter of Slinden. The Selbu parish register gives the day of her birth as 10 February 1881, which is independently identical to the birth date recorded on the Social Security applications of her children John Alvin and Barbara Ann Eidem. A Norwegian church book and a United States federal agency, with no possible contact between them, name the same day. ¶26

Why she is missing from the 1900 Norwegian census: she sailed on 25 July 1900 and the census was taken on 3 December 1900. She had been gone four and a half months. A search of the 1900 census for an Oline born about 1881 on the Slind farms returns nothing, and that negative means only that she had already left. ¶29

Records that disagree 1

Marriage: 'on September 25, 1901 she was united in marriage to John P. Eidem and they settled on a farm in Mehurin township, Lac qui Parle county, where she lived all her life.' Find a Grave's family panel gives only the year; the obituary gives the day. ¶37

Corrections 2

Correction: the middle initial is G, not S, and the reason is now documented. 'Oline G.' on the Zoar Lutheran gravestone, and on her children's Social Security applications, is her Norwegian patronymic - she was the daughter of GUNDER Olsen, and the Trondheim emigration clerk wrote her name out in full as 'Oline Gundersen Slind', patronymic first and farm name second. 'Oline S.' in the 1950 census is the enumerator using her farm and maiden name Slind as an American middle initial. The two initials are initials of two different names, both of them hers, and the older and more informative is G, because it names her father. ¶30

Correction: her middle initial is G., not S. The gravestone at Zoar reads 'OLINE G. 1881 - 1955' beside 'JOHN P. 1876 - 1963'; the South Dakota state burial register writes her OLINE G EIDEM; and her obituary calls her Oline G. Slind. The 1950 census's 'Oline S.' is the enumerator putting her surname initial in the middle-name column. ¶36

Searches that found nothing 1

Clean negative with a structural reason: Oline is in none of the three NUMIDENT files. She died 6 Oct 1955, which is before the Social Security Death Master File has any usable coverage, and as a farm wife she is unlikely ever to have applied for a number of her own. There is therefore no federal record that names HER parents. ¶17
