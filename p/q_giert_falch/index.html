Life story

A probate for 'Kari Olsdtr, Falch Balholmen' (1772-1776) is probably his first wife (speculative). His father was presumably a Niels Falch (the Falch family of Bergen?); not researched. ¶2

Probate: Ytre Sogn skifteprotokoll A 12, fol. 510b-511a, No. 11, read from the scan. Probate (skifte) after 'forrige Giestgiver Giert Nielsön Falck' (the former innkeeper) at the gjestgiversted Balholmen in Kvamsøy skipreide (court sub-district), registered 9 October 1805 by sorenskriver (district judge) Hesselberg; the proclama (public notice to creditors) was dated 23 July 1805, so he died before then. Heirs: widow Citrone Samuelsdatter (lauverge, i.e. legal guardian of a woman: Christen Christensen Fiærestad the elder) and the 7 children they had together in lawful marriage: Niels 20 (in Bergen), Giert 12 1/2 (in Bergen), Samuel 10, Kari (m. Albrigt Jørgensen, cottager under the farm Arvelund[?]), Citrone (m. Christen Christensen Fiærestad the younger), Marie (m. innkeeper John Biørnsen of Balholmen) and Guri 16. Gross estate 109 rd 3 ort 11 s. ¶3

Probate of his first wife: the April 1775 probate after Kari Olsdatter at Balholmen (Ytre Sogn skifteprotokoll A 9a, p. 520, No. 15) names him as widower and 'priviligeret Giestgiver' (licensed innkeeper) at Balholmen. Her heirs were her mother and siblings, so there were no children of this first marriage. The heading says nothing about his own parents or origin; his father 'Niels' still rests only on his patronymic. ¶5

Innkeeper (giestgiver) at the Balholmen trading post. 1801 census: 68, married twice, wife Zithone Samuelsdatter 48, children Mari 23, Guri 12, Giert 8, Samuel 6, Jonas 3. Burial 1805 ('Gerdt Falch', b. 1733) and probate 1800-1805 ('Gjert Nilsen Falck, Balholmen'). ¶1

Probate 1805: no child of an earlier marriage is named among the heirs, and the parts read (preamble, heirs, inventory summary, first debt, division) say nothing of his own parents or origin. His 1801 'married twice' and the Kari Olsdatter Falch probate of 1772-1776 therefore remain the only hints of a first marriage, and his father 'Niels' rests only on his patronymic. Two sons were in Bergen in 1805, which fits but does not prove a Bergen connection. ¶4
