About the family

Family: Hunt thinks he had two wives, the older children (Thomas, John, Jenet, Agnes) by the first and Cuthbert, Francis, Richard, Marie and Margaret perhaps by a second, possibly the mother of John and William Parke (who called him father-in-law). Neither wife is named in any record read, so no wife is entered. Other children per Hunt: Thomas Smythe of Stainforth (will 24 Mar 1568, proved 22 Jul 1569, York P&E 18:157, naming brothers John, Richard and Francis); John Smythe, alderman and three times mayor of Hull (1563, 1572, 1583), will 1592; Francis Smythe, vicar of Crowle, Lincs; Richard; Cuthbert of Fishlake (d. 1551); Jenet (m. John Grene of Barnby, then Nicholas Cressy; will 1575, York P&E 19:840, leaving an ewe to 'Dorothy Simkinson, her niece'); Agnes (m. John Parke of Stainforth, will 1582); Margaret (m. Thomas Saile of Smeaton). ¶4

His parents (negative). No Smythe or Smyth of Stainforth, Hatfield, Thorne or Fishlake appears in the York registry indexes for 1389-1514 (YAS RS 6) or 1514-1553 (RS 11), apart from his son Cuthbert of Fishlake (1551). The Stainforth and Hatfield testators of 1514-1553 are Butler, Dowrynge, Hyde, Laurenson, Ranolde, Sheffeld and others. So his father's will is not in the York registers. Grade unchanged (Probable, graded on his link to his daughter Mary Smythe). ¶11a

Life story

Will: 'William Smythe, elder, of Stanforth in parish of Hatfield', will dated 25 Jan 1558/9, proved 2 May 1560 (York Prerogative & Exchequer Court, register 16, fo. 46), as abstracted with verbatim quotation by J. G. Hunt, NEHGR 124 (1970): 252 n.4. He asked to be buried in the church of Our Lady of Hatfield; left money to the poor of Hatfield, Woodhouse, Stainforth, Thorne, Fishlake, Sykehouse, Barnby, Bramwith, Armthorpe, Kirk Sandall and Stristhorpe; legacies to 'my son Francis Smythe vicar of Crowle', 'my sons Thomas and Richard', 'my son John Smythe of Hull', 'my brother in lawe Symkinson', and a ewe and lamb to every child of his sons and daughters; and the residue to 'John Grene and Jenet his wife; John Parke and Agnes his wife; John Symkinson and Marie his wife; and Thomas Saile and Margaret his wife whom I make executors'. ¶1

Folio query resolved. YAS Record Series 14, p. 158 (page image read) indexes 'Symkinson, Thomas, Doncastre, alderman', will 29 Jan 1558/9, proved 2 May 1560, at register vol. 16 fol. 46. That is the same folio and the same probate day as William Smythe's will (vol. 16 fol. 46, proved 2 May 1560). The two wills were registered together, so Hunt's two citations of 16:46 are both correct and are not a slip. The earlier caution note on this is superseded. The joint probate of the two men's wills on one day fits Hunt's view that the families were allied by marriage (William's 'brother in lawe Symkinson'). The same page also gives 'Symkenson, Margaret, Doncaster, widow', will 15 Dec 1562, proved 8 Nov 1564, vol. 17 fol. 397. ¶10

A sixth record (Hunt, NEHGR 124: 255): the will of John Grene, gent., of Barnby-upon-Don (husband of William Smythe's daughter Jenet), dated 26 Sep 1559, proved 21 Dec 1562 (York P&E 17:138), leaves 'to my brother in law Thomas Smythe my damask jacket... to John Parke my brother in law... to my sister in law wife to John Symkinson one gold ring which I weare of my little finger... to the same John Symkinson my velvet jerkyn... to my brother in law Thomas Saile...'. So Mary, wife of John Simkinson, was sister-in-law of John Grene and sister of Thomas Smythe and of the wives of Parke and Saile, i.e. one of William Smythe's daughters. ¶5

Wives, lead only: Abraham de la Pryme (1672-1704) recorded an inscription in Hatfield church, 'Orate pro animabus Willielmi Smith et Catherinae et Agnetis uxorum ejus' (pray for the souls of William Smith and Catherine and Agnes his wives), cited by Hunt from Hunter, South Yorkshire (1828-31) 1:190; Hunter 1:150 also lists William Smith and John Parke together among the 25 regarders of Hatfield Chase. If the inscription is this William's, one of Catherine or Agnes was Mary's mother, but which is not known, so no mother is entered. ¶6

Simkinson link, lead only: his 1559 will leaves a legacy to 'my brother in lawe Symkinson', and Thomas Simkinson, alderman of Doncaster (will 29 Jan 1558/9, proved 2 May 1560, cited by Hunt as York P&E vol. 16 f. 46), left money to Thomas Smythe, John Smythe of Hull and William Smythe (witness) and made young Thomas and Dorothy, children of John Simkinson, contingent heirs. ¶7a

How sure are we?

Probable. The records fit well and nothing contradicts them, but no single record states it outright.

2 on the grade · 2 on the identification · 1 alternative set aside · 1 open question

Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Confidence: Confirmed on the testator's own will as quoted verbatim in print (Hunt prints the residuary clause), with four further wills and the Chancery bill agreeing. The original register entries at the Borthwick Institute were not seen. ¶3

Coordinator review (1 Nov 2026): graded Probable, not Confirmed, under the overnight grading rule. No record read calls Mary 'my daughter'. Her place as his daughter rests on how several records fit together: she and her husband are residuary legatees in his will alongside couples Hunt identifies as his daughters; his son Cuthbert names a sister Marie; and his son John calls her sons 'nephews'. The case is very strong, but it is inferential, and it rests on Hunt's printed abstracts rather than the Borthwick registers. ¶9

The case for this identification 2

Why Marie is his daughter and the Brewster wife: (1) in his own will she and her husband John Symkinson are residuary legatees and executors alongside his three other daughters and their husbands (Hunt: 'four sons and four daughters'); (2) the will of Cuthbert Smythe of Fishlake, proved 2 Oct 1551 (York P&E 13:767), names brothers Francis and Richard and sisters Marie and Margaret; (3) William Parke of Stainforth's will, 1551 (York P&E 13:768), calls 'Wm. Smythe my father in lawe'; (4) his son John Smythe, alderman of Hull, in his will of 8 Aug 1592 (York P&E 25:1051-1052) names 'John Grene, John Smythe, William Brewster and Thomas Simkinson, my nephews' as executors and leaves a legacy 'to Dorothy of Scrobie my neece' (Dorothy Simkinson, Mary's daughter); (5) a Chancery bill of 1558-1579 (TNA C 2 Eliz. B31/1) is the 'Bill of Complaint by William Bruester and Mary his wife, the late wife of John Symkinson late of Doncaster, Yorks.' John Simkinson was buried at Doncaster 25 Dec 1562 (parish register, per Hunt). So Marie, daughter of William Smythe, wife of John Simkinson, is the Mary who married William Brewster of Scrooby (see his page). ¶2

Index check: the printed index of York Registry wills (YAS Record Series 14) has 'Smythe, William, senr., Stenford (bur. Hatfelde), Jan. 25, 1558' at register 16 fo. 46, which matches Hunt's date (25 Jan 1558/9) and reference (16:46) exactly, and also 'Man, Christopher, Scrowbie (Notts.), Oct. 11, 1558' and 'Symkinson, Thomas, Doncastre, alderman, Jan. 29, 1558'. YAS Record Series 22 has 'Smyth, John, Hull, alderman and merchant, Aug. 8, 1592', entered twice (will and codicil, as Hunt describes). So every will Hunt quotes exists where he says. Also in the 1554-68 index: 'Symkenson, Margaret, Doncaster, widow', will 15 Dec 1562 (5 Eliz.), ten days before John Simkinson's burial; possibly his mother (lead only). ¶8

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Caution: Hunt cites the same register folio (16:46) and the same probate date (2 May 1560) for this will and for William Smythe's (dated 25 Jan 1558/9), so one of the two folio citations may be a slip; the Borthwick index will settle it. Hunt (note 21) suggests one of William's wives (perhaps Agnes) was a Simkinson. Not entered. ¶7b
