Confidence: the grade and how it changed 2

Why only Speculative: no record seen calls Joen Olsen the son of Ole Jonsen. The case rests on (1) succession on the same land (Ole Jonsen buys out his sisters after 1722; Jon Olsen is the next named holder, 1753), (2) the patronymic, and (3) naming: Joen would carry the name of Ole's father Jon Tollefsen. A named rival exists: Ole Toresen bought ½... in Midbø in 1707 (same register page), so another Ole held land on the farm in the right generation. ¶2a

Confirmed as brother-in-law: the probate of 9 Apr 1740 at Midbø after Ole Torsen and Aasild Torsdatter (25669 fol. 539, view 359) names among the heirs their daughter '[Turi/Kari] Olsdaatter gift med Ole Jonsøn Midboe'. The 1753 deed calls his wife Kari; the probate's reading of her name is uncertain (Turi or Kari). ¶4

Corrections 1

Correction: not the father of Jon (Joen) Olsen of Midbø. In the deed read at Nordbø on 28 Jun 1753 (pantebok I 5, source 10870, No 311, view 99 left) 'Ole Jonsøn Midbøe' is one of four men who sell 'paa vore Hustruer Kari, Torbior, Inger og Ragnild Olsdøttres Vegne' to 'vores k. Broder Jon Olsøn'; so his wife was Kari Olsdatter, Jon's sister, and he was Jon's brother-in-law. He appears with Jon Olsen as a witness at Nordbø on 15 Oct 1753 (view 109 right). This explains the panteregister note that he bought land in Midbø 'af sin Søster' (his sister, or sister-in-law). The father link from Joen Olsen Midbø to this person should be removed; he can be kept as a collateral relative (husband of Jon's sister Kari). ¶3

Alternatives considered and set aside 1

Candidate father of Joen Olsen Midbø (see his page), Speculative. He was the son of Jon Tollefsen of Midbø (d. before 5 Aug 1722) and Guro Torbjørnsdatter: the 1722 probate after Jon Tollefsen names 'deris sammen avlede børn' Ole Jonsen (of age, so born before about 1701), Hage (married to Torbjørn Jacobsen), Anne 20 and Torbiør 17 (25692 fol. 534b-535a, view 547; card 6856 view 794). The Midbø page of panteregister I 1 (10915 fol. 21, view 63) then records that 'Ole Jonsøn kiøbt' land in Midbø 'af sin Søster' (tinglyst at Prestegaarden 26 June, year not given), i.e. he bought out his sisters' shares of their father's land. The next owner entered is 'Jon Olsøn', who redeemed land in Midbø in 1753, and whose wife Turi Jørgensdatter's probate of 1756 laid out land there; Jon's son Tore Jonsen took over in 1766 and undertook to keep his old father. ¶1
